# 0057_lung_blines-in-pulmonary-contusion

Analysis of the frames reveals the following:

*   **Frames 1-6:** In these sequential frames, a bright, hyperechoic pleural line is clearly visible near the top of the sector (around 2 cm depth). Below this line, the lung parenchyma exhibits a granular, "sand-like" texture. Comparing consecutive frames (e.g., Frame 1 to Frame 2, or Frame 5 to Frame 6), there is a noticeable change in the speckle pattern and the position of hyperechoic foci within the sub-pleural tissue. This variation indicates movement of the visceral pleura relative to the parietal pleura, which is the sign of **lung sliding**. The appearance is consistent with the "seashore sign" seen in B-mode (solid shore of the pleura, granular sand of the lung).

*   **Frames 7-8:** The view shifts significantly. In Frame 7, the left portion of the image is largely anechoic (black), while the hyperechoic pleural line is visible on the right side. This abrupt transition from lung tissue (seen in previous frames) to a black void (indicating air or lack of lung tissue) suggests the probe is at the edge of the lung. The black area represents a region where lung sliding is **absent** (typical of a pneumothorax). Frame 8 shows a similar edge view with the pleural line visible and the rest of the sector dark.

**Conclusion:**

The sequence demonstrates regions where lung sliding is clearly visible (Frames 1-6) and a transition zone where sliding is absent (Frames 7-8, showing the lung edge/pneumothorax interface). This fits the criteria for a **Lung Point**.

**Assessment: Both** (Pleural sliding is present in the lung fields but absent at the lung edge/transition point).
