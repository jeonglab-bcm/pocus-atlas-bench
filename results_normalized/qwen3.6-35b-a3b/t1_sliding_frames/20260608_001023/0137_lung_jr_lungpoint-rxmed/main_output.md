# 0137_lung_jr_lungpoint-rxmed

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

1.  **Observation of the Pleural Line:** In all frames, a bright, horizontal echogenic line is visible near the top, representing the pleural line.
2.  **Observation of Sub-pleural Tissue:** Below the pleural line, the echotexture (speckle pattern) of the lung parenchyma changes noticeably between consecutive frames.
    *   In **Frame 1**, there is a distinct hypoechoic (darker) area in the mid-section.
    *   In **Frame 2** and **Frame 3**, this dark area is less distinct or shifted, and the texture appears slightly different.
    *   In **Frame 4**, **Frame 5**, and **Frame 6**, the dark hypoechoic region reappears or is prominent, suggesting a shift in the lung tissue or artifacts related to its movement.
    *   In **Frame 7** and **Frame 8**, the texture changes again, becoming more homogeneous or shifting downward.
3.  **Signs of Sliding:** The key indicator here is the **"Speckle pattern below the pleural line differs between consecutive frames"**. The lung tissue is not static; its appearance fluctuates, which corresponds to the lung moving against the chest wall during respiration. This is the B-mode equivalent of the "seashore sign" (where you see a granular, sandy pattern below the static chest wall lines). There is no evidence of a static "barcode" or "stratosphere" sign, nor are the A-lines perfectly unchanged.

**Conclusion:**
Pleural sliding is **present**.
