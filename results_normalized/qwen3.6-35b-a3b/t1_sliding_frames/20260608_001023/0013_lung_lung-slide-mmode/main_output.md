# 0013_lung_lung-slide-mmode

**Analysis of Frames:**

*   **M-mode (Top Panel):** Throughout the sequence of frames, the M-mode tracing consistently displays parallel, horizontal horizontal lines. This pattern is known as the **"Stratosphere sign"** or **"Barcode sign"**. The area below the pleural line (which corresponds to the lung) shows the same horizontal stratification as the chest wall above it. In a normal lung with sliding, this area would appear granular or "sandy" (the "Seashore sign") due to the movement of the lung parenchyma relative to the chest wall. The lack of this granular texture indicates a lack of motion.
*   **B-mode (Bottom Panel):** The B-mode image shows a hyperechoic pleural line. Below this line, there are parallel horizontal reverberation artifacts known as **A-lines**.
*   **Comparison across frames:** Comparing the frames from start to finish, the position of the pleural line and the A-lines remains completely static. There is no "shimmering" of the pleural line, and there is no change in the speckle pattern below the pleura. The vertical green line and the white circle (likely the M-mode cursor) remain fixed relative to the anatomy, and the anatomy itself does not shift.

**Conclusion:**

The presence of the **Stratosphere/Barcode sign** on M-mode and the static nature of the pleural line and A-lines on B-mode are definitive signs that the lung is not moving against the chest wall.

Therefore, pleural sliding is **absent**.
