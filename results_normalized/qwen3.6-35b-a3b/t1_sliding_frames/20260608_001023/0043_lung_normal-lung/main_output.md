# 0043_lung_normal-lung

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**Frame-by-Frame Observation:**
In each frame, a hyperechoic (bright) horizontal line representing the pleura is visible near the top of the sector. Below this line, the lung tissue exhibits a characteristic granular or "sand-like" texture (subpleural interstitial space). As you move from one frame to the next (e.g., from Frame 1 to Frame 2, and so on), the specific pattern of the speckles and textures deep to the pleural line changes and shifts. The horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) are visible, but the tissue underneath them is clearly moving.

**Assessment of Signs:**
*   **Signs of sliding PRESENT:** The most significant indicator is the variation in the **speckle pattern below the pleural line**. The texture is not frozen; it shifts and changes between frames. This dynamic movement of the subpleural tissue relative to the chest wall indicates that the visceral pleura is sliding against the parietal pleura during respiration. The "shimmering" quality of the tissue just below the bright pleural line is also evident.
*   **Signs of sliding ABSENT:** There is no evidence of a static, fixed lung. The lines are not perfectly rigid, and the sub-pleural texture is not unchanging. There is no "barcode" or "stratosphere" sign equivalent (a completely static pattern).

**Conclusion:**
Pleural sliding is **present**. The dynamic changes in the subpleural speckle pattern across the frames confirm normal lung movement.
