# 0045_lung_double-lung-point

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**

*   **Frames 1-4:** The images display a clear, bright, horizontal hyperechoic line representing the pleural interface. Deep to this line (below it), there is a granular, textured appearance. This texture is characteristic of the lung parenchyma. Unlike a static fluid collection or the air-filled space of a pneumothorax (which would appear black and anechoic with static horizontal A-lines), the area beneath the pleural line shows a "shimmering" or granular quality.
*   **Frames 5-8:** The granular texture persists. While static images make it harder to see motion, the subtle variations in the echotexture immediately deep to the pleural line across the frames suggest the "seashore sign" pattern where the upper layers (chest wall) differ from the lower layer (lung). The pleural line itself does not appear fixed and sharply defined in a way that suggests pathology like pneumothorax; rather, it is the interface of two moving surfaces.

**Conclusion:**

Based on the presence of the granular, textured appearance immediately deep to the pleural line (often described as the "sea" part of the seashore sign) and the lack of a completely static, anechoic space or perfectly static horizontal A-lines, the sub-pleural tissues are moving relative to the transducer.

**Pleural sliding is present.**
