# 0125_lung_jr_lack-of-lung-sliding

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames provided:

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**
*   **Anatomy:** The images display an intercostal view of the lung. We can clearly identify the hyperechoic (bright) curvilinear structures at the top, which are the ribs. Between them, acoustic shadows (dark vertical bands) are cast downwards.
*   **Pleural Line:** Below the ribs, a distinct, bright, horizontal echogenic line is visible. This represents the pleural line (interface between the parietal and visceral pleura).
*   **Artifacts:** Below the pleural line, there are multiple horizontal, parallel, hyperechoic lines. These are reverberation artifacts known as **A-lines**.
*   **Motion Analysis:** Comparing Frame 1 through Frame 8, there is **no perceptible movement**.
    *   The position of the pleural line relative to the ribs remains fixed.
    *   The A-lines remain in the exact same positions and do not shift up or down.
    *   The speckle pattern (the granular grey texture) just below the pleural line is identical in every frame. There is no "shimmering" or sliding motion of the lung tissue against the chest wall.

**Conclusion:**
The absence of movement in the pleural line and the static nature of the A-lines and sub-pleural tissue across all frames are classic signs of **absent pleural sliding**. This finding is often associated with a pneumothorax (air in the pleural space preventing the sliding motion).

**Signs of sliding ABSENT**
