# 0049_lung_pleural-shred-sign

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**
*   **Pleural Line:** In all frames, there is a distinct, bright, horizontal echogenic line representing the pleura. This line appears somewhat irregular and "shimmering" rather than a single, perfectly rigid, static line.
*   **Sub-pleural Area:** Below the pleural line, there are horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) and a vertical hyperechoic artifact (likely a B-line or comet-tail artifact).
*   **Motion/Texture:** Comparing consecutive frames (e.g., Frame 1 vs. Frame 4 vs. Frame 8), the speckle pattern (the grey "snow" texture) of the lung parenchyma immediately below the pleural line shifts and changes. The horizontal A-lines also show slight variations in intensity and position. This indicates that the lung tissue is moving relative to the chest wall/probe. This is the B-mode equivalent of the "Seashore sign" seen in M-mode, where the granular pattern of the moving lung slides beneath the static pleural line.

**Conclusion:**
The observation of a shimmering pleural line and, more importantly, the variation in the sub-pleural speckle pattern between consecutive frames confirms that the lung is expanding and contracting.

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**
