# 0057_lung_blines-in-pulmonary-contusion

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**Frame-by-frame observation:**
- **Frames 1-6:** A distinct, hyperechoic pleural line is visible near the top of the image (around the 2-3 cm depth mark). Below this line, the sub-pleural space exhibits a granular, "sandy" texture rather than the clean, anechoic appearance of a pneumothorax or the perfectly static horizontal lines of A-lines.
- **Vertical Artifacts:** There appear to be vertical hyperechoic reverberation artifacts (B-lines or comet-tails) originating from the pleural line in several frames (e.g., frames 1, 2, 5).
- **Dynamic Movement:** When comparing consecutive frames (e.g., Frame 1 to Frame 2 to Frame 3), the specific pattern of the granular lung tissue below the pleural line changes slightly. The "speckle pattern" is not static; it shifts and shimmers. This indicates relative motion between the lung surface and the chest wall.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of the granular, textured appearance below the pleural line (often described as the "sandy" appearance in M-mode, but visible as tissue texture here) which varies between frames, along with the presence of vertical artifacts, confirms that the lung is expanding and sliding against the chest wall.
