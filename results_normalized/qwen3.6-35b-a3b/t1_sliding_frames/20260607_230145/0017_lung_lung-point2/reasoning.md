**2. Evaluate the signs:**

*   **Signs of sliding PRESENT:**
    *   **Shimmering/granular pleural line:** In the 2D view, the area below the pleural line isn't just black; it has texture.
    *   **Seashore sign on M-mode:** This is the most definitive sign. The top part of the M-mode is the static chest wall (horizontal lines). The bottom part is the moving lung, which creates a granular, sandy appearance. This is clearly visible across all frames from depth ~1.5cm to 3.5cm.
    *   **Speckle pattern variation:** Comparing Frame 1 to Frame 2 (and subsequent frames), the granular pattern below the pleural line changes shape and position, indicating movement of the lung relative to the chest wall.

*   **Signs of sliding ABSENT:**
    *   I do not see a "Stratosphere" or "Barcode" sign. That would look like horizontal lines continuing all the way down. Instead, there is a clear disruption of the horizontal lines into a granular pattern at the level of the pleura.
    *   I do not see static A-lines that never change. The texture below the pleural line is dynamic.

**3. Conclusion:**

The presence of the "Seashore sign" in M-mode (granular pattern below the pleural line) and the visible movement (change in speckle pattern) between frames confirm that pleural sliding is present. This indicates that the lung is expanding and contracting against the chest wall, which is a normal finding (or indicates lung re-expansion if previously collapsed, but here it just signifies sliding).

**Final check:**
- Top panel: 2D ultrasound showing lung tissue.

Therefore, the conclusion is "Present".
