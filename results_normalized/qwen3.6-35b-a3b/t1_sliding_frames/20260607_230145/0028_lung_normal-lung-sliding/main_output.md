# 0028_lung_normal-lung-sliding

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**Observation:**
The ultrasound images display the chest wall layers and the pleural line, which appears as a bright, horizontal, hyperechoic interface. In the space beneath the pleural line, the lung tissue exhibits a granular, textured appearance.

Comparing the frames from start to finish, there is a subtle but distinct movement of the pleural line and the underlying lung tissue. The "speckle" or texture pattern just below the bright pleural line shifts slightly between frames, indicating that the visceral pleura is moving against the parietal pleura. This dynamic movement is the B-mode equivalent of the "seashore sign" seen on M-mode ultrasound. The A-lines (horizontal reverberation artifacts) might be faintly visible deeper down, but the primary characteristic is the motion at the pleural interface.

**Conclusion:**
**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.** The visible motion of the pleural line and the changing speckle pattern beneath it confirm normal lung excursion.
