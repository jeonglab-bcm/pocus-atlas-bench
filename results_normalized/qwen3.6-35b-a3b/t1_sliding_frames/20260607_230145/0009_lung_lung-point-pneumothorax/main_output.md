# 0009_lung_lung-point-pneumothorax

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**
*   **Pleural Line:** In all frames, a distinct, bright (hyperechoic), horizontal line is visible near the top of the image (approximately 2-3 cm depth). This represents the visceral and parietal pleura.
*   **Sub-pleural Region:** Below this bright line, there is a granular, "snowstorm"-like texture. This is the lung parenchyma.
*   **Deeper Structures:** Horizontal reverberation artifacts, known as A-lines, are visible deeper in the image (e.g., around 4 cm, 8 cm, and 12 cm), which are characteristic of aerated lung tissue.
*   **Comparison across frames:** When comparing the frames (e.g., Frame 1 vs. Frame 4 vs. Frame 8), the granular texture of the lung parenchyma just below the pleural line shows subtle shifts in brightness and speckle pattern. The pleural line itself appears to exhibit slight movement or "shimmering" at its interface with the lung tissue. The A-lines are present but the overall speckle pattern of the lung tissue is not static; it changes slightly between frames, which is a key indicator of motion.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of the granular "sandy" appearance of the lung tissue below the pleural line, which changes slightly between consecutive frames (speckle pattern variation), confirms that the lung is moving relative to the chest wall. This is the B-mode equivalent of the "seashore sign" seen in M-mode.

**Pleural sliding is present.**
