# 0125_lung_jr_lack-of-lung-sliding

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**
*   **Anatomy:** The images show a standard intercostal view. The bright, curved structures at the very top are the ribs. The bright, horizontal line beneath the intercostal space is the pleural line.
*   **Sub-pleural Region:** Below the pleural line, there are horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines).
*   **Movement:** By comparing the first frame to the last frame, there is a clear downward shift of the horizontal artifacts and the sub-pleural texture. The "sand" (lung tissue) is moving beneath the "water" (chest wall/transducer interface). The horizontal lines do not stay in a fixed position; they drift downwards, indicating the expansion of the lung during inspiration.

**Sign Assessment:**
*   **Signs of sliding PRESENT:** The most definitive sign here is that the **"Speckle pattern below the pleural line differs between consecutive frames"**. The horizontal A-lines shift position, which is the B-mode equivalent of the "Seashore sign" seen in M-mode ultrasound. This movement indicates that the visceral and parietal pleura are sliding past one another.
*   **Signs of sliding ABSENT:** The A-lines are not perfectly static; they are moving. Therefore, the "Stratosphere / barcode sign" is not present.

**Conclusion:**
Pleural sliding is **present**. The visible movement of the sub-pleural structures confirms normal respiratory motion.
