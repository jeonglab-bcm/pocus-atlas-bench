# 0043_lung_normal-lung

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**Observation:**
Looking at the series of images, we see a hyperechoic horizontal line at the top, which represents the pleural line. Below this line, there are horizontal reverberation artifacts known as A-lines. When comparing the frames from start to finish (e.g., Frame 1 vs. Frame 8), there is a subtle but distinct change in the texture and position of the tissue layers just beneath the pleural line. The "speckle" pattern or granular appearance of the lung parenchyma beneath the pleura is shifting. It is not a frozen, static image. This movement indicates that the visceral pleura is moving against the parietal pleura during the respiratory cycle.

**Conclusion:**
**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**

The key indicator is the **variation in the sub-pleural speckle between consecutive frames**, which signifies the movement of the lung surface. If sliding were absent (as in a pneumothorax), the A-lines and the tissue below the pleural line would remain perfectly static and unchanged across all frames.
