# 0038_lung_light-beam-sign

Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**
*   **Pleural Line:** In all frames (1 through 8), there is a distinct, hyperechoic (bright white) horizontal line running across the upper-middle section of the image. This represents the pleural interface.
*   **Motion:** Comparing the position of this bright line across the sequence, it remains in a fixed position relative to the chest wall structures above it. There is no visible "shimmering," back-and-forth movement, or granular variation that would indicate the visceral pleura sliding against the parietal pleura.
*   **Sub-pleural Region:** The area immediately below the pleural line is dark (anechoic). There are faint, horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) visible deeper in the field. These horizontal lines maintain their position and spacing across all frames, appearing perfectly static.
*   **Lung Point:** There is no evidence of a transition point where sliding occurs in one area but not another; the entire visible pleural interface appears static.

**Assessment of Signs:**
*   **Signs of sliding PRESENT:** None observed. The pleural line is not shimmering, and there is no motion in the sub-pleural speckle pattern.
*   **Signs of sliding ABSENT:** The pleural line is smooth, sharply defined, and static. The A-lines deep to the pleura are static and unchanged across frames. This is consistent with the "barcode" or "stratosphere" sign often seen in M-mode for pneumothorax, although M-mode is not provided here, the static B-mode appearance correlates with it.

**Conclusion:**
**Absent**
