# 0045_lung_double-lung-point

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**Observation:**
Across all eight frames, the image remains completely static.
- The hyperechoic pleural line does not change position or shape; it appears fixed.
- The horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) below the pleural line are perfectly preserved in the same location in every frame.
- There is no "shimmering" or granular movement of the pleural interface that would indicate the visceral and parietal pleura sliding against each other.
- The speckle pattern in the lung tissue deep to the pleura is identical in every frame, showing no variation.

**Conclusion:**
**Pleural sliding is ABSENT.**

The lack of movement between the lung and chest wall, characterized by static A-lines and a fixed pleural line, is a classic sign of a pneumothorax (where air prevents the lung from expanding against the chest wall). The specific sign often associated with this in M-mode would be the "Stratosphere" or "Barcode" sign.
