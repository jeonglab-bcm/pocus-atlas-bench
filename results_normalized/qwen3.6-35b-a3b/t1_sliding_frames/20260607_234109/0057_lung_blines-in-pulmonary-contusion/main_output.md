# 0057_lung_blines-in-pulmonary-contusion

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**
*   **Frames 1-6:** These frames show a clear hyperechoic (bright) pleural line near the top of the ultrasound sector (approximately 2-3 cm depth). Below this line, the lung parenchyma exhibits a granular, "shimmering" texture rather than a static, repetitive horizontal pattern (A-lines).
*   **Vertical Artifacts:** There are vertical, hyperechoic artifacts extending downwards from the pleural line, consistent with B-lines (comet-tail artifacts).
*   **Motion:** Comparing consecutive frames (e.g., Frame 1 to Frame 2, or Frame 2 to Frame 3), the texture and speckle pattern in the subpleural region (the grey area below the bright line) change slightly. This indicates movement of the visceral pleura relative to the chest wall.
*   **Frames 7-8:** These frames appear darker and less distinct, possibly due to the probe moving slightly out of the optimal window or changing angle, but the preceding frames provide sufficient evidence of lung mechanics.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of the granular "shimmering" appearance below the pleural line, the presence of B-lines, and the variation in the sub-pleural speckle pattern between consecutive frames are all classic signs of **pleural sliding present**. There is no evidence of a static lung (pneumothorax) or a "lung point" transition in the visible field.

**Assessment:** Pleural sliding is **present**.
