# 0019_lung_no-lung-sliding

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**Observations:**
- The ultrasound images display a standard intercostal view, showing the chest wall layers at the top, followed by two ribs (bright, curved structures with dark acoustic shadows behind them).
- Between the ribs, the pleural line is visible as a distinct, bright horizontal interface.
- Below the pleural line, horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) are clearly visible.
- Comparing the frames from start to finish (Frame 1 to Frame 8), there is a subtle but distinct variation in the position of the horizontal lines (A-lines) and the texture of the tissue just below the pleural line. The sub-pleural structures appear to shift slightly downwards in each successive frame.
- This motion indicates that the visceral pleura is gliding against the parietal pleura. The "shimmering" or granular appearance of the pleural line and the shifting speckle pattern below it are classic indicators of this movement.

**Conclusion:**
**Pleural sliding is present.** The visible movement of the pleural line and the underlying lung tissue (sub-pleural speckle) between consecutive frames confirms the presence of lung sliding, which is a normal finding indicating that the lung is fully expanded and apposed to the chest wall.
