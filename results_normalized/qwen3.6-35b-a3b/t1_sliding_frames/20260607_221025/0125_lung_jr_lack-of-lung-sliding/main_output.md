# 0125_lung_jr_lack-of-lung-sliding

**Analysis of Frames:**

1.  **Observation of Structures:** The ultrasound images show a horizontal, hyperechoic (bright) line near the top of the screen, which represents the pleural line. Beneath this line, there are multiple parallel, horizontal hyperechoic lines extending downwards. These are reverberation artifacts known as A-lines.
2.  **Comparison of Consecutive Frames:** When examining the sequence of frames from 1 to 8, the position of the pleural line and the A-lines below it appears completely static. The horizontal lines maintain their exact depth and spacing across all frames. There is no visible "shimmering" or movement of the pleural line or the underlying tissue.
3.  **Sign Identification:**
    *   The presence of perfectly static, parallel horizontal lines (A-lines) is a key feature.
    *   The absence of movement in the area below the pleural line means the characteristic "granular" or "speckled" pattern of the lung parenchyma (the "sea" in the seashore sign) is not visible or is stationary.
    *   This pattern corresponds to the **Stratosphere sign** (or Barcode sign), which is seen when lung sliding is absent.

**Conclusion:**

Based on the observation that the pleural line and the A-lines remain perfectly static and unchanged across all consecutive frames, showing no signs of movement or shimmering, the assessment is that **pleural sliding is absent**. This is consistent with the presence of the stratosphere/barcode sign.
