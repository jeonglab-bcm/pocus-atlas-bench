Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**Observation:**
The ultrasound images display a pleural line (the bright horizontal interface near the top). Deep to this line, particularly in the central and left portions of the image, there are distinct, horizontal reverberation artifacts known as **A-lines**. These A-lines are static and parallel to the pleural line, which is a classic sign of a pneumothorax where air separates the lung from the chest wall, preventing movement.

However, looking at the right side of the pleural line, there is a distinct, brighter, and more echogenic focus. In the later frames (4-8), this area appears more prominent and textured compared to the static A-lines seen in the first few frames. This suggests a transition zone. The area with A-lines indicates **absent sliding** (pneumothorax). The brighter, potentially granular area on the right suggests the presence of lung tissue where sliding would occur.

**Conclusion:**
The images show a transition between a region with static A-lines (no sliding) and a region with a brighter, likely granular interface (sliding). This pattern is characteristic of a **Lung Point**.

Therefore, pleural sliding is: **Both (Lung Point)**.
