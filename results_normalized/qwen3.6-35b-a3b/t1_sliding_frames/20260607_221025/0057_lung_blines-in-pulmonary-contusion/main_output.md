# 0057_lung_blines-in-pulmonary-contusion

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**

*   **Frames 1-6:** These frames display a bright, horizontal pleural line near the top of the sector. Below this line, the tissue exhibits a granular, heterogeneous texture (often described as the "sandy" appearance of lung parenchyma). Crucially, looking closely at the sub-pleural tissue and the vertical artifacts (B-lines), their position and intensity change slightly between consecutive frames. For example, the bright vertical artifacts shift position and the speckle pattern evolves. This movement of the sub-pleural tissue relative to the fixed probe indicates that the lung is moving against the chest wall. This is a sign of **Sliding Present**.

*   **Frame 7:** The view shifts significantly. The left portion of the image is largely anechoic (black). In lung ultrasound, a large anechoic area adjacent to a bright pleural line often represents the "black zone" of a pneumothorax, where air in the pleural space prevents ultrasound transmission, resulting in no signal below the pleura. The pleural line is visible on the far right edge. This area represents a region of **Absent Sliding** (pneumothorax).

*   **Frame 8:** The probe appears to have moved further. The pleural line is visible on the left side, and the area to the right is dark/anechoic, consistent with the pneumothorax field seen in Frame 7.

**Conclusion:**

The sequence demonstrates a transition from a region of normal lung with moving tissue (Frames 1-6, **Sliding Present**) to a region of pneumothorax with no signal (Frame 7, **Absent Sliding**). This visible transition point where sliding is present on one side (the lung) and absent on the other (the pneumothorax) is the pathognomonic sign of a **Lung Point**.

Therefore, the assessment is: **Both** (signs of both present and absent sliding are observed, indicating a Lung Point).
