# 0023_lung_pneumothorax-with-lung-point

Analysis of the frames:

1.  **Pleural Line:** In all frames, a distinct, hyperechoic (bright) horizontal line is visible near the top of the fan-shaped ultrasound field. This represents the pleural line.
2.  **Sub-pleural Region:** Below the pleural line, the tissue exhibits a granular, "sandy" appearance. This is characteristic of the lung parenchyma.
3.  **Artifacts:** There are vertical hyperechoic lines extending downwards from the pleural line. These are B-lines (comet-tail artifacts). B-lines are a sign of lung sliding because they move with the pleura during respiration.
4.  **Motion Analysis:** Comparing the consecutive frames, there is a subtle change in the speckle pattern (texture) of the lung tissue below the pleural line. The texture is not static; it exhibits the "shimmering" or granular movement associated with the lung expanding and contracting against the chest wall. This corresponds to the "seashore sign" seen on M-mode, where the granular lung tissue moves below the static pleural line (though in B-mode video, we see the granular texture shifting).
5.  **Exclusion of Absence:** The image does not show the "stratosphere" or "barcode" sign (which would indicate a pneumothorax with a static pleural line and only horizontal A-lines). Instead, the presence of B-lines and the granular lung tissue moving indicates that the lung is in contact with the chest wall.

**Conclusion:**

Based on the presence of the granular subpleural lung tissue (consistent with the seashore sign), the presence of B-lines originating from the pleural line, and the visible speckle pattern variation between frames indicating motion, pleural sliding is **present**.
