# 0045_lung_double-lung-point

**Frame-by-frame Analysis:**

*   **Frames 1-8:** Each frame displays a standard lung ultrasound view. At the top, there is the chest wall (muscle and subcutaneous tissue) appearing as layered, horizontal hyperechoic and hypoechoic bands.
*   **Pleural Line:** Below the chest wall, there is a distinct, bright, hyperechoic horizontal line representing the pleural interface.
*   **Sub-pleural Region (Below the line):** Immediately beneath the pleural line, the tissue appears granular and echogenic. This texture is often described as "sandy" or speckled. It is distinct from the layered structure of the chest wall above.
*   **Vertical Artifacts:** In some frames (e.g., Frame 4, 5), there appear to be faint vertical hyperechoic lines extending downwards from the pleura, which could be B-lines (comet-tail artifacts) or reverberation artifacts.
*   **Comparison:** The "sand" pattern below the pleural line is consistent across the frames. There are no clear, repeating horizontal A-lines (which would appear as parallel bright lines equidistant from the pleura) dominating the field in a way that suggests the "stratosphere" sign. The texture is characteristic of lung parenchyma.

**Conclusion:**

The images exhibit the **Seashore sign**. The chest wall represents the "sea" (layered horizontal structures), the pleural line is the "shore," and the granular, speckled lung tissue below represents the "sand." The presence of this granular "sand" pattern below the pleural line indicates that the lung is expanding and sliding against the chest wall. If pleural sliding were absent (as in pneumothorax), the area below the pleural line would show the "Stratosphere" or "Barcode" sign (identical horizontal lines above and below). The granular texture and lack of a perfect barcode pattern confirm that **pleural sliding is present**.
