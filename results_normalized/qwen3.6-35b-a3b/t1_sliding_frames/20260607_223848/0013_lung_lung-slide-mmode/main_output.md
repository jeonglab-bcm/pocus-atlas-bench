# 0013_lung_lung-slide-mmode

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**
*   **B-Mode (Bottom Image):** In all frames, we see a hyperechoic (bright) horizontal line which represents the pleural line. Above this line, there are horizontal reverberation artifacts known as A-lines. Below the pleural line, there is lung parenchyma. The area marked with "B" is on the left side.
*   **M-Mode (Top Image):** The vertical teal line in the B-mode corresponds to the M-mode scan line.
    *   **Above the pleural line:** We see distinct, horizontal parallel lines. This represents the static chest wall (skin, subcutaneous tissue, muscle) and is the "sky" part of the sign.
    *   **Below the pleural line:** Instead of continuing horizontal lines (which would indicate a static lung/pneumothorax, known as the Stratosphere or Barcode sign), we see a granular, speckled, or "noisy" pattern. This represents the movement of the lung parenchyma against the chest wall. This is the "sand" part of the sign.
*   **Comparison:** The granular texture in the M-mode (the "sand") indicates motion. The pattern is not static; it represents the back-and-forth movement of the lung during respiration.

**Conclusion:**
The images demonstrate a classic **Seashore sign** on M-mode (horizontal static lines above the pleural line and a granular, moving pattern below it). This confirms that the visceral and parietal pleura are moving against each other.

**Pleural sliding is present.**
