# 0125_lung_jr_lack-of-lung-sliding

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**
- The images show a standard view of the lung interface, including the chest wall muscles and the hyperechoic (bright) pleural line.
- Below the pleural line, there are repetitive, horizontal, equidistant lines. These are **A-lines**, which are reverberation artifacts caused by the reflection of sound waves off the pleural surface.
- In the center of the image, there is a vertical dark shadow, likely caused by the acoustic shadow of a rib.
- Comparing Frame 1 through Frame 8, the position of the pleural line and the horizontal A-lines remains perfectly stationary. There is no "shimmering" or vertical displacement of the pleural line, and the texture of the tissue below does not change between frames.

**Conclusion:**
The pleural line and the sub-pleural artifacts are static. There is no variation in the speckle pattern or position of structures between frames. This lack of movement indicates that the lung is not expanding and contracting against the chest wall. This pattern is characteristic of the "stratosphere sign" (or "barcode sign") seen in M-mode, which in B-mode manifests as static horizontal lines.

Therefore, pleural sliding is **absent**. This finding is typically associated with a pneumothorax.
