# 0043_lung_normal-lung

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**
- The images display a standard lung ultrasound interface. At the top of the sector, a bright, horizontal hyperechoic line is visible, representing the pleural line.
- Below this pleural line, there are multiple parallel horizontal hyperechoic lines. These are reverberation artifacts known as **A-lines**, which are characteristic of an aerated lung or pneumothorax.
- Comparing the frames from start to finish, the pleural line and the A-lines below it remain in a fixed position. There is no vertical movement (shimmering) of the pleural line, and the horizontal lines do not shift or change intensity. The sub-pleural speckle pattern is static across all images.

**Assessment:**
- **Signs of sliding PRESENT:** None. There is no shimmering of the pleural line, no B-lines (vertical artifacts), and no change in the speckle pattern indicating movement.
- **Signs of sliding ABSENT:** Present. The pleural line is static. The A-lines are perfectly static and unchanged across all frames, indicating no respiratory motion of the visceral and parietal pleura. This static appearance corresponds to the "Stratosphere sign" or "Barcode sign" typically seen on M-mode.
- **Signs of BOTH:** None. There is no transition zone visible.

**Conclusion:**
**Pleural sliding is absent.**
