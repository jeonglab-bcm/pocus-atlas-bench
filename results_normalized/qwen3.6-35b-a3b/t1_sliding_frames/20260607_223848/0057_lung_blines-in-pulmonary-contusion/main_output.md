# 0057_lung_blines-in-pulmonary-contusion

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**Observation:**
- **Frames 1-6:** These frames display a clear, hyperechoic (bright) horizontal line near the top, which corresponds to the pleural line. Below this line, the lung parenchyma exhibits a granular, "sandy" texture. As you move from one frame to the next (e.g., comparing Frame 1 to Frame 2), there is a subtle but distinct shift in the position of the tissue structures and the bright speckle pattern below the pleural line. This "shimmering" or granular motion is characteristic of the lung expanding and contracting against the chest wall. There are also some vertical hyperechoic artifacts (B-lines) originating from the pleural line, which are also dynamic.
- **Frames 7-8:** The view shifts, likely showing the edge of the lung field or a rib shadow (the dark area on the left), but the initial sequence provides sufficient evidence.

**Assessment:**
The key indicator here is the **granular, sandy appearance** of the sub-pleural space and the **variation in the speckle pattern** between consecutive frames. This movement confirms that the visceral and parietal pleura are moving against each other. There is no evidence of a static "barcode" pattern or completely fixed A-lines that would suggest absent sliding.

**Conclusion:**
**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**
