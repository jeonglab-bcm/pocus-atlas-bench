# 0021_lung_large-pleural-effusion

Based on the visual evidence in the sequential frames:

**Observation:**
The ultrasound images display the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point).
1.  **Effusion:** There is a prominent anechoic (black) space visible in the sector. This space is bounded superiorly by the chest wall/pleura and laterally by rib shadows, consistent with the **Quad sign**. This indicates a significant pleural effusion.
2.  **Consolidation:** Within or adjacent to the fluid collection, there is an echogenic (grey), tissue-like area. This appearance is consistent with **hepatization** (tissue-like consolidation) of the lung parenchyma. The lung tissue appears solid rather than aerated (which would show A-lines). There appear to be hyperechoic foci within this tissue, suggestive of **air bronchograms**. The interface between the fluid and the lung tissue suggests the **Jellyfish sign** (floating lung) or simply a consolidated lung compressed by the effusion.

**Conclusion:**
The images show clear signs of both a pleural effusion (fluid collection) and lung consolidation (solid, hepatized lung tissue). Therefore, the PLAPS pattern is true, and the type is "both".
