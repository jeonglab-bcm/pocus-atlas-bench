# 0018_lung_pericardial-pleural-effusions

Based on the visual evidence in the sequential frames:

1.  **Effusion Signs:** There is a large, anechoic (black) space visible in the dependent portion of the thoracic cavity. This space is bounded laterally by hyperechoic rib shadows with posterior acoustic shadowing and superiorly by the pleural line, forming a **Quad sign**. This indicates a significant pleural effusion.
2.  **Consolidation Signs:** Within or adjacent to the fluid collection, there is a large area of lung parenchyma that has lost its normal aeration and appears solid, homogeneous, and echogenic, resembling liver tissue (**Tissue-like hepatization**). The interface between this consolidated lung and the surrounding fluid is irregular and jagged, consistent with the **Shred sign**.
3.  **Combined Signs:** The appearance of the consolidated/atelectatic lung tissue floating within the fluid collection is reminiscent of a **Jellyfish sign**.

The presence of both significant free fluid (effusion) and solid, hepatized lung tissue (consolidation/atelectasis) in the posterior dependent zone confirms the PLAPS pattern.

**Conclusion:**
**plaps = true**
**type = "both"**
