# 0051_lung_jellyfish-sign

The ultrasound images display a large anechoic (black) area in the posterior thoracic space, which is characteristic of a significant pleural effusion. This fluid collection creates a "Quad sign" appearance, bounded by the chest wall superiorly and the lung/diaphragm inferiorly.

Within this fluid collection, there is a distinct hyperechoic, tissue-like structure that appears to be floating or undulating. This represents the lung parenchyma, which has become consolidated (hepatized) and atelectatic, likely due to compressive atelectasis from the fluid. This specific appearance of lung tissue floating within a pleural effusion is known as the "Jellyfish sign." The presence of this solid, tissue-like structure within the fluid confirms underlying consolidation/atelectasis.
