# 0123_lung_jr_large-pleural-effusion

Based on the visual analysis of the ultrasound frames:

**Observation:**
The images display a large, anechoic (black) area occupying the majority of the lower and central field of view. This represents a significant fluid collection. Within this fluid, there is a distinct, echogenic (grey/white), floating structure. This structure resembles the undulating body of a jellyfish, which is a classic presentation of atelectatic lung tissue floating within a pleural effusion. This is known as the **Jellyfish sign**. The presence of this sign, along with the large anechoic space (which resembles the **Quad sign** where the fluid is bounded by anatomical structures), indicates a significant pleural effusion in the posterior dependent zone. The floating lung tissue itself appears solid (hepatized), which is expected for compressive atelectasis.

**Conclusion:**
- **plaps = true**: The presence of the Jellyfish sign and the large anechoic fluid collection confirms a PLAPS pattern.
- **type = "effusion"**: The dominant findings are fluid collection signs (the large anechoic space and the Jellyfish sign). While the floating lung is consolidated (atelectatic), it is secondary to the effusion and classified under the effusion signs in this context.
