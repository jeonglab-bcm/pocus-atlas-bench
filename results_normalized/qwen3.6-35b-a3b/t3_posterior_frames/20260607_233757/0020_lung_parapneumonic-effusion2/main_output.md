# 0020_lung_parapneumonic-effusion2

Based on the visual analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**Observation:**
The images display a classic appearance of the PLAPS (Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome) point.
1.  **Effusion:** There is a large, anechoic (black) space visible in the field of view. This fluid collection is bounded by the rib shadows (visible as hyperechoic lines with posterior acoustic shadowing on the sides) and the pleural/lung interface. This configuration creates the **Quad sign**, which is diagnostic for pleural effusion.
2.  **Consolidation:** Adjacent to and compressed by the fluid, there is a region of lung parenchyma that appears solid, homogeneous, and echogenic (gray/white), losing the normal spongy, aerated texture. This appearance is described as **tissue-like hepatization**. The lung tissue appears to be floating or adjacent to the fluid collection, resembling the **Jellyfish sign** (where the atelectatic lung floats in the effusion).

**Conclusion:**
The scan reveals both a significant pleural effusion and underlying lung consolidation (likely compressive atelectasis or pneumonia).
