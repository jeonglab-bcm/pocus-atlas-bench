# 0073_lung_2017-10-9-dove-in-pleural-fluid

The ultrasound images demonstrate a large anechoic (black) area in the dependent posterior zone, which corresponds to a pleural effusion. Within this fluid collection, there is a hyperechoic (bright), irregular structure that appears to be floating and undulating. This structure represents atelectatic (consolidated) lung tissue. This specific appearance—floating, undulating lung within a surrounding effusion—is known as the **Jellyfish sign**. Additionally, the fluid collection is bounded by rib shadows laterally, consistent with the **Quad sign**. The presence of these signs indicates fluid in the pleural space. While the floating lung is technically consolidated (atelectatic), the primary pattern observed is defined by the effusion signs, specifically the Jellyfish sign.
