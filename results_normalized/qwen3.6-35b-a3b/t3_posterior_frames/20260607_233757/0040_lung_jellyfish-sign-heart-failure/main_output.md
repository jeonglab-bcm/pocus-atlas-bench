Based on the visual evidence in the ultrasound frames:

1.  **Effusion Signs**: There is a prominent anechoic (black) space visible in the center of the images, particularly clear in frames 1-3 (above the tissue on the left) and frames 4-8 (to the left of the rib shadow on the right). This space is bounded by hyperechoic structures (ribs) laterally and appears to sit above the lung/diaphragm interface. This appearance is consistent with the **quad sign** (an anechoic space bounded by ribs and pleural line) or the presence of free fluid.
2.  **Consolidation Signs**: Adjacent to the fluid collection, particularly on the left side in the earlier frames and generally surrounding the fluid, the tissue appears echogenic and solid, resembling liver parenchyma (**tissue-like hepatization**). In frame 1, there is a distinct border that could represent the **shred sign** (irregular deep border) between the consolidated lung and the fluid. The tissue floating within or adjacent to the fluid suggests **atelectasis**, which often appears as consolidation in this context.
3.  **Jellyfish Sign**: In frames 1-3, the echogenic tissue structure on the left appears to be undulating or floating within the anechoic fluid space, which is characteristic of the **jellyfish sign** (atelectatic lung floating in pleural effusion).

**Conclusion:**
The images demonstrate both pleural effusion (anechoic space) and alveolar consolidation (tissue-like hepatized lung).
