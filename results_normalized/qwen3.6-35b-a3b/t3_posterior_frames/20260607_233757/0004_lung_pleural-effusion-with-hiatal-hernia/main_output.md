# 0004_lung_pleural-effusion-with-hiatal-hernia

The ultrasound images display a posterior lung zone (PLAPS point) with distinct pathological findings.

**Observations:**
*   **Consolidation:** There is a large, central area of lung parenchyma that appears echogenic and heterogeneous, resembling liver tissue (tissue-like hepatization). This indicates the loss of normal air content in the alveoli.
*   **Air Bronchograms:** Within the consolidated, hepatized lung tissue, there are distinct, irregular hyperechoic (bright) linear and punctate foci. These are static air bronchograms, confirming the presence of air-filled bronchi within the fluid-filled or consolidated alveoli.
*   **Effusion:** Adjacent to the consolidated lung tissue (visible primarily on the left side of the sector in the earlier frames and surrounding the consolidated area), there is a hypoechoic to anechoic space. This represents a pleural effusion. The appearance of the consolidated lung floating or abutting this fluid collection is consistent with the PLAPS pattern often seen in pneumonia with parapneumonic effusion.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of both lung consolidation (hepatization with air bronchograms) and pleural effusion confirms the PLAPS pattern.
