# 0030_lung_parapneumonic-effusion-with-b-lines

Based on the visual analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**Observation:**
The images display a large region of lung parenchyma that has lost its normal aerated, lung-line appearance. Instead, the tissue exhibits a heterogeneous, solid, and echogenic texture that resembles the liver (hepatization). This "tissue-like" appearance is a hallmark of alveolar consolidation. Within this consolidated area, there are visible hyperechoic (bright) linear and punctate structures, which are characteristic of **air bronchograms** (air-filled bronchi visible within the fluid-filled or consolidated alveoli). While there are some darker, hypoechoic areas, there is no large, distinct anechoic fluid collection forming a "quad sign" or "jellyfish sign" that would indicate a significant primary pleural effusion dominating the view. The primary pathology is the solidification of the lung tissue.

**Conclusion:**
- **plaps = true**
- **type = "consolidation"**
