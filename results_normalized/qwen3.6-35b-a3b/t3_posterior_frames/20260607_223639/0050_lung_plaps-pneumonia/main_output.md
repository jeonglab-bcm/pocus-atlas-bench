# 0050_lung_plaps-pneumonia

Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**Observation:**
The images display a posterior lung view, likely at the PLAPS point. The most prominent finding is a large, anechoic (black) space, which is indicative of a significant pleural effusion. This fluid collection is situated between the chest wall/pleura superiorly and the diaphragm/liver inferiorly. Within this fluid, there is an echogenic structure that appears to be the lung tissue. In the later frames, the relative positioning suggests the lung tissue is floating or undulating within the fluid, which is characteristic of the **Jellyfish sign** (atlectatic lung floating in pleural fluid). The fluid collection is large enough to create a space resembling the **Quad sign** (though full rib boundaries are not perfectly distinct in every frame, the anechoic space is the dominant feature). While the floating lung represents atelectasis (a form of consolidation/collapse), the primary and predominant pathology visualized is the large fluid collection.

**Conclusion:**
- **plaps = true**
- **type = "effusion"**
