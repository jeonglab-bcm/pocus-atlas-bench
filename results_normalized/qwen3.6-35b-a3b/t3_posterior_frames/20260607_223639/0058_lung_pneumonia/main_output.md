# 0058_lung_pneumonia

Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**Observation:**
The images show a longitudinal view of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point).
1.  **Effusion Signs:** There is an anechoic (black) space visible in the upper part of the image, deep to the chest wall muscles and ribs but superficial to the lung tissue. This fluid collection separates the parietal pleura from the visceral pleura. While the full "Quad sign" is not perfectly framed, the presence of a significant anechoic space consistent with fluid is evident.
2.  **Consolidation Signs:** Deep to the fluid, the lung parenchyma is visible and appears abnormal.
    *   **Hepatization:** The lung tissue has lost its normal aerated, sponge-like appearance and instead appears solid, homogeneous, and echogenic, resembling liver tissue (hepatization).
    *   **Air Bronchograms:** Within this consolidated, solid-appearing area, there are multiple punctate and linear hyperechoic (bright white) foci. These represent air-filled bronchi floating within the fluid/solid lung, a classic sign of consolidation.

**Conclusion:**
The image displays signs of both pleural effusion (the anechoic space) and alveolar consolidation (hepatized lung with air bronchograms). This confirms the presence of a PLAPS pattern.
