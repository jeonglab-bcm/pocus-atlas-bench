# 0015_lung_plaps-consolidation

The ultrasound sequence displays signs characteristic of the Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome (PLAPS) point.

**Observation:**
*   **Effusion:** The first frame clearly demonstrates the **Quad sign**. There is a large, anechoic (black) space bounded superiorly by the pleural line/chest wall and laterally by rib shadows (hyperechoic lines with acoustic shadowing). This indicates a significant pleural effusion.
*   **Consolidation/Atelectasis:** Below the fluid in the first frame, and visible in the subsequent frames, is lung parenchyma that appears solid and hypoechoic/heterogeneous. This appearance is consistent with **tissue-like hepatization** of the lung, likely due to compressive atelectasis or pneumonia. The appearance of the lung tissue floating or compressed within/adjacent to the fluid is suggestive of the **Jellyfish sign** (atelectatic lung in effusion) or simply the interface of consolidation next to effusion. The subsequent frames (2-8) show lung tissue that lacks the normal A-line artifacts of aeration and instead displays a solid, tissue-like texture, confirming consolidation.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of a large anechoic fluid collection (effusion) alongside solid, hepatized lung tissue (consolidation) indicates a positive PLAPS finding involving both pathologies.
