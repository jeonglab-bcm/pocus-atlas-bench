# 0078_lung_jellyfish

The ultrasound images of the right posterior lung zone reveal a significant pathology in the dependent PLAPS point.

1.  **Effusion:** There is a large, anechoic (black) space occupying the majority of the image, particularly visible in the upper and central portions. This represents a pleural effusion. In the later frames (6-9), the fluid collection is well-defined, bounded superiorly by the chest wall/pleural line and laterally by rib shadows, consistent with the **quad sign**.
2.  **Consolidation:** Within the anechoic fluid, there is a distinct area of echogenic, tissue-like material. This lung parenchyma has lost its normal air content and appears solid, resembling liver tissue (**tissue-like hepatization**).
3.  **Combined Signs:** The consolidated lung fragment appears to be floating or suspended within the pleural fluid, creating the appearance of a **jellyfish sign**. The interface between the fluid and the lung is somewhat irregular.

Since both pleural effusion (fluid collection) and alveolar consolidation (solid, hepatized lung) are clearly present in the dependent posterior zone, this confirms the PLAPS pattern.
