# 0014_lung_septated-pleural-effusion

The ultrasound images display a large anechoic (black) space in the posterior costophrenic recess, which is characteristic of a pleural effusion. Within this fluid collection, there is a distinct, hyperechoic, undulating line and structure that represents the visceral pleura and the underlying lung parenchyma floating in the fluid. This is the **Jellyfish sign**, indicating atelectatic (collapsed) or consolidated lung floating in a pleural effusion.

Additionally, the floating lung tissue appears solid and echogenic, consistent with **tissue-like hepatization**, which is a sign of lung consolidation. The interface between the floating lung and the fluid is somewhat irregular, suggestive of the **shred sign**. The presence of a large fluid collection (anechoic space) with the lung floating within it fits the criteria for the **Quad sign** as well (though rib shadows are not clearly delineated, the geometry of the fluid pocket is evident).

Since there is clear evidence of free fluid (effusion) and the floating lung tissue appears consolidated/atelectatic, both patterns are present.
