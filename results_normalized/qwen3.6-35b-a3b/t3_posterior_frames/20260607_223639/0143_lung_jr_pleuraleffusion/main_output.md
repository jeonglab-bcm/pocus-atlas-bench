# 0143_lung_jr_pleuraleffusion

Based on the provided ultrasound frames of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point):

**Observations:**
1.  **Effusion Signs:** In the sequence, particularly in frames 5 through 8, a large, anechoic (dark) space is visible deep to the pleural line. This fluid collection is consistent with a **pleural effusion**. The appearance of this space bounded by the chest wall structures suggests the **Quad sign** (though the rib shadows are most distinct in the earlier frames on the left).
2.  **Consolidation Signs:** In the earlier frames (1-4), deep to the pleural line, there is an area of increased echogenicity that appears solid and "tissue-like" (resembling liver texture), which is indicative of **alveolar consolidation (hepatization)**. Additionally, prominent vertical hyperechoic artifacts (B-lines) arise from the pleural surface in these frames, which are often associated with interstitial syndrome or subpleural consolidation. The irregular border of the tissue-like area against the fluid could represent the **shred sign**.

**Conclusion:**
The images demonstrate signs of both a fluid collection (effusion) and solidified lung tissue (consolidation) in the dependent posterior zone.
