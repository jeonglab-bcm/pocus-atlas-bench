# 0007_lung_pleural-effusion-with-compressed-lung-and-spine-sign

The ultrasound images show a large anechoic (black) space in the posterior aspect of the chest, which is characteristic of a pleural effusion. Within this fluid collection, there is a floating, echogenic structure that corresponds to the atelectatic (collapsed) lung. This appearance creates the **"Jellyfish sign,"** where the floating lung parenchyma resembles a jellyfish in water. The fluid collection is bounded by the chest wall, consistent with the **"Quad sign"** seen in frames like the first and fifth. The presence of significant free fluid in the dependent posterior zone confirms the PLAPS pattern.
