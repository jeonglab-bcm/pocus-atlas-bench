The ultrasound images display a large, anechoic (black) space in the center, which is characteristic of a pleural effusion. Adjacent to and partially within this fluid collection is a region of echogenic, solid-appearing tissue that resembles the texture of the liver (hepatization). This solid tissue represents consolidated lung parenchyma or atelectasis. The appearance of the consolidated lung floating within the anechoic fluid is suggestive of the "Jellyfish sign," or at least significant consolidation associated with a moderate-to-large effusion. The combination of the anechoic fluid collection and the echogenic, tissue-like consolidation indicates the presence of both effusion and consolidation.
