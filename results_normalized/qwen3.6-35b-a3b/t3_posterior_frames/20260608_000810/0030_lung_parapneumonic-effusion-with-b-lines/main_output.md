# 0030_lung_parapneumonic-effusion-with-b-lines

Based on the visual analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**Observations:**
The images display a sector scan of the posterior lung zone (indicated by "RT H/T ZONE 2 LOW"). The most prominent feature is a large area of lung parenchyma that has lost its normal aerated, A-line appearance. Instead, the tissue exhibits a **tissue-like, echogenic, and granular texture** that closely resembles the liver parenchyma. This is the **hepatization sign**, indicative of alveolar consolidation.

Within this consolidated area, particularly visible in frames 3, 5, and 7, there are small, punctate hyperechoic (bright) foci. These are consistent with **static air bronchograms**, representing air-filled bronchi within the fluid-filled or solid alveoli.

There is no evidence of a large, anechoic (black) space characteristic of a significant pleural effusion (such as the "Quad sign"). While a small, round anechoic structure is visible within the consolidation in some frames, it appears to be a small vessel or localized fluid pocket rather than a free pleural effusion. The normal lung sliding and horizontal A-lines are absent.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of tissue-like hepatization and air bronchograms confirms the PLAPS pattern. The pattern is dominated by consolidation without a significant associated effusion.
