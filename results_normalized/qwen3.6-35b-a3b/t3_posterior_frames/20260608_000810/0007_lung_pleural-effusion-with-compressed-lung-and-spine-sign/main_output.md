# 0007_lung_pleural-effusion-with-compressed-lung-and-spine-sign

Based on the visual evidence in the ultrasound frames:

**Observation:**
The images display a sector scan of the posterior lung zone. There is a large, anechoic (black) region visible, particularly prominent in the left side of the images (e.g., Image 2, Image 3), which is characteristic of a pleural effusion. Adjacent to this fluid collection is a large area of echogenic, homogeneous tissue that resembles the texture of the liver ("tissue-like hepatization"), indicating alveolar consolidation. Within this consolidated lung tissue, there are small anechoic (black) tubular structures (visible clearly in Image 6), which likely represent the "vascular sign" (dilated pulmonary vessels within consolidated lung). The interface between the fluid and the lung is distinct. The combination of a dependent pleural effusion and underlying consolidated lung is the hallmark of the PLAPS (Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome) pattern.
