The ultrasound images display a sector scan of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point).

**Observations:**
*   **Effusion Signs:** There is a large, anechoic (black) space visible in the deep/posterior aspect of the image. This space is bounded superiorly by the pleural line (the interface between the chest wall and the lung/effusion) and inferiorly by the lung tissue. This appearance is consistent with the **Quad sign** (a quadrilateral anechoic space) or a large pleural effusion.
*   **Jellyfish Sign:** The red arrow points to a hyperechoic line/structure at the bottom of the anechoic space (deep to the fluid). This represents the atelectatic (collapsed) lung floating within the pleural fluid, creating the **"Jellyfish sign"**. This is a specific sign of a significant pleural effusion where the lung is compressed and floats in the surrounding fluid.
*   **Consolidation Signs:** While the floating lung is atelectatic (a form of compression), there is no clear evidence of "tissue-like hepatization" (solid liver-like texture) of the surrounding parenchyma distinct from the floating lung, nor are distinct "air bronchograms" or a "shred sign" clearly visible as the primary feature. The primary pathology is the fluid collection.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of the large anechoic space and the floating lung (Jellyfish sign) confirms the PLAPS pattern. The predominance of fluid signs points to an effusion.
