# 0040_lung_jellyfish-sign-heart-failure

The ultrasound images display a large anechoic (black) space, which is characteristic of a pleural effusion. This fluid collection creates a "Quad sign" where the space is bounded by the pleural line superiorly and the chest wall/rib shadows laterally. Adjacent to and within the region of the fluid, there is echogenic tissue with a homogeneous, solid texture resembling liver parenchyma, which is indicative of lung consolidation (hepatization). The presence of the fluid and the solid-appearing lung tissue together suggests a combination of pleural effusion and alveolar consolidation, often seen in the PLAPS (Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome) pattern. The echogenic structure floating within or adjacent to the fluid may represent the "Jellyfish sign" (atelectatic lung floating in fluid) or the edge of consolidated lung.
