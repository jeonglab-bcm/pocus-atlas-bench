# 0021_lung_large-pleural-effusion

The ultrasound images display a sector scan of the lung, likely at the PLAPS (Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome) point.

**Observations:**
*   **Effusion:** There is a distinct anechoic (black) area visible, particularly on the left side of the scan. This represents a pleural effusion. The shape and boundaries suggest the "quad sign" or a simple fluid collection in the pleural space.
*   **Consolidation:** Adjacent to the fluid collection (towards the right and bottom right), there is a region of echogenic, tissue-like parenchyma. This "hepatized" appearance (resembling liver tissue) indicates lung consolidation or atelectasis due to compression by the fluid or underlying pneumonia.
*   **Interface:** The boundary between the fluid and the consolidated lung is somewhat irregular, which can be described as the "shred sign" (irregular deep border of consolidation).

**Conclusion:**
The presence of both a pleural effusion (anechoic space) and lung consolidation (tissue-like hepatization) in the posterior dependent zone confirms the diagnosis.
