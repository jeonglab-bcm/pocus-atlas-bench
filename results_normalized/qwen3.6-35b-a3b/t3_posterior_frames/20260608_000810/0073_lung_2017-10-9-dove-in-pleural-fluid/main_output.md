# 0073_lung_2017-10-9-dove-in-pleural-fluid

Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**Observations:**
*   **Effusion Signs:** The most prominent feature is a large anechoic (black) region, indicating a significant fluid collection. This fluid creates a **Quad sign**, where the space is bounded superiorly by the parietal pleura, inferiorly by the visceral pleura/lung interface, and laterally by rib shadows (visible as acoustic shadows dropping down).
*   **Jellyfish Sign:** Within the anechoic fluid collection, there is a distinct, hyperechoic (bright), triangular or wedge-shaped structure that appears to be floating and undulating slightly between frames. This represents the atelectatic (collapsed) lung floating in the pleural effusion, known as the **Jellyfish sign**.
*   **Consolidation Signs:** While the floating lung tissue is atelectatic (and thus dense), there is no clear evidence of parenchymal hepatization (tissue-like texture of the lung itself) or air bronchograms separate from the atelectatic lung. The large echogenic area at the bottom right is consistent with the liver below the diaphragm.

**Conclusion:**
The image demonstrates classic signs of a pleural effusion in the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point), specifically the Quad sign and the Jellyfish sign.
