# 0029_lung_parapneumonic-effusion

Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

1.  **Observation:** The images display a posterior lung view (PLAPS point). There is a prominent anechoic (black) region, which represents a fluid collection, consistent with a pleural effusion. This fluid collection is bounded laterally by rib shadows (part of the quad sign structure, though the full quadrilateral isn't perfectly framed in every slice).
2.  **Consolidation:** Adjacent to and partially within the fluid, there is a large area of homogeneous, echogenic tissue that resembles the liver (hepatization). This indicates lung consolidation.
3.  **Specific Signs:**
    *   **Jellyfish sign:** In the initial frames (e.g., frame 1 and 2), a distinct, somewhat triangular or V-shaped echogenic structure is visible floating within the anechoic fluid space. This represents atelectatic lung tissue floating in the pleural effusion.
    *   **Tissue-like hepatization:** The lung parenchyma in the non-fluid areas appears solid and liver-like, lacking the normal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) of aerated lung.
    *   **Air bronchograms:** There are punctate hyperechoic foci visible within the consolidated tissue, suggestive of air bronchograms.

**Conclusion:**
The images show clear evidence of both pleural effusion (fluid) and lung consolidation (hepatization/atelectasis). The presence of floating lung tissue within the fluid confirms the "Jellyfish sign."
