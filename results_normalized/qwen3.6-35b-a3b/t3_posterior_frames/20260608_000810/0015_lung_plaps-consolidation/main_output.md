# 0015_lung_plaps-consolidation

Based on the visual evidence in the sequential frames:

1.  **Effusion Signs:** The first image clearly displays a large, anechoic (black) space. This is characteristic of a fluid collection. In the context of a posterior lung ultrasound (PLAPS point), this represents a pleural effusion. The geometry, with boundaries formed by the chest wall/pleura and the diaphragm, is consistent with the **Quad sign**.
2.  **Consolidation Signs:** The later images (particularly frames 5, 6, and 7) reveal a large area of lung parenchyma that has lost its normal spongy, dark appearance. Instead, it appears homogeneous and echogenic, resembling the texture of the liver. This is known as **tissue-like hepatization**. Within this "hepatized" tissue, there are distinct bright, linear, and punctate hyperechoic structures. These are **air bronchograms**, which are pathognomonic for alveolar consolidation (pneumonia).

The presence of both the large anechoic fluid collection (effusion) and the solid, air-bronchogram-containing lung tissue (consolidation) indicates that both pathologies are present.

**Conclusion:**
plaps = true
type = "both"
