# 0025_lung_malignant-pleural-effusion

Based on the visual analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound frames:

**Observation:**
The images display a significant portion of the lung field with altered echotexture. Instead of the normal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) seen in aerated lung, the tissue appears solid, heterogeneous, and echogenic, resembling the texture of the liver. This is known as **tissue-like hepatization**. Within this consolidated parenchyma, there are numerous small, irregular anechoic (black) spaces. These represent air-filled bronchi trapped within the consolidated alveoli, a finding known as **static air bronchograms** or the **multivesicular sign**. The deep border of this consolidated area is irregular and ill-defined, consistent with the **shred sign**. While the diaphragm and underlying abdominal organs (likely liver/stomach) are visible at the bottom, there is no distinct, large anechoic collection typical of a significant pleural effusion (e.g., Quad sign) separating the lung from the chest wall. The primary pathology is the consolidation of the lung tissue itself.

**Conclusion:**
- **plaps = true**
- **type = "consolidation"**
