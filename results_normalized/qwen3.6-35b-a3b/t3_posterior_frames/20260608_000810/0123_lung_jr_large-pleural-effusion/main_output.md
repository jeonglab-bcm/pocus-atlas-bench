# 0123_lung_jr_large-pleural-effusion

Based on the visual evidence in the ultrasound frames:

**Observation:**
The images display a significant anechoic (black) region in the posterior dependent zone, which is characteristic of a **pleural effusion**. This fluid collection creates a "Quad sign" appearance, bounded superiorly by the chest wall/pleura and inferiorly by the diaphragm or lung base. Within this anechoic fluid, there is echogenic (gray/white) tissue visible, particularly towards the right side in later frames. This tissue appears solid and floating, resembling the **"Jellyfish sign"** (atelectatic lung floating in effusion) or potentially the **"Shred sign"** at the interface of consolidated lung and fluid. The echogenic tissue exhibits a tissue-like, liver-like texture (**hepatization**), indicating **alveolar consolidation** or atelectasis.

**Conclusion:**
There are clear signs of both pleural effusion (large anechoic space) and lung consolidation (echogenic, solid tissue). Therefore, the PLAPS pattern is present.
