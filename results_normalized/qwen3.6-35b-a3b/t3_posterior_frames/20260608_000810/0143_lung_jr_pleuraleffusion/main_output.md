# 0143_lung_jr_pleuraleffusion

Based on the analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound frames:

**Observation:**
The images display a longitudinal view of the posterior chest wall and underlying lung parenchyma. A distinct, hyperechoic line representing the pleura is visible near the top of the scan. Beneath this pleural line, there is a large, anechoic (black) space, which is characteristic of a fluid collection (pleural effusion). Within this anechoic space, an echogenic, irregular structure is visible that appears to be floating or undulating. This structure represents atelectatic lung tissue suspended in the fluid, which corresponds to the **"Jellyfish sign"**. The large anechoic pocket itself is consistent with the **"Quad sign"** of pleural effusion. There is no clear evidence of primary parenchymal consolidation (tissue-like hepatization) separate from the floating atelectatic lung, although the floating tissue is technically consolidated/atelectatic. The dominant feature is the fluid collection.

**Conclusion:**
*   **plaps = true**
*   **type = "effusion"**
