# 0132_lung_jr_empyema

Based on the analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound frames:

**Observations:**
1.  **Effusion Signs:** The later frames (especially images 5, 6, 7, and 8) clearly demonstrate the **Quad sign**. This is identified by a large anechoic (black) space representing fluid. The boundaries are visible: the ribs on the right (casting acoustic shadows), the hyperechoic diaphragm inferiorly, and the chest wall/pleura superiorly. This confirms the presence of a significant pleural effusion in the posterior zone.
2.  **Consolidation Signs:** In the earlier frames (1-4) and visible on the left side in later frames, there is a large area of lung tissue that appears solid and echogenic, resembling liver parenchyma. This is the **tissue-like hepatization** sign. Additionally, within this consolidated tissue, there are multiple small, round, anechoic spaces (visible clearly in images 3, 4, and 5). This corresponds to the **multivesicular sign**, which is a specific sign of lung consolidation (often pneumonia).
3.  **Synthesis:** The images show a combination of a large pleural effusion (Quad sign) and adjacent consolidated lung tissue (hepatization/multivesicular sign). The tissue may also represent atelectatic lung floating within the effusion, suggestive of the **Jellyfish sign** (though dynamic movement isn't visible in static frames, the static appearance fits).

**Conclusion:**
- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**
