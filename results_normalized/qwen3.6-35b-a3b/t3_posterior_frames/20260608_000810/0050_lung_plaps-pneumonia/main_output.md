# 0050_lung_plaps-pneumonia

Based on the visual evidence in the sequential frames:

1.  **Observation:**
    *   **Effusion Signs:** In frames 1 through 6, there is a prominent, large anechoic (black) space in the posterior aspect of the scan. This space is bounded laterally by hyperechoic rib shadows and superiorly/inferiorly by echogenic lines (pleural/diaphragmatic interfaces), creating a quadrilateral shape. This is the **Quad sign**, indicating a significant pleural effusion.
    *   **Jellyfish Sign:** Within this anechoic fluid collection, there is a distinct, undulating, echogenic structure that appears to be floating. This is the **Jellyfish sign**, representing atelectatic (collapsed) lung parenchyma floating in the pleural fluid.
    *   **Consolidation Signs:** While the floating lung tissue appears echogenic (hepatized) due to compression (atelectasis), there are no distinct air bronchograms or a "shred sign" (irregular border between aerated and non-aerated lung) typically associated with infectious consolidation (pneumonia). The primary pathology here is the fluid collection causing compressive atelectasis.

2.  **Conclusion:**
    *   The presence of the Quad sign and the Jellyfish sign confirms a PLAPS pattern.
    *   The predominant finding is the pleural effusion with floating atelectatic lung.

**plaps = true**
**type = "effusion"**
