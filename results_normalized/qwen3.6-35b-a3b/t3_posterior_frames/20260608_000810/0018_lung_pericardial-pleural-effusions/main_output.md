# 0018_lung_pericardial-pleural-effusions

The ultrasound images display the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point).

**Observations:**
- **Effusion:** There is a prominent anechoic (black) space visible, particularly clear in frames 3, 4, and 5. This fluid collection separates the chest wall from the underlying lung parenchyma.
- **Consolidation/Atelectasis:** Adjacent to and floating within the fluid, there is echogenic, solid-appearing tissue. This represents lung parenchyma that has lost its aeration, appearing "tissue-like" or hepatized.
- **Specific Signs:** In frames 3 and 4, the consolidated/atelectatic lung tissue appears to be floating within the pleural effusion, which corresponds to the **Jellyfish sign** (or floating lung). The interface is somewhat irregular.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of significant pleural fluid (effusion) alongside consolidated, non-aerated lung tissue (consolidation/atelectasis) indicates a PLAPS pattern involving both pathologies.
