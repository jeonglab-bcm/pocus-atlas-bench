# 0100_lung_hemothorax

The ultrasound images demonstrate a large anechoic (black) space in the dependent posterior region, which is characteristic of a pleural effusion. Within this fluid collection, there is a distinct, linear, and somewhat undulating structure that appears to be floating. This is the **"Jellyfish sign"**, representing atelectatic lung tissue suspended in the pleural fluid. The fluid collection is bounded laterally by rib shadows and superiorly by the chest wall, resembling the **"Quad sign"**. The tissue at the very bottom of the image appears to be the liver or spleen (below the diaphragm), separating the pleural space from the abdominal cavity. There is no clear evidence of independent "tissue-like hepatization" or "shred sign" suggesting primary alveolar consolidation (pneumonia) distinct from the compressive atelectasis associated with the effusion.
