# 0018_lung_pericardial-pleural-effusions

Based on the sequential ultrasound frames of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point):

1.  **Effusion Signs:** There is a prominent anechoic (black) space visible in the central and lower portions of the sector scan. This represents a pleural effusion. In several frames (e.g., image 1, 6), a distinct, undulating, echogenic structure is seen floating within or adjacent to this fluid collection. This is characteristic of the **"jellyfish sign,"** where the atelectatic (collapsed) lung floats in the pleural fluid.
2.  **Consolidation Signs:** The lung tissue adjacent to the effusion appears solid, echogenic, and tissue-like, resembling the liver (hepatization). This indicates **alveolar consolidation** or atelectasis. The "jellyfish sign" itself confirms that the floating tissue is atelectatic/consolidated lung.

**Conclusion:**
The images demonstrate both a pleural effusion (indicated by the anechoic space and jellyfish sign) and lung consolidation/atelectasis (indicated by the hepatized texture of the floating lung tissue).
