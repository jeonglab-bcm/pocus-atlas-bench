# 0015_lung_plaps-consolidation

The ultrasound images display a posterior lung zone with significant pathology.

**Observations:**
*   **Effusion:** There is a large, anechoic (black) space visible, particularly in the upper and right portions of the scans (frames 1, 5, 6, 7, 8). This fluid collection is bounded superiorly by the chest wall/pleura and laterally by rib shadows, consistent with the **Quad sign**.
*   **Consolidation:** Adjacent to and inferior to the fluid collection, there is a region of lung tissue that appears solid, homogeneous, and echogenic, resembling the texture of the liver. This is **tissue-like hepatization**, indicating alveolar consolidation.
*   **Air Bronchograms:** Within the consolidated, hepatized tissue, there are punctate and linear hyperechoic (bright white) structures. These represent air-filled bronchi floating within the fluid-filled alveoli, known as **air bronchograms**.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of both a significant pleural effusion (anechoic space) and alveolar consolidation (hepatization with air bronchograms) in the dependent posterior zone confirms the PLAPS pattern.
