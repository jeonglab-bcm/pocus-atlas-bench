# 0020_lung_parapneumonic-effusion2

The ultrasound images display a sector scan of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS).

**Observations:**
*   **Effusion Signs:** There is a prominent, large anechoic (black) space, indicative of a significant pleural effusion. This space exhibits the **Quad sign**: it appears quadrilateral, bounded superiorly by the pleural line/chest wall, laterally by the vertical shadow artifacts of the ribs, and inferiorly by the interface with the underlying tissue. Additionally, there appears to be a **Jellyfish sign**, where a hyperechoic line (visceral pleura/atelectatic lung) is seen floating within the anechoic fluid collection.
*   **Consolidation Signs:** Adjacent to the fluid collection (to the right), there is a region of tissue that is echogenic and homogeneous, resembling liver parenchyma. This appearance is consistent with **tissue-like hepatization**, which indicates lung consolidation (pneumonia) or atelectasis. The interface between the fluid and this solid tissue appears somewhat irregular.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of the Quad sign confirms a pleural effusion in the posterior dependent zone. The adjacent solid, hepatized-appearing lung tissue confirms consolidation/atelectasis. Therefore, both effusion and consolidation patterns are visible.
