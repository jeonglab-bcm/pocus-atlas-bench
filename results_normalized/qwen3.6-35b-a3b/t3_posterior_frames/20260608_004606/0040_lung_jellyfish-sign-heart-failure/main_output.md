# 0040_lung_jellyfish-sign-heart-failure

Based on the visual evidence in the sequential frames:

1.  **Consolidation Signs:** There is a prominent area of echogenic (bright), tissue-like material on the left side of the image. This appearance is consistent with **tissue-like hepatization**, where the lung parenchyma has become solid. Within this echogenic area, there are punctate and linear hyperechoic foci, which represent **air bronchograms** (air-filled bronchi within the consolidated lung). The border between this consolidated tissue and the adjacent space appears irregular, suggestive of the **shred sign**.

2.  **Effusion Signs:** Adjacent to the consolidated lung tissue, there is a large anechoic (black) space. This indicates the presence of **pleural fluid**. The arrangement of the consolidated lung floating within or adjacent to this fluid collection is characteristic of the **jellyfish sign** (or simply effusion with compressive atelectasis). While the classic "quad sign" is hard to fully delineate in every frame due to angle, the presence of the anechoic space is clear.

**Conclusion:**
The image displays signs of both alveolar consolidation (hepatization with air bronchograms) and pleural effusion in the posterior lung zone.
