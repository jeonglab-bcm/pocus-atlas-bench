The ultrasound images display the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point).

**Observations:**
- **Effusion Signs:** There is a prominent, large anechoic (black) space occupying the upper/middle portion of the sector, consistent with a significant pleural effusion. Within this fluid collection, there is a hyperechoic (bright), somewhat irregular structure that appears to be floating. This corresponds to the **"Jellyfish sign"** (or plunging sign), where atelectatic lung tissue floats within the pleural fluid. The boundaries of the fluid space suggest the presence of the **"Quad sign"**, although the specific rib shadows are less distinct in these single frames, the quadrilateral shape of the fluid pocket is apparent.
- **Consolidation Signs:** The floating tissue (the "jellyfish") and the tissue immediately below the fluid appear solid and echogenic, lacking the normal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) of aerated lung. This "tissue-like" appearance is consistent with **hepatization** (lung consolidation). The interface between the fluid and the floating lung is irregular, which could suggest a **"Shred sign"** (though more typical of the deep border of consolidation against aerated lung, here it marks the boundary of the atelectatic mass).

**Conclusion:**
The image exhibits clear signs of a large pleural effusion (Jellyfish sign, Quad sign) and alveolar consolidation (hepatization of the atelectatic lung).
