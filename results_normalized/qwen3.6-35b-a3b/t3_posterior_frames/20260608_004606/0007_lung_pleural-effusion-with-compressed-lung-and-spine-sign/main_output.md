# 0007_lung_pleural-effusion-with-compressed-lung-and-spine-sign

Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**Observation:**
The ultrasound images display the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point).
1.  **Effusion:** There is a large, anechoic (black) space visible in multiple frames (e.g., images 2, 4, 6, 7, 8). This represents a significant pleural effusion. In some frames (like image 3), the view is dominated by this fluid collection.
2.  **Consolidation:** Adjacent to the fluid, there is a large area of lung parenchyma that appears solid, homogeneous, and echogenic, resembling the texture of the liver (hepatization). This indicates alveolar consolidation.
3.  **Air Bronchograms:** Within the consolidated tissue, there are small, irregular anechoic tubular structures (visible clearly in image 1 and faintly in others), which are consistent with air bronchograms.

The combination of a significant pleural effusion and underlying lung consolidation is characteristic of the PLAPS sign.

**Conclusion:**
- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**
