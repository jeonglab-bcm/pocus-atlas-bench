# 0029_lung_parapneumonic-effusion

Based on the provided sequential frames from the lung ultrasound video clip of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point):

**Observation:**
The images reveal significant pathology in the dependent posterior lung zone.
1.  **Effusion:** There is a large anechoic (black) area visible, particularly in the upper and central parts of the sector in the initial frames (1-3). This represents a pleural effusion. The fluid appears to be surrounding lung tissue.
2.  **Consolidation:** Adjacent to and seemingly surrounded by the fluid is a region of lung parenchyma that has lost its normal aerated, sponge-like texture. Instead, it appears solid, homogeneous, and echogenic, resembling liver tissue (**Tissue-like hepatization**).
3.  **Air Bronchograms:** Within this consolidated, solid-appearing lung tissue, there are distinct, linear, and punctate hyperechoic (bright white) foci. These represent air-filled bronchi within the fluid-filled/alveolar-filled lung, a hallmark of consolidation.
4.  **Jellyfish Sign:** The arrangement of the hepatized/atelectatic lung floating within the surrounding anechoic fluid collection resembles a jellyfish in water. This is a specific sign combining features of both effusion and consolidation/atelectasis.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of a pleural effusion (anechoic space) combined with lung consolidation (hepatization with air bronchograms) and the specific "Jellyfish sign" confirms the PLAPS pattern.
