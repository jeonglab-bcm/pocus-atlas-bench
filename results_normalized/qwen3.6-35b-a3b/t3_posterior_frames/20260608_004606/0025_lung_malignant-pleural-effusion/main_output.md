# 0025_lung_malignant-pleural-effusion

The ultrasound images display a large area of lung parenchyma that has undergone "tissue-like hepatization." The lung tissue appears solid, echogenic, and granular, resembling the texture of the liver, which indicates a loss of normal aeration. Within this consolidated, hepatized tissue, there are multiple irregular, hyperechoic (bright) punctate and linear structures. These are characteristic of **static air bronchograms**, representing air-filled bronchi surrounded by fluid-filled or consolidated alveoli.

There is no evidence of a large, anechoic fluid collection bounded by rib shadows (Quad sign) or floating lung tissue (Jellyfish sign) to suggest a predominant pleural effusion. The primary pathology observed is alveolar consolidation.

**Conclusion:**
*   **plaps = true**
*   **type = "consolidation"**
