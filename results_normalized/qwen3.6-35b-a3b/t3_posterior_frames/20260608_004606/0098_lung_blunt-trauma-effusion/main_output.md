# 0098_lung_blunt-trauma-effusion

Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**Observations:**
*   **Tissue-like hepatization:** The most prominent feature is a large area of lung parenchyma that appears solid and echogenic (grey/white), losing its normal spongy, aerated texture. This "hepatization" suggests the alveoli are filled with fluid, pus, or cells, making the lung density similar to that of the liver.
*   **Air bronchograms:** Within the consolidated (hepatized) tissue, there are distinct, bright (hyperechoic) linear and punctate structures. These represent air-filled bronchi surrounded by fluid-filled or solid alveoli, a hallmark sign of consolidation.
*   **Shred sign:** The border of the consolidated area is irregular, jagged, and "shredded." This represents the interface between the solid, consolidated lung and the adjacent aerated lung tissue.

There are no clear signs of a significant pleural effusion such as a large anechoic (black) "quad sign" or a "jellyfish sign" (floating lung lobe). The primary pathology visible is the solidification of the lung tissue.

**Conclusion:**
**plaps = true**
**type = "consolidation"**
