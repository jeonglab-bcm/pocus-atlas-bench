# 0021_lung_large-pleural-effusion

Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**Observation:**
The images display a longitudinal view of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point).
- **Effusion:** There is a prominent anechoic (black) space visible, particularly on the left side of the sector. This indicates a significant pleural effusion. The space is bounded by structures that suggest the "Quad sign" (though the full quadrilateral isn't perfectly framed, the anechoic pocket between the chest wall/pleura and the lung surface is evident).
- **Consolidation/Atelectasis:** Floating within the anechoic fluid is a mobile, echogenic structure. This structure has a solid, tissue-like appearance (resembling liver parenchyma), which is characteristic of lung consolidation (hepatization) or compressive atelectasis. It lacks the normal aeration artifacts (A-lines) seen in healthy lung. This floating appearance of the atelectatic lung within the fluid is often referred to as the "Jellyfish sign" or "Sinusoid sign" (if movement is observed). The interface between the fluid and the solid lung tissue is somewhat irregular.

**Conclusion:**
The images clearly show signs of both pleural effusion (the fluid collection) and lung consolidation (the floating, solid-appearing lung tissue). Therefore, this represents a PLAPS pattern with both features.
