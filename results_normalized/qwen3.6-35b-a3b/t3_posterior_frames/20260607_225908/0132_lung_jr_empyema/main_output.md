# 0132_lung_jr_empyema

The ultrasound sequence demonstrates features of both pleural effusion and lung consolidation in the posterior lung zone.

1.  **Effusion:** There is a prominent anechoic (black) space visible between the chest wall (superiorly) and the lung parenchyma (inferiorly). This space is bounded laterally by rib shadows, creating a quadrilateral shape consistent with the **Quad sign**. Additionally, there is echogenic tissue floating within this fluid collection, resembling the **Jellyfish sign**, which indicates atelectatic lung tissue suspended in a pleural effusion.
2.  **Consolidation:** The lung parenchyma adjacent to and below the fluid collection appears solid, homogenous, and echogenic, resembling liver tissue. This is known as **hepatization** or tissue-like consolidation. The normal air-filled lung patterns (A-lines) are absent in this region.

Since both significant pleural effusion and lung consolidation are clearly visible, the classification indicates the presence of both pathologies.
