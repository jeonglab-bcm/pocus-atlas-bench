# 0123_lung_jr_large-pleural-effusion

Based on the visual analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**Observation:**
The ultrasound images display a sector scan of the posterior lung zone.
- **Effusion Signs:** There is a prominent, large anechoic (black) space occupying the central and lower portions of the field. This fluid collection is bounded superiorly by the pleural interface and inferiorly by a hyperechoic curvilinear structure, likely the diaphragm. Laterally, there are vertical hyperechoic shadows consistent with rib shadows. This configuration creates a quadrilateral shape, characteristic of the **Quad sign**, indicating a significant pleural effusion.
- **Consolidation Signs:** Superior and adjacent to the fluid collection, there is a region of echogenic tissue that appears solid and lacks the normal horizontal A-line artifacts of aerated lung. This "tissue-like" appearance is consistent with **hepatization** of the lung parenchyma, indicating alveolar consolidation or compressive atelectasis. The interface between this solid tissue and the fluid is somewhat irregular, which may represent the **Shred sign** (the irregular deep border of consolidated lung). Additionally, the appearance of lung tissue floating or adjacent within the fluid collection resembles the **Jellyfish sign**.

**Conclusion:**
The images demonstrate signs of both pleural effusion (Quad sign, anechoic space) and alveolar consolidation/hepatization (solid tissue appearance) in the posterior dependent zone.
