# 0051_lung_jellyfish-sign

Based on the visual evidence in the sequential frames:

1.  **Effusion Signs:** The images prominently feature a large, anechoic (black) space, which is characteristic of a significant pleural effusion. This space is bounded superiorly by the chest wall, laterally by rib shadows (creating the **Quad sign**), and inferiorly by the diaphragm/liver interface. Inside this fluid collection, there is a hyperechoic, undulating structure (visible clearly in frames 4-6) that resembles a flap of tissue floating in water. This is consistent with the **Jellyfish sign**, representing atelectatic lung floating within the effusion.

2.  **Consolidation Signs:** The lung tissue itself, particularly the structure floating within the effusion and the tissue visible to the left in the later frames (7-8), appears solid, echogenic, and lacking the normal reverberation artifacts of aerated lung. This "tissue-like" appearance is known as **hepatization**, indicating alveolar consolidation.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of a large pleural effusion (Quad sign, Jellyfish sign) alongside solid, consolidated lung tissue (hepatization) confirms the PLAPS pattern involving both fluid and lung parenchyma changes.
