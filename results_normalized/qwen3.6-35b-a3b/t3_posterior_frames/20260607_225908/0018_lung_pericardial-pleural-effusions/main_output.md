# 0018_lung_pericardial-pleural-effusions

The ultrasound images display a sequence of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS).
- **Effusion signs:** A prominent anechoic (black) space is visible, particularly in the lower and central portions of the sector. Within this fluid collection, there is a mobile, echogenic structure that undulates or floats. This is the **"Jellyfish sign"**, where atelectatic (collapsed) lung tissue floats within the pleural effusion. The fluid also creates a **"Quad sign"** appearance, bounded by the pleural line and chest wall structures.
- **Consolidation signs:** The floating tissue and the adjacent lung parenchyma appear solid and echogenic, losing the normal sponge-like appearance of aerated lung. This **"tissue-like hepatization"** indicates that the lung is consolidated (in this case, likely due to compression atelectasis from the fluid).

Since both significant fluid collection (effusion) and solid-appearing lung tissue (consolidation/atelectasis) are clearly visible, the pattern represents a PLAPS with both features.
