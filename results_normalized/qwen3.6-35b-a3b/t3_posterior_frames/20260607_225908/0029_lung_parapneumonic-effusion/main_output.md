# 0029_lung_parapneumonic-effusion

Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**Observation:**
The images display a view of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point). There is a prominent, large anechoic (black) space visible, particularly clear in the early frames (1-4) and later frames (7-8). This space is bounded laterally by hyperechoic rib shadows, creating a "Quad sign" indicative of a **pleural effusion**.

Adjacent to this fluid collection, particularly visible in the central and right portions of the sector in frames 1-6, is a large area of tissue that appears solid, echogenic, and homogeneous. This texture resembles the liver ("tissue-like hepatization"), which is a sign of **alveolar consolidation** (or atelectasis). The border between the fluid and this consolidated tissue is somewhat irregular. In frame 7, there appears to be a linear hyperechoic structure within the fluid/tissue interface, which could represent an air bronchogram or a vessel, further supporting the presence of consolidation/atelectasis.

**Conclusion:**
- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**
