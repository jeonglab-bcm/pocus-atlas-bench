# 0058_lung_pneumonia

Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames of the PLAPS point:

**Observation:**
The images reveal a significant pathological finding in the posterior lung zone.
1.  **Effusion Signs:** There is a large, anechoic (black) space occupying the majority of the ultrasound field. This space is bounded superiorly by the chest wall/pleural line and laterally by rib shadows, consistent with the **Quad sign**. Within and adjacent to this fluid collection, there is an echogenic structure that represents the lung tissue. The presence of this fluid indicates a pleural effusion. The description of a floating, undulating structure aligns with the **Jellyfish sign** (atelectatic lung floating in effusion), although primarily we see the static collection (Quad sign).
2.  **Consolidation Signs:** Deep to the fluid collection (or within the dependent zone), the lung parenchyma appears solid and highly echogenic, resembling the texture of the liver. This is **tissue-like hepatization**. There are also punctate hyperechoic foci visible within this solid area, suggestive of **air bronchograms**, which confirms the tissue is consolidated lung rather than solid organ like the liver.

**Conclusion:**
The image displays clear signs of both pleural effusion (Quad sign, large anechoic space) and alveolar consolidation (hepatization, air bronchograms).
