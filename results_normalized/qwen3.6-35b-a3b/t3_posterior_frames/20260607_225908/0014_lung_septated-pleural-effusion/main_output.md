# 0014_lung_septated-pleural-effusion

Based on the analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound images:

**Observations:**
*   **Fluid Collection:** There is a large, well-defined anechoic (black) space visible in the majority of the frame. This is characteristic of a pleural effusion.
*   **Quad Sign:** The anechoic space is bounded superiorly by the chest wall/parietal pleura and inferiorly by a hyperechoic line (the visceral pleura of the lung). While rib shadows are not clearly distinct as a perfect quad in every frame, the geometry fits the description of the **Quad sign** (anechoic space bounded by pleural and lung lines).
*   **Jellyfish Sign:** Within the anechoic fluid collection, there is a distinct, hyperechoic linear structure that appears to be the visceral pleura and the underlying lung. Throughout the sequence of images, this structure appears to undulate or float within the fluid. This movement and appearance correspond to the **Jellyfish sign**, which represents atelectatic lung floating in a pleural effusion.
*   **Absence of Consolidation Signs:** While the floating lung is atelectatic (collapsed), there are no clear signs of distinct tissue-like hepatization with visible air bronchograms or a "shred sign" separating consolidated aerated lung. The primary pathology visualized is the fluid and the floating membrane.

**Conclusion:**
The images show a large posterior pleural effusion with compressive atelectasis (Jellyfish sign). According to the classification provided, the presence of the Quad sign and Jellyfish sign (listed under Effusion signs) indicates a PLAPS pattern dominated by effusion.
