# 0143_lung_jr_pleuraleffusion

Based on the analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound frames:

**Observations:**
1.  **Effusion Signs:** The images display a distinct anechoic (black) space situated between the chest wall structures (top) and the lung parenchyma (bottom). This space is bounded superiorly by the parietal pleura and inferiorly by the visceral pleura/consolidated lung surface. This appearance is characteristic of the **Quad sign**, indicating a pleural effusion. The rib shadow is visible on the right side, forming the lateral boundary of this sign.
2.  **Consolidation Signs:** Beneath the fluid collection, the lung parenchyma appears solid, heterogeneous, and echogenic, resembling the texture of the liver. This is known as **tissue-like hepatization**, which indicates lung consolidation. Within this consolidated tissue, there are punctate and linear hyperechoic foci, consistent with **air bronchograms** (air-filled bronchi visible within the fluid-filled or solid alveoli).

**Conclusion:**
The presence of both a significant pleural effusion (fluid collection) and underlying lung consolidation (hepatization with air bronchograms) in the posterior dependent zone (PLAPS point) confirms the pathology.
