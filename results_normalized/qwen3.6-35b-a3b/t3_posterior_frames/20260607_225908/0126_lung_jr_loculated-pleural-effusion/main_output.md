# 0126_lung_jr_loculated-pleural-effusion

The ultrasound images demonstrate a classic presentation of pathology in the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point).

**Observations:**
- **Pleural Effusion:** There is a large, anechoic (black) space occupying the upper/middle portion of the scan field. This indicates a significant accumulation of fluid in the pleural space.
- **Consolidation (Hepatization):** Adjacent to and partially within the fluid collection, there is a region of lung tissue that appears solid, homogeneous, and echogenic, resembling liver parenchyma (hepatization). This indicates alveolar consolidation.
- **Jellyfish Sign:** Within the anechoic fluid collection, there is a mobile, irregular, echogenic structure that appears to be floating. This represents atelectatic lung tissue floating in the pleural effusion, known as the "jellyfish sign."
- **Air Bronchograms:** Within the consolidated lung tissue, there are punctate and linear hyperechoic foci. These represent air-filled bronchi surrounded by fluid-filled alveoli, a hallmark of consolidation.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of both a significant pleural effusion (anechoic space) and lung consolidation (tissue-like hepatization with air bronchograms and floating atelectatic lung) confirms the PLAPS pattern.
