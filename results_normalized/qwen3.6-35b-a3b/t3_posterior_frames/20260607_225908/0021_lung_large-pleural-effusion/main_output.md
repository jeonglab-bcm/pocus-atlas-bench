# 0021_lung_large-pleural-effusion

Based on the visual analysis of the sequential frames:

**Observation:**
The ultrasound images display a sector scan of the posterior lung zone.
1.  **Effusion:** There is a prominent anechoic (black) space occupying a significant portion of the image, particularly on the left side and center. This represents a pleural effusion.
2.  **Consolidation:** Adjacent to the fluid, specifically on the right side of the image, there is a large area of lung parenchyma that appears solid, homogeneous, and echogenic, resembling the texture of the liver (hepatization). This indicates alveolar consolidation.
3.  **Specific Signs:** The echogenic consolidated lung tissue appears to be floating or undulating within the anechoic fluid collection, which is characteristic of the **"jellyfish sign"** (atelectatic lung floating in effusion). Additionally, the interface between the consolidated lung and the aerated lung (or fluid) appears irregular, suggestive of the **"shred sign"**. The presence of fluid adjacent to solid lung tissue confirms the co-existence of both pathologies in the dependent PLAPS zone.

**Conclusion:**
The image clearly demonstrates signs of both pleural effusion and lung consolidation (hepatization) in the posterior dependent lung zone.
