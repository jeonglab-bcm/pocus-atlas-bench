# 0020_lung_parapneumonic-effusion2

Based on the visual analysis of the provided ultrasound frames:

**Observations:**
1.  **Pleural Effusion:** There is a prominent anechoic (black) region in the field of view, consistent with fluid accumulation in the pleural space. This fluid collection appears to form a quadrilateral shape bounded by the chest wall superiorly, the consolidated lung inferiorly, and rib shadows laterally (visible as vertical dark bands on the right side). This is consistent with the **Quad sign**.
2.  **Alveolar Consolidation:** Adjacent to and seemingly submerged within the fluid is a region of lung parenchyma that has lost its normal aerated, sponge-like appearance. It appears solid, homogeneous, and echogenic (gray), resembling liver tissue. This is known as **tissue-like hepatization**.
3.  **Air Bronchograms:** Within the consolidated tissue, there are distinct punctate and linear hyperechoic (bright white) foci. These represent air-filled bronchi surrounded by fluid-filled alveoli, a hallmark of consolidation.
4.  **Jellyfish Sign:** The appearance of the solid, atelectatic/consolidated lung floating or suspended within the surrounding pleural effusion is suggestive of the **Jellyfish sign**.

**Conclusion:**
The images demonstrate clear signs of both a pleural effusion and lung consolidation in the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point).
