# 0004_lung_pleural-effusion-with-hiatal-hernia

The ultrasound images display a posterior lung zone with significant pathology.
- **Consolidation:** There is a large, central area of echogenic, tissue-like parenchyma ("hepatization"), which indicates alveolar consolidation. This area lacks normal A-lines and has a texture similar to liver tissue.
- **Effusion:** Adjacent to the consolidation (specifically on the left side of the image), there is an anechoic (black) space. This represents a pleural effusion.
- **Signs:** The combination of the anechoic fluid and the adjacent consolidated lung creates a pattern consistent with the **Quad sign** (though the full quadrilateral boundaries are implied by the fluid collection next to the solid lung). Additionally, there appears to be a floating, undulating structure within the fluid or at the interface, suggestive of the **Jellyfish sign** (atelectatic lung floating in effusion) or the irregular border of the **Shred sign**. The presence of punctate hyperechoic foci within the consolidation suggests **air bronchograms**.

Since both pleural effusion (anechoic space) and alveolar consolidation (tissue-like hepatization) are clearly visible, this represents a combined pattern.
