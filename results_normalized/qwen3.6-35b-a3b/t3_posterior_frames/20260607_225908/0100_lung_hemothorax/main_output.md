# 0100_lung_hemothorax

The images show a longitudinal view of a dependent lung zone (likely the posterior costophrenic sulcus, or PLAPS point). There is a large, anechoic (black) space occupying a significant portion of the pleural cavity. Within this fluid collection, there is a distinct, echogenic, undulating structure that appears to be floating. This is characteristic of the **Jellyfish sign**, where atelectatic lung tissue floats within a pleural effusion. The fluid creates a space that separates the chest wall/pleura from the floating lung tissue. This confirms the presence of a significant pleural effusion. While the floating tissue represents atelectasis (a form of tissue consolidation), the primary finding and the specific sign identified (Jellyfish) classify this predominantly as an effusion pattern in this context.
