# 0040_lung_jellyfish-sign-heart-failure

The ultrasound images display a posterior lung zone (PLAPS point) with significant pathology.
*   **Effusion signs:** There is a large anechoic (black) space, indicative of pleural fluid. In several frames (e.g., frame 5), a vertical hyperechoic line is visible, likely representing a rib shadow, which helps delineate the fluid collection. The fluid collection creates a space that could correspond to the "Quad sign" (bounded by ribs, pleural line, and lung/consolidation). Additionally, the appearance of the tissue suggests the "Jellyfish sign," where atelectatic lung tissue appears to float within the effusion.
*   **Consolidation signs:** Adjacent to the fluid (particularly visible on the left side in the earlier frames and persisting), there is a hyperechoic, tissue-like area. This represents "hepatization" of the lung, where the lung parenchyma has lost its air content and becomes solid. Within this consolidated tissue, there are punctate and linear hyperechoic foci, which are characteristic of "air bronchograms." The border of the consolidation appears somewhat irregular, suggestive of the "shred sign" where it meets the aerated lung or fluid.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of both significant pleural effusion (anechoic space) and lung consolidation (tissue-like hepatization with air bronchograms) is evident.
