# 0030_lung_parapneumonic-effusion-with-b-lines

Based on the provided ultrasound images of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point):

**Observation:**
The images reveal a large, anechoic (black) space occupying the dependent posterior zone, which is characteristic of a **pleural effusion**. Within this fluid collection, there is a distinct, hypoechoic, solid-appearing structure that resembles lung tissue. This tissue lacks the normal air-filled artifacts (like A-lines or B-lines) and appears solid, consistent with **lung consolidation** or atelectasis.

In specific frames (e.g., the third and fifth images), this consolidated lung tissue is seen floating within the anechoic fluid. This appearance is known as the **"jellyfish sign"** (or plankton sign), where atelectatic lung floats in the surrounding pleural fluid. The arrangement of the fluid collection, bounded by the chest wall superiorly and the diaphragm inferiorly (the bright hyperechoic line at the bottom right), is consistent with the **"quad sign"** of pleural effusion.

**Conclusion:**
There are clear signs of both a fluid collection and consolidated lung tissue within the posterior zone.
