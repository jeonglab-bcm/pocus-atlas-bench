# 0005_lung_subpleural-consolidation-with-shred-sign

**B-lines Assessment**
The image sequence demonstrates the presence of B-lines (lung rockets). These appear as hyperechoic, vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen without fading.
- In the right portion of the intercostal space, distinct, well-spaced vertical lines are visible, consistent with a **septal** pattern.
- In the central region, these lines appear to merge and become more confluent, creating a denser, white sheet-like appearance, which is consistent with a **ground_glass** pattern.
- Given the presence of both discrete and confluent patterns in different regions of the clip, the subtype is classified as **mixed**.

**Consolidation Assessment**
There is evidence of alveolar consolidation in the anterior zone, particularly in the central region.
- **Tissue-like hepatization**: The lung parenchyma in the center appears solid and echogenic, resembling liver texture, rather than the typical black, aerated lung.
- **Air bronchograms**: Within this hepatized area, there are punctate and linear hyperechoic foci (bright white spots), which represent air-filled bronchi surrounded by fluid-filled alveoli.
- **Shred sign**: The border between the consolidated area and the adjacent aerated lung (visible on the right) appears irregular and shredded.
- Based on the presence of distinct bright foci within the consolidated tissue, the predominant type is **air_bronchogram** (though hepatization and shred sign are also present).

**Conclusion**
- **lung_rockets**: true
- **lung_rockets_subtype**: mixed
- **consolidation**: true
- **consolidation_type**: air_bronchogram
