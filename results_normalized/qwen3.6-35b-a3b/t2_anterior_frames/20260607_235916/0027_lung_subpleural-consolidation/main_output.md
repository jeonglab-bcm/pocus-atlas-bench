# 0027_lung_subpleural-consolidation

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
The images display horizontal, parallel reverberation artifacts (A-lines) deep to the pleural line, which are characteristic of normally aerated lung. There are no vertical, hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line that extend to the bottom of the screen without fading. Therefore, B-lines are absent.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
In several frames (particularly frames 1, 7, and 8), there are distinct, rounded, hypoechoic (dark gray) structures located immediately beneath the pleural line (subpleural). These areas have a solid, tissue-like appearance similar to liver parenchyma (hepatization). They lack the air-filled texture of normal lung. The borders of these structures appear somewhat irregular against the adjacent aerated lung. This appearance is consistent with subpleural consolidation.
