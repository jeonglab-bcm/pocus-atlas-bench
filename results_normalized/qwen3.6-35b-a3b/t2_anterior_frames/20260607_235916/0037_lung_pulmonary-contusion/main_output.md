# 0037_lung_pulmonary-contusion

Based on the analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
The images display prominent vertical, hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen without fading. These are characteristic B-lines (lung rockets).
- In the earlier frames (e.g., frame 1, 2), the B-lines are somewhat discrete and spaced, which could suggest a septal pattern.
- In the middle frames (e.g., frame 4, 5, 6), the vertical artifacts become extremely dense and coalesce, creating a broad, bright vertical band that obscures the deeper lung, resembling a "ground glass" or confluent pattern.
- In the later frames, the pattern shifts back to being more distinct.
- This variation between discrete/spaced lines and confluent/coalescing lines within the clip fits the **mixed** subtype classification.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
- There is no evidence of lung parenchyma appearing solid or "tissue-like" (hepatization). The areas between the B-lines remain dark, indicating aerated lung tissue underneath the artifacts.
- There are no irregular "shred signs" at the lung border.
- There are no visible air bronchograms (punctate/linear hyperechoic foci) within a consolidated area.
- Therefore, there is no consolidation present.

**Conclusion:**
- **lung_rockets:** true
- **B-line subtype:** mixed
- **consolidation:** false
- **consolidation_type:** null
