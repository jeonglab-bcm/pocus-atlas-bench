# 0006_lung_dynamic-air-bronchograms-2

Based on the analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
The images display multiple vertical hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line. These artifacts extend to the bottom of the screen without fading and appear to move with lung sliding (implied by the sequence). The B-lines are numerous and appear to be merging or coalescing into a dense, bright vertical pattern, often referred to as a "white lung." This confluent appearance obscures the underlying horizontal A-lines. This pattern fits the description of **ground_glass** B-lines (confluent/coalescing).

**Consolidation Assessment:**
There is no clear evidence of alveolar consolidation. The bright vertical appearance is due to the dense B-lines (interstitial syndrome) rather than tissue-like hepatization. There are no distinct irregular borders (shred sign) separating solid lung from aerated lung, nor are there clearly defined static air bronchograms within a solid mass. The echogenicity is generated by the reverberation artifacts (B-lines) rather than solid tissue.

**Conclusions:**
- **lung_rockets**: true
- **subtype**: ground_glass
- **consolidation**: false
- **consolidation_type**: null
