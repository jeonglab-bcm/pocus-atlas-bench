# 0001_lung_lung-cavitary-lesion

The ultrasound images show the anterior left lung zone.

**B-lines Assessment:**
In several frames (particularly frames 6, 7, and 8 on the right side of the sector), distinct hyperechoic vertical artifacts are visible arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen without fading. These artifacts do not move with lung sliding in a way that suggests they are part of the consolidation, but rather arise from the pleura into the aerated lung tissue visible on the periphery. They are discrete and well-spaced.
- **B-lines present**: true
- **Subtype**: septal

**Consolidation Assessment:**
The images clearly demonstrate an area of lung consolidation.
- **Tissue-like hepatization**: The lung parenchyma in the central and left portion of the image appears solid and echogenic, resembling liver tissue (hepatization), rather than the typical dark, air-filled lung.
- **Air bronchograms**: In frames 3, 4, and 5, distinct punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are visible within the consolidated tissue. These represent air-filled bronchi surrounded by fluid-filled alveoli.
- **Shred sign**: In frames 6, 7, and 8, the border between the consolidated (solid) lung and the adjacent aerated lung (visible on the right) is irregular and jagged, characteristic of the shred sign.

Based on the presence of tissue-like echogenicity (hepatization), which is the fundamental appearance of the consolidated parenchyma, this is the predominant type, although air bronchograms and the shred sign are also prominent features.

- **consolidation**: true
- **consolidation_type**: "hepatization" (Note: Air bronchograms and shred sign are also present, but hepatization describes the parenchymal state).

**Conclusion:**
- **lung_rockets**: true
- **lung_rockets_subtype**: septal
- **consolidation**: true
- **consolidation_type**: "hepatization"
