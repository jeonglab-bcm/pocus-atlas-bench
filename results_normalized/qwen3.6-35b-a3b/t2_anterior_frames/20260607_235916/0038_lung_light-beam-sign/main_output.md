# 0038_lung_light-beam-sign

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**
The sequence of images shows a standard anterior lung ultrasound view.
- **Top Layer:** The chest wall (skin, subcutaneous tissue, and intercostal muscles) is visible as layered hypoechoic and hyperechoic structures at the top.
- **Pleural Line:** A distinct, hyperechoic horizontal line is visible, representing the pleural interface.
- **Artifacts:**
    - In most frames (e.g., 1, 2, 6, 7, 8), the area deep to the pleural line is characterized by horizontal reverberation artifacts known as A-lines. This indicates normally aerated lung tissue.
    - In the central portion of the image, particularly noticeable in frames 3, 4, and 5, there are vertical hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending towards the bottom of the screen. These are distinct and separate from one another.

**B-lines Assessment:**
- **Presence:** Vertical hyperechoic artifacts (B-lines) are visible, particularly in the central region of the scan (frames 3-5). They arise from the pleural line and extend downwards.
- **Subtype:** The B-lines appear as discrete, laser-like lines. They are well-spaced, and the underlying lung parenchyma (with A-lines) is still visible between them. They do not coalesce into a diffuse white sheet. This pattern is characteristic of **septal** B-lines.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
- **Findings:** The lung parenchyma below the pleural line appears aerated, evidenced by the presence of A-lines. There is no evidence of "tissue-like hepatization" (liver texture), no irregular "shred sign" at the deep border, and no visible air bronchograms within a solidified area.
- **Conclusion:** There is no consolidation.

**Conclusions:**
- **lung_rockets:** true
- **B-line subtype:** septal
- **consolidation:** false
