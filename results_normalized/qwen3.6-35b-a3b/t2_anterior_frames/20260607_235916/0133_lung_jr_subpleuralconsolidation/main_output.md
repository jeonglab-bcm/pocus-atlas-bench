# 0133_lung_jr_subpleuralconsolidation

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**B-lines Assessment**
*   **Observation:** In several frames (particularly 1, 4, and 7), there is a distinct, hyperechoic vertical artifact arising from the pleural line (or just deep to it) and extending to the bottom of the screen without fading. This is located to the right of the consolidated area. In other frames (3, 5, 6, 8), fainter vertical artifacts are visible in the aerated lung regions. These artifacts are discrete and separated by dark lung parenchyma (except where consolidation is present).
*   **Conclusion:** **B-lines present (lung_rockets = true)**.
*   **Subtype:** The B-lines appear as discrete, vertical lines (especially the prominent one in frames 1, 4, 7). They do not merge into a diffuse white sheet (which would be ground glass). Thus, the pattern is **septal**.

**Consolidation Assessment**
*   **Observation:** In frames 1, 4, and 7, there is a significant area of hyperechoic, tissue-like material located subpleurally (just below the pleural line). This area resembles the texture of the liver (hepatization). The deep border of this area is highly irregular and jagged, separating it from the darker aerated lung below (shred sign). Within this tissue-like area, there are punctate and linear hyperechoic foci (static air bronchograms).
*   **Conclusion:** **consolidation = true**.
*   **Type:** The predominant feature of the lung parenchyma in the affected area is its solid, liver-like echogenicity. While the shred sign is visible at the border and air bronchograms are present within, the overall tissue appearance is best described as **hepatization**.

**Final Classifications:**
*   **lung_rockets**: true
*   **subtype**: septal
*   **consolidation**: true
*   **consolidation_type**: hepatization
