# 0147_lung_jr_acute-chest-syndrome

Based on the analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound frames:

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**
- The images display an anterior lung zone view.
- Ribs are visible as hyperechoic curved structures with posterior acoustic shadowing.
- Below the ribs, the pleural line is visible.
- Arising from the pleural line are distinct, vertical, hyperechoic artifacts that extend to the bottom of the screen without fading. These are characteristic of B-lines (lung rockets).
- To the right of the B-lines, there is a large area of increased echogenicity with a tissue-like texture, resembling the liver (hepatization). This indicates lung consolidation.
- The border of this consolidated area appears irregular and jagged, consistent with the "shred sign".
- Within the consolidated area (particularly visible in frames 5 and 6), there are small, punctate/linear hyperechoic foci, which represent air bronchograms.

**B-lines Assessment:**
- **Presence:** B-lines are clearly present. They are vertical, hyperechoic, and reach the bottom of the screen.
- **Subtype:** The B-lines appear as discrete, well-spaced vertical lines (typically 2 distinct lines are visible in the intercostal space). They do not merge into a diffuse white sheet. Therefore, they fit the **septal** pattern.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
- **Presence:** There is clear evidence of alveolar consolidation. The lung parenchyma in the right/central part of the image has lost its normal aeration artifacts and appears solid and echogenic.
- **Type:** The predominant appearance is that of tissue-like **hepatization** (liver-like texture). Additionally, the **shred sign** (irregular border) is visible, and **air bronchograms** are present within the consolidated tissue. However, the bulk of the finding is the hepatized tissue.

**Conclusions:**
- **lung_rockets = true**
- **B-line subtype = septal**
- **consolidation = true**
- **consolidation_type = hepatization**
