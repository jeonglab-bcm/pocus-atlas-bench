**B-lines Assessment**
Analysis of the sequential frames reveals that the lung parenchyma in the non-consolidated areas (visible in frames 1, 2, 5, and 8) is dominated by horizontal reverberation artifacts known as A-lines. There is a distinct absence of hyperechoic vertical artifacts (B-lines or "lung rockets") that arise from the pleural line and extend to the bottom of the screen without fading. While there is a vertical interface in some frames, it corresponds to the boundary of the consolidated area rather than a B-line.

**Consolidation Assessment**
Frames 3, 4, 6, and 7 demonstrate a clear abnormality in the subpleural region.
- **Tissue-like hepatization:** There is a hypoechoic area beneath the pleural line that lacks the normal aeration artifacts (A-lines). The texture is homogeneous and resembles the echotexture of the liver, which is characteristic of alveolar consolidation (hepatization).
- **Air bronchograms:** In frame 7, distinct punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are visible within the hypoechoic consolidated area. These represent air-filled bronchi surrounded by fluid-filled or consolidated alveoli.
- **Shred sign:** The deep border of the consolidated area appears irregular and jagged where it interfaces with the adjacent aerated lung tissue, consistent with the "shred sign" or "irregular border sign" of consolidation.

**Conclusions**
- **lung_rockets = false**
- **consolidation = true**
- **consolidation_type = "hepatization"**
