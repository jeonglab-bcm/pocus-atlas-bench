**B-lines Assessment**
The ultrasound frames display distinct hyperechoic vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line. These artifacts extend all the way to the bottom of the screen without fading, which is characteristic of B-lines (lung rockets).
- In the sequence, particularly in frames 1, 4, 6, and 7, these vertical bands are clearly visible as discrete, tall columns.
- While bright, they remain distinct and separated by darker spaces (lung parenchyma), rather than merging into a single, diffuse "white sheet" that obscures all underlying structures. This discrete, columnar appearance fits the description of **septal** B-lines, indicating thickened interlobular septa.

**Consolidation Assessment**
- There is no evidence of tissue-like hepatization (liver-like texture) in the lung parenchyma. The texture is dominated by the reverberation artifacts (B-lines) rather than solid tissue.
- There are no signs of the "shred sign" (irregular border between consolidated and aerated lung).
- There are no visible air bronchograms (punctate or linear hyperechoic foci within a consolidated area).
- Therefore, consolidation is not present.

**Conclusions**
- **B-lines present:** true
- **Subtype:** septal
- **Consolidation present:** false
- **Consolidation type:** null
