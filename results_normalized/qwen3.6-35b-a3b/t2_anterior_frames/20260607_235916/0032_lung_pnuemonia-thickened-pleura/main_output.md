# 0032_lung_pnuemonia-thickened-pleura

## B-lines Assessment

The ultrasound frames display multiple vertical hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line. These artifacts extend all the way to the bottom of the screen and maintain their brightness, which are the defining characteristics of B-lines (lung rockets).

Regarding the subtype:
- The B-lines appear as distinct, vertical columns.
- Although they are prominent and somewhat numerous, there are visible darker regions (representing aerated lung parenchyma) between the bright vertical lines, particularly noticeable between the distinct columns on the left and right sides of the image.
- They do not merge into a single, continuous "white sheet" that completely obscures the underlying structures in a way characteristic of ground-glass opacity (where the lung surface appears uniformly bright and hazy).
- Therefore, the pattern fits the **septal** subtype (discrete, well-spaced B-lines with dark lung parenchyma visible between them).

## Consolidation Assessment

- **Tissue-like hepatization:** The lung parenchyma does not appear solid or liver-like. Instead, the area below the pleura is dominated by the vertical B-line artifacts. The spaces between the artifacts are dark, indicating aerated lung tissue, not consolidation.
- **Shred sign:** There is no irregular, shredded border between a solid mass and aerated lung visible.
- **Air bronchograms:** There are no punctate or linear hyperechoic foci (air bronchograms) within a consolidated area. The hyperechoic features seen are the vertical B-lines arising from the pleura, not air-filled bronchi within tissue.

## Conclusion

- **lung_rockets = true**
- **lung_rockets_subtype = septal**
- **consolidation = false**
- **consolidation_type = null**
