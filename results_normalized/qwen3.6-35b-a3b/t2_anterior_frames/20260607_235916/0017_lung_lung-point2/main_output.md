# 0017_lung_lung-point2

Based on the analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
The M-mode image (bottom panel) clearly displays vertical, hyperechoic artifacts originating from the pleural line (the horizontal interface at the top of the M-mode). These artifacts, known as B-lines or "lung rockets," extend to the bottom of the screen without fading and move synchronously with the horizontal lung sliding seen above them.
*   **Subtype:** The B-lines are discrete and well-spaced. There is visible dark lung parenchyma (A-lines) between the vertical artifacts. They do not merge into a diffuse white sheet. This pattern is consistent with **septal** B-lines.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
The B-mode image (top panel) and the M-mode background show horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines), which indicate normally aerated lung tissue. There is no evidence of "tissue-like hepatization" (a liver-like, solid echogenic appearance), no irregular "shred sign" border, and no "air bronchograms" (hyperechoic foci within solid tissue). While there is a large anechoic (dark) area to the right of the pleural line which may suggest a pleural effusion, the lung parenchyma itself in the region of interest does not show signs of alveolar consolidation.

**Conclusion:**
*   **lung_rockets = true**
*   **subtype = "septal"**
*   **consolidation = false**
