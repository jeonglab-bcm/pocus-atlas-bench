# 0125_lung_jr_lack-of-lung-sliding

The ultrasound images display the anterior lung zone. The pleural line is visible as a bright, horizontal structure near the top. Lateral to the center, horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) are clearly visible, indicating normal aerated lung tissue. In the center of the image, there is a prominent, vertical anechoic (black) band. This is an acoustic shadow, likely caused by the sternum or a rib blocking the ultrasound waves, rather than pathological lung tissue.

The absence of these "lung rockets" and the presence of normal A-lines suggest no interstitial syndrome. Furthermore, there is no evidence of lung parenchyma transforming into a solid, liver-like texture (hepatization), nor are there irregular borders (shred sign) or air-filled bronchi (air bronchograms). The central dark area is an artifact, not consolidation.
