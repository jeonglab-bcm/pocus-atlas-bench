# 0057_lung_blines-in-pulmonary-contusion

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**

*   **Frames 1-3:** The images show the pleural line at the top. Arising from the pleural line are vertical, hyperechoic artifacts (B-lines). In Frame 1, these artifacts are somewhat discrete and separated by areas of darker, aerated lung parenchyma, particularly on the right side. This appearance is consistent with **septal B-lines**. As we move to Frames 2 and 3, the artifacts appear to become more numerous and start to coalesce, particularly in the central and right portions of the image.
*   **Frames 4-6:** The ultrasound image is dominated by a large area of hyperechogenicity (brightness) in the anterior zone.
    *   In **Frame 4**, the area is uniformly bright, resembling a "white lung" appearance. This transition from discrete lines to a confluent sheet is characteristic of **ground-glass B-lines** or the transition into consolidation.
    *   In **Frames 5 and 6**, the texture of the bright area changes. It becomes heterogeneous and solid, resembling the texture of the liver (hepatization). The deep border of this area is irregular and jagged, which is the **shred sign** (the interface between consolidated and aerated lung). Within this consolidated tissue, there are small, punctate hyperechoic foci, which are **air bronchograms**.

**B-lines Assessment:**
*   **Presence:** B-lines are clearly visible.
*   **Subtype:** The clip shows a progression. Frames 1-3 display discrete, vertical artifacts consistent with **septal** B-lines. Frames 4-5 show these artifacts coalescing into a diffuse bright sheet, consistent with **ground-glass** B-lines. Since both patterns are evident across the sequence, the subtype is **mixed**.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
*   **Presence:** The later frames (4-6) show a distinct area of lung parenchyma that has lost its aeration and appears solid. This confirms the presence of consolidation.
*   **Type:** The predominant feature is the **tissue-like hepatization** (liver-like echogenicity). Additional features supporting this are the **shred sign** (irregular border) and **air bronchograms** (bright spots within the tissue). Given the "tissue-like" description is the primary texture of the consolidation, this is the predominant type.

**Conclusions:**

*   **lung_rockets:** true
*   **B-line subtype:** mixed
*   **consolidation:** true
*   **consolidation_type:** hepatization
