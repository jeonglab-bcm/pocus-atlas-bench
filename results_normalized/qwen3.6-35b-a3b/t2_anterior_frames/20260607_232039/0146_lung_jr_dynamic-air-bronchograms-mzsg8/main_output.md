The images display multiple vertical, hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen. These artifacts are numerous and closely spaced, creating a dense vertical pattern. They are not discrete and well-spaced (which would be septal), nor do they appear in a mixed pattern. Instead, they coalesce to form a diffuse, bright, vertical haze that obscures the underlying A-lines. This appearance is characteristic of confluent B-lines, often referred to as "white lung."

There are no visible air bronchograms (punctate or linear hyperechoic foci within a consolidated area) or an irregular "shred sign" border. The abnormality is diffuse and consistent with severe interstitial syndrome rather than focal consolidation.
