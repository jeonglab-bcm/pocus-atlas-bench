**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**

*   **Frame 1:** The image shows a clear, horizontal pleural line. Below the pleura, there are distinct horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines), which are parallel to the pleural line. This indicates normal, aerated lung. There are no prominent vertical artifacts.
*   **Frame 2:** Similar to Frame 1, the A-lines are visible. There may be very faint vertical artifacts starting to appear, but the dominant pattern is still horizontal A-lines.
*   **Frame 3:** A distinct, hyperechoic vertical line (laser-like beam) arises from the pleural line and extends all the way to the bottom of the screen without fading. This is a classic B-line (lung rocket).
*   **Frame 4:** There are now two visible vertical artifacts arising from the pleura. They are discrete and separated by areas of A-lines. This represents the "septal" pattern of B-lines.
*   **Frame 5:** The vertical artifacts appear slightly more dense.

*   **Frame 8:** The image continues to show the dense vertical "white lung" appearance. The pleural line is visible at the top, but the area below is dominated by confluent vertical artifacts.

**B-lines Assessment:**
*   **Presence:** B-lines are clearly present in frames 3 through 8.
*   **Subtype:** The clip shows a progression. Frames 3 and 4 show discrete, well-spaced B-lines (septal pattern). Frames 6, 7, and 8 show confluent, coalescing B-lines that merge into a diffuse white sheet (ground glass pattern). Therefore, the subtype is **mixed**.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
*   **Presence:** There is no evidence of alveolar consolidation. While the lung appears "white" in the later frames, this is due to the coalescence of B-lines (ground glass), not solid tissue (hepatization). There are no visible air bronchograms (punctate/linear hyperechoic foci within solid tissue) or a distinct "shred sign" (irregular border of consolidated lung). The texture remains consistent with reverberation artifacts rather than solid hepatized tissue.
