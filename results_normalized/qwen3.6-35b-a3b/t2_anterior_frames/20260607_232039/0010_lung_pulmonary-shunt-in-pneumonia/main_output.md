# 0010_lung_pulmonary-shunt-in-pneumonia

**B-lines Assessment**
The image displays a Color Doppler overlay (green box with blue and red signals). The vertical structures visible within the box are colored signals indicating blood flow (vascularity), not the hyperechoic, vertical, laser-like artifacts characteristic of B-lines (lung rockets). B-lines are grayscale artifacts arising from the pleural line. In this clip, the lung parenchyma appears solid and echogenic with visible vascularity, which rules out the presence of typical B-lines (which are associated with interstitial syndrome in aerated lung). Therefore, B-lines are absent.

**Consolidation Assessment**
The region within the color box exhibits a homogeneous, echogenic texture that resembles the liver (tissue-like hepatization). This "hepatized" appearance is confirmed by the presence of Color Doppler signals (red and blue pixels), indicating blood flow within the tissue. In normal aerated lung, sound waves are reflected by air, preventing Doppler detection; the presence of flow signals here confirms that the lung tissue is consolidated and solid. While there are some punctate hyperechoic foci visible in certain frames (e.g., frame 6 and 8) which could represent air bronchograms, the predominant finding is the solid, tissue-like appearance of the parenchyma.

**Conclusions**
- **lung_rockets**: false
- **consolidation**: true
- **consolidation_type**: "hepatization"
