# 0038_lung_light-beam-sign

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**

*   **Image 1:** Shows the pleural line as a bright, horizontal hyperechoic line. Below it, horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) are visible. The area deep to the pleura is dark, indicating aerated lung. No vertical artifacts are clearly seen extending to the bottom.
*   **Image 2 - 8:** The images maintain a consistent pattern. The pleural line is clearly visible. Horizontal A-lines are present, creating a "seashore" or parallel line pattern typical of normal aerated lung. There is a general lack of vertical hyperechoic streaks. In some frames (e.g., frame 3, 7), faint vertical shadowing might be visible, but they do not exhibit the characteristics of full B-lines (they appear to fade or are too thin/irregular to be "rockets" and do not obliterate the A-lines). The lung parenchyma remains dark and aerated throughout the sequence.

**Assessment:**

*   **B-lines Assessment:** The images are dominated by A-lines (horizontal reverberation artifacts). There are no hyperechoic vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line that extend to the bottom of the screen without fading. Therefore, B-lines are absent.
*   **Consolidation Assessment:** There is no evidence of tissue-like hepatization (liver-like echogenicity), shred sign, or air bronchograms. The lung tissue appears normally aerated (dark).

**Conclusions:**

*   **lung_rockets = false**
*   **consolidation = false**
