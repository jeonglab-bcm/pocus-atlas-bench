# 0031_lung_pneumoniashred-sign

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
In the initial frames (1-5), there are prominent vertical hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line. These artifacts extend to the bottom of the screen.
- In the central region of these frames, the artifacts are dense and coalescing, merging into a diffuse, bright "white sheet" that obscures the underlying lung details. This fits the description of **ground_glass** appearance.
- However, looking closely at the periphery (especially in frame 1), there are some distinct, discrete vertical lines with some darker space between them, which could be interpreted as **septal** B-lines.
- Since both confluent (ground glass) and somewhat discrete patterns are visible in different regions of the scan, the pattern is best classified as **mixed**.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
In the later frames (6-8), the appearance changes significantly.
- There is a region of lung parenchyma that appears echogenic and solid, resembling liver tissue, which indicates **tissue-like hepatization**.
- The border of this consolidated area is irregular and jagged, forming an interface with the adjacent (likely aerated) lung. This irregular, jagged border is the classic **shred sign**.
- Within the echogenic consolidated area, there are visible punctate and linear hyperechoic foci, which represent **air bronchograms**.
- The most distinctive morphological feature here is the irregular border, making **shred_sign** the predominant classification for the consolidation pattern.

**Conclusion:**
- **lung_rockets**: true
- **lung_rockets_subtype**: mixed
- **consolidation**: true
- **consolidation_type**: shred_sign
