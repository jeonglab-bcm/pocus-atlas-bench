# 0034_lung_covid19-pneumonia

Based on the analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
The images consistently display vertical, hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the ultrasound sector. These artifacts, often referred to as "lung rockets," do not fade and maintain their vertical orientation. They appear as discrete, well-spaced lines with the darker lung parenchyma visible between them. This pattern is characteristic of **septal** B-lines, which indicate thickened interlobular septa. They are not confluent enough to form a diffuse white sheet (ground-glass pattern), nor do they appear as completely absent.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
There is no evidence of alveolar consolidation in these anterior lung zone images. The lung parenchyma retains its characteristic interstitial pattern with visible B-lines and does not exhibit the "tissue-like" or liver-like echogenicity (hepatization) associated with consolidation. There are no irregular "shred signs" at the lung boundary, nor are there any visible air bronchograms (punctate or linear hyperechoic foci) within a consolidated area.

**Conclusions:**
- **lung_rockets**: true
- **lung_rocket_subclass**: septal
- **consolidation**: false
- **consolidation_type**: null
