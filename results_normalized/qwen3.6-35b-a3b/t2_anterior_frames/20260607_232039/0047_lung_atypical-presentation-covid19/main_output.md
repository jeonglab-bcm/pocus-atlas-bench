**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**

*   **Frame 1:** The pleural line is visible as a bright, horizontal interface. Below it, there are several distinct, vertical hyperechoic lines (B-lines) extending to the bottom of the screen. They are relatively well-spaced with dark lung parenchyma visible between them. This suggests a **septal** pattern.
*   **Frame 2:** The image shows horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) below the pleural line. There are no vertical B-lines visible in this frame. This represents a normal, aerated lung appearance or a transition.
*   **Frame 3:** Similar to Frame 1, distinct vertical B-lines are visible, appearing spaced apart.
*   **Frame 4:** The density of vertical hyperechoic lines has increased significantly. There are multiple lines close together. The area below the pleural line is brighter, and the lines appear closer, suggesting they are beginning to coalesce. This represents a more severe interstitial pattern, approaching **ground_glass**.
*   **Frame 5:** Multiple vertical artifacts are present, densely packed. They create a brighter vertical column.
*   **Frame 6:** Similar to Frame 5, dense vertical artifacts are seen.
*   **Frame 7:** Similar to Frame 6.
*   **Frame 8:** Similar to Frame 6. The pattern of numerous, closely spaced B-lines persists.

**B-lines Assessment:**
*   **Presence:** Vertical hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen are clearly visible in the majority of the frames (1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8). Thus, **lung_rockets = true**.
*   **Subtype:** The clip exhibits variation. Frames 1 and 3 show discrete, well-spaced lines characteristic of a **septal** pattern. Frames 4 through 8 show a higher density of lines that are closely packed, creating a brighter, almost confluent appearance that obscures the A-lines, fitting the description of a **ground_glass** pattern (or severe interstitial syndrome). Since both patterns are visible at different time points, the classification is **mixed**.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
*   **Signs:** There is no evidence of tissue-like hepatization (liver texture) replacing the lung parenchyma. The pleural line remains distinct and smooth (no **shred sign**). There are no punctate or linear hyperechoic foci within a solid mass (no **air bronchograms**). The presence of B-lines indicates the lung is still aerated (interface with fluid/interstitial thickening), ruling out frank consolidation.
*   **Conclusion:** **consolidation = false**.

**Conclusions:**

*   **lung_rockets**: true
*   **subtype**: mixed
*   **consolidation**: false
*   **consolidation_type**: null
