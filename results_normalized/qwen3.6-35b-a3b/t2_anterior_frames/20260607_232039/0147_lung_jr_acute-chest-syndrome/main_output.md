# 0147_lung_jr_acute-chest-syndrome

The ultrasound sequence displays an anterior lung zone.

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**
*   **Frames 1, 2, 3, 4, 7:** These frames show the pleural line with distinct, vertical hyperechoic artifacts arising from it. These artifacts extend to the bottom of the screen without fading. They are discrete and separated by dark spaces (indicating aerated lung in between), which is characteristic of **septal B-lines**. In the center of the image, there is a vertical band of hyperechogenicity that appears denser, possibly indicating coalescing B-lines or the edge of the consolidated area.
*   **Frames 5, 6, 8:** These frames reveal a significant area of abnormality. The lung parenchyma appears solid and echogenic, resembling liver tissue (**tissue-like hepatization**). Within this solid area, there are punctate and linear hyperechoic foci (**air bronchograms**). The border between this consolidated tissue and the adjacent aerated lung (where B-lines are seen) is irregular and jagged, suggestive of the **shred sign**.

**B-lines Assessment:**
Vertical hyperechoic artifacts (B-lines) are clearly visible arising from the pleural line in multiple frames. They are discrete and well-spaced, particularly on the left side of the image, fitting the criteria for **septal** B-lines. While there is a denser vertical band in the center of some frames, the distinct separation in others suggests a septal pattern rather than a confluent "white lung" ground-glass pattern, although there might be a component of confluence. However, given the distinct nature in frames 1 and 7, "septal" is the most accurate classification for the B-line subtype.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
There is clear evidence of alveolar consolidation. The parenchyma in the lower right and center of the image has a solid, gray, liver-like texture (**hepatization**). Within this hepatized area, bright punctate spots are visible (**air bronchograms**). The interface between the consolidated and aerated lung is irregular (**shred sign**).

**Conclusion:**
*   **lung_rockets:** true
*   **lung_rockets_subtype:** septal
*   **consolidation:** true
*   **consolidation_type:** hepatization (Note: Air bronchograms and shred sign are also present, but hepatization describes the predominant tissue appearance).
