# 0120_lung_jr_blines

Based on the analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
The images clearly display hyperechoic vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen without fading. These artifacts move with the pleura (implied by the sequence nature, though static here, their appearance is characteristic). They are distinct, discrete, and separated by dark areas of lung parenchyma. There are typically 2 to 3 such lines visible within the intercostal space in the provided frames. They do not form a confluent, solid "white sheet" that completely obscures the underlying structure (which would be the ground-glass pattern). Therefore, B-lines are present, and the pattern is classified as **septal**.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
There is no evidence of tissue-like hepatization (a solid, liver-like appearance of the lung parenchyma), the "shred sign" (irregular border between consolidated and aerated lung), or air bronchograms (linear/punctate hyperechoic foci within a solid mass). The vertical artifacts observed are B-lines, which are artifacts in aerated lung, rather than a sign of consolidation. The lung parenchyma appears aerated.

**Conclusion:**
- **lung_rockets:** true
- **subtype:** septal
- **consolidation:** false
- **consolidation_type:** null
