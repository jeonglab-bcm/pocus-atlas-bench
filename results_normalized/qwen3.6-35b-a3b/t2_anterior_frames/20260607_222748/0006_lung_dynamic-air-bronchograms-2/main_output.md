# 0006_lung_dynamic-air-bronchograms-2

Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
The images display the pleural line at the top of the sector. Arising from this pleural line are numerous, hyperechoic vertical artifacts (B-lines or "lung rockets") that extend to the bottom of the screen. These artifacts are confluent and coalescing, merging to form a diffuse, bright "white lung" appearance that obscures the underlying A-lines (horizontal reverberation artifacts). This pattern is characteristic of **ground_glass** B-lines, which typically indicates severe interstitial syndrome (e.g., alveolar edema). While there are vertical striations, they are too numerous and confluent to be classified as discrete "septal" B-lines.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
There is no evidence of tissue-like hepatization (a solid, liver-like texture devoid of vertical artifacts). The vertical artifacts (B-lines) are clearly visible, indicating that the lung parenchyma is not fully consolidated. There is no distinct irregular deep border ("shred sign") separating consolidated tissue from aerated lung, nor are there clear air bronchograms (fixed hyperechoic foci within solid tissue). The brightness is due to the confluent B-lines rather than solid tissue. Therefore, consolidation is absent.

**Conclusion:**
- **lung_rockets**: true
- **B-line subtype**: ground_glass
- **consolidation**: false
- **consolidation_type**: null
