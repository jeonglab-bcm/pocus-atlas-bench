# 0138_lung_jr_clines

Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
- In the first few frames (1-3), the image shows a rib shadow (the dark vertical band with hyperechoic cortex above) and horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) in the adjacent lung field, indicating normal aerated lung.
- In the subsequent frames (4-8), the probe position changes, revealing a distinct hyperechoic vertical artifact arising from the pleural line. This artifact extends to the bottom of the screen without fading. This is a classic **B-line** (lung rocket).
- The B-line appears as a discrete, well-defined vertical line (or a small cluster) rather than a confluent sheet of white. It is distinct from the background lung texture. This fits the criteria for **septal** B-lines (discrete, well-spaced).

**Consolidation Assessment:**
- The lung parenchyma visible in the images (particularly to the right of the rib shadow in frames 1-3 and to the right of the B-line in frames 4-8) appears dark and speckled, consistent with aerated lung tissue.
- There is no evidence of "tissue-like hepatization" (solid, liver-like echogenicity).
- There is no "shred sign" (irregular border between consolidated and aerated lung).
- The vertical hyperechoic structure is a B-line artifact, not an air bronchogram within consolidated tissue.
- Therefore, **consolidation is absent**.

**Conclusion:**
- **lung_rockets**: true
- **subtype**: septal
- **consolidation**: false
- **consolidation_type**: null
