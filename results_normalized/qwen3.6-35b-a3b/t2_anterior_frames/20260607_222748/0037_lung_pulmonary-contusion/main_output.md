# 0037_lung_pulmonary-contusion

Based on the analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound frames:

**B-lines Assessment**
The images display multiple vertical, hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line. These artifacts extend to the bottom of the screen and appear to move with lung sliding (implied by the sequence), which identifies them as B-lines (lung rockets).
- The B-lines are visible as distinct, bright vertical columns.
- Crucially, there is dark lung parenchyma visible between these artifacts, indicating they are discrete rather than confluent.
- There appear to be roughly 3 distinct columns of B-lines in the field of view, which fits the description of a limited number (typically ≤3 per intercostal space).
- This pattern corresponds to **septal** B-lines, indicating thickened interlobular septa, rather than the confluent "white lung" sheet seen in ground-glass patterns.

**Consolidation Assessment**
- There is no evidence of tissue-like hepatization (solid, liver-like texture) in the lung parenchyma.
- There is no "shred sign" (irregular border between consolidated and aerated lung).
- There are no air bronchograms visible.
- The echogenicity is dominated by the vertical reverberation artifacts (B-lines) rather than solid tissue.
- Therefore, no consolidation is present.

**Conclusions**
- **B-lines present**: true
- **B-line subtype**: septal
- **Consolidation present**: false
- **Consolidation type**: null
