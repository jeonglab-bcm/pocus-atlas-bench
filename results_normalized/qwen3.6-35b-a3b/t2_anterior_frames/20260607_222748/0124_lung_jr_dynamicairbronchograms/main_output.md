# 0124_lung_jr_dynamicairbronchograms

The ultrasound images display sequential frames of an anterior lung zone.

**B-lines Assessment:**
- **Presence:** In the lateral aspect of the image (right side), there are multiple hyperechoic vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line. These artifacts extend to the bottom of the screen (depth > 3 cm) without fading. This is characteristic of B-lines (lung rockets).
- **Subtype:** In the initial frames (1-3), the B-lines appear somewhat discrete. However, in the later frames (4-8), they become numerous, dense, and coalescent, merging to create a diffuse vertical brightness that obscures the underlying lung structures. This pattern is consistent with **ground_glass** opacities (confluent B-lines). The transition from discrete to confluent could also suggest a **mixed** pattern, but the ground-glass appearance is prominent in the later frames.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
- **Presence:** There are no definitive signs of alveolar consolidation such as tissue-like hepatization (solid gray appearance resembling liver), an irregular "shred sign" border, or distinct air bronchograms within a consolidated mass.
- The large anechoic (black) area on the left side of the image appears to be an acoustic shadow, likely from a rib, rather than a fluid collection or consolidated lung (which would be echogenic). The lung parenchyma visible on the right, despite the dense B-lines, retains the appearance of aerated lung (albeit with significant interstitial syndrome) rather than a solid consolidated mass. The vertical artifacts are reverberation artifacts (B-lines), not air bronchograms.

**Conclusion:**
- **B-lines:** Present (lung_rockets = true).
- **Subtype:** ground_glass (confluent/coalescing).
- **Consolidation:** Absent (consolidation = false).
