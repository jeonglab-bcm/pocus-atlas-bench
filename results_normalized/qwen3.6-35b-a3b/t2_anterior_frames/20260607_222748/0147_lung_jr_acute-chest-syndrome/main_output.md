# 0147_lung_jr_acute-chest-syndrome

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
- **Presence:** Hyperechoic vertical artifacts are clearly visible arising from the pleural line in multiple frames (e.g., frames 1, 2, 4, 7, 8). They extend towards the bottom of the screen without fading, consistent with B-lines (lung rockets).
- **Subtype:** The appearance varies across the frames. In frames 1, 2, and 4, the artifacts appear as distinct, somewhat spaced vertical lines, which could be interpreted as septal B-lines. However, in frames 3, 7, and 8, these artifacts become very dense, confluent, and merge into a diffuse white sheet that obscures the underlying lung parenchyma, fitting the description of ground-glass B-lines. Since both distinct (septal-like) and confluent (ground-glass-like) patterns are observed in the sequence, the subtype is classified as **mixed**.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
- **Presence:** In frames 5 and 6, there is a distinct area of tissue-like echogenicity that resembles the liver (hepatization). This area lacks the normal aeration artifacts and has a solid texture.
- **Type:** The consolidated area exhibits a **tissue-like hepatization** appearance. Within this hepatized region, particularly visible in frame 5, there are hyperechoic foci and linear structures, which are consistent with **air bronchograms**. The border between the consolidated tissue and adjacent areas appears somewhat irregular, suggesting a **shred sign**. Given the extensive tissue-like texture, **hepatization** is the predominant feature describing the consolidation.

**Conclusions:**
- **B-lines present (lung_rockets = true)**
- **B-line subtype = mixed**
- **Consolidation present (consolidation = true)**
- **Consolidation type = hepatization**
