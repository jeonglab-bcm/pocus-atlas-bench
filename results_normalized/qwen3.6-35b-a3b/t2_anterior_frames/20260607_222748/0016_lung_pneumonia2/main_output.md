# 0016_lung_pneumonia2

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
The images consistently show vertical, hyperechoic artifacts originating from the pleural line (the bright horizontal line near the top, around 1-2 cm depth). These artifacts extend downwards to the bottom of the screen without fading, which is the characteristic appearance of B-lines (lung rockets).
- In frames like 1, 2, and 6, the B-lines appear as discrete, somewhat spaced vertical lines. This fits the description of **septal** B-lines (thickened interlobular septa).
- In frame 3 (and partially in 7), the vertical artifacts become extremely dense and merge together, creating a diffuse white sheet that obscures the underlying lung parenchyma. This fits the description of **ground_glass** B-lines (confluent/merging B-lines).
- Since both discrete (septal-like) and confluent (ground_glass-like) patterns are visible in different frames of the clip, the pattern is classified as **mixed**.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
I do not observe clear signs of alveolar consolidation.
- There is no distinct area of "tissue-like hepatization" (solid, liver-grey echogenicity) deep to the pleura. The echogenicity is dominated by the reverberation artifacts of the B-lines.
- There is no visible "shred sign" (irregular border between consolidated and aerated lung).
- There are no clear "air bronchograms" (static hyperechoic foci within a solid mass). The bright spots seen are vertical streaks originating from the pleura, consistent with B-lines, not static air bronchograms.

**Conclusions:**
- **B-lines present:** true
- **B-line subtype:** mixed
- **Consolidation:** false
- **Consolidation type:** null
