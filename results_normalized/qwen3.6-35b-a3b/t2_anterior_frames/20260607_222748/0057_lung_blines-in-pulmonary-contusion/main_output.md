# 0057_lung_blines-in-pulmonary-contusion

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**

*   **Frames 1-3:** The images display a pleural line with distinct, vertical hyperechoic artifacts arising from it and extending towards the bottom of the screen. These artifacts are spaced apart, allowing the underlying lung parenchyma to be seen between them. This appearance is characteristic of **septal B-lines**.
*   **Frame 4:** The image shows a transition. There is a diffuse, confluent brightness in the upper region that obscures the deeper structures. This "white sheet" appearance, where B-lines merge together, is characteristic of **ground-glass B-lines**. The border between the consolidated/abnormal area and the aerated lung (if visible) appears irregular.
*   **Frame 5:** This frame clearly shows **tissue-like hepatization**, where the lung parenchyma appears solid and echogenic, resembling the liver. Within this consolidated area, there are distinct punctate and linear hyperechoic foci. These are **air bronchograms**, representing air-filled bronchi within the fluid-filled alveoli. The border of this area is irregular, consistent with the **shred sign**.
*   **Frame 6:** The image continues to show the solid, liver-like echogenicity of **hepatization**. The air bronchograms are less distinct here, but the consolidated texture remains.
*   **Frames 7-8:** These frames show darker areas, likely representing the probe moving to different intercostal spaces or deeper structures, with less visualization of the primary pathology.

**B-lines Assessment:**
*   **Presence:** B-lines are present.
*   **Subtype:** **Mixed**. The sequence shows discrete, spaced vertical lines in the earlier frames (septal pattern) and a confluent, bright "white sheet" appearance in later frames (ground-glass pattern).

**Consolidation Assessment:**
*   **Presence:** Consolidation is present.
*   **Type:** **Air bronchogram**. While hepatization (tissue-like texture) and shred sign (irregular border) are visible, the presence of distinct punctate/linear hyperechoic foci within the hepatized lung in Frame 5 is a prominent specific sign of alveolar consolidation.

**Conclusion:**
- **lung_rockets:** true
- **subtype:** mixed
- **consolidation:** true
- **consolidation_type:** air_bronchogram
