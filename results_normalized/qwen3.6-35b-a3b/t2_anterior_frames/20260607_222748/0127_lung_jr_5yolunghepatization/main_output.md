**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**

This area appears somewhat heterogeneous but generally tissue-like (isoechoic to hypoechoic relative to muscle, but with internal brightness). It does not show the classic horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) seen in normal lung. Inside this area, there are some hyperechoic foci, which could represent air bronchograms. The lower border of this area is irregular and jagged, separating it from the darker, aerated lung tissue below or adjacent to it. This irregular border is characteristic of the "shred sign."
*   **Frames 4-6:** The probe appears to be moving laterally. The focal area of abnormality is fading from view.
*   **Frames 7-8:** The view shifts to adjacent lung tissue which appears normal. We see a clear pleural line and horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines), indicating normal, aerated lung parenchyma. No vertical artifacts are seen extending to the bottom of the screen.

**B-lines Assessment:**

The vertical structures seen are part of the consolidated tissue or air bronchograms. In the normal frames (7-8), only A-lines are visible.
*   **Conclusion:** **lung_rockets = false** (B-lines absent).

**Consolidation Assessment:**
*   **Observation:** Frames 1-3 clearly demonstrate a region of lung tissue that has lost its normal aeration. It has a "tissue-like" or "hepatized" appearance. The border between this consolidated tissue and the aerated lung is irregular (shred sign). Within the consolidated tissue, there are punctate hyperechoic foci (air bronchograms).
*   **Conclusion:** **consolidation = true**.
*   **Type:** The predominant feature describing the lung parenchyma itself is its tissue-like, liver-like texture. While the shred sign and air bronchograms are present, the fundamental nature of the consolidation is hepatization.
*   **Conclusion:** **consolidation_type = "hepatization"**.
