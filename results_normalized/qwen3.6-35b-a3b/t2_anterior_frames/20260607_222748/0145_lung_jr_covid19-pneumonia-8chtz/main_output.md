**B-lines Assessment**
The image sequence displays vertical, hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen without fading. These are characteristic of B-lines (lung rockets).
- In the initial frames (e.g., frame 1), there are distinct, somewhat discrete vertical lines visible, with dark lung parenchyma between them. This resembles a septal pattern (though potentially bordering on numerous).
- As the sequence progresses (frames 4-8), these vertical artifacts become denser, thicker, and begin to merge. By the final frames, they coalesce into a dense, diffuse vertical white band/sheet that obscures the underlying lung texture and A-lines. This "white sheet" appearance is characteristic of a ground-glass pattern of B-lines.
- Since both discrete (septal-like) and coalescing (ground-glass-like) patterns are visible at different time points in the clip, the subtype is best classified as mixed.

**Consolidation Assessment**
- There is no evidence of tissue-like hepatization (solid, liver-like texture). The brightness seen is due to the vertical artifacts (B-lines), not a loss of aeration into a solid tissue state.
- There is no visible "shred sign" (irregular border between consolidated and aerated lung).
- There are no distinct air bronchograms (punctate/linear hyperechoic foci within a solid area).
- Therefore, there is no consolidation.

**Conclusion**
- **B-lines:** Present (`lung_rockets = true`)
- **B-lines Subtype:** Mixed (`septal` and `ground_glass` patterns are visible across the time sequence)
- **Consolidation:** Absent (`consolidation = false`)
