# 0122_lung_jr_pna

The ultrasound clip displays sequential frames from a lung scan.

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**
*   **Frames 1-3:** The image shows the pleural line at the top. Below it, the lung parenchyma is visible with horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines), indicating aerated lung. Arising from the pleural line are distinct, vertical hyperechoic artifacts that extend to the bottom of the screen. These are B-lines. They are discrete and well-spaced, with dark lung parenchyma visible between them. This is the "septal" pattern.
*   **Frames 4-8:** The image appearance changes significantly. The background lung tissue becomes brighter (more echogenic), appearing gray rather than black. The distinct vertical B-lines seen in the previous frames become less distinct and appear to merge or become more confluent, contributing to a diffuse "white" appearance. The horizontal A-lines are largely obscured or less distinct. This appearance is consistent with "ground-glass" opacity.

**B-lines Assessment:**
*   **Presence:** B-lines are clearly present.
*   **Subtype:** The clip shows a transition from discrete, spaced B-lines (Frames 1-3) to a more confluent, bright appearance where B-lines merge (Frames 4-8). This fits the definition of **mixed** B-lines, as both septal (discrete) and ground-glass (confluent) patterns are visible at different time points/frames.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
*   **Presence:** There are no definitive signs of alveolar consolidation such as tissue-like hepatization, a shredded deep border, or distinct air bronchograms within a solid mass. The brightness in frames 4-8 is consistent with ground-glass opacity (interstitial/alveolar edema) rather than solid consolidation, especially given the persistence of vertical artifacts and lack of a solid liver-like texture.
