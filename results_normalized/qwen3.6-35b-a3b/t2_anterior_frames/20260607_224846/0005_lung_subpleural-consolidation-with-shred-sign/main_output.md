# 0005_lung_subpleural-consolidation-with-shred-sign

Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
The images display multiple hyperechoic vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen without fading. These are characteristic of B-lines (lung rockets). They appear as discrete, distinct lines rather than a confluent "white sheet" obscuring the entire field. While present on the right side, they do not merge completely into a single band of echogenicity, and distinct vertical shafts are visible. This pattern fits the description of **septal** B-lines, which are typically well-spaced (likely ≤3 per intercostal space here) with visible lung parenchyma between them.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
In the central and left-central regions of the image, the lung parenchyma exhibits a tissue-like, hypoechoic to isoechoic texture compared to the surrounding aerated lung, consistent with **hepatization**. Within this consolidated area, there are distinct punctate and linear hyperechoic foci (particularly visible in frames 4 and 5). These bright structures represent air-filled bronchi surrounded by fluid-filled alveoli. This is a classic sign of alveolar consolidation. The presence of these hyperechoic foci within the hepatized lung tissue identifies the **shred sign** or simply **air bronchograms**. Given the prominence of the bright linear/punctate structures, the predominant type is **air_bronchogram**.

**Conclusion:**
- **B-lines:** true (subtype: septal)
- **Consolidation:** true (type: air_bronchogram)
