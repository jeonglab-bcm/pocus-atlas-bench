# 0006_lung_dynamic-air-bronchograms-2

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**

*   **Frame 1:** The ultrasound image shows a sector scan of the lung. A hyperechoic pleural line is visible at the top. Below the pleural line, there are vertical, hyperechoic artifacts (B-lines) extending downwards. In the central region, these lines are quite numerous and dense. Horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) are visible in the lower periphery, indicating aerated lung in those areas.
*   **Frame 2 - 3:** The pattern persists. The vertical artifacts in the central zone appear to be merging or coalescing. They are bright and extend towards the bottom of the screen. The density of these artifacts seems high.
*   **Frame 4 - 6:** The central area becomes increasingly hyperechoic ("white"). The vertical lines are so dense that they merge into a diffuse sheet of light, obscuring the underlying lung tissue and any A-lines that might be present in that specific zone. This appearance is characteristic of confluent B-lines.
*   **Frame 7 - 8:** The "white lung" appearance in the center remains consistent. The vertical artifacts continue to be the dominant feature. There is no distinct, solid-appearing mass with a clear irregular border (shred sign) that would suggest focal alveolar consolidation (pneumonia/atelectasis). The echogenicity is consistent with the reverberation artifacts of dense B-lines.

**Assessment:**

*   **B-lines:**
    *   **Presence:** Vertical, hyperechoic artifacts are clearly visible arising from the pleural line. They extend to the bottom of the screen without fading.
    *   **Subtype:** The B-lines are numerous and appear to be merging or coalescing, creating a diffuse white sheet in the central part of the image. This fits the description of **ground_glass** (confluent/coalescing B-lines). They are not discrete and well-spaced (septal).

*   **Consolidation:**
    *   **Presence:** There is no clear evidence of alveolar consolidation. While the area is hyperechoic, the texture is due to the vertical artifacts (B-lines) rather than solid tissue (hepatization).
    *   **Signs:** There is no distinct "tissue-like hepatization" resembling liver parenchyma distinct from the artifacts. There is no clear "shred sign" (irregular border between aerated and non-aerated lung). While there are bright spots, they appear to be part of the B-line complex rather than distinct air bronchograms within a solid mass. The presence of A-lines at the periphery confirms that aerated lung is adjacent, and the central pathology is dominated by interstitial/alveolar fluid artifacts (B-lines) rather than solid consolidation.

**Conclusions:**

*   **lung_rockets = true**
*   **subtype = ground_glass**
*   **consolidation = false**
*   **consolidation_type = null**
