# 0147_lung_jr_acute-chest-syndrome

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
*   **Presence:** In the first four frames, hyperechoic vertical artifacts are clearly visible arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen. These meet the criteria for B-lines (lung rockets).
*   **Subtype:** In the earlier frames (1-2), the B-lines are discrete and well-spaced, fitting the **septal** description. However, in later frames (3-4) of this sequence, the B-lines become very dense and bright, appearing to merge or coalesce significantly, which aligns with the **ground_glass** pattern (confluent B-lines). Since both discrete and confluent patterns are visible across the time points of the clip, the subtype is classified as **mixed**.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
*   **Presence:** Starting from frame 5 and continuing through frame 8, there is a large area of echogenic tissue that replaces the normal aerated lung pattern. This tissue has a homogeneous, granular texture similar to the liver, indicating **tissue-like hepatization**.
*   **Signs:** Within this hepatized area, particularly in frames 5, 6, and 8, there are punctate and linear hyperechoic foci. These are **air bronchograms**. The border between the consolidated lung and the adjacent aerated lung (seen in earlier frames or at the periphery) is irregular, suggesting a **shred sign**.
*   **Type:** The predominant feature is the solid, liver-like appearance of the lung parenchyma, which defines **hepatization**. While air bronchograms are present, they are features within the hepatized tissue, making hepatization the primary classification of the consolidation type.

**Conclusion:**
*   **B-lines:** Present (Mixed subtype).
*   **Consolidation:** Present (Hepatization type).

**Final Classifications:**
*   **lung_rockets:** true
*   **B-line subtype:** mixed
*   **consolidation:** true
*   **consolidation_type:** hepatization
