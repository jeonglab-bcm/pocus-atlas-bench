**B-lines Assessment**
The images display multiple hyperechoic vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen without fading. These are characteristic B-lines (lung rockets).
- In the initial frames (1-4), the B-lines appear as discrete, well-spaced vertical bands with dark lung parenchyma visible between them. This corresponds to a **septal** pattern.
- In the subsequent frames (5-8), the vertical artifacts become more numerous and merge together, forming a broader, denser area of brightness that obscures the underlying A-lines. This corresponds to a **ground_glass** (confluent) pattern.
- Since both discrete (septal) and confluent (ground_glass) patterns are visible within the sequence, the classification is **mixed**.

**Consolidation Assessment**
- There is no evidence of the lung parenchyma appearing solid and echogenic (hepatization).
- No irregular "shred sign" border between consolidated and aerated lung is visible.
- No distinct air bronchograms (punctate or linear hyperechoic foci within a consolidated area) are observed. The vertical hyperechoic lines are artifacts (B-lines), not air bronchograms within solid tissue.
- Therefore, consolidation is not present.
