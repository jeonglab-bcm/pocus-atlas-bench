# 0043_lung_normal-lung

**B-lines Assessment**
The ultrasound images display a clear horizontal hyperechoic line at the top, representing the pleural line. Beneath the pleural line, there are multiple, parallel, horizontal hyperechoic lines extending deep into the field of view at regular intervals. These are characteristic A-lines (reverberation artifacts), which indicate normally aerated lung tissue.
Upon close inspection, there are no distinct, hyperechoic vertical artifacts (B-lines or "lung rockets") arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen without fading. While there is some vertical texturing or noise, it does not meet the criteria for distinct B-lines (which would appear as laser-like beams). The A-lines remain the dominant feature, suggesting the absence of interstitial syndrome.

**Consolidation Assessment**
The presence of prominent A-lines throughout the image indicates that the lung parenchyma is well-aerated. There is no evidence of "tissue-like hepatization" (a liver-like echotexture replacing the lung), nor are there irregular borders suggestive of the "shred sign." No hyperechoic foci consistent with air bronchograms are visible within a consolidated area. The lung appears to be in a normal, aerated state.

**Conclusion**
- **B-lines present**: false
- **Consolidation present**: false
- **Consolidation type**: null
