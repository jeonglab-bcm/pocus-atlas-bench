# 0048_lung_covid19-pneumonitis2

Based on the analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound frames:

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**
- **Frames 1, 2, 7, 8:** These frames predominantly display a hyperechoic pleural line. Below the pleura, there are horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines), which are characteristic of normal, aerated lung.
- **Frames 3, 4, 5, 6:** In these frames, distinct vertical hyperechoic artifacts are visible arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen. These artifacts move with the lung sliding (implied by their appearance in sequential frames) and obliterate the A-lines in their path. These are characteristic B-lines (lung rockets). Specifically, in Frame 5, two distinct vertical lines can be seen. In Frame 3 and 6, single lines are visible.

**B-lines Assessment:**
- **Presence:** Yes, B-lines are present in several frames (e.g., frames 3, 5, 6, 8).
- **Subtype:** The B-lines appear as discrete, vertical, hyperechoic lines that are well-spaced. They do not merge to form a diffuse white sheet (ground-glass pattern). This appearance is consistent with **septal** B-lines, which indicate thickened interlobular septa.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
- **Presence:** No. The lung parenchyma deep to the pleural line appears largely anechoic/dark (hypoechoic), which is consistent with aerated lung tissue. There is no evidence of tissue-like hepatization (liver-like echogenicity), an irregular "shred sign," or air bronchograms. The horizontal A-lines are also preserved in areas without B-lines, further supporting aeration.

**Conclusion:**
- **lung_rockets = true**
- **B-line subtype: septal**
- **consolidation = false**
- **consolidation_type = null**
