# 0023_lung_pneumothorax-with-lung-point

Based on the analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound frames:

**B-lines Assessment**
In the provided frames, specifically visible on the left side of the sector scan, there are hyperechoic vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line. These artifacts extend downwards towards the bottom of the screen. They appear as discrete, well-spaced lines with dark (anechoic) lung parenchyma visible between them. They do not merge into a diffuse white sheet. This pattern is characteristic of **septal** B-lines, indicating thickened interlobular septa (interstitial syndrome).

**Consolidation Assessment**
The lung parenchyma generally appears hypoechoic and granular, consistent with normal aeration, except for the areas occupied by the B-lines. There is no evidence of a solid, tissue-like "hepatized" area that resembles the liver. The pleural line is intact and distinct. There are no visible air bronchograms (punctate/linear hyperechoic foci) within a consolidated area, nor is there an irregular "shred sign" border. Therefore, there is no evidence of alveolar consolidation.

**Conclusions**
- **lung_rockets**: true
- **lung_rockets_subtype**: septal
- **consolidation**: false
- **consolidation_type**: null
