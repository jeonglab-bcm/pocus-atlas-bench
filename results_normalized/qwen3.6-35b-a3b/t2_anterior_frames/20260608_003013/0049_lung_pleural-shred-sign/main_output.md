# 0049_lung_pleural-shred-sign

Based on the analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound frames:

**B-lines Assessment**
The images demonstrate the presence of vertical, hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line. These artifacts extend to the bottom of the screen without fading, which is characteristic of **B-lines (lung rockets)**.
- **Subtype**: The B-lines appear as distinct, vertical streaks. There is visible dark lung parenchyma between these vertical lines, and they do not merge into a single, solid "white sheet" of echogenicity that completely obscures the background. This discrete spacing indicates a **septal** pattern, typically associated with thickened interlobular septa.

**Consolidation Assessment**
There are no signs of alveolar consolidation in the anterior zone.
- The lung parenchyma beneath the pleura appears dark (anechoic), indicating it is aerated, which is consistent with the presence of B-lines.
- There is no evidence of "tissue-like hepatization" (solid, liver-like echogenicity).
- The pleural line is smooth and regular, showing no signs of the "shred sign" (irregular border).
- No air bronchograms are visible.

**Conclusions**
- **lung_rockets**: true
- **B-line subtype**: septal
- **consolidation**: false
