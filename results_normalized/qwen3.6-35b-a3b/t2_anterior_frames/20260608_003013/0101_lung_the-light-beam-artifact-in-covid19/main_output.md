# 0101_lung_the-light-beam-artifact-in-covid19

Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**B-lines Assessment**
In the provided frames, there are clear vertical hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen.
-   **Frame 1:** A faint vertical line is visible extending downwards from the pleural interface.
-   **Frame 4 & 6:** Distinct, bright vertical lines (B-lines) are clearly visible. They appear as "lung rockets."
-   These artifacts are discrete and well-spaced. They do not merge into a diffuse, confluent white sheet that obscures the entire lung field. Horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) are still discernible in the background, particularly in the upper regions of the lung field. This pattern is consistent with **septal** B-lines, which are typically discrete and spaced.

**Consolidation Assessment**
-   The lung parenchyma in the background appears largely anechoic (dark), consistent with aerated lung.
-   There is no evidence of "tissue-like hepatization" (where the lung looks like liver tissue).
-   There are no visible "shred signs" (irregular borders of consolidated lung) or "air bronchograms" (hyperechoic foci within consolidated tissue).
-   Therefore, there is no evidence of consolidation.

**Conclusion**
-   **lung_rockets = true**
-   **Subtype: septal**
-   **consolidation = false**
-   **consolidation_type = null**
