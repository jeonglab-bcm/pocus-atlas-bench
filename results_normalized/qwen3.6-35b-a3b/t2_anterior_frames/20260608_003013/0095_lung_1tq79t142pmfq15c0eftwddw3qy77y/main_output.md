Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
The images display multiple hyperechoic vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen without fading. These are characteristic B-lines (lung rockets).
- In the earlier frames (e.g., frame 1), there are distinct, somewhat discrete vertical lines visible, although there appear to be more than the typical 1-3 seen in pure "septal" patterns.
- In later frames (e.g., frames 6, 7, 8), the vertical artifacts become much denser and appear to coalesce, forming a diffuse white sheet that obscures the deeper lung structures. This confluent pattern matches the description of "ground_glass" B-lines.
- Since both discrete/sectal-like patterns (in earlier frames) and confluent/ground-glass-like patterns (in later frames) are observed, the pattern is best classified as mixed.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
- There is no evidence of tissue-like hepatization (a solid, liver-like echotexture replacing the lung parenchyma). The dominant finding is the vertical streaking of B-lines.
- Deep horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) are visible in the deeper parts of the image (especially in frame 1), which indicates the presence of aerated lung tissue deep to the artifacts.
- There is no visible "shred sign" (irregular border between consolidated and aerated lung) or distinct air bronchograms within a solid mass.
- Therefore, alveolar consolidation is not present.

**Conclusions:**
- **lung_rockets**: true
- **lung_rockets_subtype**: mixed
- **consolidation**: false
