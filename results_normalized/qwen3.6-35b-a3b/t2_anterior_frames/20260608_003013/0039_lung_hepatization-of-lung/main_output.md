# 0039_lung_hepatization-of-lung

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
*   **Observation:** In the central to left-central region of the ultrasound image, there are vertical, hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line. These artifacts extend downwards.
*   **Characteristics:** The artifacts appear as discrete, well-spaced vertical lines (particularly visible in frames 1, 4, 5, 6). They do not appear to coalesce into a diffuse "white sheet" (ground glass), nor are they absent. The background lung parenchyma between the lines appears relatively dark (anechoic/hypoechoic), consistent with aeration, although the image quality is somewhat low. The vertical lines are distinct.
*   **Conclusion:** B-lines are present. Based on the discrete nature of the visible lines, they fit the description of **septal** B-lines.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
*   **Observation:** In the right portion of the image (particularly noticeable in frames 6, 7, and 8), there is an area of abnormal echogenicity. It appears hypoechoic to isoechoic compared to the chest wall, resembling tissue (hepatization) rather than the normal aerated lung (which would be grey with A-lines) or simple anechoic fluid (though it is quite dark, it has some texture).
*   **Characteristics:** The border between this abnormal area and the adjacent lung tissue (left side) appears irregular and jagged. This is consistent with the **shred sign**, which indicates the interface between consolidated (atelectatic) lung and aerated lung (or sometimes fluid). Within this tissue-like area, there are some punctate hyperechoic foci, suggestive of **air bronchograms**. The overall texture is solid and tissue-like.
*   **Conclusion:** Consolidation is present. The predominant feature is the tissue-like appearance of the parenchyma, which fits the definition of **hepatization**.

**Final Classifications:**
*   **lung_rockets:** true
*   **subtype:** septal
*   **consolidation:** true
*   **consolidation_type:** hepatization
