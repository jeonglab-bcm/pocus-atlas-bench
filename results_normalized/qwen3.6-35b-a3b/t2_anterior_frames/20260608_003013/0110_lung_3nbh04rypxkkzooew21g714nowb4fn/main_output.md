Based on the analysis of the sequential frames:

**B-lines Assessment:**
*   **Presence:** B-lines (lung rockets) are clearly visible throughout the sequence. They appear as hyperechoic vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen.
*   **Subtype:** The pattern evolves across the frames.
    *   In the initial frames (1-3), the B-lines are discrete, distinct, and well-spaced, consistent with a **septal** pattern.
    *   In the later frames (6-8), the B-lines become extremely numerous and coalesce, merging into a diffuse white sheet that obscures the underlying lung parenchyma. This is consistent with a **ground glass** pattern.
    *   Since both discrete (septal) and confluent (ground glass) patterns are visible at different points in the clip, the subtype is classified as **mixed**.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
*   **Presence:** There is evidence of consolidation.
*   **Signs:**
    *   **Shred Sign:** In frames 7 and 8 (specifically the right panels), an irregular, jagged, hyperechoic border is visible deep within the lung field. This represents the irregular interface between the consolidated lung tissue and the aerated lung (or pleural line irregularity due to subpleural consolidation), known as the shred sign.
    *   **Air Bronchograms:** There are punctate/linear hyperechoic foci visible deep within the tissue in several frames (e.g., frame 1 left, frame 6 left), which may represent air bronchograms, further supporting the presence of consolidation.
*   **Type:** The most distinct structural sign of the consolidation boundary is the **shred sign**. While there is hepatization (tissue-like echogenicity) associated with the consolidation, the specific irregular border is the defining feature shown.

**Conclusions:**
*   **lung_rockets = true**
*   **lung_rockets_subtype = mixed** (Transitioning from septal to ground glass)
*   **consolidation = true**
*   **consolidation_type = shred_sign**
