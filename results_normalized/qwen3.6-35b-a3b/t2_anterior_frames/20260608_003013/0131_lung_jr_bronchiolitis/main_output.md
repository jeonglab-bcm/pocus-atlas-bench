# 0131_lung_jr_bronchiolitis

**B-lines Assessment**
The sequence of images demonstrates the presence of B-lines (lung rockets).
- In the initial frames (1-2), distinct, discrete vertical hyperechoic lines can be seen arising from the pleural line and extending downwards. These are separated by dark lung parenchyma and A-lines, fitting the description of **septal** B-lines.
- In the later frames (6-8), the vertical artifacts become very numerous and coalesce. They merge into a dense, diffuse vertical haze (a "white sheet") that obscures the deeper A-lines. This fits the description of **ground_glass** B-lines.
- Since both discrete (septal) and confluent (ground_glass) patterns are visible at different time points within the clip, the subtype is classified as **mixed**.

**Consolidation Assessment**
There are no signs of alveolar consolidation.
- The bright area seen in the later frames is characteristic of confluent B-lines (interstitial syndrome) rather than tissue-like hepatization. The texture retains a vertical component typical of reverberation artifacts, rather than the homogeneous, granular "liver-like" texture of solid consolidation.
- There is no visible "shred sign" (irregular border between aerated and consolidated lung).
- There are no visible air bronchograms (punctate/linear hyperechoic foci within consolidated lung).

**Conclusions**
- **lung_rockets = true**
- **subtype = mixed**
- **consolidation = false**
