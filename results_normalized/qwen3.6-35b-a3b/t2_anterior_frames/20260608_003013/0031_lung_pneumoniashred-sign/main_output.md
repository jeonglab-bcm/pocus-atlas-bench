# 0031_lung_pneumoniashred-sign

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**

*   **Frames 1-5:** These frames display a "white lung" appearance. There are multiple vertical, hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen without fading. These are characteristic of B-lines. The artifacts are dense and confluent, merging into a diffuse bright sheet, particularly in the central and upper regions. This pattern is consistent with severe interstitial syndrome.
*   **Frames 6-8:** The appearance shifts to a more solid, tissue-like echogenicity. The lung parenchyma appears homogeneous and hypoechoic to isoechoic compared to the liver (hepatization), losing the normal aeration artifacts. Within this consolidated tissue, there are distinct hyperechoic (bright) linear and punctate structures, which represent air-filled bronchi (air bronchograms). The border between the consolidated area and the adjacent tissue appears irregular, suggestive of a shred sign.

**B-lines Assessment:**
*   **Presence:** B-lines are clearly visible in frames 1-5. They arise from the pleural line, are vertical, and extend to the bottom of the screen.
*   **Subtype:** The B-lines in the initial frames are dense and confluent, merging into a diffuse white sheet that obscures the underlying lung architecture. This fits the description of **ground_glass** (confluent or coalescing B-lines). While there are distinct lines in frame 1, the overall dominant pattern is the confluent "white lung".

**Consolidation Assessment:**
*   **Presence:** Frames 6-8 clearly show alveolar consolidation. The lung parenchyma has a solid, liver-like texture (hepatization) instead of the normal spongy, air-filled appearance.
*   **Type:** The predominant texture is **hepatization** (tissue-like appearance). Additionally, visible **air bronchograms** (the bright spots/lines within the solid tissue) and an irregular border (**shred sign**) are present. Given the solid tissue fills the majority of the visual field in these frames, hepatization is the primary classification, though air bronchograms are a key feature.

**Conclusion:**

*   **lung_rockets:** true
*   **B-lines subtype:** ground_glass
*   **consolidation:** true
*   **consolidation_type:** hepatization
