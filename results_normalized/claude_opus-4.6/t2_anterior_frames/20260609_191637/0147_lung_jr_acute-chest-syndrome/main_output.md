# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Frame-by-Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Frames 1–4:** The pleural line is visible at the top. Below it, a large **anechoic (dark) space** is present, consistent with pleural effusion. Adjacent to this fluid collection, there is an area of **tissue-like echogenicity** (hepatized lung) that appears solid, similar to liver parenchyma.

**Frames 5–6:** The consolidated lung tissue becomes more prominent. Within the hepatized parenchyma, **punctate hyperechoic foci** are visible — these are air bronchograms (air-filled bronchi trapped within the solidified lung).

**Frames 7–8:** The large anechoic effusion is again prominently seen. The consolidated lung appears to be partially surrounded by fluid, with an irregular deep border (shred-like margin) at the transition to aerated lung.

**Frames 9–10:** More echogenic consolidated tissue is visible with scattered **bright punctate and linear foci** within it, further confirming air bronchograms. The parenchyma maintains its tissue-like (hepatized) character.

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

Multiple consolidation signs are clearly present:

1. **Hepatization:** The lung parenchyma appears solid, tissue-like, and echogenic — resembling liver texture — indicating complete loss of aeration.
2. **Air bronchograms:** Punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are clearly visible within the hepatized lung in multiple frames (especially frames 5–6, 9–10), representing air trapped in bronchi within consolidated tissue.
3. **Associated pleural effusion:** The large anechoic space surrounding the consolidated lung is consistent with a significant effusion.

**consolidation_type = "air_bronchogram"**

The predominant and most conspicuous sign is air bronchograms within extensively hepatized lung tissue, surrounded by pleural effusion — a pattern consistent with pneumonia, ARDS, or other causes of dense alveolar consolidation in the anterior zone.
