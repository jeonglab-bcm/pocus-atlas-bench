# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1-2:** A curved hyperechoic pleural line is visible at the top. To the left, a large anechoic triangular area represents rib acoustic shadow. Through the intercostal window (right side), I observe tissue-like echotexture with scattered bright hyperechoic foci at 1.5–3 cm depth.

**Frames 3-5:** The pleural line remains visible. Through the intercostal window, punctate and short linear hyperechoic structures are seen within a subtly echogenic (tissue-like) parenchyma. These bright foci are clustered and do NOT extend as clean vertical lines to the bottom of the screen.

**Frame 6:** A slightly different angulation shows a clearer transition between the pleural line and underlying tissue. Small hyperechoic linear foci are visible within consolidated tissue — classic appearance of air bronchograms.

**Frames 7-10:** The tissue-like (hepatized) pattern is more evident. Clusters of bright punctate/linear hyperechoic foci are embedded within solidified-appearing lung parenchyma. No horizontal A-lines are visible. The foci are clearly within tissue rather than isolated vertical artifacts.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = false**

- No classic B-lines are identified (no discrete vertical hyperechoic lines arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen without fading)
- No A-lines are visible either, which is consistent with consolidated lung replacing normal aerated lung
- The bright structures seen are punctate/clustered foci WITHIN tissue, not laser-like vertical artifacts
- **Subtype: null**

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Evidence:**
1. **Hepatization:** Tissue-like echogenicity is visible through the intercostal space, replacing normal aerated lung pattern. The lung appears solid and echogenic, resembling liver texture.
2. **Air bronchograms:** Multiple punctate and short linear hyperechoic foci are clearly visible within the hepatized lung parenchyma, representing air-filled bronchi trapped within consolidated tissue. These are especially prominent in frames 6–10.
3. **Loss of normal artifacts:** Absence of both A-lines and B-lines in the visualized lung confirms complete airspace consolidation.

**consolidation_type = "air_bronchogram"**

The predominant and most conspicuous finding is the presence of bright hyperechoic foci (air bronchograms) within hepatized lung tissue, consistent with alveolar consolidation in the anterior zone.
