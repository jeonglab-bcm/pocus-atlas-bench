# Frame-by-Frame Analysis

## Sequential Frame Assessment

**Frames 1-3:** A large homogeneous gray organ (liver) occupies most of the image. A bright, curvilinear hyperechoic line (diaphragm) is visible at the inferior/deep aspect. Above the diaphragm, a relatively hypoechoic area is visible representing the supradiaphragmatic thoracic space. Some tissue-like echogenicity is noted above the diaphragm.

**Frames 4-6:** As the probe sweeps, more of the supradiaphragmatic space becomes visible. The lung tissue above the diaphragm appears increasingly solid and echogenic — resembling the liver parenchyma below (hepatization pattern). A small anechoic stripe suggesting pleural effusion may be present between the diaphragm and the consolidated lung.

**Frames 7-8:** The consolidated lung is now prominently visible. It has a tissue-like, liver-like echotexture. The deep border of the consolidation appears somewhat irregular.

**Frames 9-10:** The largest extent of consolidated lung is seen. The entire visible lung field above the diaphragm appears hepatized with homogeneous tissue-like echogenicity. Small hyperechoic punctate foci within the consolidated parenchyma may represent air bronchograms.

- Occupying a large area of the visualized lung field
- Showing some punctate hyperechoic foci suggestive of air bronchograms within the hepatized tissue

## Summary

This RUQ sagittal view demonstrates a large area of pulmonary consolidation with hepatization pattern in the right lung base, visualized through the liver acoustic window. The findings are consistent with significant alveolar consolidation (e.g., pneumonia, ARDS, or atelectasis). A small accompanying pleural effusion is likely also present.
