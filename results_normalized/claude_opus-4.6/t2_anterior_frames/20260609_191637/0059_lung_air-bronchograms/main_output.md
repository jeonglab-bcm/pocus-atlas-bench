# 0059_lung_air-bronchograms

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1-3:** The near field shows chest wall structures. Below the pleural line, a large hypoechoic/tissue-like region is visible, appearing solid and resembling liver parenchyma (hepatization). Within this region, multiple scattered punctate hyperechoic foci are seen. The deep border appears irregular.

**Frames 4-6:** The consolidated area persists with consistent hepatized appearance. The punctate bright echoes within the consolidated region remain visible, corresponding to air bronchograms. The deep margin of consolidation shows irregular, shredded borders transitioning to aerated lung.

**Frames 7-8:** Similar findings continue. The air bronchograms appear as bright linear/punctate foci within the solid-appearing tissue. Some confluent bright artifacts are visible at the transition zone between consolidated and partially aerated lung.

**Frames 9-10:** The consolidation remains visible. At the periphery/deep border of the consolidation, there are confluent vertical bright artifacts extending toward the far field, suggestive of B-lines in adjacent partially aerated lung.

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: ground_glass**

At the transition zones between consolidated and aerated lung, confluent hyperechoic vertical artifacts are visible, coalescing into bright sheets rather than discrete separated lines. No clear A-lines are identifiable. The pattern is consistent with diffuse interstitial/alveolar disease surrounding the consolidation.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: air_bronchogram**

The predominant finding is a large area of subpleural consolidation with:
- **Hepatization**: Tissue-like, liver-resembling echogenicity of the lung parenchyma
- **Air bronchograms**: Multiple punctate and linear hyperechoic foci within the hepatized lung tissue — the dominant feature
- **Shred sign**: Irregular, fragmented deep border between consolidated and aerated lung

The air bronchograms are the most prominent and consistent finding across all frames, making this the predominant consolidation type. This pattern is consistent with pneumonia or ARDS with significant alveolar filling in the right anterior zone.
