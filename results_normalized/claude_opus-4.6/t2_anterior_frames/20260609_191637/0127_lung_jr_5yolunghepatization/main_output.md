# 0127_lung_jr_5yolunghepatization

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Frames 1–3:
- The pleural line is clearly visible as a bright hyperechoic curvilinear structure.
- Below the pleural line, a **heterogeneous, tissue-like (hepatized) area** is seen occupying a significant subpleural region.
- The deep border of this area is **irregular and fragmented** — consistent with shred sign.
- **Punctate hyperechoic foci** are scattered within the consolidated area, suggestive of air bronchograms.

### Frames 4–6:
- The consolidated area becomes less prominent as the probe sweeps laterally/medially.
- The transition between consolidated and aerated lung is visible with an irregular border (shred sign).
- Some faint **vertical hyperechoic artifacts** (possible B-lines) are seen emanating from the margins of the consolidated area into the deeper field.

### Frames 7–10:
- The lung field becomes progressively more hypoechoic (more aerated).
- The pleural line remains visible.
- A few subtle vertical artifacts are still present but not prominently extending to the bottom of the screen.
- No dominant A-line pattern is seen.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: B-lines = true**

In several frames (particularly 4–6), discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts arise from the pleural line at the margins of the consolidated area. These are limited in number and well-spaced.

**Subtype: septal**

The B-lines are discrete, few in number per intercostal space, with dark lung visible between them — characteristic of septal B-lines at the periphery of a consolidation.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

Findings supporting consolidation:
1. **Hepatization**: Tissue-like echogenicity below the pleural line, particularly in frames 1–3, resembling liver parenchyma.
2. **Shred sign**: The deep margin of the consolidated zone is irregular and "shredded," with a jagged interface between consolidated and aerated lung.
3. **Air bronchograms**: Punctate hyperechoic foci within the consolidated area represent air-filled bronchi.

**Predominant consolidation type: shred_sign**

The most consistent and prominent finding across multiple frames is the irregular, fragmented deep border between the consolidated subpleural lung and the deeper aerated parenchyma.
