# 0008_lung_subpleural-consolidation-covid

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–6:** Below the chest wall (ribs and soft tissue forming the "bat sign" at top), there is a large echogenic/bright structure extending from the pleural line toward the bottom of the screen. Two brighter limbs are seen flanking a dark area between them, and the parenchymal texture appears solid and tissue-like (liver-like). There is loss of normal A-line reverberation artifacts.

**Frames 7–10:** The scanning plane shifts slightly toward a more central view. A distinct round/oval **anechoic (dark) structure** becomes clearly visible within the surrounding echogenic, hepatized lung tissue. This is characteristic of air-filled bronchi within consolidated parenchyma (air bronchograms). The surrounding lung maintains its solid, tissue-like echotexture.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: B-lines present → ground_glass**

In the earlier frames, confluent bright artifacts extend from the pleural line to the bottom of the screen without fading, obliterating any A-line pattern. These coalesce into a diffuse "white lung" appearance rather than remaining discrete and well-separated. This is consistent with **ground glass (confluent) B-lines**, indicating significant alveolar-interstitial pathology.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: Consolidation present → air_bronchogram**

Key findings:
- **Hepatization (tissue-like appearance):** The lung below the pleural line appears solid and echogenic throughout all frames, resembling liver parenchyma, indicating complete loss of aeration.
- **Air bronchograms:** In frames 7–10, distinct round/oval hyperechoic-bordered dark foci are visible within the consolidated lung, representing air-containing bronchi surrounded by non-aerated, hepatized parenchyma.
