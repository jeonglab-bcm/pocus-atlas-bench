# 0039_lung_hepatization-of-lung

# Lung Ultrasound Frame-by-Frame Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Assessment

**Frames 1–3:** The pleural line is identifiable at the upper portion. Below it, I observe tissue-like (hepatized) echogenicity — solid-appearing lung parenchyma resembling liver texture. Scattered bright punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are visible within the consolidated tissue. Some vertical bright artifacts are noted at the margins.

**Frames 4–6:** Consolidation becomes more clearly delineated. The hepatized lung contains increasingly conspicuous air bronchograms — linear and punctate hyperechoic structures within the solid tissue. A transition zone between consolidated and partially aerated lung is visible, with some confluent vertical artifacts (B-lines) at the interface.

**Frames 7–10:** The consolidated segment remains prominent with continued air bronchograms. In some frames, an irregular deep border (shred sign) between consolidated and aerated lung is suggested. Confluent B-line artifacts are visible in partially aerated zones adjacent to the consolidation.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: ground_glass**

Confluent, coalescing vertical hyperechoic artifacts are visible in partially aerated zones adjacent to the consolidation margins, creating a "white lung" appearance in those regions. These are consistent with ground-glass pattern B-lines indicating surrounding alveolar/interstitial disease.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: air_bronchogram**

The dominant finding across all frames is:
- **Hepatization:** Large region of tissue-like echogenicity resembling liver parenchyma, indicating complete loss of aeration
- **Air bronchograms:** Multiple punctate and linear hyperechoic foci within the hepatized lung, representing air-filled bronchi surrounded by consolidated, non-aerated parenchyma — this is the **predominant** feature
- **Shred sign:** Some irregular borders at the deep margin of consolidated lung

This pattern in the anterior zone is consistent with significant alveolar consolidation (e.g., pneumonia, ARDS).
