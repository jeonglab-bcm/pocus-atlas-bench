# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–10:** Across all frames, I observe a consistent pattern:
- **Pleural line**: Clearly visible as a bright hyperechoic horizontal line at the top of the imaging field
- **Rib shadows**: Two dark vertical acoustic shadows from the ribs flanking the intercostal space ("bat sign")
- **Sub-pleural space**: Below the pleural line, there are **horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines)** repeating at regular intervals — these are equidistant horizontal echogenic lines that parallel the pleural line
- **No vertical artifacts**: No bright vertical hyperechoic lines extending from the pleural line to the bottom of the screen are seen in any frame
- **Lung sliding**: Slight variation in the pleural line appearance between frames suggests preserved lung sliding

## B-lines Assessment

**Observation:** All 10 frames consistently demonstrate a dominant **A-line pattern**. The horizontal reverberation artifacts are clearly visible and no vertical hyperechoic artifacts (B-lines) arising from the pleural line and extending without fading to the bottom of the screen are identified.

**Conclusion:**
- **lung_rockets = false**
- **subtype = null**

## Consolidation Assessment

**Observation:** There is no tissue-like (hepatized) echotexture below the pleural line. No irregular shredded borders between aerated and consolidated lung are seen. No hyperechoic punctate or linear foci suggesting air bronchograms are identified. The lung below the pleural line maintains its normal artifact pattern throughout all frames.

**Conclusion:**
- **consolidation = false**
- **consolidation_type = null**

## Summary

This is a **normal anterior lung ultrasound** demonstrating an A-line pattern with preserved pleural line integrity and no evidence of B-lines or consolidation, consistent with normal lung aeration.
