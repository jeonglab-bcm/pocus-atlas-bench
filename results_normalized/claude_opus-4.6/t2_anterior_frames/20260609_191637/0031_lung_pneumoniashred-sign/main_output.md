# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Right Lung (Anterior Zone)

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–3:** The pleural line is visible in the near field. Below it, multiple hyperechoic vertical artifacts extend toward the bottom of the screen. These B-lines are numerous, and in several areas they coalesce, creating a diffusely white ("white lung") appearance. Some subpleural echogenicity is noted.

**Frames 4–5:** Similar pattern persists. The B-lines remain confluent in areas, obscuring any A-line reverberation pattern. A brighter, more tissue-like region begins to emerge in the near field adjacent to the pleural line.

**Frames 6–8:** A clear subpleural area of tissue-like echogenicity (hepatization) is now prominent. Within this consolidated area, punctate hyperechoic foci are visible, consistent with **air bronchograms**. The deep border of this consolidated zone is irregular and fragmented — a classic **shred sign** — where aerated and non-aerated lung interface creates a ragged margin.

**Frames 9–10:** The consolidation remains visible with its irregular deep border. Below the consolidated zone, confluent B-lines extend to the far field. The tissue-like area persists with embedded bright foci.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: ground_glass**

**Rationale:** Across most frames, B-lines are numerous (>3 per intercostal space) and frequently coalesce into confluent sheets of white, obscuring underlying A-lines. The lung field below the pleural line/consolidation appears diffusely bright rather than showing discrete, well-separated vertical artifacts. This is consistent with a ground-glass B-line pattern indicating significant interstitial-alveolar disease.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Predominant type: shred_sign**

**Rationale:** 
- **Hepatization:** A tissue-like, liver-echogenicity region is visible in the subpleural space (most prominent in frames 6–10).
- **Shred sign:** The deep border of the consolidated area is irregular and fragmented — the hallmark "shredded" interface between consolidated and remaining aerated lung.
- **Air bronchograms:** Punctate hyperechoic foci within the hepatized zone are present in several frames.

The most dominant and consistently identifiable feature is the **shred sign** — the irregular, ragged deep margin of the consolidation that distinguishes partial from complete alveolar consolidation.

## Summary

| Finding | Result | Subtype |
|---------|--------|---------|
| B-lines | **Present** | Ground glass (confluent) |
| Consolidation | **Present** | Shred sign (predominant) |

**Clinical correlation:** This combination of confluent B-lines with subpleural consolidation and shred sign in the anterior zone is highly suggestive of significant lung pathology such as pneumonia, ARDS, or severe pulmonary edema with secondary alveolar collapse.
