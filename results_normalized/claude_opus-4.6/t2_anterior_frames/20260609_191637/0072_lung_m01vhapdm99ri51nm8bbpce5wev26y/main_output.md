These frames display the classic **bat sign** configuration:

- **Ribs** on either side casting posterior acoustic shadows
- **Pleural line** visible between the ribs — appears irregular and somewhat thickened/nodular
- **Lung parenchyma** below the pleural line shows some heterogeneity with subtle vertical artifacts extending into the far field

The pleural line is not smooth; there are small focal irregularities suggesting pathology at the lung surface.

### Frames 7–10 (Annotated Pathology)
These frames clearly demonstrate:
- **Sub-pleural nodules**: Small, hypoechoic/echogenic nodular structures disrupting the normally smooth pleural line on both sides. These represent tiny consolidated foci (granulomas in miliary TB) reaching the visceral pleura.
- **B-lines**: Multiple discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen without fading. They are individually distinguishable with dark lung visible between them.

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: hepatization**

They appear as solid, echogenic/hypoechoic nodular structures — essentially tiny hepatized foci — consistent with miliary tuberculous granulomas reaching the visceral pleura. There is no shred sign or air bronchogram pattern; instead, these are discrete solid tissue-like nodules.
