# 0005_lung_subpleural-consolidation-with-shred-sign

# Lung Ultrasound Frame-by-Frame Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Frames 1-3:** The pleural line is clearly visible as a hyperechoic horizontal line. Below it, there is an area of tissue-like echogenicity (hepatization) in the central/mid-field. Punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are seen within this area. Some vertical artifacts are noted at the periphery.

**Frames 4-6:** The consolidated area persists with bright echogenic foci within the hepatized tissue. The irregular deep border of the consolidation is visible, showing some shredding between aerated and non-aerated lung. Vertical artifacts continue to be present adjacent to the consolidation.

**Frames 7-8:** Similar findings with hepatized tissue and bright internal echoes. The borders of the consolidated area appear irregular.

**Frames 9-10:** Consistent with prior frames. The hyperechoic foci within consolidated tissue remain visible. Some vertical artifacts extend from the pleural line in areas peripheral to the consolidation.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: ground_glass**

Vertical hyperechoic artifacts are present arising from the pleural line, particularly in zones adjacent to the consolidated areas. These appear confluent and coalescing in several frames, creating a diffuse bright appearance that obscures normal A-line reverberation patterns, consistent with a ground-glass B-line pattern surrounding the consolidated region.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: air_bronchogram**

The dominant finding across all frames is:
- **Hepatization:** Lung parenchyma appears solid and tissue-like with liver-like echogenicity, indicating complete loss of aeration
- **Air bronchograms:** Punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are clearly visible within the hepatized lung tissue, representing air trapped in bronchi surrounded by consolidated parenchyma
- **Shred sign:** Some irregular deep borders are noted between consolidated and remaining aerated lung

The **predominant type is air_bronchogram**, as the most conspicuous finding is the multiple bright echogenic foci within the hepatized, consolidated lung parenchyma in this anterior zone — a finding highly suggestive of pneumonia or ARDS.
