# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: RT H/T Zone 3 Low

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1-3:** The pleural line is clearly visible as a bright hyperechoic line between rib shadows (dark vertical bands). Multiple discrete hyperechoic vertical artifacts arise from the pleural line and extend toward the bottom of the screen. A-lines are not dominant.

**Frames 4-6:** The same pattern persists. I count approximately 3-4 distinct vertical bright lines (B-lines) in the intercostal spaces, each well-separated by dark lung parenchyma. Some slight movement is noted, consistent with lung sliding.

**Frames 7-9:** B-lines remain visible and discrete. The dark spaces between individual B-lines are maintained. No tissue-like echotexture is seen below the pleural line — the subpleural area remains artifact-dominated rather than solid.

**Frame 10:** Slight increase in echogenicity in the subpleural zone on the right side, likely related to the diaphragm/liver interface at this low zone, rather than true consolidation.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: B-lines present (lung_rockets = true)**

- Multiple discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts arise from the pleural line
- They extend to the far field without fading
- They move synchronously with respiration
- Dark lung parenchyma is clearly visible between individual lines
- Approximately 3–5 B-lines per intercostal space

**Subtype: Septal**

The B-lines are well-defined and individually separated, with clearly visible dark intervals. They do not coalesce into a diffuse white sheet. This pattern suggests thickened interlobular septa (interstitial syndrome).

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: Consolidation = false**

- No tissue-like hepatization pattern identified in the lung parenchyma
- No shred sign (irregular deep border between consolidated and aerated lung)
- No air bronchograms (punctate/linear hyperechoic foci within solid tissue)
- The subpleural area is dominated by B-line artifacts, not solid tissue
- Any tissue-like echogenicity in the deep field is attributable to the diaphragm/liver at this low scanning position

**Consolidation type: null**
