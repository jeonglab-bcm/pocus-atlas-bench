# Lung Ultrasound Frame-by-Frame Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Observations:

**Frames 1 & 4:** Superficial soft tissue layers and pleural line are visible. Below the pleural line, there is a hypoechoic, tissue-like region with scattered hyperechoic foci. Some faint vertical artifacts are visible at the margins of the abnormal area.

**Frames 2 & 5:** The pleural line is well-defined. Below it, a sub-pleural region of tissue-like echogenicity (similar to liver) is visible with an irregular deep border transitioning into normally aerated lung.

**Frames 3, 6 & 8:** A clearly irregular, shredded deep margin is evident between the consolidated sub-pleural lung and the deeper aerated lung. This "torn" or jagged boundary is the hallmark shred sign.

**Frames 7 & 10:** More extensive echogenic/tissue-like material below the pleural line with some punctate hyperechoic foci that may represent small air bronchograms within the consolidated region.

**Frame 9:** Shows the consolidation with a relatively well-demarcated but irregular deep border.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**
**Subtype: septal**

Discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts are visible in several frames, particularly at the periphery/margins of the consolidated areas. These are spaced and do not coalesce into a confluent white-out pattern. They likely represent B-lines arising from the transition zone between consolidated and aerated lung.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**
**Consolidation type: shred_sign**

Across multiple frames, there is clear sub-pleural consolidation characterized by:
- Tissue-like (hepatized) echogenicity immediately below the pleural line
- A distinctly **irregular, shredded deep border** (shred sign) at the interface between consolidated and aerated lung
- Some scattered hyperechoic foci within the consolidated tissue (minor air bronchograms), but the predominant pattern is the shred sign

This pattern is consistent with an anterior zone sub-pleural consolidation, which in the appropriate clinical context may suggest pneumonia, ARDS, or atelectasis.
