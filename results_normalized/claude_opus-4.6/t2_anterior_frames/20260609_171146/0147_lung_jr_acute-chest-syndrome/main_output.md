## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

Multiple consolidation signs are present:
1. **Hepatization:** The lung parenchyma in the dependent portion appears solid and echogenic, resembling liver tissue, indicating complete loss of aeration.
2. **Air bronchograms:** Punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are visible within the hepatized lung tissue (best seen in frames 5–6 and 9–10), representing air trapped in bronchi surrounded by non-aerated lung.
3. An irregular deep border is noted in some frames.

**consolidation_type = "air_bronchogram"**

The predominant and most conspicuous sign is the presence of hyperechoic air bronchograms within hepatized, consolidated lung tissue, seen in the context of an associated pleural effusion — a pattern consistent with pneumonia with parapneumonic effusion or compressive atelectasis.
