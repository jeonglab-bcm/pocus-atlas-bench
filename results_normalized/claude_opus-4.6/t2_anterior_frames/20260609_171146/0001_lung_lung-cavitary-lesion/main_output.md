# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Left Anterior Lung

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1-3:** The pleural line is visible at the top. Below it, there is a large area of tissue-like echogenicity (hepatized lung) with hyperechoic punctate and short linear foci within it — consistent with air bronchograms. The deep border appears irregular.

**Frames 4-5:** The consolidated region remains prominent. The hyperechoic foci become more linear in appearance. Cardiac pulsation may be contributing to movement of the consolidated tissue.

**Frames 6-8:** Prominent linear hyperechoic structures (dynamic air bronchograms) are clearly visible within the hepatized lung parenchyma. The tissue maintains its liver-like echotexture.

**Frames 9-10:** The pattern continues with bright linear air bronchograms clearly seen extending through consolidated lung tissue. An irregular (shredded) deep border is visible between consolidated and partially aerated lung.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = false**

No convincing B-lines are identified. There are no vertical hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending uninterrupted to the bottom of the screen. The bright structures seen are within hepatized lung tissue (air bronchograms), not arising from the pleural interface into aerated lung.

- **Subtype:** N/A

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

Multiple consolidation signs are clearly present:
- **Hepatization:** Lung parenchyma appears solid with liver-like echotexture, completely replacing normal aeration artifacts
- **Air bronchograms:** Prominent linear and punctate hyperechoic foci within the consolidated tissue, appearing dynamic (moving with respiration)
- **Shred sign:** Irregular, fragmented deep border visible in several frames

**consolidation_type = "air_bronchogram"**

The predominant and most conspicuous feature is the linear/punctate hyperechoic air bronchograms within extensively hepatized lung tissue, indicating significant alveolar consolidation in this anterior zone.
