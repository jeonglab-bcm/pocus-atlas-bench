## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1-3:** A bright pleural line is visible at the top of the image. Below it, there is a large area of tissue-like echogenicity (hepatized lung). Scattered punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are visible within this consolidated tissue. A relatively hypoechoic/anechoic stripe is seen superficial to the consolidation, possibly representing a small effusion.

**Frames 4-6:** The consolidated area persists with an irregular, shredded deep border (shred sign) visible where consolidated lung meets remaining aerated lung. Bright punctate foci (air bronchograms) remain clearly visible within the hepatized tissue.

**Frames 7-9:** Similar appearance continues. The deep boundary of the consolidated lung is irregular. Above/adjacent to the consolidation margins, there are confluent vertical hyperechoic artifacts extending from the pleural interface, suggesting B-lines in the transition zone.

**Frames 10:** The consolidated tissue-like area with air bronchograms remains the dominant finding. Some confluent vertical artifacts are noted at the margins of consolidation.

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**
**Subtype: ground_glass**

At the transition zones between consolidated and aerated lung, confluent vertical hyperechoic artifacts are visible, merging into a diffuse bright pattern, consistent with a ground-glass B-line pattern. This is expected adjacent to consolidation in the setting of severe parenchymal disease.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**
**Consolidation type: air_bronchogram**

**Findings:**
- **Hepatization:** Large area of tissue-like echogenicity resembling liver parenchyma, indicating complete loss of aeration
- **Air bronchograms:** Multiple punctate and linear hyperechoic foci within the hepatized tissue, representing air-trapped bronchi — this is the *predominant* pattern
- **Shred sign:** Irregular, shredded deep border between consolidated and partially aerated lung visible in several frames

This pattern is consistent with **significant alveolar consolidation** (e.g., pneumonia, ARDS) in the right lung zone.
