# Lung Ultrasound Analysis - Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1-3:** The pleural line is identifiable at the upper portion of the image. Below the pleural line, there is a large area of homogeneous, hypoechoic tissue-like echotexture (similar to liver parenchyma). Scattered punctate hyperechoic foci are visible within this tissue, particularly in the mid-to-deep field.

**Frames 4-6:** The same tissue-like pattern persists. The hyperechoic punctate foci remain visible and appear relatively static (not moving with respiration in the way B-lines would). The deep border of the consolidated region appears somewhat irregular/shredded.

**Frames 7-8:** Similar pattern continues. Some discrete vertical artifacts may be seen at the margins/transition zone between consolidated and aerated lung.

**Frames 9-10:** Consistent appearance with previous frames. The hepatized tissue and hyperechoic foci remain the dominant findings.

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: B-lines = true (septal subtype)**

At the transition zones/margins of the consolidated region, some discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts are visible extending from the pleural line. These are spaced and not confluent, consistent with a **septal** pattern. However, B-lines are a secondary finding here.

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: Consolidation = true**

**Key findings:**
- **Hepatization:** A large area of tissue-like, liver-resembling echotexture below the pleural line, indicating complete loss of aeration
- **Air bronchograms:** Multiple scattered punctate and short linear hyperechoic foci within the hepatized parenchyma, representing air-filled bronchi surrounded by consolidated alveolar tissue
- **Irregular deep border:** Suggestive of shred sign at the transition to aerated lung

**Consolidation type: "air_bronchogram"**

The predominant finding is hepatized lung tissue with clearly visible static air bronchograms, consistent with significant alveolar consolidation in the anterior zone (e.g., pneumonia, ARDS).
