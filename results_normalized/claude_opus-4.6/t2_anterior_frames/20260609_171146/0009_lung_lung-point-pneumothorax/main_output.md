# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Sequential Frame Review

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Frames 1-3:** A bright, well-defined pleural line is visible near the top of the image. Below the pleural line, multiple hyperechoic vertical artifacts arise and extend to the bottom of the screen. These B-lines are closely spaced and begin to merge/coalesce, particularly in the central portion of the field.

**Frames 4-6:** The pattern continues with numerous B-lines originating from the pleural line. They are densely packed, and in several regions they coalesce into a nearly continuous "white sheet," obliterating normal A-line reverberation artifacts. Some thin dark intervals remain between a few lines, but the dominant appearance is confluent.

**Frames 7-8:** The confluent pattern persists. The entire field below the pleural line is heavily occupied by merging vertical artifacts, with very little dark (aerated) lung parenchyma visible between them.

**Frames 9-10:** Similar confluent B-line pattern. No focal hypoechoic or tissue-like structure is identified. The pleural line remains smooth and well-defined without irregular borders or subpleural consolidation.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: ground_glass**

**Rationale:** Across all frames, there are innumerable B-lines arising from the pleural line that extend to the far field without fading. These B-lines are confluent and coalescing, creating a diffuse "white lung" appearance. Normal A-lines are completely obscured. The dark intercostal spaces between individual B-lines are largely lost, consistent with a ground-glass (alveolar-interstitial syndrome) pattern rather than discrete septal B-lines.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = false**

**Consolidation type: null**

**Rationale:** 
- No tissue-like hepatization is seen — there is no subpleural region resembling liver parenchyma.
- No shred sign — the deep border is not irregular or "shredded"; instead, the artifacts fade gradually.
- No air bronchograms — no punctate or linear hyperechoic foci within solid-appearing tissue.
- The pleural line remains smooth and intact across all frames.
- The bright appearance below the pleural line is entirely artifact-based (confluent B-lines) rather than representing consolidated, airless lung tissue.
