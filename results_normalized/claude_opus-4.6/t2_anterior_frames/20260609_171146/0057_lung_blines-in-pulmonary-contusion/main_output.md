# Lung Ultrasound Analysis - Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–3:** The pleural line is identified at approximately 2–3 cm depth. Arising from it are hyperechoic vertical artifacts extending toward the bottom of the screen. In frames 1–2, these artifacts are somewhat confluent, creating areas of diffuse brightness. In frame 3, the artifacts appear slightly more discrete but still numerous.

**Frames 4–5:** These are the most striking frames. Multiple **punctate hyperechoic foci** are clearly visible within subpleural tissue that appears solid/hepatized (tissue-like echogenicity). These bright dots within consolidated-appearing parenchyma are characteristic of **air bronchograms**. The tissue lacks normal aeration artifacts.

**Frames 6:** Shows the pleural line with some vertical artifacts (B-lines) and areas of subpleural increased echogenicity suggesting partial consolidation.

**Frames 7–8:** The probe angle reveals an **irregular, shredded deep margin** between consolidated (echogenic) tissue superficially and more aerated lung deeper — consistent with a **shred sign**.

**Frame 9:** A well-defined hyperechoic rounded area is visible subpleurally within tissue-like parenchyma, with surrounding B-line artifacts extending deeper.

**Frame 10:** Shows residual B-lines with less prominent consolidation features; the pattern is more of confluent vertical artifacts.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: B-lines = TRUE**

Multiple vertical hyperechoic artifacts are present arising from the pleural line and extending to the far field in several frames. In some frames (1–3, 10), they coalesce into near-confluent sheets obscuring A-lines, while in others they appear more discrete.

**Subtype: ground_glass**

The predominant pattern shows confluent, coalescing B-lines creating a diffusely bright lung surface, particularly in the earlier and later frames, consistent with significant alveolar-interstitial syndrome.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: Consolidation = TRUE**

Multiple consolidation signs are present:
1. **Hepatization** — Tissue-like echogenicity at the subpleural level (frames 4–5, 7–8)
2. **Air bronchograms** — Punctate hyperechoic foci within the consolidated tissue (frames 4–5)
3. **Shred sign** — Irregular deep border between consolidated and aerated lung (frames 7–8)

**Consolidation type: air_bronchogram**

The most prominent and consistent feature across the clip is the **punctate hyperechoic foci within hepatized lung parenchyma**, representing air-filled bronchi surrounded by non-aerated, consolidated tissue. This is the predominant pattern.

---

## Summary

| Finding | Result | Subtype |
|---------|--------|---------|
| B-lines | **Present** | **Ground glass** |
| Consolidation | **Present** | **Air bronchogram** |

This pattern (confluent B-lines + anterior consolidation with air bronchograms) is concerning for severe bilateral lung pathology such as ARDS, severe pneumonia, or advanced pulmonary edema with secondary consolidation.
