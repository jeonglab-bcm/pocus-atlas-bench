# 0133_lung_jr_subpleuralconsolidation

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–3:** The chest wall layers and pleural line are visible. Below the pleural line, there is tissue-like echogenic material (hepatized lung) with an irregular deep border. Some scattered bright punctate foci are visible within the solid-appearing tissue. A few discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts can be seen arising from the pleural line at the margins of consolidation.

**Frame 4:** Extensive subpleural consolidation with clearly hepatized (liver-like) parenchyma. Multiple bright punctate and short linear echogenic foci are visible within the consolidated tissue, consistent with air bronchograms. The deep border is irregular.

**Frames 5–6:** Large area of consolidation is prominent. A hypoechoic/anechoic region (likely a bronchial or vascular structure) is visible within hepatized tissue. Surrounding consolidated tissue contains punctate hyperechoic air bronchograms.

**Frames 7–10:** Hepatized lung tissue remains the dominant finding. The deep boundary between consolidated and aerated lung is irregular and fragmented (shred sign). Bright punctate/linear foci (air bronchograms) are consistently seen within the consolidated parenchyma. Some discrete vertical artifacts (B-lines) are visible at the transition zone margins.

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**
**Subtype: septal**

Discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts are visible in several frames, particularly at the margins/transition zones where consolidation meets partially aerated lung. These are well-spaced and not confluent.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**
**Predominant type: air_bronchogram**

The findings are unequivocal:
- **Hepatization**: Lung parenchyma appears solid and tissue-like, resembling liver echogenicity across virtually all frames.
- **Air bronchograms**: Multiple punctate and short linear hyperechoic foci within the hepatized tissue are the most prominent feature, representing air trapped in bronchi within the consolidated lung.
- **Shred sign**: The deep border of consolidation is irregular and fragmented in several frames (especially 7–10).

The predominant pattern is **air bronchograms within hepatized tissue**, consistent with significant alveolar consolidation in the right upper anterior lung zone.
