# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Frame-by-Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Description

**Frames 1–2:** A bright, curved pleural line is visible in the upper portion. Below the pleural line, discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts (B-lines) extend downward toward the bottom of the screen. Some posterior acoustic shadowing/reverberation is also noted.

**Frames 3–4:** The pleural line remains well-defined. A small subpleural area of increased echogenicity becomes apparent just below the pleural line. Vertical artifacts (B-lines) persist, particularly in frame 4, extending from the pleural line inferiorly.

**Frames 5–6:** A more prominent subpleural area of tissue-like echogenicity is visible below the pleural line. The deep border of this area appears irregular and fragmented — consistent with a shred sign. Some discrete B-lines are still visible at the margins.

**Frames 7–8:** The consolidation becomes more prominent. The echogenicity of the subpleural tissue resembles liver-like parenchyma (hepatization), with an irregular, shredded deep boundary between consolidated and aerated lung.

**Frames 9–10:** The subpleural consolidation is clearly visible with an irregular, ragged deep margin. Some punctate hyperechoic foci may be present within the consolidated area, potentially representing small air bronchograms.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: septal**

In multiple frames (particularly frames 1, 2, and 4), discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts arise from the pleural line and extend toward the bottom of the screen. These are well-spaced with dark lung parenchyma visible between them, consistent with the septal B-line pattern. They likely arise from the interface between consolidated and aerated lung.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**consolidation_type = "shred_sign"**

**Findings:**
- A subpleural area of tissue-like, heterogeneous echogenicity is present throughout most frames and becomes increasingly apparent in the later frames (5–10).
- The deep border of the consolidated area is irregular and fragmented (shredded), characteristic of the **shred sign**.
- There is partial hepatization visible, but the predominant pattern is the irregular, shredded interface between consolidated and aerated lung.
- Some punctate hyperechoic foci within the consolidated area in later frames may represent small air bronchograms.

---

| Finding | Result | Subtype |
