# Lung Ultrasound Frame-by-Frame Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Assessment

**Frames 1–2:** The pleural line is well-visualized as a bright hyperechoic line between two rib shadows. Below the pleural line, there are horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines). No convincing vertical artifacts extending to the screen bottom. Lung appears relatively normally aerated in this intercostal space.

**Frames 3–4:** The pleural line remains visible. Beneath it, a subpleural area of increased echogenicity begins to appear — tissue-like in nature, with a more solid appearance than normal aerated lung. The transition from aerated to non-aerated lung starts to become visible.

**Frames 5–6:** Clear consolidation is now visible. The subpleural lung parenchyma appears hepatized (solid, liver-like echogenicity). A distinct border between consolidated and aerated lung can be seen, with an irregular, jagged margin (shred sign).

**Frames 7–8:** The shred sign is prominent — the deep boundary of the consolidation is irregular and fragmented. The consolidated tissue has homogeneous echogenicity resembling liver. No clear air bronchograms are identified.

**Frame 9:** A more prominent area of subpleural consolidation with hepatization is visible. The border remains irregular.

**Frame 10:** A large consolidated area is visible, appearing as a round/oval tissue-density structure with defined borders. This represents significant alveolar consolidation.

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Key findings:**
- **Tissue-like hepatization:** Present in frames 5–10. The lung parenchyma below the pleural line appears solid and echogenic, resembling liver tissue, indicating loss of normal aeration.
- **Shred sign:** Clearly present in frames 5–9. The deep border between the consolidated and aerated lung is irregular, jagged, and "shredded" — not smooth or well-defined.
- **Air bronchograms:** Not convincingly identified.

**consolidation_type = "shred_sign"**

The predominant finding is the irregular, fragmented deep border of the consolidated region, characteristic of the shred sign. This pattern, combined with hepatization, is consistent with alveolar consolidation in the anterior lung zone — which in a supine patient raises concern for pneumonia or significant atelectasis.
