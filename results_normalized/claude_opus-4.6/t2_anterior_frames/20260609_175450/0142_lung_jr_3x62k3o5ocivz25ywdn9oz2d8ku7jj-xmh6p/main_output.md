# Lung Ultrasound Analysis - Anterior Zone

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–4:** The pleural line is visible at the top of the image. Below it, multiple discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts extend from the pleural line toward the bottom of the screen. These are clearly B-lines. Dark lung parenchyma is visible between individual lines. Approximately 3–5 B-lines are countable per intercostal space.

**Frames 5–7:** The B-lines become more numerous and closely spaced. In some areas, they begin to approach coalescence, though individual lines can still be distinguished. The pleural line becomes more echogenic and slightly irregular.

**Frames 8–10:** A clear tissue-like (hepatized) echogenic area becomes visible immediately below the pleural line. This subpleural area has an **irregular, jagged deep border** where consolidated and aerated lung meet. B-lines arise from this irregular deeper border rather than directly from the pleural line. Small hyperechoic foci may be present within the consolidated area.

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: mixed**

**Rationale:** In earlier frames, B-lines appear discrete and well-spaced (septal pattern). In later frames, B-lines become more numerous and begin to coalesce in certain regions (approaching ground glass). The co-existence of both discrete and semi-confluent B-lines across the clip qualifies as a mixed pattern.

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: shred_sign**

**Rationale:** In frames 8–10, there is a clearly visible subpleural area of tissue-like echogenicity (hepatization) with a distinctly **irregular, shredded deep border** between consolidated and aerated lung. This "shred sign" is the predominant consolidation feature. B-lines emanate from the irregular interface, confirming that the deeper lung remains partially aerated while the superficial portion is consolidated.
