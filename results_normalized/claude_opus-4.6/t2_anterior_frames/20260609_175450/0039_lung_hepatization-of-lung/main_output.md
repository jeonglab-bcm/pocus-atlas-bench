# 0039_lung_hepatization-of-lung

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–3:**
- The pleural line is identified beneath the soft tissue and rib shadows
- Below the pleural line, the lung parenchyma has a tissue-like echotexture rather than normal aerated lung
- No clear horizontal A-lines are visible
- The subpleural region already appears "solidified"

**Frames 4–5:**
- A more prominent anechoic/hypoechoic region appears centrally (possibly small effusion or fluid-filled bronchus)
- On either side, hepatized tissue-like lung is visible
- Bright punctate echoes begin to appear within the consolidated tissue

**Frames 6–8:**
- The consolidated lung tissue dominates the field with liver-like echogenicity (hepatization)
- Clear hyperechoic punctate and linear foci are visible within the hepatized parenchyma — consistent with **air bronchograms**
- The deep border in some frames appears irregular (suggesting shred sign at transitions)

**Frames 9–10:**
- Linear bright echogenic structures within consolidated tissue persist
- The hepatized appearance continues with air bronchograms clearly demonstrated

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = false**

No classic B-lines (vertical hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen) are identified. The lung is extensively consolidated, eliminating the air-fluid interface at the pleural level necessary to generate B-lines. A-lines are also absent due to the consolidation.

- **Subtype:** null

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

Multiple consolidation signs are present:
1. **Hepatization:** The lung parenchyma appears solid and tissue-like, resembling liver echotexture, with complete loss of normal aeration artifacts
2. **Air bronchograms:** Punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are clearly visible within the hepatized lung across multiple frames (most prominent in frames 6–10)
3. **Shred sign:** Irregular deep borders between consolidated and partially aerated lung in some frames

**consolidation_type = "air_bronchogram"**

This pattern is consistent with significant alveolar consolidation (e.g., pneumonia, ARDS) in the anterior lung zone.
