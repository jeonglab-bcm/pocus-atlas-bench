# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–10:** All frames demonstrate a consistent pattern:
- **Superficial layers:** Horizontally-oriented subcutaneous tissue planes in the near field
- **Ribs:** Two dark, rounded structures with posterior acoustic shadowing visible (one predominantly on the left, one partially on the right)
- **Pleural line:** Bright, smooth hyperechoic line visible at approximately 1 cm depth between the rib shadows
- **Subpleural space:** Multiple horizontal, hyperechoic lines parallel to and equidistant from the pleural line — these are **A-lines** (reverberation artifacts)
- **Lung sliding:** Subtle frame-to-frame variation at the pleural line suggests preserved lung sliding

There is slight movement/variation between frames consistent with real-time scanning, but the fundamental pattern remains unchanged across all 10 frames.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Observations:**
- A-lines dominate the subpleural field in every frame
- No vertical hyperechoic artifacts are seen arising from the pleural line
- No laser-like lines extend to the bottom of the screen
- No artifacts move synchronously with respiration in a vertical orientation
- The spaces between A-lines remain dark (air-filled lung)

**Conclusion:**
- **lung_rockets = false**
- **Subtype = null**

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Observations:**
- No tissue-like (hepatized) echotexture is visible below the pleural line
- No irregular shredded borders between aerated and non-aerated lung
- No punctate or linear hyperechoic foci (air bronchograms) within subpleural tissue
- The lung surface maintains a normal aerated appearance with clean A-line reverberation

**Conclusion:**
- **consolidation = false**
- **consolidation_type = null**

---

## Summary

This is a **normal anterior lung ultrasound** demonstrating an **A-line pattern** — indicating normal lung aeration. There is no evidence of B-lines (interstitial syndrome) or consolidation. The findings are consistent with a dry, normally aerated lung in this anterior zone.
