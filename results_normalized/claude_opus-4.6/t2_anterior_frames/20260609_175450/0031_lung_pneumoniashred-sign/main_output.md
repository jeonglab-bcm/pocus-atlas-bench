## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–2:** The pleural line is identified in the near field. Below it, multiple hyperechoic vertical artifacts arise and extend to the far field. These artifacts are densely packed and appear to coalesce in several regions, creating broad sheets of whiteness that obscure normal A-line reverberation.

**Frames 3–5:** B-lines remain prominent. Some frames show slightly more discrete artifacts with narrow dark intervals between them, but the overall impression is of confluent vertical artifacts merging together, particularly centrally in the image sector.

**Frames 6–7:** A striking change occurs — a subpleural area of tissue-like echogenicity becomes visible just deep to the pleural line. This area appears solid, with an irregular ("shredded") deep border transitioning into aerated lung. B-lines emanate from the margins of this consolidated zone.

**Frames 8–10:** The subpleural consolidation persists with an irregular deep margin. The echogenicity is tissue-like but the border with deeper aerated lung is jagged and irregular rather than smooth. Confluent B-lines continue to be visible emanating from the deeper boundary. Small punctate hyperechoic foci within the consolidated region suggest trapped air (possible air bronchograms), though the shredded border is more dominant.

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: ground_glass**

Multiple B-lines are present throughout the clip. In the majority of frames, these vertical artifacts are confluent — they coalesce into broad white sheets that obscure A-lines entirely, creating a "white lung" appearance. This is consistent with a **ground glass** pattern, suggesting significant alveolar-interstitial edema or diffuse interstitial disease.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: shred_sign**

In frames 6–10, a subpleural area of tissue-like (hepatized) echogenicity is clearly visible. The dominant feature is the **irregular, shredded deep border** between the consolidated lung and the remaining aerated parenchyma. This "shred sign" indicates a non-translobar consolidation pattern with patchy alveolar filling. The irregular interface with scattered B-lines arising from the boundary is characteristic of pneumonia or ARDS-related consolidation in an anterior lung zone.
