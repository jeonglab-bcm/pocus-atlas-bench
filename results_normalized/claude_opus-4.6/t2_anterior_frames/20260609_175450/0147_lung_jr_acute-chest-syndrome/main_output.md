# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Frame-by-Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–4 & 7–8:** A bright pleural line/rib complex is visible at the top. Below this, a large **anechoic (dark) space** is present, consistent with **pleural effusion**. At the deeper aspect of each frame, there is echogenic, tissue-like material with an irregular border — consistent with consolidated lung floating within or adjacent to the effusion.

**Frames 5–6 & 9–10:** These frames provide closer/different-angle views of the consolidated lung parenchyma. The tissue appears solid with **liver-like echogenicity (hepatization)**. Within this hepatized tissue, multiple **punctate and linear hyperechoic foci** are visible, representing **air bronchograms** — air trapped within bronchi of the non-aerated lung.

Throughout all frames, the deep border of the consolidation appears somewhat irregular (suggesting a shred sign component), though the air bronchograms are the most prominent feature.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = false**

No classic B-lines are identified. B-lines require a normally aerated pleural interface to generate vertical reverberation artifacts. In these images, the lung is extensively consolidated and surrounded by effusion — there is no normal aerated pleural line from which B-lines could arise. A-lines are also absent due to the effusion/consolidation replacing the normal air interface.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Findings:**
1. **Hepatization:** The lung parenchyma appears solid, echogenic, and tissue-like — resembling liver texture. It has lost all normal aeration artifacts.
2. **Air bronchograms:** Multiple punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are clearly visible within the hepatized lung tissue across several frames (especially frames 5–6, 9–10).
3. **Shred sign:** Some irregularity of the deep border between consolidated and partially aerated lung is seen.

**consolidation_type = "air_bronchogram"**

The predominant and most conspicuous consolidation feature is the presence of air bronchograms within extensively hepatized lung, accompanied by a significant pleural effusion. This pattern is consistent with pneumonia, ARDS, or atelectasis with air trapping in the bronchial tree.
