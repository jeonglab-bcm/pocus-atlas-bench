# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–6:** The pleural line is clearly visualized at the top. Below it, two broad hyperechoic vertical structures extend from the pleural line to the bottom of the screen, separated by narrow dark zones (consistent with rib shadow borders). The bright areas between/below the ribs show a diffuse, coalescing white pattern — individual B-lines are no longer distinguishable as separate entities. The echotexture in some subpleural regions appears tissue-like.

**Frames 7–10:** The view becomes more centered on the intercostal window. A distinct **round/oval anechoic structure** is visible just below the pleural line within a tissue-like (hepatized) subpleural region. Below this, the broad hyperechoic sheet continues to the far field. The dark rounded structure has the morphology of a **bronchogram** within consolidated lung tissue.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: B-lines = TRUE**

**Subtype: ground_glass**

**Evidence:**
- The B-lines have coalesced into a diffuse, confluent white sheet obliterating any A-line pattern
- No discrete, individually separable B-lines are visible — they have merged into a "white lung" appearance
- The hyperechoic artifact extends uninterrupted from the pleural line to the bottom of the screen
- This indicates severe interstitial-alveolar syndrome (e.g., ARDS, pulmonary edema)

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: Consolidation = TRUE**

**Type: air_bronchogram**

**Evidence:**
- A subpleural region of tissue-like (hepatized) echogenicity is seen immediately below the pleural line, most clearly in frames 7–10
- Within this hepatized region, **round/oval anechoic-to-hypoechoic foci** are visible, consistent with air/fluid bronchograms
- The consolidated area transitions into the confluent B-line (ground glass) pattern at depth, suggesting a gradient from full consolidation to interstitial-alveolar involvement
- The shredded/irregular deep border of the consolidation merging into B-lines supports the diagnosis
