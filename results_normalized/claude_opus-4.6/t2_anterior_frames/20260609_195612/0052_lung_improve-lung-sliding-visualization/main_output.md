# Lung Ultrasound Frame-by-Frame Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Frame 1 (2/270):** The pleural line is visible at ~2 cm depth. Below it, there is a heterogeneous, tissue-like echogenic region with a bright central vertical artifact extending deep. Hyperechoic punctate foci are visible within the subpleural tissue.

**Frame 2 (29/270):** Similar appearance. A rounded hypoechoic structure is seen superficially (likely a rib shadow/vessel). Below the pleural line, hepatized tissue-like echogenicity persists with scattered bright foci within.

**Frame 3 (56/270):** Pleural line visible. Subpleural consolidated tissue is present with bright hyperechoic punctate foci (air bronchograms). There are also vertical bright artifacts extending from the deeper border.

**Frame 4 (89/270):** Same pattern — tissue-like echogenicity below the pleural line with air bronchograms and some confluent vertical artifacts at the margins.

**Frame 5 (116/270):** Narrower field of view. Consolidation is still visible with irregular deep borders (shred sign) and bright foci.

**Frame 6 (147/270):** Transition zone. Some residual tissue-like echogenicity but less prominent. The deeper field is more anechoic.

**Frame 7 (174/270):** Less consolidation visible. The pleural line is clear, with some heterogeneous subpleural tissue and possible A-lines forming deeper.

**Frame 8 (206/270):** Predominantly normal-appearing. Pleural line visible with horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) below.

**Frame 9 (234/270):** A-line dominant pattern. Minimal pathology.

**Frame 10 (265/270):** A-line dominant. Normal aeration pattern in this region.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: B-lines = true**

**Subtype: ground_glass**

In frames 1–5, there are confluent, coalescing vertical hyperechoic artifacts extending from the pleural line and the margins of consolidated tissue toward the bottom of the screen. These merge into broad bright sheets in several frames, obscuring any A-line pattern. This is consistent with a ground-glass B-line pattern, indicating significant alveolar/interstitial disease surrounding the consolidated zone.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: Consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: air_bronchogram**

In frames 1–6, there is clear subpleural **hepatization** — the lung parenchyma appears solid and tissue-like, resembling liver echogenicity, with loss of normal aeration artifacts. Within this hepatized lung, multiple **punctate and linear hyperechoic foci** are visible, representing air-filled bronchi (air bronchograms). An **irregular, shredded deep border** (shred sign) is also appreciable in frames 4–5 at the interface between consolidated and aerated lung.

The predominant feature is the **air bronchograms within hepatized lung**, making this the primary consolidation subtype.

---

## Summary

| Finding | Result | Subtype |
|---------|--------|---------|
| B-lines | **True** | Ground glass |
| Consolidation | **True** | Air bronchogram |

This pattern in an anterior lung zone is highly concerning for significant pulmonary pathology such as pneumonia, ARDS, or severe pulmonary edema with associated consolidation.
