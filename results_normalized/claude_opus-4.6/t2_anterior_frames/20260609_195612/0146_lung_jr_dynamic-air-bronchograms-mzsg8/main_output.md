# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Anterior Zone

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–3:** The pleural line is well-defined and hyperechoic. Below it, I observe predominantly horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines). There is a rounded, relatively hypoechoic structure visible on the right side of the field that moves in and out of view. A few discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts extend from the pleural line downward.

**Frames 4–5:** Similar pattern with a prominent pleural line, A-lines dominating, and a few scattered vertical bright artifacts. The hypoechoic structure on the right becomes less prominent.

**Frames 6–10:** The A-line pattern becomes more dominant. Multiple clear horizontal reverberation artifacts are visible at regular intervals below the pleural line. A few discrete vertical artifacts (B-lines) are intermittently visible, well-separated with normal-appearing dark lung parenchyma between them.

## B-lines Assessment

**Findings:** Across the sequence, I identify several discrete, well-spaced hyperechoic vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line that extend toward the far field. Between these artifacts, A-lines remain clearly visible and the lung parenchyma appears dark/normal. The B-lines are few (estimated ≤3 per intercostal space) and do not coalesce.

**Conclusion:**
- **lung_rockets = true**
- **Subtype = septal** (discrete, well-spaced B-lines with dark lung parenchyma visible between them, A-lines partially preserved)

## Consolidation Assessment

**Findings:** While a rounded, relatively hypoechoic area is seen transiently in the right portion of the field in early frames, it lacks:
- Clear hepatized (liver-like) tissue echotexture
- Definitive irregular/shredded deep borders (shred sign)
- Punctate or linear hyperechoic foci consistent with air bronchograms

The structure is likely a rib shadow or adjacent soft tissue rather than true alveolar consolidation.

**Conclusion:**
- **consolidation = false**
- **consolidation_type = null**
