# 0047_lung_atypical-presentation-covid19

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–2:** A smooth, curved hyperechoic pleural line is visible. Arising from it, 2–3 discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts extend toward the bottom of the screen — classic **B-lines**. Between them, the lung field is dark (aerated). Faint A-line reverberation artifacts are visible in the far field.

**Frames 3–4:** The pleural line remains visible. Multiple discrete B-lines continue to be seen, well-separated by dark intervening lung. The overall pattern remains consistent with individually countable B-lines.

**Frames 5–6:** As the probe sweeps, a change in subpleural morphology becomes apparent. A hypoechoic-to-isoechoic tissue-like region appears just beneath the pleural line. Its deep border appears irregular and "shredded" — not a smooth linear margin. B-lines arise from the periphery of this region.

**Frames 7–8:** The subpleural tissue-like area becomes more prominent. It demonstrates echogenicity approaching that of soft tissue (hepatized appearance) with an irregular, non-linear deep boundary separating it from the aerated lung below. Vertical artifacts (B-lines) persist at the margins.

**Frames 9–10:** The consolidated area is broadest here. The deep border remains ragged and irregular. No convincing hyperechoic punctate foci (air bronchograms) are seen within the consolidated region. Discrete B-lines are visible at the lateral edges.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: septal**

Justification: Throughout the clip, discrete, individually countable B-lines are seen arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen. Dark lung parenchyma is clearly visible between adjacent B-lines. They do not coalesce into a "white lung" or ground-glass pattern.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: shred_sign**

Justification: In the middle-to-later frames (5–10), a subpleural region of tissue-like echogenicity is identified. Its key feature is the **irregular, shredded deep border** between the consolidated (non-aerated) lung and the deeper aerated parenchyma. This is the classic "shred sign." While there is mild hepatization of the superficial lung, no convincing air bronchograms are seen within. The predominant finding is the irregular transition border, making shred sign the primary classification.
