# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Frame-by-Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Description

**Frames 1–3:**
- A bright pleural line is clearly visible in the superficial field
- Below the pleural line, there is a large area of **heterogeneous, tissue-like echogenicity** (resembling liver parenchyma)
- The deep border of this area is **irregular and jagged** (shredded appearance)
- Within the consolidated region, there are **punctate hyperechoic foci** suggestive of air bronchograms
- The consolidation occupies a significant subpleural area

**Frames 4–6:**
- The consolidation becomes less prominent as the probe appears to slide
- A transition zone is visible between consolidated and aerated lung
- Some **discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts** arise from the pleural line at the margins of the consolidation
- A-lines are partially obscured in some areas

**Frames 7–10:**
- The lung appears progressively more aerated
- The pleural line is clearly seen with mild irregularity
- Scattered **discrete B-lines** (1–2 per field) are visible, extending from the pleural line
- Deeper lung zones are relatively hypoechoic (more normal aeration)

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: septal**

**Rationale:** In the transition zones (frames 4–9), discrete, well-spaced vertical hyperechoic artifacts arise from the pleural line, consistent with septal B-lines. They are few in number (≤3 per intercostal space), clearly separated by dark lung parenchyma between them. These likely represent perilesional interstitial thickening adjacent to the consolidation.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Predominant type: shred_sign**

**Rationale:**
- **Hepatization:** Frames 1–4 demonstrate tissue-like echogenicity below the pleural line, with the lung resembling liver parenchyma — indicating loss of aeration
- **Shred sign (predominant):** The deep border of the consolidated area is highly irregular, "shredded," and jagged — representing the interface between consolidated and partially aerated lung
- **Air bronchograms:** Punctate hyperechoic foci are visible within the hepatized tissue in frames 1–3, representing air-trapped bronchi within consolidated parenchyma

The **shred sign** is classified as predominant because the irregular deep margin is the most conspicuous and consistent finding across multiple frames.

---

## Summary

| Finding | Result | Subtype |
|---------|--------|---------|
| B-lines | **Present** | Septal |
| Consolidation | **Present** | Shred sign |

This pattern is consistent with **anterior lung consolidation**, potentially from pneumonia, ARDS, or atelectasis, with perilesional interstitial involvement (septal B-lines at margins).
