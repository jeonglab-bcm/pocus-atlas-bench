# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Right Lung, Anterior Zone

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–3:** The pleural line is visible in the near field. Below it, multiple vertical hyperechoic artifacts (B-lines) arise and extend to the far field. Many of these B-lines coalesce, creating broad sheets of white that obscure normal A-line reverberation patterns. Some discrete B-lines are visible at the periphery.

**Frames 4–5:** The pattern continues with confluent B-lines dominating. The lung surface below the pleural line appears diffusely bright. There is increasing brightness in the subpleural region suggesting evolving tissue-like changes.

**Frames 6–8:** A notable area of subpleural tissue-like echogenicity (hepatization) appears in the near field, with an irregular, fragmented deep border transitioning into aerated lung beyond. Within this area, punctate hyperechoic foci are visible, consistent with air bronchograms. Confluent B-lines continue to emanate from the border of this region.

**Frames 9–10:** The consolidation area persists with an irregular shredded deep margin. Confluent B-lines extend from the consolidation border into the far field. The tissue-like echogenicity remains visible.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: B-lines = true**

**Subtype: ground_glass**

Multiple B-lines are present throughout all frames, and they predominantly coalesce into broad confluent sheets, creating a diffuse white-out of the lung field. A-lines are obliterated. This ground-glass pattern is consistent with significant alveolar-interstitial syndrome (e.g., pulmonary edema or ARDS).

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: shred_sign**

In frames 6–10, a subpleural area of tissue-like (hepatized) echogenicity is visible with an **irregular, shredded deep border** (shred sign) where consolidated lung transitions into partially aerated lung. Some punctate hyperechoic foci within this area suggest air bronchograms, but the predominant finding is the irregular fragmented interface. This pattern is consistent with a non-translobar (anterior) consolidation, as seen in ARDS or pneumonia.
