# 0148_lung_jr_pediatric-consolidation

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–3:** The pleural line is visible at approximately 1–2 cm depth. Below it, there is a large hyperechoic mass with irregular borders extending downward. A strong vertical artifact or consolidated tissue is seen. The surrounding lung appears dark, and the tissue has a somewhat solid/echogenic quality.

**Frames 4–5:** A clearer view emerges of a subpleural consolidated region. The lung parenchyma below the pleural line appears tissue-like (hepatized) — solid and echogenic, resembling liver. Within this consolidated area, there are punctate hyperechoic foci consistent with **air bronchograms**. The deep border appears somewhat irregular.

**Frames 6–7:** The consolidation is prominent. A large area of hepatized lung is visible (approximately 2–5 cm depth), with clearly defined bright punctate/linear echogenic foci within it representing air-filled bronchi trapped in non-aerated lung. The tissue-like echotexture is very evident.

**Frames 8–9:** The view transitions to show vertical hyperechoic artifacts extending from the pleural line downward. These appear confluent/coalescing, consistent with B-lines at the margins of the consolidation. Some frames show the "shred sign" — an irregular, ragged deep border between consolidated and aerated lung.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: ground_glass**

Confluent, coalescing B-lines are visible in frames 8–9 and at the periphery of the consolidation in earlier frames. They merge into broad white sheets, obscuring A-lines, consistent with surrounding alveolar edema/interstitial disease adjacent to the consolidated region.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: air_bronchogram**

Findings:
- **Hepatization:** Lung parenchyma appears solid and tissue-like in frames 4–7
- **Air bronchograms:** Punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are clearly visible within the hepatized lung (most prominent in frames 5–7)
- **Shred sign:** Irregular deep border visible in some frames (frame 8)

The predominant pattern is **air bronchograms** within hepatized lung tissue, consistent with significant alveolar consolidation (e.g., pneumonia, ARDS) in the anterior zone.
