# 0005_lung_subpleural-consolidation-with-shred-sign

# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Anterior Zone

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–10:** Across all sequential frames, I consistently observe:
- **Near field:** Chest wall soft tissue with rib shadows laterally
- **Pleural line:** A bright hyperechoic linear structure visible in the upper mid-portion
- **Sub-pleural region:** An area of tissue-like echogenicity (hepatization) immediately below the pleural line, appearing solid and resembling liver parenchyma
- **Within the hepatized area:** Multiple punctate and short linear hyperechoic foci — consistent with air bronchograms
- **Deep field:** Relatively hypoechoic/dark below the consolidated region
- **Absence of A-lines:** No horizontal reverberation artifacts are seen
- **No convincing discrete vertical B-lines** extending uninterrupted from the pleural line to the bottom of the screen

The findings remain remarkably stable across all frames, suggesting a static, non-tidal consolidation pattern.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: B-lines = false**

No vertical hyperechoic artifacts meeting B-line criteria (arising from pleural line, extending to the far field without fading) are identified. The absence of A-lines is explained by the consolidation itself replacing the normally aerated lung interface, rather than by interstitial syndrome artifacts.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: Consolidation = true**

**Findings supporting consolidation:**
1. **Tissue-like hepatization:** The sub-pleural lung parenchyma appears solid, echogenic, and liver-like — consistent with complete loss of aeration
2. **Air bronchograms:** Multiple bright punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are visible within the hepatized tissue, representing residual air within bronchi surrounded by non-aerated lung
3. **Irregular deep border:** The transition between consolidated and deeper lung appears somewhat irregular (shred-like in some frames)

**Consolidation type: air_bronchogram**

The predominant consolidation sign is the presence of air bronchograms within hepatized lung tissue, visible in virtually every frame.

---

## Summary

| Finding | Result | Subtype |
|---------|--------|---------|
| B-lines | **False** | N/A |
| Consolidation | **True** | **Air bronchogram** |

**Clinical correlation:** Anterior zone consolidation with air bronchograms in an adult is concerning for pneumonia (bacterial, viral, or aspiration), ARDS, or atelectasis. The anterior location and static air bronchograms may suggest non-dependent consolidation consistent with severe pneumonia or ARDS.
