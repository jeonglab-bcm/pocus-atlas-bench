## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1-2:** The pleural line is visible as a bright hyperechoic horizontal line. Below it, there is a region of tissue-like echogenicity (hepatized lung). A bright hyperechoic focus is seen within this tissue. The area below shows dampened A-line artifacts.

**Frames 3-5:** The consolidated region becomes more prominent. Multiple punctate and short linear hyperechoic foci are seen within the hepatized tissue, consistent with air bronchograms. The deep border appears irregular, with some shredding where consolidated lung meets aerated tissue.

**Frames 6-8:** The consolidation is well-visualized with clear air bronchograms (bright linear hyperechoic structures within solid-appearing lung tissue). The deep margin shows an irregular, shredded border transitioning to more aerated lung.

**Frames 9-10:** The transition zone becomes more apparent. Discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts (B-lines) are visible extending from the deeper margins of the consolidated area into aerated lung below, reaching toward the bottom of the screen. Some bright linear reverberation artifacts are present.

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: septal**

Discrete B-lines are visible, particularly in the later frames (9-10), at the transition zone between consolidated and aerated lung. They appear as well-spaced vertical hyperechoic artifacts extending toward the bottom of the screen, with dark lung visible between them.

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: air_bronchogram**

Key findings:
- **Hepatization:** Lung parenchyma appears solid and tissue-like, resembling liver echogenicity
- **Air bronchograms:** Multiple punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are clearly visible within the hepatized tissue, representing air-filled bronchi surrounded by consolidated lung
- **Shred sign:** An irregular, shredded deep border is seen at the interface between consolidated and aerated lung

The predominant finding is **air bronchograms** within hepatized lung tissue, making this the primary consolidation type. This pattern in the left anterior lung zone is consistent with significant alveolar consolidation (e.g., pneumonia, ARDS).
