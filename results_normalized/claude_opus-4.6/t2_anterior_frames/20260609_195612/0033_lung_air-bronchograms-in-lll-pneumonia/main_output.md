# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: LT Lung Zone 2

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1-3:** The pleural line is visible at the top of the image. Below it, a large region of tissue-like echotexture (solid-appearing parenchyma) is present. Multiple bright hyperechoic punctate and linear foci are scattered within this solid region. Vertical artifacts (B-lines) are seen at the periphery of the consolidated area.

**Frames 4-6:** The consolidated tissue remains clearly visible with persistent air bronchograms (bright dots/lines) within it. The deep border of the consolidation appears irregular and shredded. Confluent B-lines are visible in adjacent aerated lung.

**Frames 7-8:** Similar findings. The hepatized lung maintains its liver-like echotexture. Dynamic air bronchograms (hyperechoic foci) are prominent within the consolidated tissue.

**Frames 9-10:** The pattern is consistent throughout the clip. Consolidation with internal hyperechoic foci persists, along with surrounding confluent B-lines.

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: ground_glass**

Confluent, coalescing B-lines are visible in areas adjacent to the consolidation, creating a diffusely bright appearance in the surrounding aerated lung zones. These merge together, obscuring A-lines.

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: air_bronchogram**

### Findings:
- **Hepatization:** Large area of tissue-like echogenicity below the pleural line resembling liver parenchyma, indicating complete loss of aeration
- **Air bronchograms:** Multiple punctate and linear hyperechoic foci within the hepatized lung, representing air-filled bronchi trapped within the solid consolidated parenchyma — this is the **predominant** finding
- **Shred sign:** The deep border of the consolidation is irregular and shredded where it interfaces with remaining aerated lung
