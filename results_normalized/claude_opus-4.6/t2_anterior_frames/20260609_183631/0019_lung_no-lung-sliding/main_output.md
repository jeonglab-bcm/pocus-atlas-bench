**Frames 1–2:** The superficial layers show well-defined subcutaneous tissue and chest wall musculature with horizontal layering. A bright pleural line is identified. Between the ribs, prominent horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) repeat at regular intervals below the pleural line.

**Frames 3–5:** The pattern remains consistent. A-lines dominate the sub-pleural space. A small bright echogenic focus appears at the right edge (likely a cursor/artifact). No vertical artifacts are seen arising from the pleural line.

**Frames 6–8:** Similar A-line dominant pattern persists. The pleural line appears smooth and continuous between rib shadows. The lung field below shows repetitive horizontal A-lines with no disruption of the normal aeration pattern.

**Frames 9–10:** Consistent with prior frames. A-lines remain clearly visible. No tissue-like echotexture or irregular borders are identified below the pleural line.

## B-lines Assessment

**Observation:** Across all 10 frames, the sub-pleural space is dominated by well-defined, evenly spaced horizontal A-lines. No hyperechoic vertical artifacts extending from the pleural line to the bottom of the screen are identified. There is no white-out pattern or discrete vertical lines meeting B-line criteria.

**Conclusion:**
- **lung_rockets = false**
- **Subtype = null**

## Consolidation Assessment

**Observation:** The lung parenchyma below the pleural line maintains a normal aeration pattern throughout all frames. There is no tissue-like hepatization (no liver-like echogenicity), no irregular shredded deep border (shred sign), and no punctate/linear hyperechoic foci within a consolidated region (air bronchograms). The dark structures are rib shadows, not consolidated lung.

**Conclusion:**
- **consolidation = false**
- **consolidation_type = null**

## Summary

This anterior lung zone demonstrates a **normal A-profile** — a reassuring pattern of preserved lung aeration with no evidence of interstitial syndrome (B-lines) or alveolar consolidation.
