# Lung Ultrasound Frame-by-Frame Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Assessment

**Frames 1–3:** The pleural line is identifiable at the upper portion. Below it, there is a large area of tissue-like (hepatized) echogenicity, resembling liver parenchyma. Bright punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are visible within this tissue.

**Frames 4–5:** The consolidated region becomes more clearly delineated. Two distinct areas of solid-appearing lung tissue are visible, separated by a darker (more aerated) zone. Bright echogenic spots (air bronchograms) are clearly seen within the hepatized tissue.

**Frames 6–7:** The tissue-like pattern persists with scattered hyperechoic foci. The deep border of the consolidated tissue appears somewhat irregular.

**Frames 8–10:** Some vertical hyperechoic artifacts are visible at the margins of consolidation extending deeper, consistent with B-lines arising from the transitional zone between consolidated and partially aerated lung. Linear bright artifacts within the tissue persist.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: septal**

Discrete B-line artifacts are identified at the margins/borders of the consolidation, arising where partially aerated lung interfaces with consolidated tissue. They are spaced and do not coalesce into a "white-out" pattern.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: air_bronchogram**

**Key findings:**
- **Hepatization:** Extensive tissue-like echogenicity below the pleural line with a liver-like echotexture, indicating loss of aeration
- **Air bronchograms:** Multiple punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are clearly visible within the hepatized lung parenchyma throughout the clip, representing air trapped in bronchi surrounded by consolidated/fluid-filled alveoli
- **Irregular deep border:** Some frames suggest a shred-like interface, but air bronchograms predominate

This pattern in the **anterior lung zone** is highly concerning for significant alveolar consolidation (e.g., pneumonia, ARDS).
