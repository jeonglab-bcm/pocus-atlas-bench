# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–4, 7–8:** The pleural line is visible at the top. Below it, there is a large area of hypoechoic/anechoic space, followed by dense hyperechoic structures deep in the field. The tissue below the pleural line has a solid, organ-like echotexture rather than normal aerated lung. Bright linear/punctate foci are visible within the deeper structures.

**Frames 5–6, 9–10:** These frames show heterogeneous tissue-like echogenicity occupying much of the field below the pleural line. Scattered hyperechoic punctate and linear foci are clearly visible within this consolidated parenchyma — classic **air bronchograms**. An irregular, shredded border between consolidated and partially aerated lung is also seen in some regions.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: B-lines = TRUE | Subtype: ground_glass**

In the transitional zones between consolidated and residual aerated lung (most visible in frames 5, 9, 10), there are confluent vertical hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line that coalesce into a diffusely bright/white appearance, obliterating A-lines. This represents a **ground glass** pattern consistent with severe alveolar/interstitial disease surrounding the consolidation.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: Consolidation = TRUE | Type: air_bronchogram**

Key findings across multiple frames:
- **Hepatization**: The lung parenchyma appears solid and echogenic, resembling liver tissue, with complete loss of normal aeration artifacts
- **Air bronchograms**: Punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are clearly visible within the hepatized parenchyma (best seen in frames 5, 6, 9), representing air trapped in bronchi surrounded by non-aerated lung
- **Shred sign**: Irregular, jagged borders between consolidated and aerated lung visible in some frames

The **predominant finding is air bronchograms** within hepatized tissue, indicating significant anterior zone alveolar consolidation — a finding highly suggestive of pneumonia or ARDS.
