# Lung Ultrasound Frame-by-Frame Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Frames 1-2:** The pleural line is visible as a hyperechoic horizontal line in the upper field. Below it, there is a relatively hypoechoic region with some scattered hyperechoic foci. A few discrete vertical artifacts are faintly visible extending from the pleural line.

**Frames 3-5:** The pleural line remains well-defined. Below the pleural line, there is a small subpleural area of increased echogenicity with tissue-like texture. Some discrete vertical hyperechoic lines (B-lines) can be identified arising from the pleural surface.

**Frames 6-8:** These frames show a more clearly defined area of subpleural consolidation. The deep border between the consolidated tissue and the aerated lung beneath is irregular and "shredded" — classic shred sign. Some punctate hyperechoic foci within the consolidated area may represent small air bronchograms.

**Frames 9-10:** The consolidation persists with an irregular deep margin. A discrete B-line artifact is visible extending from the edge of the consolidated area toward the bottom of the screen. The surrounding lung shows normal aeration pattern.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: septal**

Discrete, well-separated B-lines are visible in several frames arising from the pleural line and the borders of the consolidated area. They are individually identifiable with dark lung parenchyma between them, consistent with a **septal** pattern (≤3 per intercostal space).

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: shred_sign**

Multiple frames (particularly frames 6–9) demonstrate a small subpleural consolidation with:
- Tissue-like echogenicity (mild hepatization) immediately below the pleural line
- An **irregular, shredded deep border** between the consolidated and aerated lung — the hallmark shred sign
- Scattered punctate hyperechoic foci suggesting small air bronchograms within the consolidated region

The predominant pattern is **shred sign**, indicating a small, non-translobar consolidation with an irregular interface between atelectatic/consolidated and aerated lung tissue. This pattern is consistent with non-cardiogenic causes such as pneumonia or ARDS.
