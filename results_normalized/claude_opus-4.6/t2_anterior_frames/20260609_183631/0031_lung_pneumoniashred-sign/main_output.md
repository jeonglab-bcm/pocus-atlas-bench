# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Right Lung (Anterior Zone)

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–3:** A bright, thickened pleural line is visible near the top of the image. Below it, multiple hyperechoic vertical artifacts arise from the pleural line and extend to the bottom of the screen. These are numerous and in several areas begin to coalesce, creating regions of diffuse brightness. No clear A-lines are visible.

**Frames 4–5:** The pattern continues with dense, coalescing vertical artifacts. The lung field appears diffusely bright ("white lung" regions) with individual B-lines becoming difficult to distinguish in some sectors.

**Frames 6–8:** A distinct, tissue-like echogenic area becomes visible in the subpleural region. This area demonstrates an irregular, jagged deep border transitioning into aerated lung with B-lines. Some punctate hyperechoic foci are visible within this subpleural echogenic zone, suggesting air trapped within consolidated tissue.

**Frames 9–10:** The subpleural consolidation remains visible with its irregular shredded border. Confluent B-lines continue below and adjacent to the consolidated area, extending to the bottom of the screen.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: B-lines = true**

**Subtype: ground_glass**

**Rationale:** Throughout the clip, numerous B-lines are present that coalesce and merge in most intercostal views, creating a diffusely bright, "white lung" appearance. Individual B-lines are difficult to separate in most frames. A-lines are completely abolished. This confluent pattern is consistent with ground_glass B-lines, indicating significant alveolar-interstitial syndrome.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: shred_sign**

**Rationale:** In multiple frames (particularly 6–10), a subpleural tissue-like echogenic region is visible with:
- Irregular, shredded deep margin between consolidated and aerated lung
- Small hyperechoic foci within the consolidated zone (possible air bronchograms)
- Hepatized tissue appearance in the near-field

The **predominant pattern is shred sign**, as the irregular, jagged border between the consolidated subpleural lung and the underlying aerated (B-line-containing) parenchyma is the most conspicuous finding.

| Finding | Result | Subtype |
