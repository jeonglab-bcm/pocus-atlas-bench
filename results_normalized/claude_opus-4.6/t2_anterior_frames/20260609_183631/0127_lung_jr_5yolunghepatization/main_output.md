## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Frames 1–3:
- A bright, well-defined pleural line is visible superficially.
- Immediately below the pleural line, there is a large area of **tissue-like echogenicity** — solid-appearing parenchyma that resembles liver (hepatization).
- The **deep border of this consolidated region is irregular and jagged**, with a shredded transition zone between aerated and non-aerated lung (shred sign).
- Within the consolidated tissue, scattered **punctate hyperechoic foci** are visible, consistent with air bronchograms.
- No clear A-lines are present in the consolidated region.

### Frames 4–6:
- The consolidated area becomes less prominent as the probe appears to translate laterally.
- Some residual subpleural consolidation persists with heterogeneous echotexture.
- At the margins of the consolidation, a few **discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts** can be appreciated extending from the pleural line toward the deeper field, consistent with B-lines at the boundary of aerated/non-aerated lung.

### Frames 7–10:
- The image transitions to more normally aerated lung.
- The pleural line is smooth and bright.
- The deeper field is predominantly hypoechoic without clear consolidation.
- Occasional subtle vertical artifacts are visible but sparse.

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

**Subtype: septal**

At the transition zones between consolidated and aerated lung (frames 4–6), discrete, well-spaced B-lines are visible arising from the pleural line. They are separated by dark (aerated) lung parenchyma and do not coalesce into a confluent "white-out" pattern. These represent thickened interlobular septa at the periphery of the consolidation.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: hepatization**

### Key Findings:
1. **Hepatization (predominant):** Frames 1–3 show a large subpleural area of tissue-like echogenicity with a solid, liver-like appearance indicating complete loss of aeration.
2. **Shred sign (secondary):** The deep border of the consolidation is irregular and jagged — a classic "shredded" margin between consolidated and partially aerated lung.
3. **Air bronchograms (supportive):** Punctate hyperechoic foci within the hepatized tissue represent residual air within bronchi.
