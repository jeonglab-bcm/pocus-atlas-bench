# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Frames 1–6:
These frames display a classic **"bat sign"** view with:
- Bilateral ribs casting posterior acoustic shadows
- A clearly visible pleural line spanning between the two ribs
- The pleural line appears **irregular and slightly thickened** in segments
- Below the pleural line, there are faint vertical artifacts extending downward
- No dominant A-lines (horizontal reverberation artifacts) are visible

- The B-lines are individually countable with dark lung parenchyma visible between them

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: B-lines are PRESENT (lung_rockets = true)**

**Subtype: Septal**

The B-lines are discrete, well-separated vertical artifacts with clearly visible dark spaces between them. They are individually identifiable (approximately 3–4 per intercostal space), consistent with interstitial thickening (as seen in miliary TB with interstitial/interlobular septal involvement). They do not coalesce into a white-out/ground-glass pattern.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: Consolidation is PRESENT (consolidation = true)**

**Consolidation type: Hepatization**

The sub-pleural nodules represent small focal areas of consolidation at the pleural surface. They appear as:
- Small (few mm), hypoechoic, tissue-like nodular structures
- Located immediately deep to the pleural line
- Disrupting the normal smooth, hyperechoic pleural reflection
- Consistent with miliary granulomas reaching the lung periphery

No classic shred sign or air bronchograms are identified — the pattern is one of small focal sub-pleural hepatization.
