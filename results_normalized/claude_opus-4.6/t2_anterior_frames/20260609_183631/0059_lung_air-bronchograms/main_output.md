# 0059_lung_air-bronchograms

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1-3:** The chest wall is visible at the top, followed by the pleural line. Below the pleural line, there is a large area of heterogeneous, tissue-like echogenicity occupying much of the field of view. Scattered bright punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are visible within this tissue-like region. Some vertical artifacts are visible at the periphery.

**Frames 4-6:** The pattern persists — a solid-appearing, hepatized lung parenchyma with multiple hyperechoic dots and short linear echoes within it (air bronchograms). The deep border appears irregular and shredded in some areas. The tissue texture resembles liver.

**Frames 7-8:** Similar findings. The consolidated area remains prominent with air bronchograms clearly visible. At the superior margin, a transition zone with some vertical artifacts (B-lines) can be seen at the border between consolidated and partially-aerated lung.

**Frames 9-10:** Consistent with prior frames. The hepatized lung with embedded air bronchograms persists. The irregular deep border (shred sign) is visible in some areas.

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: lung_rockets = true**

At the margins of the consolidated area, confluent vertical hyperechoic artifacts extend from the pleural line, coalescing into a bright "white-out" appearance where some residual aeration exists.

**Subtype: ground_glass**

The B-lines at the periphery of the consolidation are confluent and coalescing rather than discrete, consistent with severe interstitial/alveolar disease.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

Findings:
- **Hepatization:** The lung parenchyma appears solid and tissue-like, resembling liver echogenicity, indicating complete loss of aeration.
- **Air bronchograms:** Multiple punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are clearly visible within the hepatized lung tissue, representing air trapped in bronchi surrounded by consolidated alveoli.
- **Shred sign:** The deep margin of consolidation shows an irregular, fragmented border.

**consolidation_type = "air_bronchogram"**

The predominant and most conspicuous finding is the presence of multiple hyperechoic air bronchograms scattered throughout hepatized lung parenchyma, indicative of significant alveolar consolidation (e.g., pneumonia, ARDS).
