# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Anterior Zone

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–10:** All frames demonstrate a consistent pattern:
- **Chest wall** visible in the upper portion of the image
- **Pleural line** identified as a hyperechoic horizontal interface
- **Below the pleural line:** A hypoechoic area with tissue-like echotexture (not fully anechoic like fluid, nor normally aerated with A-lines)
- **Scattered hyperechoic punctate foci** are visible within the subpleural parenchyma in the mid-to-deep field, appearing consistently across all frames
- **No horizontal A-line reverberation artifacts** are identified
- **No vertical B-line artifacts** extending from the pleural line to the bottom of the screen are seen

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: B-lines ABSENT (lung_rockets = false)**

No vertical hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen are observed. There are no discrete or confluent B-lines. The typical A-line pattern of normal aeration is also absent, which itself supports an alternative pathology.

- **Subtype:** N/A

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: Consolidation PRESENT (consolidation = true)**

Key findings supporting consolidation:
1. **Tissue-like hepatization:** The subpleural lung parenchyma demonstrates a hypoechoic, tissue-like echotexture, with loss of normal aeration artifacts (no A-lines, no normal reverberation pattern)
2. **Air bronchograms:** Multiple scattered punctate hyperechoic foci are visible within the consolidated parenchyma, representing residual air trapped in bronchi surrounded by non-aerated (hepatized) lung
3. These findings are consistent across all 10 frames, confirming a static consolidation pattern

- **Consolidation type: "air_bronchogram"**

## Summary

| Finding | Result |
|---------|--------|
| B-lines | **Absent** |
| Consolidation | **Present** |
| Consolidation type | **Air bronchogram** |

This pattern—anterior zone consolidation with air bronchograms—is concerning for pneumonia, ARDS, or atelectasis with air trapping in the anterior lung field.
