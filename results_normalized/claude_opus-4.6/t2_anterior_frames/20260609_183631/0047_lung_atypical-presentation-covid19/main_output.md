# Lung Ultrasound Analysis - Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1–2:** A bright, curved pleural line is visible. Below it, the space is predominantly dark with some discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts extending downward (consistent with B-lines). Posterior acoustic shadowing from ribs is noted laterally.

**Frames 3–4:** The pleural line remains visible. Below it, more echogenic material appears with clear vertical reverberation artifacts extending toward the bottom of the screen. A transition zone between aerated and non-aerated lung begins to emerge.

**Frames 5–6:** Subpleural echogenic tissue becomes clearly visible below the pleural line. The tissue takes on a solid, hepatized appearance. The deep border between this consolidated tissue and deeper aerated lung is irregular and fragmented.

**Frames 7–8:** The consolidation becomes more prominent. The deep margin is clearly shredded/irregular. Some punctate hyperechoic foci are visible within the consolidated area, suggesting trapped air.

**Frames 9–10:** A large area of subpleural consolidation with tissue-like echotexture is seen. The irregular, ragged deep border (shred sign) is the dominant feature. The consolidated lung resembles liver parenchyma.

---

## B-lines Assessment

**Conclusion: B-lines = true**

**Subtype: septal**

Discrete vertical hyperechoic artifacts are visible in the early frames (1–4), arising from the pleural line and extending toward the bottom of the screen. They are well-spaced with dark lung parenchyma between them, consistent with the septal pattern. These B-lines are seen at the periphery/transition zone adjacent to the consolidation.

---

## Consolidation Assessment

**Conclusion: consolidation = true**

**Consolidation type: shred_sign**

**Key findings:**
- **Hepatization:** Tissue-like echogenic parenchyma below the pleural line visible in frames 5–10, resembling liver echotexture
- **Shred sign (predominant):** The deep border between consolidated and aerated lung is clearly irregular, fragmented, and "shredded" — this is the most consistent and prominent feature across multiple frames
- **Possible air bronchograms:** Scattered punctate hyperechoic foci within the hepatized lung in frames 7–8

---

## Summary

| Finding | Result | Subtype |
|---------|--------|---------|
| B-lines | **Present** | Septal |
| Consolidation | **Present** | Shred sign |

This pattern is consistent with an anterior lung consolidation (e.g., pneumonia, ARDS) with a peripheral transition zone showing discrete B-lines and a central area of non-translobar consolidation with characteristic irregular deep margins.
