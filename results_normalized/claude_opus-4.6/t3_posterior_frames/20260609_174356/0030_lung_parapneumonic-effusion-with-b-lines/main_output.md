## Observations

**Pleural Effusion Signs:**
- **Quad sign**: A large anechoic (black) space is clearly visible, bounded superiorly by the parietal pleural line (chest wall) and inferiorly by a lung line/diaphragm, forming a quadrilateral shape. This is especially well-demonstrated in frames 4, 6, and 9.

- **Sinusoid sign (implied)**: The dynamic movement of the lung line toward the pleural line across frames confirms free-flowing fluid.

**Consolidation Signs:**
- **Hepatization**: The visible lung tissue within/adjacent to the effusion appears solid and tissue-like (liver-like echogenicity), consistent with compressive atelectasis/consolidation.
- **Air bronchograms**: Scattered punctate hyperechoic foci are visible within the consolidated lung parenchyma (frames 1, 5, 7, 8).
