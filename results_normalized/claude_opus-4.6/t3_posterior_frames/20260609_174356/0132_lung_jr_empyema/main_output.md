- The chest wall and subcutaneous tissue are visible at the top of each frame.

### Anechoic Space (Effusion)
- A large **anechoic (black) space** is present between the chest wall/pleural line and deeper structures, forming a **quad sign** — bounded superiorly by the parietal pleura, inferiorly by the visceral pleura/lung line, and laterally by rib shadows.
- This represents a **pleural effusion**.

### Floating Consolidated Lung (Jellyfish Sign)
- Within the effusion, there is an irregular, echogenic, tissue-like structure that appears to float and undulate across the sequential frames — consistent with the **jellyfish sign** (atelectatic/consolidated lung floating in effusion).

### Consolidation Features
- The echogenic lung tissue demonstrates **hepatization** — it appears solid and tissue-like, resembling liver parenchyma.
- Within this consolidated tissue, multiple small **round anechoic structures** are visible (best seen in frames 3, 5, 8, and 10), consistent with **fluid bronchograms** (fluid-filled bronchi within consolidated lung).
- The deep border of the consolidated tissue is irregular, suggesting a **shred sign** interface with residual aerated lung.

### Dynamic Diaphragm
- The bright hyperechoic curvilinear line running diagonally represents the **diaphragm**, confirming this is the dependent posterior zone.
