# PLAPS Point Lung Ultrasound Analysis

## Observations:

**Frame 1:** A large anechoic (black) space dominates the field, consistent with a significant fluid collection. Minimal internal echoes are present.

**Frames 2–4:** The diaphragm/liver is visible superiorly as a curvilinear hyperechoic structure. Below it, a clearly delineated anechoic space is present. Deeper in the field, hyperechoic linear structures consistent with the **spine sign** (vertebral bodies visible through the effusion) are identified — a reliable sign of pleural effusion.

**Frames 5–7:** Within and adjacent to the anechoic collection, a tissue-like structure is visible — appearing echogenic and resembling liver parenchyma (**hepatization**). This represents atelectatic/consolidated lung. Punctate hyperechoic foci within this tissue suggest **air bronchograms**.

**Frames 8–10:** The atelectatic lung is seen moving/floating within the surrounding effusion, showing undulating motion consistent with the **jellyfish sign**. The **quad sign** is also evident with the bounded fluid space.

## Key Findings:
- ✅ **Quad sign** — bounded anechoic space
- ✅ **Spine sign** — vertebrae visible through effusion
- ✅ **Jellyfish sign** — floating atelectatic lung
- ✅ **Hepatization** — tissue-like consolidated lung
- ✅ **Air bronchograms** — hyperechoic foci within consolidated tissue
