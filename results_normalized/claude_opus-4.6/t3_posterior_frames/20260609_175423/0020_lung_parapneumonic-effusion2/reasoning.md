# 0020_lung_parapneumonic-effusion2 — Reasoning

Let me analyze these sequential frames from a lung ultrasound video clip at the PLAPS point.

Looking at the images systematically:

1. In all frames, I can see a large anechoic (black) space that appears to be a fluid collection.

2. There is a clear quadrilateral shape formed by:
   - The pleural line superiorly
   - A lung line inferiorly
   - Rib shadows laterally
   This is the **quad sign**, indicating pleural effusion.

3. Within the fluid collection, I can see what appears to be a structure that looks like compressed/atelectatic lung tissue floating within the effusion. This tissue appears to move and undulate across the frames, resembling a **jellyfish sign**.
   
The floating echogenic structure within the effusion shows characteristics of atelectatic lung tissue, and as I track it across the sequential frames, it clearly moves and undulates through the fluid—a classic jellyfish sign. The tissue also demonstrates some hepatization, suggesting concurrent consolidation alongside the effusion.

This points to a PLAPS pattern with both components present: a significant pleural effusion with the quad sign and jellyfish sign, combined with consolidated or atelectatic lung tissue visible within the fluid. The predominant finding is the large effusion with floating atelectatic lung rather than primary consolidation.
