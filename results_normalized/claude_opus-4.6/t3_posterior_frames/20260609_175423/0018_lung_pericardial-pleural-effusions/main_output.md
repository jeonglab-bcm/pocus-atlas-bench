# 0018_lung_pericardial-pleural-effusions

## Observations

Analyzing the sequential frames from this PLAPS point ultrasound:

### Frame-by-Frame Analysis:

1. **Anechoic (black) space**: In multiple frames (particularly frames 1-4 and 6-7), there is a clearly defined anechoic region in the dependent zone, bounded superiorly by the pleural line and inferiorly by a visible lung line — consistent with a **quad sign** indicating pleural effusion.

2. **Tissue-like structure within/adjacent to fluid**: In frames 2-5 and 8-10, there is an echogenic, solid-appearing structure with a texture resembling liver parenchyma — consistent with **hepatization** of atelectatic/consolidated lung.

3. **Dynamic lung movement within fluid**: Across the sequence, the consolidated lung segment appears to undulate and move within the surrounding fluid collection, suggesting a **jellyfish sign** — atelectatic lung floating in effusion.

4. **Air bronchograms**: Punctate hyperechoic foci are visible within the hepatized lung tissue in several frames, representing air-filled bronchi trapped within consolidated parenchyma.

5. **Shred sign**: In some frames (particularly 9-10), the deep border of the consolidated area appears irregular/shredded where it transitions to aerated lung.

---

## Conclusion

- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**

**Rationale:** There is a clearly visible **pleural effusion** (quad sign, jellyfish sign with floating atelectatic lung) combined with **alveolar consolidation** (hepatized lung tissue with air bronchograms). This represents a classic PLAPS pattern with both free fluid and associated compressive atelectasis/consolidation in the dependent posterior lung zone.
