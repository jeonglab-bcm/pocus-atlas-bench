## Observations

### Sequential Frame Analysis:

Across all 10 frames, I observe the following consistent features:

**Superficial structures**: Normal layered appearance of soft tissue (skin, subcutaneous fat, intercostal muscles) at the top of each image.

**Anechoic (black) space**: A clearly defined fluid-filled space is present in the upper-to-middle portion of the images. This dark region is bounded:
- Superiorly by the parietal pleural line
- Inferiorly by a brighter lung/visceral pleural line
- Laterally by rib shadows

This forms the classic **Quad sign** indicating pleural effusion.

**Consolidated lung tissue**: Below and within the fluid collection, there is echogenic, solid-appearing tissue with a liver-like echotexture (**hepatization**). Within this consolidated tissue, multiple small round hypoechoic/anechoic structures are visible — these represent **fluid bronchograms** (anechoic tubular bronchi seen in cross-section within consolidated, non-aerated lung).

**Dynamic features**: Across the sequential frames, the consolidated lung tissue appears to undulate slightly within the surrounding effusion, suggesting a **jellyfish sign** — atelectatic/consolidated lung floating within pleural fluid.

**Spine sign**: The bright diagonal linear structure visible deep to the fluid collection likely represents vertebral bodies, which are only visible through fluid (not aerated lung), further confirming effusion.
