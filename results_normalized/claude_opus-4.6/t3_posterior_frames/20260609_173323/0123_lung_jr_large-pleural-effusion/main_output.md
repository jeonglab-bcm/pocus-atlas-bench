# 0123_lung_jr_large-pleural-effusion

## Observations

### Image Analysis

**Superficial structures (chest wall):**
- The chest wall is visible at the top of each frame with soft tissue layers clearly identified.

**Large anechoic space:**
- Across all sequential frames, there is a prominent **anechoic (black) space** occupying the majority of the image field. This space is bounded:
  - **Superiorly** by the parietal pleural line/chest wall
  - **Laterally** by rib shadows (partially visible at edges)
  - **Inferiorly/deeply** by an echogenic structure representing either compressed lung or the lung line

This constitutes a **Quad sign**.

**Deep structures:**
- In frames 6–10 (later frames), there is increasingly visible **echogenic tissue** at the deep border of the fluid collection. This tissue has a:
  - **Hepatized (tissue-like) appearance** — solid echogenicity resembling liver parenchyma
  - Irregular borders suggesting **atelectatic/compressed lung**
  - Possible **jellyfish-like movement** suggested by the changing position of this tissue across sequential frames (floating/undulating within the effusion)

**Dynamic features (across frames):**
- The echogenic lung structure at the bottom appears to move toward and away from the chest wall, consistent with respiratory motion within fluid — suggestive of the **sinusoid sign** pattern.

---

## Conclusion

- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**

**Reasoning:** There is a large **pleural effusion** (quad sign, likely sinusoid sign given respiratory movement across frames, and possible jellyfish sign) with associated **consolidated/atelectatic lung** visible at the deep border showing hepatized, tissue-like echogenicity. This combination of significant free fluid with compressive atelectasis/consolidation is classic for PLAPS at the posterior dependent zone.
