# 0014_lung_septated-pleural-effusion

## Observation

Across the sequential frames, I identify the following findings:

### Effusion Signs:
- **Quad sign**: A large anechoic (black) space is clearly visible, bounded superiorly by the parietal pleural line (chest wall) and inferiorly by a brighter lung line, with rib shadows framing laterally. This quadrilateral configuration is classic for pleural effusion.
- **Jellyfish sign**: In multiple frames, the compressed/atelectatic lung tissue is seen floating and undulating within the surrounding effusion. The lung moves rhythmically with respiration, giving the characteristic "jellyfish in water" appearance as it drifts toward and away from the chest wall.

### Consolidation Signs:
- **Tissue-like hepatization**: The atelectatic lung visible within the effusion appears echogenic and solid, resembling liver parenchyma (hepatized), indicating loss of normal aeration.
- **Air bronchograms**: Within the consolidated lung tissue, punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are seen, representing air-filled bronchi trapped within non-aerated lung parenchyma.
- **Shred sign**: Some frames show an irregular, shredded border at the interface between consolidated and partially aerated lung.

### Dynamic Assessment:
Across frames, the consolidated lung tissue moves within the effusion, confirming free-flowing fluid and compressive atelectasis/consolidation.

## Conclusion

- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**

A significant pleural effusion is present with associated compressive atelectasis and consolidation of the dependent lung. The jellyfish sign with hepatized, air bronchogram-containing lung floating in a large effusion is the hallmark finding in this clip.
