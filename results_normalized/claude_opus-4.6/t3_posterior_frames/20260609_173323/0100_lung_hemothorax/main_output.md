## Observations

Analyzing the sequential frames from this PLAPS point ultrasound:

### Effusion Signs:
- **Quad sign**: A large **anechoic (black) space** is clearly visible, bounded superiorly by the parietal pleural line and inferiorly by the visceral pleural/lung line, with rib shadows laterally — forming the classic quadrilateral shape of pleural effusion.
- **Jellyfish sign**: Throughout the sequence, a tongue-like structure of atelectatic lung tissue is seen **floating and undulating** within the surrounding fluid collection. The movement across frames shows it swaying with respiration, closely resembling a jellyfish in water.

### Consolidation Signs:
- **Tissue-like hepatization**: The dependent lung tissue visible at the bottom of the effusion demonstrates a **solid, liver-like echotexture** — loss of normal aeration pattern consistent with compressive atelectasis/consolidation.
- **Air bronchograms**: Within the hepatized lung tissue, there are scattered **punctate hyperechoic foci** representing air-filled bronchi trapped within the consolidated parenchyma.

### Dynamic Assessment:
Across the frames, the lung tissue progressively moves toward and away from the chest wall, confirming the **sinusoid sign** principle — lung motion within a free-flowing effusion.
