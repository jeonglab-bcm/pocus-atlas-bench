# 0100_lung_hemothorax

## Observations

Analyzing these sequential frames from a PLAPS-point lung ultrasound:

### Frame-by-Frame Analysis:

1. **Large anechoic space**: Throughout all frames, there is a prominent **anechoic (black) fluid collection** that dominates the image, consistent with a significant pleural effusion.

2. **Quad sign**: The fluid collection is bounded by the pleural line superiorly, a lung line inferiorly, and rib shadows laterally, forming the classic quadrilateral shape.

3. **Jellyfish sign**: Across the sequential frames, there is a clearly visible echogenic structure (atelectatic lung) **floating and undulating** within the effusion. It moves dynamically with respiration — compressing toward the pleural line and drifting back — resembling a jellyfish in water. This is particularly evident comparing frames 1-4 (more collapsed) versus frames 7-10 (more expanded/floating).

4. **Hepatization**: The floating lung tissue appears **solid and tissue-like**, with echogenicity resembling liver parenchyma, indicating complete loss of aeration (compressive atelectasis/consolidation).

5. **Air bronchograms**: Within the hepatized lung tissue, there are **punctate hyperechoic foci** visible, representing residual air within bronchi surrounded by consolidated parenchyma.

## Conclusion

- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**

**Rationale**: There is a **large pleural effusion** (quad sign, jellyfish sign) with **compressive atelectasis/consolidation** of the adjacent lung (hepatization, air bronchograms). The jellyfish sign — with atelectatic lung freely floating within a large effusion — is the dominant finding, indicating both significant free fluid and non-aerated, consolidated lung tissue.
