# 0126_lung_jr_loculated-pleural-effusion

## Observations

**Near Field (Chest Wall):**
- Ribs and intercostal soft tissues are visible at the top of each frame, creating typical rib shadow boundaries laterally.

**Mid-Field (Fluid Space):**
- A large **anechoic (black) space** is clearly present between the chest wall/pleural line and the deeper structures. This forms a **quad sign** — bounded superiorly by the pleural line, inferiorly by a "lung line," and laterally by rib shadows.
- Across sequential frames, the deeper boundary (lung line) shows movement toward and away from the pleural line, suggesting a **sinusoid sign** (respiratory variation of the visceral pleura within the effusion).

**Deep Field (Lung/Diaphragm):**
- Below the effusion, there is **tissue-like echogenic material** resembling liver parenchyma — consistent with **hepatization** of atelectatic/consolidated lung.
- The deep border of this consolidated tissue is **irregular and shredded** in several frames (frames 6–9), representing the **shred sign** — the interface between consolidated and residual aerated lung.
- Within the hepatized tissue, **punctate hyperechoic foci** are visible, consistent with **air bronchograms**.
- In frames 6–8, the consolidated lung appears to **float and undulate** within the surrounding fluid, resembling the **jellyfish sign**.

## Conclusion

- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**

**Rationale:** There is a significant **pleural effusion** (quad sign, sinusoid sign, jellyfish sign) combined with **alveolar consolidation** (hepatized/tissue-like lung with shred sign and air bronchograms) in the posterior dependent zone. This is a classic PLAPS pattern seen in conditions such as pneumonia with parapneumonic effusion or compressive atelectasis.
