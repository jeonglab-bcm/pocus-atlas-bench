## Observations

**Chest Wall and Pleural Line:**
- The upper portion of each frame shows normal chest wall layers (skin, subcutaneous tissue, intercostal muscles) with a clearly identifiable bright pleural line at approximately 4-5 cm depth.

**Below the Pleural Line:**
- There is **no significant anechoic (black) fluid collection** — no classic quad sign or jellyfish sign is identified.
- Instead, immediately below the pleural line, there is a **tissue-like echotexture** occupying the subpleural space. The parenchyma appears solid and echogenic, resembling liver (hepatization).
- Multiple **punctate hyperechoic foci** are scattered throughout this consolidated region across all frames, consistent with **air bronchograms** — air trapped within bronchi surrounded by non-aerated, consolidated lung.
- The deep border of the consolidated tissue appears **irregular and shredded** (shred sign), representing the transition zone between consolidated and aerated lung.
- Normal A-line pattern is completely absent in this zone.

**Dynamic Assessment (across frames 2–229):**
- The air bronchograms appear relatively static in position, suggesting **static air bronchograms** (as opposed to dynamic ones that move with respiration).
- The overall pattern remains consistent throughout the clip without significant respiratory-dependent change suggestive of free fluid.
