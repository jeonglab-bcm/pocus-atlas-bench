# 0014_lung_septated-pleural-effusion

## Observations

### Frame-by-Frame Analysis:

Across all 10 sequential frames, I observe the following consistent findings:

1. **Large anechoic (black) space**: A substantial dark, fluid-filled region occupies much of the image between the chest wall/pleural line superiorly and a deeper echogenic structure inferiorly. This forms a clear **quad sign** — bounded by the pleural line above, the lung line below, and rib shadows laterally.

2. **Echogenic tissue-like structure**: Below/within the effusion, there is a bright, irregularly shaped, solid-appearing structure with a texture resembling liver parenchyma — consistent with **hepatized/atelectatic lung**.

3. **Jellyfish sign**: Across the sequential frames, this echogenic lung tissue appears to undulate and float within the surrounding fluid collection, with subtle positional changes between frames, resembling a jellyfish moving in water.

4. **Irregular deep border**: The consolidated lung shows an irregular, somewhat shredded margin at its interface with remaining aerated lung in some frames — suggestive of a **shred sign**.

5. **Possible air bronchograms**: Punctate hyperechoic foci are visible within the consolidated lung parenchyma in several frames.

---

## Conclusion

- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**

**Rationale**: There is a large **pleural effusion** (quad sign, jellyfish sign) with **compressive atelectasis/consolidation** of the underlying lung (hepatization, shred sign, possible air bronchograms). The jellyfish sign confirms both free fluid and floating atelectatic lung, characteristic of a significant PLAPS pattern with combined effusion and consolidation.
