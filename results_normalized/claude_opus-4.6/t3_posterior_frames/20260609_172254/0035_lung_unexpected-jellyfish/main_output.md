# 0035_lung_unexpected-jellyfish

## Observations

Reviewing the sequential frames from this PLAPS point examination:

1. **Large anechoic space**: Throughout all frames, there is a prominent dark (anechoic) fluid collection occupying a significant portion of the image, consistent with a **large pleural effusion**.

2. **Quad sign**: The fluid collection is bounded by the pleural line superiorly and a lung line inferiorly, with rib shadows laterally, forming a quadrilateral — classic quad sign.

3. **Jellyfish sign**: Within the effusion, there is a clearly visible mass of atelectatic lung tissue that appears to float and undulate across the frames. This tissue moves toward and away from the chest wall with respiration, creating the characteristic "jellyfish" appearance.

4. **Hepatization/Tissue-like pattern**: The atelectatic lung within the effusion appears solid and echogenic, resembling liver parenchyma — consistent with compressive atelectasis and consolidation.

5. **Air bronchograms**: Punctate hyperechoic foci are visible within the consolidated/atelectatic lung tissue, representing residual air within bronchi.

## Conclusion

- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**

**Rationale**: There is a large pleural effusion (quad sign, jellyfish sign) with associated compressive atelectasis/consolidation (hepatized lung tissue with air bronchograms floating within the effusion). This represents a classic PLAPS pattern with both effusion and consolidation components, commonly seen in significant pleural fluid collections causing passive lung collapse.
