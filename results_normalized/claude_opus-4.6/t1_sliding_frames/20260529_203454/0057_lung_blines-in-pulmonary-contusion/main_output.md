# 0057_lung_blines-in-pulmonary-contusion

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Frames 1-3:
- The pleural line is visible at approximately 4-5 cm depth
- The pleural line appears **textured/granular** with some shimmer quality
- Sub-pleural speckle pattern shows **variation between consecutive frames**
- Faint comet-tail artifacts can be appreciated
- **Interpretation: Signs of sliding PRESENT**

### Frames 4-6:
- Multiple hyperechoic structures visible at the pleural interface (rib shadows and pleural line)
- **B-lines/comet-tail artifacts** are clearly visible extending from the pleural line
- Sub-pleural tissue appears heterogeneous with inter-frame variation
- The pleural line maintains a granular, textured appearance
- **Interpretation: Signs of sliding PRESENT**

### Frames 7-8:
- Striking change in appearance — the pleural line becomes **smooth, sharply defined, and static**
- The sub-pleural pattern appears **homogeneous and unchanging**
- Horizontal lines below the pleura suggest **static A-lines**
- No granularity or shimmer at the pleural interface
- Minimal to no variation in speckle pattern between these frames
- **Interpretation: Signs of sliding ABSENT**

### Frames 9-10:
- Return of some sub-pleural variation and texture
- Hyperechoic spot visible, with some mild sub-pleural heterogeneity
- **Interpretation: Transitional/mixed**

## Conclusion

**BOTH (Lung Point)**

This sequence demonstrates a **lung point** — the pathognomonic sign of pneumothorax. There is a clear transition between:
- **Sliding present** (frames 1-6): granular pleural line, B-lines, and inter-frame speckle variation
- **Sliding absent** (frames 7-8): smooth, static pleural line with no sub-pleural variation

This transition point represents the boundary where the partially collapsed lung intermittently contacts the chest wall, alternating between normal sliding and absent sliding. This finding is **100% specific for pneumothorax**.
