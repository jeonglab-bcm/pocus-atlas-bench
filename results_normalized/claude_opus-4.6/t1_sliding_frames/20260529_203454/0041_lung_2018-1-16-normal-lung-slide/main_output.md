# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Frame-by-Frame Assessment

## Structural Overview
Each frame shows:
- Superficial soft tissue layers (skin, subcutaneous fat, muscle) in the upper portion
- A bright, hyperechoic **pleural line** located approximately 1.5–2 cm from the top
- Sub-pleural lung parenchyma with A-line reverberation artifacts below

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Frames 1–3:** The pleural line appears bright with a somewhat granular/textured quality. Sub-pleural speckle pattern shows subtle variation between consecutive frames. A-lines are visible but not perfectly static.

**Frames 4–6:** The pleural line continues to shimmer slightly. There is noticeable variation in the sub-pleural echotexture compared to earlier frames. Some frames show subtle comet-tail artifacts originating from the pleural line.

**Frames 7–9:** More pronounced changes in sub-pleural texture are evident. Frame 8 shows increased echogenicity below the pleural line. The tissue pattern below the pleura is distinctly different between these frames, consistent with lung tissue moving against the parietal pleura.

**Frame 10:** Returns to a pattern similar to frames 4–6, with continued subtle variation in sub-pleural speckle.

## Key Signs Identified

✅ **Signs of sliding PRESENT:**
- Granular/shimmering pleural line (not smooth or perfectly static)
- Variation in sub-pleural speckle pattern between consecutive frames
- Subtle comet-tail artifacts in some frames
- A-lines show slight variation (not perfectly unchanged)

❌ **Signs of sliding ABSENT:** Not identified
- No barcode/stratosphere pattern
- No perfectly static, sharply defined pleural line
- No perfectly unchanged A-lines across all frames

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.** The sequential frames demonstrate consistent variation in the sub-pleural speckle pattern, a granular/textured pleural line, and subtle dynamic changes below the pleura — all consistent with normal visceral-parietal pleural apposition and movement during respiration.
