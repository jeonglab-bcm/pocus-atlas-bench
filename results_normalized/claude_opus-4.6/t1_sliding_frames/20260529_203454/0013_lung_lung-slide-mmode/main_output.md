# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### B-mode (lower panels):
- A bright, hyperechoic **pleural line** is visible at approximately 1.5-2 cm depth across all frames
- **A-lines** (horizontal reverberation artifacts) are present below the pleural line
- Between consecutive frames, there is **subtle variation in the sub-pleural speckle pattern**, suggesting tissue movement at the pleural interface
- The pleural line appears somewhat textured/granular rather than perfectly smooth and static

### M-mode (upper panels):
- **Above the pleural line**: Horizontal, parallel, static lines representing the motionless chest wall ("waves" portion)
- **At the pleural line**: A bright, well-defined hyperechoic line
- **Below the pleural line**: As the M-mode fills across frames 3–10, a **granular, sandy pattern** emerges below the pleural line, which is distinctly different from the organized horizontal lines above

This constitutes the classic **"Seashore Sign"** — waves (chest wall) above, sand (moving lung) below.

## Key Findings Summary

| Sign | Present? |
|------|----------|
| Seashore sign on M-mode | ✅ Yes |
| Granular sub-pleural pattern | ✅ Yes |
| Speckle variation between frames | ✅ Yes |
| Stratosphere/barcode sign | ❌ No |
| Static, sharply defined pleural line | ❌ No |
| Lung point transition | ❌ No |

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.** The M-mode demonstrates a clear seashore sign with a granular/sandy pattern below the pleural line, and the B-mode frames show sub-pleural speckle variation consistent with normal visceral-parietal pleural apposition and movement. This is a **normal lung ultrasound finding**, effectively ruling out pneumothorax at this scanning location.
