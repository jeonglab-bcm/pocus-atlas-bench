# 0019_lung_no-lung-sliding

# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Anatomical Orientation:**
- Linear high-frequency probe (L14-6Ns) is applied to the chest wall
- Superficial soft tissue layers (skin, subcutaneous fat, muscle) are seen as horizontal lines in the near field
- A bright hyperechoic pleural line is identified at approximately 1 cm depth
- A rib shadow (dark acoustic shadow) is visible on the left side of the image
- A-lines (horizontal reverberation artifacts) are present below the pleural line

**Frame Comparison:**

| Feature | Observation |
|---------|-------------|
| Pleural line texture | Granular/shimmering appearance; slight textural variation frame-to-frame |
| Sub-pleural speckle | Subtle but definite changes in the speckle pattern between consecutive frames |
| A-lines | Present but show minor variation in intensity/position across frames |
| Superficial tissues | Remain static (as expected — internal control) |
| Deep structures | Show variation consistent with lung movement beneath the pleura |

## Key Findings

✅ **Signs of sliding PRESENT:**
- The sub-pleural speckle pattern differs between consecutive frames, indicating tissue movement at the visceral-parietal pleural interface
- The pleural line has a granular, slightly textured (shimmering) appearance rather than a smooth, sharply defined static line
- Subtle comet-tail-like artifacts are intermittently seen originating from the pleural line

❌ **Signs of sliding ABSENT — NOT identified:**
- No stratosphere/barcode pattern
- No perfectly static A-lines across all frames
- No frozen, sharply defined pleural line

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**

This is a normal lung ultrasound demonstrating lung sliding at the pleural interface. The variation in sub-pleural speckle across frames confirms visceral pleural movement against the parietal pleura, effectively ruling out pneumothorax at this scanning location.
