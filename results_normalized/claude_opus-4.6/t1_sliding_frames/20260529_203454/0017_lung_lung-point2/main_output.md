# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Frame-by-Frame Assessment

## B-mode (Upper Panel) Observations:

Across the sequential frames, the B-mode image shows:
- A visible pleural line at approximately 1-1.5 cm depth
- The area below the pleural line alternates between appearing more echogenic/granular (suggesting lung tissue) and more anechoic/dark (suggesting air interface)
- The pleural line itself appears to transition between shimmering and static states across frames

## M-mode (Lower Panel) Observations:

This is the critical diagnostic portion:

- **Above the pleural line (0–1 cm):** Horizontal, parallel, static lines throughout — representing the motionless chest wall (consistent across all frames)
- **Below the pleural line (~1–1.5 cm):** A clearly **alternating pattern**:
  - **Dark granular/sandy columns** (seashore sign) — indicating periods where lung sliding IS present
  - **Horizontal stratified lines between columns** (barcode/stratosphere sign) — indicating periods where lung sliding is ABSENT
- This alternation repeats **rhythmically with respiration**

## Key Diagnostic Signs Identified:

| Sign | Present? |
|------|----------|
| Seashore sign (sandy pattern) | ✅ Intermittent |
| Barcode/stratosphere sign | ✅ Intermittent |
| Alternating M-mode pattern | ✅ Yes |
| Respiratory-phase variation | ✅ Yes |

## Conclusion: **BOTH — Lung Point**

This is a classic **lung point sign**, pathognomonic for **pneumothorax**. The M-mode demonstrates alternating seashore and barcode patterns corresponding to respiratory phases where:
- During **inspiration**: the lung partially inflates into the scanning field → sliding present (seashore)
- During **expiration**: the lung retracts and free air occupies the space → sliding absent (barcode)

This identifies the **border of the pneumothorax** and is 100% specific for its diagnosis.
