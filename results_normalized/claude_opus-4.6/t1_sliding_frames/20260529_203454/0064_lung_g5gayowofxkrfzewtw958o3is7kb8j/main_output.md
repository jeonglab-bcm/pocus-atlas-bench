## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1-3:** A bright pleural line is visible in the upper portion. Below it, there is a large **anechoic (dark) region** occupying much of the field. At the bottom, a hyperechoic structure is seen. The dark region appears relatively large and stable.

**Frames 4-6:** The extent of the dark anechoic region begins to **change/shrink**, with more echogenic, granular tissue becoming visible from one side. The boundary between the dark area and the textured tissue shifts.

**Frames 7-8:** The echogenic tissue (with a speckled/granular pattern suggesting aerated lung) occupies more of the field, while the dark region narrows.

**Frames 9-10:** The dark region appears to **re-expand**, pushing the granular tissue back. The boundary oscillates back and forth with the respiratory cycle.

## Key Findings

1. **Transition point visible:** There is a clear boundary between an anechoic/static region (no sliding) and a granular/dynamic region (sliding present)
2. **Oscillating boundary:** The interface between these two zones shifts rhythmically across frames, consistent with respiratory movement
3. **Sub-pleural speckle variation:** Present only in the echogenic portion, absent in the dark region

## Conclusion

**BOTH (Lung Point)**

This clip demonstrates a **lung point** — the pathognomonic sign of pneumothorax. The alternating appearance of:
- Aerated lung with sliding (granular, dynamic tissue), and
- Pneumothorax without sliding (static, anechoic region)

...whose boundary moves with respiration, confirms a transition zone between normal visceral-parietal pleural apposition and free air separating the pleural layers.
