# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Frame-by-Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Observations:

**Frames 1-3:**
- A bright, hyperechoic pleural line is visible in the upper portion
- Below the pleural line, there are clustered hyperechoic structures (left/central portion) with active variation between frames
- A distinctly **dark, static region** occupies the right side of the sub-pleural space
- Sub-pleural speckle clearly changes between these frames in the left portion

**Frames 4-6:**
- The boundary between the bright/active area and the dark/static area appears to shift
- The granular, shimmering tissue below the pleural line recedes slightly
- The static, dark area expands

**Frames 7-10:**
- The bright, active sub-pleural tissue reappears/advances
- Clear variation in speckle pattern returns in portions of the sub-pleural space
- The transition point between moving and static tissue is again visible

## Key Findings:

| Sign | Observation |
|------|-------------|
| Shimmering pleural line | Present on **one side** of a transition point |
| Static pleural line | Present on the **other side** |
| Sub-pleural speckle variation | Present in left/central area, absent on right |
| Transition point | Visible — shifts with respiration |

## Conclusion: **BOTH (Lung Point)**

This clip demonstrates a **lung point** — the pathognomonic sign of pneumothorax. There is a visible transition zone where:
- **Sliding is PRESENT** (left/central): granular, moving sub-pleural tissue with frame-to-frame variation
- **Sliding is ABSENT** (right): dark, static region with no speckle change

The intermittent in-and-out movement of the lung at this boundary across the respiratory cycle confirms this as a lung point, indicating the exact edge where the partially collapsed lung meets the pneumothorax space.
