# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### Superficial Structures
- All frames show consistent layered chest wall tissue (skin, subcutaneous tissue, muscle layers) superficially.
- A **bright, hyperechoic pleural line** is clearly identified in every frame.

### Pleural Line Characteristics
- **Left portion**: The pleural line appears relatively smooth, sharply defined, and static across multiple frames. The sub-pleural area on the left shows a more hypoechoic, unchanging pattern.
- **Right portion**: The pleural line shows more granular/textured quality with subtle changes in brightness and texture between consecutive frames.

### Sub-pleural Pattern Changes Between Frames
- **Left side**: Minimal to no variation in the speckle pattern below the pleural line — the tissue appears relatively static across frames (Frames 3, 5, 9).
- **Right side**: Subtle but visible changes in speckle/texture below the pleural line between consecutive frames, particularly notable comparing Frames 4→5, 6→7, and 7→8.

### Transition Zone
- A **visible transition point** is observed where the character of the pleural line and sub-pleural tissue changes — the left portion remains fixed/static while the right portion intermittently shows movement and textural variation.

## Conclusion

**BOTH — Lung Point Identified**

This clip demonstrates a **lung point**, which is pathognomonic for **pneumothorax**. There is a visible transition zone where:
- **Absent sliding** (left portion): static pleural line, no sub-pleural variation — representing the pneumothorax interface
- **Present sliding** (right portion): intermittent granular movement and speckle variation — representing aerated lung intermittently contacting the chest wall

This finding confirms a partial pneumothorax and delineates its boundary along the chest wall.
