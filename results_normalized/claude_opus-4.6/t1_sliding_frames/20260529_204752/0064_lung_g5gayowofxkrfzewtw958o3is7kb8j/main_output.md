## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Pleural Line Identification
Across all frames, I identify a bright pleural line in the upper portion of the image (approximately 1-1.5 cm depth). Above it, chest wall layers (subcutaneous tissue and intercostal muscles) are visible.

### Key Observations:

**Frames 1-3:** A large **anechoic (black) area** dominates below the pleural line, with a sharp, well-defined border. The sub-pleural region appears static with no granular variation — consistent with **absent sliding** (air interface from pneumothorax).

**Frames 4, 8, 10:** The echogenic lung tissue **encroaches** into the previously anechoic space. The sub-pleural speckle pattern becomes more granular and textured, and the boundary of the dark area shifts — consistent with **sliding present** (lung re-entering the field).

**Frames 5-7, 9:** The dark anechoic area fluctuates in size/shape between frames, alternating with echogenic parenchyma sliding in and out of view.

### Critical Finding:
There is a **visible transition point** where:
- One portion shows a static, anechoic region (no lung = no sliding)
- The adjacent portion shows granular, echogenic lung tissue (sliding present)
- This boundary **alternates with respiration** across frames

## Conclusion: **BOTH (Lung Point)**

This clip demonstrates a **lung point** — the pathognomonic sign of pneumothorax. The intermittent appearance and disappearance of lung tissue at a fixed anatomic point, alternating between sliding and non-sliding patterns, confirms the edge of a partially collapsed lung moving in and out of view with respiration.
