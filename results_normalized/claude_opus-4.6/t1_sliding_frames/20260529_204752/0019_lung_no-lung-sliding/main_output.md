# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Anatomical Orientation:**
- Linear high-frequency probe (L14-6Ns) at ~3 cm depth
- Two rib shadows visible as dark vertical structures (left and center-left)
- Pleural line identified as the bright hyperechoic interface at ~1 cm depth between rib shadows
- Prominent A-lines (horizontal reverberation artifacts) below the pleural line

**Frame-to-Frame Comparison:**

| Feature | Observation |
|---------|-------------|
| Pleural line texture | Appears granular/shimmering with subtle variation between frames |
| Sub-pleural speckle | Shows mild changes in pattern between consecutive frames |
| A-lines | Present throughout but show slight variation in intensity/definition across frames |
| Rib shadows | Remain fixed (internal control for motion artifact) |

## Key Findings

**Signs of sliding PRESENT:**
- ✅ The pleural line demonstrates a textured, granular appearance that varies slightly between frames
- ✅ Sub-pleural speckle pattern shows frame-to-frame variation (compare frames 1, 5, and 9)
- ✅ Subtle changes in tissue echogenicity immediately deep to the pleural line

**Signs of sliding ABSENT:**
- ❌ No perfectly static/stratified appearance across all frames
- ❌ A-lines show subtle variation (not perfectly fixed)

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**

This represents a normal **A-line pattern with lung sliding**, consistent with a normal aerated lung. The subtle but definite frame-to-frame variations in the sub-pleural speckle pattern and the granular quality of the pleural line confirm visceral-parietal pleural motion. In the context of an EM FAST exam, this effectively rules out pneumothorax at this scanning location.
