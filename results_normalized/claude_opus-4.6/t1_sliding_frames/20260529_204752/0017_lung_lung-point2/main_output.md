# Lung Ultrasound Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### B-mode (upper portion):
- A pleural line is visible at approximately 1–1.5 cm depth
- Across sequential frames, there is **variation** in the appearance of sub-pleural structures — some frames show more echogenic lung-like tissue, while others show a darker, more air-filled appearance
- This suggests the lung edge is intermittently entering and leaving the field of view

### M-mode (lower portion):
- **Superficial layers (0–1 cm):** Continuous horizontal parallel lines representing the static chest wall — normal and expected
- **At the pleural line (~1 cm):** A bright continuous line
- **Below the pleural line (~1.5–3.5 cm):** A critical **alternating pattern** is visible:
  - **Sections with granular/sandy texture** (seashore sign) → indicates sliding PRESENT
  - **Sections with dark vertical columns** interrupting horizontal lines (barcode-to-seashore transition) → indicates cyclical alternation between sliding and no-sliding

## Key Findings

| Sign | Present? |
|------|----------|
| Seashore sign (intermittent) | ✅ |
| Barcode/stratosphere sign (intermittent) | ✅ |
| Alternating M-mode pattern | ✅ |
| B-mode variation between frames | ✅ |

## Conclusion: **BOTH — Lung Point Present**

This clip demonstrates a **lung point**, the pathognomonic sign of **pneumothorax**. The M-mode shows cyclical alternation between seashore (sliding present, when lung contacts chest wall) and barcode (sliding absent, when air interposes). The B-mode confirms intermittent lung contact at this transition zone. This represents the edge of a partial pneumothorax.
