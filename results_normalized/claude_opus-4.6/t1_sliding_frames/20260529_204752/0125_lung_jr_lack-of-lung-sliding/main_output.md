# 0125_lung_jr_lack-of-lung-sliding

# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Anatomical Orientation:**
All frames show a standard intercostal view with:
- Two clear rib shadows (vertical dark columns)
- A bright pleural line visible between the ribs
- Sub-pleural space with horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines)

**Frames 1–4:**
- The pleural line appears **smooth, sharply defined, and static** in position
- A-lines below are prominent and horizontally oriented
- Sub-pleural speckle pattern shows **minimal to no variation** between these frames
- No comet-tail artifacts or B-lines visible

**Frames 5–7:**
- Pleural line remains **fixed and well-defined** — no shimmering or granular texture
- A-lines remain in identical positions, unchanged in appearance
- Sub-pleural texture is essentially **identical** across consecutive frames

**Frames 8–10:**
- Some subtle change in superficial tissue (possible transducer motion)
- However, the pleural line and sub-pleural pattern remain **static**
- A-lines persist unchanged in position and echogenicity

## Key Findings Summary

| Sign | Present? |
|------|----------|
| Shimmering/granular pleural line | ❌ No |
| B-lines/comet-tail artifacts | ❌ No |
| Sub-pleural speckle variation between frames | ❌ No |
| Smooth, sharply defined static pleural line | ✅ Yes |
| Static, unchanging A-lines across frames | ✅ Yes |
| No variation in sub-pleural texture | ✅ Yes |

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is ABSENT.**

The findings are consistent with a **stratosphere/barcode sign equivalent** in B-mode: a static, sharply defined pleural line with perfectly unchanged A-lines and no sub-pleural speckle variation across all frames. This pattern raises concern for **pneumothorax**, apneic lung, or pleurodesis at this scanning location. Clinical correlation and assessment of additional lung zones is recommended.
