# 0045_lung_double-lung-point

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Pleural Line Appearance
Across all 10 frames, I observe:
- A **bright, hyperechoic pleural line** in the upper third of the image
- The pleural line appears **granular and textured** rather than perfectly smooth and sharply defined
- There is slight variation in the pleural line's brightness and texture between consecutive frames, suggesting movement/shimmer

### Sub-pleural Pattern Changes
Comparing consecutive frames:
- **Frames 1–3:** The sub-pleural speckle pattern shows visible granular changes, with varying degrees of echogenicity below the pleural line
- **Frames 4–6:** Subtle but detectable variations in the speckle pattern continue, with slight changes in artifact distribution
- **Frames 7–10:** Continued variation in sub-pleural granularity; the pattern is not static or identical between frames

### Artifact Assessment
- **A-lines:** Some horizontal reverberation artifacts are visible but they are NOT perfectly static — slight variation exists between frames
- **Comet-tail artifacts/B-lines:** Small, short vertical artifacts are intermittently visible arising from the pleural line in several frames
- No barcode/stratosphere pattern is suggested

### No Evidence of Lung Point
- There is no visible transition zone between a sliding and non-sliding segment
- The entire pleural line behaves uniformly across the field of view

---

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**

Evidence supporting this:
1. ✅ Shimmering/granular pleural line appearance
2. ✅ Sub-pleural speckle pattern varies between consecutive frames
3. ✅ Small comet-tail artifacts originating from the pleural line
4. ✅ No static barcode pattern or perfectly unchanged A-lines

This is consistent with **normal lung sliding** at the left lung, indicating the visceral and parietal pleura are in apposition and moving against each other with respiration.
