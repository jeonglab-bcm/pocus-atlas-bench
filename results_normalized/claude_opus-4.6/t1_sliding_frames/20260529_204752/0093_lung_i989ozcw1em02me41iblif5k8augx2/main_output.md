# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Frame-by-Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Observations:

**Frames 1-3:** The pleural line is visible as a hyperechoic line. On the **right side** of the image, there is a brighter, more granular/textured appearance at the pleural level with some sub-pleural speckle variation. The **left side** shows a smoother, more static pleural line with unchanging A-lines beneath.

**Frames 4-5:** The chest wall layers are clearly delineated as parallel horizontal lines. A transition zone becomes apparent — one portion of the pleural line shows movement (granular texture changes), while the other portion appears fixed and sharply defined.

**Frames 6-8:** There is an intermittent appearance of tissue-like echogenicity at the pleural line that "slides in" from one side, then recedes. This represents lung parenchyma intermittently contacting the chest wall.

**Frames 9-10:** The alternating pattern continues — in some frames, more of the field shows sub-pleural motion; in others, the area appears static with unchanged A-lines.

## Key Findings:

| Sign | Observation |
|------|-------------|
| Sub-pleural speckle variation | Present on ONE side only |
| Static A-lines | Present on the OTHER side |
| Transition point | Visible where sliding/non-sliding meet |
| Intermittent lung contact | Lung edge slides in and out with respiration |

## Conclusion: **BOTH (Lung Point)**

This clip demonstrates a **lung point** — the pathognomonic sign of pneumothorax. There is a visible transition zone where:
- **Sliding is ABSENT** (pneumothorax side): static pleural line, unchanged A-lines
- **Sliding is PRESENT** (normal lung side): granular pleural movement, sub-pleural variation

The lung edge intermittently contacts the chest wall with respiration, creating the characteristic alternating pattern. This confirms a **pneumothorax** and marks its lateral/inferior boundary.
