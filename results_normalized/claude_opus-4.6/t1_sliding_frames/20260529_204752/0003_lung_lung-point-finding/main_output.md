# 0003_lung_lung-point-finding

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Structural Identification
Across all 10 frames, I can identify:
- **Skin/subcutaneous tissue** at the top
- **A bright hyperechoic pleural line** running obliquely across the mid-image
- **A-lines** (horizontal reverberation artifacts) below the pleural line
- **Sub-pleural speckle pattern** in the deeper field

### Frame-to-Frame Comparison

**Pleural line characteristics:**
- The pleural line appears **bright and somewhat granular/textured** rather than perfectly smooth and razor-sharp
- Its position remains relatively consistent, as expected

**Sub-pleural speckle variation:**
- Comparing consecutive frames (particularly frames 1→2→3 and 7→8→9→10), there are **subtle but discernible changes** in the speckle/texture pattern immediately below the pleural line
- The granular pattern shifts slightly between frames, indicating tissue movement at the visceral-parietal interface

**A-lines:**
- Horizontal reverberation lines are visible but show **slight variation** in their intensity/pattern across frames, not perfectly static

### Signs Observed
✅ Granular/textured pleural line appearance  
✅ Subtle variation in sub-pleural speckle between consecutive frames  
❌ No perfectly static, sharply defined pleural line  
❌ No barcode/stratosphere pattern indicators  
❌ No clear lung point transition

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.** The subtle but real frame-to-frame variation in sub-pleural speckle and the textured (rather than smooth/static) appearance of the pleural line indicate visceral pleura moving against parietal pleura, consistent with normal lung sliding.
