# 0057_lung_blines-in-pulmonary-contusion

# Lung Ultrasound Frame-by-Frame Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Observations:

**Frames 1–3:**
- Pleural line visible at ~4–5 cm depth
- Granular/textured pleural line appearance
- Sub-pleural speckle pattern shows **variation between consecutive frames**, indicating movement
- Some vertical artifacts (B-lines) originating from the pleural line

**Frames 4–6:**
- Multiple bright reflectors at the pleural line level
- Prominent **B-lines/comet-tail artifacts** visible
- Significant sub-pleural echotexture with clear **frame-to-frame variation**
- Pleural line appears irregular and shimmering

**Frames 7–8:** ⚠️ **Notable change**
- Pleural line appears **smoother, more sharply defined, and static**
- Sub-pleural pattern becomes **homogeneous and uniform**
- Minimal to no speckle variation between these two frames
- Loss of B-lines; pattern below pleural line appears relatively featureless

**Frames 9–10:**
- Return of some echotexture and brightness at the pleural line
- Sub-pleural variation reappears
- Pattern more similar to early frames

## Key Findings Summary:

| Feature | Frames 1–6, 9–10 | Frames 7–8 |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|
| Pleural line | Granular, textured | Smooth, static |
| B-lines | Present | Absent |
| Sub-pleural speckle variation | Present | Absent |
| Overall pattern | Dynamic | Static |

## Conclusion: **BOTH — Lung Point Present**

The alternation between frames demonstrating lung sliding (B-lines, speckle variation, granular pleural line) and frames showing absent sliding (smooth static pleural line, no speckle variation) within a single clip is characteristic of a **lung point**. This represents the intermittent contact of the lung with the chest wall at the boundary of a pneumothorax — pathognomonic for pneumothorax.
