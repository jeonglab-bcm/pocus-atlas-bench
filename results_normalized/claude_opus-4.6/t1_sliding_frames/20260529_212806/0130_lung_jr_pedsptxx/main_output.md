# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Pleural Sliding Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Anatomical Orientation (all frames):**
- **Superficial layers**: Skin, subcutaneous tissue, and chest wall musculature in the upper portion
- **Pleural line**: Bright hyperechoic linear structure at approximately 1.5-2 cm depth
- **Sub-pleural space**: Lung parenchyma below with visible artifacts

**Frame-to-Frame Comparison:**

| Feature | Observation |
|---------|-------------|
| Pleural line texture | Granular/shimmering appearance; not perfectly smooth or static |
| Sub-pleural speckle | Varies between consecutive frames — particularly noticeable between frames 1-3, 6-7, and 9-10 |
| A-lines | Present but show subtle variation in intensity/definition across frames |
| B-lines/comet tails | Short comet-tail artifacts visible intermittently originating from the pleural line |

## Key Findings

**Signs of sliding PRESENT:**
- ✅ **Granular/shimmering pleural line** — the pleural interface is not perfectly smooth or static; it has a textured appearance that changes subtly between frames
- ✅ **Sub-pleural speckle variation** — the echogenicity pattern below the pleural line changes between consecutive frames, indicating relative motion of visceral against parietal pleura
- ✅ **Short comet-tail artifacts** — visible in several frames, arising from the pleural interface
- ✅ **No barcode/stratosphere sign** — no evidence of purely horizontal lines in the sub-pleural region

**Signs of sliding ABSENT:**
- ❌ None identified

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.** The granular texture of the pleural line, variation in sub-pleural speckle patterns across sequential frames, and intermittent comet-tail artifacts all confirm normal visceral-parietal pleural apposition and movement. There is no lung point or transition zone identified.
