## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Frames 1–6 (Anatomical Overview)
These frames display a standard intercostal lung ultrasound view with:
- **Soft tissue** superficially
- **Ribs** on either side creating posterior acoustic shadows
- **Pleural line** – the bright hyperechoic interface between the ribs
- **Lung** parenchyma deep to the pleura

Across these consecutive frames, I observe:
- The pleural line appears **granular and textured** rather than smooth and perfectly static
- There are **subtle variations in the sub-pleural speckle pattern** between frames, indicating tissue movement at the pleural interface
- The lung area below the pleura shows **shifting echogenicity** between frames

### Frames 7–10 (Pathological Findings)
These frames highlight:
- **Sub-pleural nodules** – small hypoechoic lesions just beneath the pleural line on both sides (consistent with miliary TB)
- **B-lines** – vertical hyperechoic comet-tail artifacts extending from the pleural line to the bottom of the screen without fading

The B-lines are clearly present and originate from the pleural line, confirming visceral-parietal pleural apposition.

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**

The evidence includes: (1) B-lines originating from the pleural line, (2) a shimmering/granular pleural line, and (3) variation in sub-pleural speckle patterns between frames. These findings, combined with the sub-pleural nodules, are consistent with **miliary tuberculosis** — the lung is in full contact with the chest wall with preserved sliding, ruling out pneumothorax at this location.
