# Lung Ultrasound Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### B-mode (Upper Panel):
- A bright pleural line is visible at approximately 1.5-2 cm depth
- Across sequential frames, the pleural line appearance **alternates** between:
  - Frames showing a shimmering, textured pleural interface with some sub-pleural movement
  - Frames showing a smoother, more static pleural line with less variation
- The sub-pleural speckle pattern changes intermittently rather than continuously

### M-mode (Lower Panel):
This is the most diagnostic portion:
- **Superficial layers (0–1.0 cm):** Consistent horizontal parallel lines (static chest wall) — expected and normal
- **Below the pleural line (~1.0–1.5 cm and deeper):** A clearly **alternating pattern**:
  - **Segments with granular/sandy texture** (seashore sign) → indicating pleural sliding PRESENT
  - **Segments with continuous horizontal lines** (barcode/stratosphere sign) → indicating pleural sliding ABSENT
- This alternation occurs at **regular intervals**, corresponding to the respiratory cycle

## Key Diagnostic Signs Identified

| Sign | Present? |
|------|----------|
| Alternating seashore and barcode on M-mode | ✅ |
| Intermittent sub-pleural speckle variation | ✅ |
| Periodic static pleural line | ✅ |
| Periodic shimmering pleural line | ✅ |

## Conclusion

**BOTH — Lung Point Identified**

This is a classic **lung point**, where the boundary of a pneumothorax intermittently moves across the ultrasound beam during respiration. During inspiration, the partially collapsed lung slides into the field (seashore sign), and during expiration, free air returns (barcode sign). This finding is **100% specific for pneumothorax** and helps delineate its extent.
