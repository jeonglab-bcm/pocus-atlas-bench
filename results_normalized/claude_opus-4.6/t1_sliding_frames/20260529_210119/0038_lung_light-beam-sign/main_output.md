# 0038_lung_light-beam-sign

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Frames 1, 5, 6, 7, 8:
- The pleural line appears as a **bright, relatively smooth, well-defined horizontal line**
- Below the pleural line, the field is predominantly **dark/anechoic** with faint horizontal A-line artifacts
- No significant vertical artifacts (B-lines) are seen
- Sub-pleural speckle appears **relatively static** with minimal variation
- These features suggest **absent sliding** (consistent with air between pleural layers)

### Frames 2, 3, 4, 9, 10:
- A prominent **vertical artifact (B-line/comet-tail)** emerges from the pleural line and extends downward
- The pleural line appears more **granular/shimmering** in texture
- Sub-pleural tissue pattern becomes visible with more **echogenic, dynamic content**
- Clear variation in the speckle pattern compared to adjacent frames
- These features suggest **present sliding** (normal lung interface)

### Transition Between Patterns:
- There is a clear **alternation** between frames showing sliding (with B-lines, granular texture) and frames showing no sliding (smooth line, A-lines, static pattern)
- This intermittent appearance of lung tissue "sliding in and out" of the field of view across sequential frames is characteristic of respiratory cycling at a boundary

## Conclusion: **BOTH (Lung Point)**

This clip demonstrates a **lung point** — the pathognomonic sign of **pneumothorax**. The alternating pattern between:
- Absent sliding (smooth pleural line, static A-lines → pneumothorax side)
- Present sliding (granular line, B-lines, sub-pleural variation → normal lung side)

...represents the edge of a pneumothorax where normal lung intermittently contacts the chest wall during respiration. This is 100% specific for pneumothorax.
