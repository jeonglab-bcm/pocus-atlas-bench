# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Frame-by-Frame

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### Frames 1–4 (2/270 to 89/270):
- **Wide sector** with clearly visible pleural line at ~3–4 cm depth
- **Granular/shimmering appearance** of the pleural line
- **Sub-pleural speckle variation** between consecutive frames — the tissue pattern below the pleural line changes, indicating movement
- Some **comet-tail artifacts** visible originating from the pleural line
- A hyperechoic structure (likely vertebral body/spine) visible deep to the pleura

### Frames 5–6 (116/270 to 147/270) — TRANSITION ZONE:
- The sector narrows
- The pleural line becomes **sharper and more static**
- Sub-pleural tissue begins to appear **fixed and anechoic**
- A visible **boundary** between the area showing motion and the static area

### Frames 7–10 (174/270 to 265/270):
- **Narrow sector**, pleural line appears as a fixed, well-defined bright line
- **No variation** in sub-pleural speckle between frames
- The area below the pleural line is **uniformly dark and static**
- Consistent with **absent pleural sliding** — no lung movement against chest wall

## Key Diagnostic Signs Identified

| Sign | Present? |
|------|----------|
| Sub-pleural speckle variation (early frames) | ✅ |
| Static pleural line (late frames) | ✅ |
| Transition point between sliding/no-sliding | ✅ |
| Granular pleural line (early) → smooth line (late) | ✅ |

## Conclusion: **BOTH — Lung Point**

This clip demonstrates a **lung point**, the pathognomonic sign of **pneumothorax**. There is a clear transition from:
- **Sliding present** (frames 1–4): granular pleural line with sub-pleural movement
- **Sliding absent** (frames 7–10): fixed, static pleural line with no sub-pleural variation

The transition zone (frames 5–6) marks the boundary where the partially collapsed lung intermittently contacts the chest wall during respiration. This confirms the presence of a pneumothorax with a visible lung point, which also indicates the pneumothorax is **not complete** (the lung still reaches this point during inspiration).
