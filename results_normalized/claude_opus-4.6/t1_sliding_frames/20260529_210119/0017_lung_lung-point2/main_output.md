# Lung Ultrasound Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### B-mode (Upper Panel):
- A bright hyperechoic pleural line is visible at approximately 1 cm depth
- Across the sequential frames, there is notable **variation** in the sub-pleural appearance: some frames show echogenic tissue beneath the pleural line (lung in contact with chest wall), while others show a more anechoic/dark space (lung falling away)
- This intermittent contact is particularly evident comparing frames 1-4 (more tissue-like) with later frames showing transitions

### M-mode (Lower Panel):
This is the most diagnostic portion:
- **Above the pleural line (0–1.0 cm):** Static horizontal parallel lines representing the immobile chest wall — normal and expected
- **Below the pleural line (~1.0–1.5 cm):** A clearly **alternating pattern** is present:
  - **Granular/sandy segments** (seashore sign) → sliding PRESENT
  - **Dark vertical columns** alternating with horizontal line segments (barcode) → sliding ABSENT
- This alternation occurs at **regular respiratory intervals**

## Key Diagnostic Signs Identified

| Sign | Present? |
|------|----------|
| Alternating seashore and barcode on M-mode | ✅ |
| Intermittent sub-pleural tissue contact in B-mode | ✅ |
| Transition point between sliding/non-sliding | ✅ |

## Conclusion: **BOTH — Lung Point Present**

The M-mode demonstrates a classic **lung point sign**, with rhythmic alternation between seashore pattern (pleural sliding present) and barcode/stratosphere pattern (sliding absent), synchronized with respiration. This is **pathognomonic for pneumothorax** and identifies the precise border where the partially collapsed lung intermittently contacts the chest wall during inspiration and falls away during expiration.
