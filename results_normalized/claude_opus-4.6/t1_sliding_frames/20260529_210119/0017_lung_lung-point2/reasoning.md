This distinctive repeating pattern is actually characteristic of a lung point, which would indicate the transition between areas of normal lung sliding and areas without sliding. The alternating pattern between seashore and barcode regions on M-mode is the classic lung point sign—it indicates the deflated lung intermittently making contact with the chest wall as it slides during respiration. During inspiration the lung touches the wall creating the seashore pattern, then during expiration it falls away leaving air-filled space that produces the barcode appearance. This is pathognomonic for pneumothorax, occurring right at the edge where the collapsed lung meets the chest wall.
