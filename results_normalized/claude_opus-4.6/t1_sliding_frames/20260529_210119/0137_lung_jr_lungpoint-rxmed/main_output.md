# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Frame-by-Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Observations:

**Frames 1, 4, 5, 6:** The pleural line appears granular/textured with visible variation in the sub-pleural speckle pattern. There is more disruption and dynamic movement in the tissue below the pleural line, suggesting the visceral pleura is in contact with the parietal pleura (sliding present).

**Frames 2, 3, 7, 8, 9:** The pleural line appears smoother and more sharply defined. The sub-pleural pattern is relatively static with well-defined horizontal A-line repetitions that remain largely unchanged. This suggests absence of sliding.

**Frame 10:** Shows a transition pattern with some areas of variation returning.

## Key Diagnostic Signs Identified:

| Sign | Observation |
|------|-------------|
| Sub-pleural speckle variation | Present in SOME frames, absent in others |
| Pleural line texture | Alternates between granular and smooth/static |
| A-lines | Static in some frames, disrupted in others |
| Temporal alternation | Clear cycling between sliding and no-sliding patterns |

## Conclusion: **BOTH (Lung Point)**

This sequence demonstrates a **lung point** — the pathognomonic sign of pneumothorax. There is a clear alternation between:
- **Sliding present** (frames with granular pleural movement and sub-pleural variation)
- **Sliding absent** (frames with a static, smooth pleural line and unchanging A-lines)

This represents the intermittent contact of the partially collapsed lung with the chest wall during the respiratory cycle, marking the boundary between normal lung and pneumothorax.
