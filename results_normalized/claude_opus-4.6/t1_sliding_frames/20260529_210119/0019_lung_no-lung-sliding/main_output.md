## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Structural Identification
Across all 10 frames, I identify:
- **Superficial layers**: Subcutaneous tissue with well-defined horizontal striations
- **Rib shadows**: Dark anechoic structures visible on the left side of the image
- **Pleural line**: Bright hyperechoic line at approximately 1 cm depth
- **Sub-pleural space**: Tissue below the pleural line with A-lines (horizontal reverberation artifacts)

### Frame-to-Frame Comparison

**Frames 1–3**: The pleural line appears bright with a granular, textured quality. The sub-pleural speckle pattern shows subtle variation between these frames — the grainy texture below the pleural line shifts slightly.

**Frames 4–6**: Continued variation in sub-pleural tissue echogenicity. The speckle pattern beneath the pleural line is not identical across these frames, indicating motion at the visceral-parietal pleural interface.

**Frames 7–10**: Similar findings — the pleural line maintains a shimmering/granular character, and the sub-pleural speckle continues to change frame-to-frame.

### Key Observations

| Feature | Finding |

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**

The evidence supporting this includes: (1) variation in sub-pleural speckle patterns between consecutive frames, indicating visceral pleura movement against parietal pleura; (2) a granular, textured pleural line rather than a smooth, sharply-defined static line; and (3) absence of a barcode/stratosphere sign pattern. This is a normal lung sliding pattern, consistent with no pneumothorax at this scanning location.
