# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Pleural Sliding Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Superficial structures (all frames):** Layered soft tissue (subcutaneous fat, muscle fascia, intercostal muscles) visible in the upper portion of the image, showing minimal variation between frames consistent with slight respiratory motion of the chest wall.

**Pleural line (all frames):** A bright, hyperechoic, well-defined line is visible at a consistent depth. Across all 10 frames:
- The pleural line appears **smooth and sharply defined** without granular or shimmering texture
- Its position remains essentially fixed
- No comet-tail artifacts or B-lines are originating from it

**Sub-pleural region (all frames):**
- Horizontal reverberation artifacts (A-lines) are visible below the pleural line
- These A-lines remain **perfectly static and unchanged** between consecutive frames
- The speckle pattern below the pleural line shows **no meaningful variation** across frames

## Key Findings

| Sign | Present? |
|------|----------|
| Shimmering/granular pleural line | ❌ No |
| B-lines/comet-tail artifacts | ❌ No |
| Static, smooth pleural line | ✅ Yes |
| Static, unchanged A-lines | ✅ Yes |
| Sub-pleural speckle variation | ❌ No |
| Lung point (transition zone) | ❌ No |

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is ABSENT.**

The findings are consistent with a **stratosphere/barcode sign equivalent in B-mode**: a smooth, immobile pleural line with perfectly static A-lines and no sub-pleural speckle variation. This pattern raises concern for **pneumothorax** at this scanning location and warrants further evaluation (M-mode confirmation, search for lung point, clinical correlation).
