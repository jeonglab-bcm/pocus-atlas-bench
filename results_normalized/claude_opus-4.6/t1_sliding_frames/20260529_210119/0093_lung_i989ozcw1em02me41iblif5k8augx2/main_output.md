# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Pleural Sliding Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1-3:** The chest wall layers (skin, subcutaneous tissue, intercostal muscles) are visible superficially. A bright, hyperechoic pleural line is identified. Below it, the sub-pleural speckle pattern shows noticeable variation between consecutive frames - the granular texture changes position and intensity.

**Frames 4-5:** The superficial layers appear well-organized with clear horizontal stratification. The pleural line retains a textured/granular quality rather than appearing smooth and sharply static. Subtle movement is visible at the pleural interface.

**Frames 6-8:** Continued variation in the sub-pleural speckle pattern is observed. The area immediately below the pleural line shows shifting echogenicity between frames. There appear to be short comet-tail artifacts arising from the pleural line in some frames.

**Frames 9-10:** The pleural line remains identifiable with a shimmering, granular quality. Sub-pleural pattern continues to vary between frames.

## Key Findings

| Sign | Observation |
|------|-------------|
| Pleural line texture | Granular/shimmering, not smooth or static |
| Sub-pleural speckle | **Varies between consecutive frames** |
| A-lines | Present but NOT perfectly static across frames |
| Comet-tail artifacts | Occasional short artifacts visible |
| Barcode/stratosphere sign | Absent |

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**

The primary evidence is the clear frame-to-frame variation in the sub-pleural speckle pattern, the granular/textured appearance of the pleural line (rather than a sharp, fixed line), and the presence of short comet-tail artifacts. There is no evidence of a static barcode pattern or unchanging A-lines. This is consistent with normal visceral-parietal pleural apposition and movement, indicating a normally aerated lung at this location.
