## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Frames 1–6 (Anatomical Labels)
These frames display a classic **"bat sign"** view with:
- Two ribs casting posterior acoustic shadows on either side
- A bright hyperechoic **pleural line** between the ribs
- Labeled soft tissue, ribs, pleura, and lung

**Key observations between frames:**
- The pleural line shows subtle textural variation — it appears **shimmering/granular** rather than perfectly smooth and static
- The sub-pleural speckle pattern shows **subtle differences** between consecutive frames, particularly in the central region between the rib shadows
- Faint vertical artifacts are visible below the pleural line in several frames

### Frames 7–10 (Pathological Labels)
These frames highlight:
- **Sub-pleural nodules**: Small, hypoechoic irregularities along/just below the pleural line (consistent with miliary TB)
- **B-lines**: Vertical hyperechoic comet-tail artifacts extending from the pleural line to the bottom of the screen without fading

**Key observations:**
- B-lines are clearly present and appear to shift subtly between frames
- The pleural line is irregular due to the sub-pleural nodules
- There is variation in the sub-pleural tissue between frames

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT**, though likely reduced. Evidence includes B-lines originating from the pleural line, subtle inter-frame variation in sub-pleural speckle, and a granular (non-static) pleural line. The pathological findings of **sub-pleural nodules** and **B-lines** are consistent with **miliary tuberculosis** causing interstitial-alveolar syndrome, with preserved (though possibly impaired) lung sliding.
