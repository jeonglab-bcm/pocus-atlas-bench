# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Frames 1-3:**
- A bright, hyperechoic pleural line is visible at approximately 3-4 cm depth
- Below the pleural line, there are numerous **confluent B-lines** creating a "white-out" or dense curtain-like pattern extending to the bottom of the screen
- The pleural line appears **granular/textured** rather than smooth and sharply defined
- Sub-pleural speckle pattern shows subtle variations between frames

**Frames 4-6:**
- The pleural line maintains its shimmering, textured appearance
- The confluent B-line pattern persists, with slight changes in brightness/texture between frames
- No perfectly static, stratified horizontal lines (no barcode pattern)

**Frames 7-10:**
- The sub-pleural pattern continues to show **frame-to-frame variation** in speckle distribution
- The pleural line remains granular and bright
- Confluent B-lines continue throughout

## Key Findings

| Sign | Observation |
|------|------------|
| Pleural line appearance | Shimmering, granular (not smooth/static) |
| B-lines | Confluent, extending to screen bottom |
| Sub-pleural speckle variation | Present between consecutive frames |
| Static A-lines | Absent — replaced by dense B-lines |
| Barcode/stratosphere pattern | Not seen |

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**

The evidence supporting this includes:
1. **Granular/shimmering pleural line** across all frames
2. **Confluent B-lines** (which are dynamic artifacts requiring visceral-parietal pleural apposition and movement)
3. **Variation in sub-pleural speckle** between consecutive frames

The overall pattern is consistent with **severe interstitial syndrome** (e.g., pulmonary edema, ARDS, or interstitial pneumonia) with preserved lung sliding — no pneumothorax.
