## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Frames 1-4:
- A well-defined, bright hyperechoic **pleural line** is visible at mid-depth
- The sub-pleural region appears relatively **static** with minimal textural variation between these consecutive frames
- **A-lines** (horizontal reverberation artifacts) appear below the pleural line and remain largely unchanged
- The pleural line appears smooth and sharply defined — consistent with **absent sliding**

### Frames 5-7 (Transition Zone):
- The pleural line character begins to change
- Frame 7 is notable: the left portion of the pleural line appears more static while the right portion appears to show some irregularity/movement
- This represents a **transitional area** where the appearance shifts

### Frames 8-10:

- The pleural line appears more **granular/shimmering** rather than sharply defined

- Consistent with **present sliding**

## Key Observations:
1. **Static pleural line with unchanging A-lines** in early frames → absent sliding
2. **Granular pleural line with variable sub-pleural speckle** in later frames → present sliding
3. **Visible transition point** between the two zones

## Conclusion: **BOTH (Lung Point)**

This sequence demonstrates a **lung point** — the pathognomonic sign of **pneumothorax**. There is a clear transition from an area of absent pleural sliding (where air separates the visceral and parietal pleura) to an area of present sliding (where the lung intermittently contacts the chest wall). This is consistent with the clinical indication of "CHEST PAIN" and confirms a partial pneumothorax at this location.
