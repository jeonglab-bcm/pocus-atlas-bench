# 0003_lung_lung-point-finding

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Structural Observations

**Pleural Line:** Across all 10 frames, I identify a bright hyperechoic pleural line located beneath the chest wall layers (subcutaneous tissue and intercostal muscles visible as layered hypoechoic bands above).

**A-lines:** Horizontal reverberation artifacts are visible below the pleural line throughout the sequence.

### Frame-to-Frame Comparison

**Frames 1–3:** The pleural line appears bright with a somewhat granular/textured quality. The sub-pleural speckle pattern shows subtle variations between consecutive frames.

**Frames 4–6:** Continued subtle changes in the echogenic pattern below the pleural line. The pleural line maintains a shimmering, textured appearance rather than appearing perfectly smooth and static.

**Frames 7–10:** Similar subtle frame-to-frame variations persist in the sub-pleural region. The tissue texture below the pleural line is not identical across frames.

### Key Findings

| Feature | Observation |
|---------|------------|
| Pleural line texture | Granular/shimmering — not perfectly smooth or static |
| Sub-pleural speckle | Shows variation between consecutive frames |
| A-lines | Present but show subtle dynamic changes |
| Comet-tail artifacts | Occasional bright foci arising from pleural line |

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**

The evidence supporting this includes:
- Subtle but discernible changes in the sub-pleural speckle pattern between frames
- The pleural line exhibits a granular/textured (shimmering) quality rather than a smooth, sharply defined static line
- A-lines are not perfectly unchanged across all frames
- No barcode/stratosphere sign characteristics are seen

The sliding appears **normal**, consistent with visceral and parietal pleura gliding against each other during respiration.
