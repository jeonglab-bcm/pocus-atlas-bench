# 0056_lung_lung-point

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Upper Tissue Layers (Chest Wall)
Across all frames, I can identify layered subcutaneous tissue and muscle with expected slight motion from respiration/probe movement.

### Pleural Line
A bright hyperechoic pleural line is identifiable at approximately 1.5-2 cm depth. It appears:
- **Granular/textured** rather than perfectly smooth and sharply defined
- Slightly irregular in contour across sequential frames
- The position and brightness show subtle variation between frames

### Sub-Pleural Region
- **Frames 1-5:** The speckle pattern below the pleural line shows subtle but definite variation between consecutive frames. The tissue texture changes, indicating movement at the visceral-parietal pleural interface.
- **Frames 6-10:** More prominent changes are visible. In frames 7-8, vertical hyperechoic structures extending below the pleural line appear, consistent with **comet-tail artifacts/short B-lines** originating from the pleural line.

### A-Lines
Some horizontal reverberation artifacts are present deep to the pleural line, but they are **not perfectly static** across all frames — their intensity and clarity fluctuate.

### Key Differentiating Findings
| Sign | Observed? |
|------|-----------|
| Sub-pleural speckle variation between frames | ✅ Yes |
| Granular/shimmering pleural line | ✅ Yes |
| Comet-tail artifacts | ✅ Yes (frames 7-8) |
| Smooth, static pleural line | ❌ No |
| Perfectly static A-lines | ❌ No |
| Transition point (lung point) | ❌ No |

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**

The evidence supporting this includes: (1) frame-to-frame variation in sub-pleural speckle pattern, (2) a granular/textured pleural line rather than a smooth static one, and (3) comet-tail artifacts originating from the pleural line in several frames. There is no evidence of a barcode/stratosphere sign equivalent, no perfectly static A-lines, and no lung point transition.
