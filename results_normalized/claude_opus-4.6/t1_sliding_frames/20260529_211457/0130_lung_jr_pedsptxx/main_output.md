# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### Structural Layout (consistent across all frames):
- **Superficial layers:** Skin, subcutaneous tissue, and muscle layers visible as organized, layered echogenic tissue
- **Pleural line:** Bright, hyperechoic line located at approximately 1.5-2 cm depth
- **Sub-pleural space:** Lung tissue visible below the pleural line to 4.0 cm depth

### Key Observations Across Frames:

**Pleural Line Characteristics:**
- The pleural line appears **granular and textured** rather than perfectly smooth and static
- There is subtle shimmering quality when comparing adjacent frames (e.g., frames 1→2, 6→7)
- The pleural line is not razor-sharp/mirror-like (which would suggest absence of sliding)

**Sub-pleural Speckle Pattern:**
- Comparing frames sequentially, the **speckle pattern below the pleural line varies** between consecutive frames
- Frames 1-3: subtle shifts in sub-pleural echogenicity
- Frames 4-8: noticeable textural changes in the sub-pleural tissue
- Frame 10: increased echogenicity in the sub-pleural zone compared to earlier frames

**A-lines:**
- Horizontal reverberations are visible below the pleural line but they are **not perfectly static** across all frames — slight variation is noted

**B-lines/Comet-tail artifacts:**
- Occasional short comet-tail artifacts are seen originating from the pleural line in several frames (particularly frames 2, 7)

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**

**Supporting evidence:**
- ✅ Granular/textured pleural line (not smooth and static)
- ✅ Sub-pleural speckle pattern changes between consecutive frames
- ✅ Occasional comet-tail artifacts at the pleural line
- ✅ A-lines are not perfectly static/unchanged across frames
- ❌ No barcode/stratosphere pattern features
- ❌ No fixed, mirror-like pleural line

The findings are consistent with normal lung sliding, indicating visceral and parietal pleura are in apposition and moving relative to each other with respiration. This effectively rules out pneumothorax at this scanning location.
