# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Sequential Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Anatomical Landmarks:**
- Linear high-frequency probe (L14-6Ns) at ~3 cm depth
- Superficial horizontal layers: skin, subcutaneous tissue, and chest wall muscles
- A prominent rib shadow (dark anechoic structure) visible in the left-center portion of the images
- Bright hyperechoic pleural line at approximately 1–1.5 cm depth
- A-lines (horizontal reverberation artifacts) visible below the pleural line

**Inter-frame Comparison:**

| Feature | Observation |
|---------|-------------|
| Superficial layers | Remain stable across frames |
| Pleural line texture | Shows subtle granular/shimmering quality; not perfectly smooth or static |
| Sub-pleural speckle | **Varies between consecutive frames** — texture and granular pattern shifts |
| A-lines | Present but show slight variation in echogenicity across frames |
| B-lines/Comet tails | Not prominently seen |

## Key Findings

1. **Speckle variation below the pleural line:** The granular pattern deep to the pleura changes subtly but perceptibly between frames, indicating tissue motion at the visceral-parietal pleural interface.

2. **Pleural line character:** The pleural line appears textured/granular rather than perfectly smooth and sharply defined — consistent with sliding.

3. **No static barcode pattern:** The sub-pleural region does not show unchanging horizontal lines characteristic of absent sliding.

## Conclusion

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**

The subtle but consistent inter-frame variation in sub-pleural speckle pattern and the granular appearance of the pleural line indicate normal visceral-parietal pleural apposition and movement. This is a **negative exam for pneumothorax** at this intercostal space.
