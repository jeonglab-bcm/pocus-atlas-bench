# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Assessment of Pleural Sliding

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Frames 1–4:**
- A bright, smooth, sharply defined pleural line is visible between two rib shadows (classic "bat sign")
- Prominent A-lines (horizontal reverberation artifacts) are seen below the pleural line
- The sub-pleural speckle pattern appears essentially unchanged between consecutive frames
- No comet-tail artifacts or B-lines identified

**Frames 5–7:**
- The pleural line remains smooth and fixed in position
- A-lines persist in the same configuration without variation
- Minimal to no change in sub-pleural tissue texture

**Frames 8–10:**
- Slight variations in superficial tissue brightness (likely transducer pressure changes)
- The pleural line and A-line pattern remain static
- Sub-pleural speckle remains constant

## Key Findings

| Sign | Observation |
|------|-------------|
| Pleural line character | Smooth, sharply defined, static |
| A-lines | Present and unchanged across all frames |
| Sub-pleural speckle variation | Minimal/absent between frames |
| B-lines/comet-tails | None identified |
| Lung point | Not observed |

## Conclusion: **Pleural Sliding is ABSENT**

The findings strongly suggest absent lung sliding based on:
1. **Static, sharply defined pleural line** without shimmering or granularity
2. **Unchanging A-lines** that remain perfectly fixed across all frames
3. **No variation in sub-pleural speckle** between consecutive frames

This pattern (A-lines + absent sliding) is consistent with **pneumothorax** until proven otherwise, though other causes (apnea, pleurodesis, mainstem intubation) should be considered clinically. A lung point should be sought to confirm pneumothorax.
