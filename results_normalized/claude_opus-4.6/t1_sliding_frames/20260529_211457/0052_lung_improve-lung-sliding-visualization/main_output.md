# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Frame-by-Frame

## Frame-by-Frame Assessment

### Early Frames (2/270 – 89/270):
- The pleural line appears **textured and granular** (shimmering quality)
- There is visible **sub-pleural speckle variation** between consecutive frames
- Echogenic material and possible **comet-tail artifacts** are seen originating from the pleural line
- The sub-pleural region shows tissue motion and dynamic patterns
- These findings are consistent with **pleural sliding PRESENT**

### Transition Frames (~116/270 – 147/270):
- The pleural line begins to appear **smoother and more sharply defined**
- Sub-pleural activity diminishes
- The field of view narrows, and the pattern begins to shift

### Late Frames (174/270 – 265/270):
- The pleural line becomes a **smooth, static, well-defined bright line**
- Below the pleural line: **static horizontal A-lines** with no variation between frames
- The sub-pleural speckle pattern is **unchanged** across consecutive frames
- No B-lines or comet-tail artifacts visible
- These findings are consistent with **pleural sliding ABSENT** (stratosphere/barcode equivalent)

## Conclusion: **BOTH (Lung Point)**

This clip demonstrates a **lung point** — a transition from pleural sliding being **present** (early frames) to **absent** (later frames) as the transducer sweeps across the chest wall. This represents the boundary between visceral pleura in contact with parietal pleura (normal lung) and separated pleural surfaces (pneumothorax).

**Clinical significance:** The lung point is **pathognomonic for pneumothorax** and helps delineate its extent.
