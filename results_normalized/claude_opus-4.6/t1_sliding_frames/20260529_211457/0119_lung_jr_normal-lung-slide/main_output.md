# Lung Ultrasound Analysis: Frame-by-Frame Assessment

## Frame-by-Frame Observations:

**Frames 1-3:**
- Bright, hyperechoic pleural line clearly visible
- A-lines (horizontal reverberation artifacts) present below the pleural line
- Sub-pleural speckle pattern shows some granular texture
- Subtle variations in sub-pleural pattern between consecutive frames

**Frames 4-6:**
- A-lines become more prominent
- A visible **transition zone** begins to appear — the left portion of the image shows more granular, dynamic texture while the right portion appears darker and more uniform/static
- The pleural line on one side appears to shimmer while the other appears fixed

**Frames 7-9:**
- The dichotomy becomes more apparent — one side of the sub-pleural area shows changing speckle (indicating lung tissue in contact with pleura), while the other remains static
- A-lines on the static side remain unchanged

**Frame 10:**
- Clear transition point visible: the left portion of the image shows a more static, barcode-like pattern beneath the pleural line, while the right side demonstrates some residual granularity/texture

| Sign | Present? |
|------|----------|

## Conclusion: **BOTH — Lung Point Present**

This clip demonstrates a **lung point**, which is the pathognomonic sign of **pneumothorax**. There is a visible transition between an area where pleural sliding is **present** (visceral and parietal pleura in contact) and an area where sliding is **absent** (air separating the pleural layers). This intermittent appearance of lung sliding at a fixed point represents the border of the pneumothorax, where the partially collapsed lung edge periodically contacts the chest wall during respiration.
