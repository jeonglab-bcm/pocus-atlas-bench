# 0015_lung_plaps-consolidation

Based on the provided ultrasound frames of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point), the following observations are made:

*   **Tissue-like hepatization**: The lung parenchyma has lost its normal aerated appearance and instead appears solid and echogenic, closely resembling the texture of liver tissue.
*   **Air bronchograms**: There are multiple small, hyperechoic (bright) punctate and linear foci scattered within the hepatized lung, which are characteristic air bronchograms.
*   **Shred sign**: The border between the consolidated lung and the potentially aerated lung is irregular and "shredded" rather than a smooth, continuous line.
*   **Effusion**: There is no evidence of an anechoic (black) fluid collection that would suggest a pleural effusion; signs like the quad sign or jellyfish sign are absent.

Because consolidation is clearly present without a significant associated effusion, the classification is as follows:

*   **plaps = true**
*   **type = "consolidation"**
