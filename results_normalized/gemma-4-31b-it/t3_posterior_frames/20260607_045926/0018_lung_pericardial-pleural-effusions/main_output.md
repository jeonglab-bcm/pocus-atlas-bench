# 0018_lung_pericardial-pleural-effusions

The sequential images from the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point) show a large area of lung consolidation. The lung parenchyma has lost its normal aerated appearance and instead exhibits a tissue-like, echogenic texture resembling the liver, which is known as hepatization. Within this consolidated area, several small, punctate hyperechoic foci are visible, representing air bronchograms. Additionally, there is an irregular border between the consolidated and aerated lung, characteristic of the shred sign. No anechoic areas suggesting a pleural effusion (such as the quad sign or jellyfish sign) are seen.
