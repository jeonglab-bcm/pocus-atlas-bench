# 0143_lung_jr_pleuraleffusion

Based on the provided ultrasound sequence of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point), the following observations can be made:

*   **Effusion signs:** There is a prominent anechoic (black) space between the pleural line and the lung parenchyma, which is characteristic of a pleural effusion. This creates a **quad sign**, where the fluid is bounded by the rib shadows and pleural lines.
*   **Consolidation signs:** The lung tissue within the effusion is not normal; it appears solid and echogenic, resembling the texture of a solid organ (liver), which is known as **tissue-like hepatization**. The lung is seen as a floating, consolidated mass within the fluid.

Because both pleural effusion and alveolar consolidation are clearly present, this is a classic PLAPS pattern.

**Conclusion:**
- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**
