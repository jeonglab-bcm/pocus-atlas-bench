The ultrasound frames of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point) demonstrate several key pathological signs:

1.  **Consolidation Signs**: There is a large area of lung parenchyma that has lost its normal aeration and appears solid and echogenic, resembling liver texture (**tissue-like hepatization**). Within this consolidated area, multiple punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are visible, which are characteristic of **air bronchograms**. An irregular, shredded border is also seen where the consolidated lung meets the surrounding area (**shred sign**).
2.  **Effusion Signs**: There is a significant anechoic (black) space beneath the consolidated lung, indicating a **pleural effusion**. The consolidated lung is seen floating and undulating within this fluid collection, which is the **jellyfish sign**.

Because both signs of alveolar consolidation and pleural effusion are present in the dependent posterior lung zone, this is a positive PLAPS finding.

**Conclusion:**
- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**
