# 0007_lung_pleural-effusion-with-compressed-lung-and-spine-sign

The ultrasound images of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point) show the following:

1.  **Tissue-like Hepatization**: The lung parenchyma has lost its normal aeration and appears solid and echogenic, mimicking the appearance of liver tissue.
2.  **Air Bronchograms**: There are small, anechoic or hyperechoic punctate and linear foci within the consolidated lung tissue, representing air-filled bronchi.
3.  **Pleural Effusion**: A significant anechoic (black) space is visible inferior to the consolidated lung, characteristic of a pleural effusion. The consolidated lung is seen floating within this fluid.

Since both signs of alveolar consolidation (hepatization and air bronchograms) and signs of pleural effusion are present, this is a classic PLAPS pattern.

- **type**: "both"
