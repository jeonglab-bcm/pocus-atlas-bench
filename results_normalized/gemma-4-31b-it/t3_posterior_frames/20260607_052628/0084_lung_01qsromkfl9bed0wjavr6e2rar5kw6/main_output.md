These ultrasound frames of a posterior lung zone demonstrate several key findings:

1.  **Pleural Effusion**: There is a large anechoic (black) hypoechoic area, which represents a significant pleural effusion.
2.  **Alveolar Consolidation**: Adjacent to the fluid, the lung parenchyma has lost its normal aeration and exhibits a solid, echogenic appearance similar to liver tissue, known as **hepatization**.
3.  **Air Bronchograms**: Within the consolidated lung tissue, there are small, bright, hyperechoic punctate and linear foci, which are air bronchograms (trapped air in the bronchi).
4.  **Shred Sign**: The border between the consolidated lung and the surrounding fluid/aerated lung is irregular and "shredded," characteristic of the shred sign.

Because both signs of pleural effusion and alveolar consolidation are present, this is a classic PLAPS pattern.

- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**
