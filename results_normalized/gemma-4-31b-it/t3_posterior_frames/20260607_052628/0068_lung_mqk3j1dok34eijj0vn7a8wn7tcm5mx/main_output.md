The ultrasound images of the posterior lung zone demonstrate the following findings:

*   **Consolidation signs**: The lung parenchyma exhibits a "tissue-like hepatization" appearance, where it looks solid and similar to liver tissue instead of having normal aeration. Within this consolidated area, there are several bright, hyperechoic punctate and linear foci, which are characteristic **air bronchograms**.
*   **Effusion signs**: There is a prominent anechoic (black) space surrounding the consolidated lung, indicating a **pleural effusion**. The consolidated lung appears to be floating within this fluid, which is consistent with the **jellyfish sign**.

Because both signs of alveolar consolidation (hepatization and air bronchograms) and pleural effusion are present in the dependent zone, this is a classic PLAPS pattern.

**Conclusion:**
- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**
