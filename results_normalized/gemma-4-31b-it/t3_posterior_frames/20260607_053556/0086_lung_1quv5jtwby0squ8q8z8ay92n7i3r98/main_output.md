The ultrasound images of the posterior lung zone show large, anechoic (black) spaces characteristic of a pleural effusion. Within these fluid collections, there are irregular, hyperechoic, floating fragments of lung tissue that exhibit a characteristic undulating movement, which is known as the "jellyfish sign." These fragments represent consolidated or atelectatic lung parenchyma. The shredded appearance of the lung borders within the fluid is consistent with the "shred sign."

Since both a pleural effusion and consolidated lung tissue are clearly visible, this represents a Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome (PLAPS).
