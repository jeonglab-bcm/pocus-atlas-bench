The ultrasound frames of the posterior lung zone demonstrate several key findings:

1.  **Consolidation:** There is a large area of tissue-like, echogenic parenchyma that resembles liver tissue, indicating **hepatization** of the lung. Within this consolidated area, multiple small, bright hyperechoic spots are visible, which are characteristic **air bronchograms**.
2.  **Effusion:** An anechoic (black) space is present adjacent to the consolidated lung, representing a **pleural effusion**. The consolidated lung is seen floating and undulating within this fluid, which is the **jellyfish sign**.

Because both signs of pleural effusion and alveolar consolidation are present, this is a positive PLAPS pattern.
