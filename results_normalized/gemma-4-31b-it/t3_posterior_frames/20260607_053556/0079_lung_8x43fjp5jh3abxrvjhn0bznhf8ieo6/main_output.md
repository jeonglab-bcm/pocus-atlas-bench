The ultrasound images of the posterior lung zone show a combination of signs indicative of both pleural effusion and alveolar consolidation:

- **Effusion signs**: There are large, anechoic (black) areas representing pleural fluid. Within this fluid, fragmented, consolidated lung tissue is visible and appears to be floating, which is characteristic of the **jellyfish sign**.
- **Consolidation signs**: The lung tissue exhibits **tissue-like hepatization**, appearing solid and echogenic. There is an irregular, "shredded" border between the consolidated lung and the surrounding fluid (**shred sign**). Additionally, multiple bright, linear, and punctate hyperechoic foci are visible within the consolidated areas, representing **air bronchograms**.

Because both fluid collection and lung hepatization with air bronchograms are present, this is a classic PLAPS pattern.

- **type = "both"**
