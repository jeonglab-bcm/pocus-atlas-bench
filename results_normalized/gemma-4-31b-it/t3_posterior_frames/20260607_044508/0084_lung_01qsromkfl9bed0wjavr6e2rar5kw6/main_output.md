The ultrasound images of the posterior lung zone show a large anechoic (black) space, which is characteristic of a pleural effusion. Within this fluid collection, there is a consolidated area of lung parenchyma that exhibits a solid, echogenic, liver-like texture, known as hepatization. This consolidated lung is floating and undulating within the effusion, which is referred to as the "jellyfish sign." Additionally, small, hyperechoic punctate foci can be seen within the consolidated lung, representing air bronchograms.

Because both signs of pleural effusion (anechoic space, jellyfish sign) and lung consolidation (hepatization, air bronchograms) are clearly present, this represents a PLAPS pattern.
