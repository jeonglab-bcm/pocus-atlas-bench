# 0007_lung_pleural-effusion-with-compressed-lung-and-spine-sign

The provided ultrasound frames from the posterior lung zone show the following:

1.  **Consolidation**: There is a large area of lung parenchyma that has lost its normal aeration and instead exhibits **tissue-like hepatization**, meaning it looks similar to the texture of the liver. Within this consolidated area, there are several small anechoic/hypoechoic foci, which are consistent with **air bronchograms**.
2.  **Effusion**: Adjacent to the consolidated lung, there is a significant anechoic (black) space, which represents a **pleural effusion**.

The presence of both hepatized lung and a surrounding fluid collection is a classic PLAPS (Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome) pattern.

**Conclusion:**
- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**
