# 0029_lung_parapneumonic-effusion

Based on the provided lung ultrasound frames from the posterior zone (PLAPS point), the following observations can be made:

1.  **Consolidation Signs**: The lung parenchyma has lost its normal aerated appearance and instead exhibits **tissue-like hepatization**, resembling the texture of a solid organ like the liver. Within this hepatized area, there are multiple small, punctate, hyperechoic (bright) foci, which are characteristic of **air bronchograms**. An irregular, jagged border is visible between the consolidated lung and the surrounding area, known as the **shred sign**.
2.  **Effusion Signs**: Adjacent to the consolidated lung, there are distinct **anechoic (black) spaces**, which represent a pleural effusion. The consolidated lung appears to be floating or bordering this fluid collection.

Because both signs of lung consolidation (hepatization, air bronchograms, shred sign) and pleural effusion (anechoic fluid space) are present, this is a classic PLAPS pattern.

**Conclusion:**
- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**
