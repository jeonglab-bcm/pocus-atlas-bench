# 0027_lung_subpleural-consolidation

Based on the provided sequential ultrasound frames of the right hemithorax (Zone 1), here is the analysis:

### Frame-by-Frame Analysis
The images show a cross-sectional view of the chest wall. A prominent feature across all frames is a large, anechoic (black) area, which is characteristic of a **pleural effusion**. Within and adjacent to this fluid collection, there is a large area of consolidated lung tissue. This consolidated area lacks normal lung aeration and exhibits a solid, echogenic appearance similar to liver tissue.

### B-lines Assessment
There are no hyperechoic vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line that extend to the bottom of the screen. The normal aeration artifacts (A-lines) are mostly obscured by the fluid and the consolidated lung mass.
- **lung_rockets = false**

### Consolidation Assessment
The images demonstrate a significant area of lung parenchyma that has lost its normal airy texture and instead appears solid and echogenic. This is classic **tissue-like hepatization**, where the lung resembles the texture of the liver. While there is an irregular border between the consolidated lung and the surrounding pleural fluid, a classic "shred sign" (border between aerated and non-aerated lung) is not clearly seen here because the surrounding area is fluid, not aerated lung. No definitive air bronchograms are visible in these frames.
- **consolidation = true**
- **consolidation_type = "hepatization"**

**Conclusion:**
- **B-lines:** Absent
- **Consolidation:** Present (predominantly hepatization)
