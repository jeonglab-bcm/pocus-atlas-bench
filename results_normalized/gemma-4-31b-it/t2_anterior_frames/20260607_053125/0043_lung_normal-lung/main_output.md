# 0043_lung_normal-lung

Based on the sequential frames provided from the lung ultrasound (LUS) of an anterior lung zone:

### B-lines Assessment
The images demonstrate multiple, dense, hyperechoic vertical artifacts that arise from the pleural line and extend all the way to the bottom of the screen without fading. These B-lines are not discrete; they are confluent and coalesce into a diffuse white sheet that completely obscures the normal horizontal A-lines. This appearance is characteristic of a "ground glass" pattern, typically indicating alveolar edema or diffuse interstitial disease.
- **lung_rockets**: true
- **b_line_subtype**: ground_glass

### Consolidation Assessment
There is no evidence of tissue-like hepatization of the lung parenchyma. No "shred sign" (irregular border between aerated and non-aerated lung) is visible, nor are there any punctate or linear hyperechoic foci representing air bronchograms. The bright areas are vertical artifacts, not consolidated solid tissue.
- **consolidation**: false
- **consolidation_type**: null

**Conclusion:**
The LUS shows diffuse, confluent B-lines creating a ground-glass appearance with no signs of consolidation.
