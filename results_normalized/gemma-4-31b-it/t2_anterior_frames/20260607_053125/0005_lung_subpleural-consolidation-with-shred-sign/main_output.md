# 0005_lung_subpleural-consolidation-with-shred-sign

Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames of the anterior lung zone:

**B-lines Assessment:**
Across the frames, multiple hyperechoic vertical artifacts (B-lines) are visible, arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen. In some areas, these B-lines are discrete, while in other regions, they coalesce into confluent white sheets, obscuring the normal A-line pattern. This combination of discrete and confluent lines indicates a **mixed** pattern.
- **lung_rockets**: true
- **b_lines_subtype**: mixed

**Consolidation Assessment:**
There is clear evidence of alveolar consolidation in the lung. Specifically:
- **Tissue-like hepatization**: Large areas of the lung parenchyma have lost their normal aeration and now exhibit a solid, liver-like echogenic texture.
- **Shred sign**: The border between the consolidated (hepatized) tissue and the more aerated lung is irregular and fragmented, creating a characteristic "shredded" appearance.
- **Air bronchograms**: Multiple punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are visible within the hepatized lung tissue, representing air-filled bronchi.
Due to the prominence of these hyperechoic foci within the consolidated area, the predominant consolidation type is air bronchograms.
- **consolidation**: true
- **consolidation_type**: air_bronchogram
