# 0046_lung_light-beam-sign-covid19

Based on the sequential frames of the lung ultrasound (LUS) from the anterior zone, here is the analysis:

### B-lines Assessment
Across all frames, there are numerous hyperechoic vertical artifacts that originate from the pleural line and extend to the bottom of the screen. These artifacts are not discrete and well-spaced; instead, they are coalescing and merging to form a diffuse, bright "white sheet" appearance, particularly on the right side of the image. This pattern is characteristic of confluent B-lines.
- **lung_rockets**: true
- **b_line_subtype**: ground_glass

### Consolidation Assessment
The lung parenchyma does not exhibit tissue-like hepatization (it does not look like liver tissue). There is no irregular, shredded border between aerated and non-aerated lung (shred sign), nor are there punctate or linear hyperechoic foci within a solid-looking area (air bronchograms). The findings are dominated by interstitial/alveolar patterns rather than alveolar consolidation.
- **consolidation**: false
- **consolidation_type**: null

**Conclusion:**
The images show a ground-glass pattern characterized by confluent B-lines, with no evidence of lung consolidation.
