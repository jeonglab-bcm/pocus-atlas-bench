# 0005_lung_subpleural-consolidation-with-shred-sign

Based on the provided ultrasound frames of the anterior lung zone, here is the detailed analysis:

### B-lines Assessment
The images show the presence of vertical, hyperechoic artifacts arising from the pleural line. In several regions, these B-lines are not discrete but instead are coalescing and confluent, merging into a diffuse white sheet that obscures the normal A-line pattern. This "white lung" appearance is characteristic of a **ground glass** pattern, typically associated with alveolar edema or severe interstitial disease.
- **B-lines present**: True
- **Subtype**: ground_glass

### Consolidation Assessment
There is clear evidence of alveolar consolidation in this anterior zone. Specifically:
- **Tissue-like hepatization**: A significant portion of the lung parenchyma has lost its normal aeration and exhibits a solid, echogenic appearance resembling the texture of the liver.
- **Shred sign**: There is an irregular, "shredded" border between the consolidated (solid) tissue and the more aerated areas of the lung.
- **Air bronchograms**: Small, punctate hyperechoic foci are visible within the consolidated region, representing air trapped within the bronchi.

The predominant feature of the consolidation is the overall tissue-like appearance.
- **Consolidation present**: True
- **Consolidation type**: hepatization
