# 0057_lung_blines-in-pulmonary-contusion

Based on a frame-by-frame analysis of the provided lung ultrasound (LUS) images:

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**
*   **Frames 1-5:** These frames show a region of **consolidated lung tissue** (often referred to as "hepatization" or "tissue-like" appearance). There is a visible, shimmering pleural line, and the sub-pleural speckle pattern changes slightly between frames, which is indicative of **pleural sliding**. The irregular border of this consolidated area is characteristic of the "shred sign."
*   **Frame 6:** There is a sudden and dramatic change in the image. A large, anechoic (dark) area appears on the left side of the screen. This represents air in the pleural space, indicating a **pneumothorax**. At this moment, the image shows a transition: a region where the lung is still attached (on the right) and a region where it has detached (on the left).
*   **Frame 7:** The anechoic area expands further, and the pleural line becomes a smooth, static interface on the right, with no sliding present in the dark region.

**Conclusion:**
The sequence demonstrates a transition from a region with active pleural sliding (consolidated lung) to a region where sliding is absent (pneumothorax). This specific dynamic transition is known as a **lung point**, which is the most pathognomonic sign of a pneumothorax.

**Final Assessment: BOTH (Lung Point)**
