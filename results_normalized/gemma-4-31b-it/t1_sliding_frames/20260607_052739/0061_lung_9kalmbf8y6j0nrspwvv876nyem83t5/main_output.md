Based on an analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound (LUS) frames:

**Observations:**
*   **Pleural Line:** A bright, hyperechoic pleural line is visible across all frames.
*   **Artifacts:** In every frame, there are multiple, prominent vertical hyperechoic artifacts extending from the pleural line to the bottom of the screen. These are **B-lines** (also known as comet-tail artifacts).
*   **Dynamics:** While these are static frames, the presence of B-lines is a key diagnostic marker. B-lines occur when the visceral pleura is in contact with the parietal pleura, which typically allows for pleural sliding.
*   **Absence of other signs:** There are no static, horizontal A-lines typical of a pneumothorax, nor is there a transition point (lung point) where B-lines suddenly disappear and are replaced by a static pleural line.

**Conclusion:**
**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.** The definitive sign is the presence of multiple B-lines originating from the pleural line, which indicates that the lung is in contact with the chest wall and moving during respiration.
