2.  **Check for signs of sliding PRESENT:**
    *   **Shimmering/granular pleural line:** The pleural line is bright and has a slightly irregular appearance.
    *   **Comet-tail artifacts or B-lines:** There are clear B-lines originating from the pleural line in all frames. This is a strong indicator of lung sliding (even if sliding isn't overtly "shimmering" in static frames, B-lines are generally associated with lung contact/sliding).
    *   **Speckle pattern variation:** Comparing consecutive frames, there are subtle changes in the appearance and density of the B-lines and the sub-pleural area, suggesting movement.

    *   **Stratosphere/barcode sign:** This is a B-mode clip, not M-mode, but the B-lines are a B-mode equivalent of "seashore" (though technically B-lines are B-mode, and seashore is M-mode). The presence of B-lines contradicts the barcode sign.
    *   **Static A-lines:** There are no clear A-lines; instead, there are B-lines.

4.  **Check for signs of BOTH (lung point):**
    *   There is no obvious transition from an area of sliding to an area of no sliding within these frames. The whole field shows similar patterns.

5.  **Conclusion:**
    *   The presence of numerous B-lines originating from the pleural line is a key sign that the visceral and parietal pleura are in contact and moving. This is consistent with the presence of lung sliding.
    *   The frames are almost identical, but the nature of the artifacts (B-lines) strongly suggests sliding is present. In a real video, you'd see the "shimmering" of the line and the dynamic nature of the B-lines. From these frames, the B-lines are the most diagnostic feature.

6.  **Final Formulation:**
    *   Observations: I see a bright pleural line with multiple vertical B-lines extending downwards. These B-lines originate directly from the pleural line.
    *   Assessment: B-lines are an indicator that the lung is in contact with the chest wall and sliding is occurring.
    *   Conclusion: Pleural sliding is PRESENT.
