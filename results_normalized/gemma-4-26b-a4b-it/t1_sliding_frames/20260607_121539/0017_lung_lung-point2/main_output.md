Based on the provided ultrasound video frames, here is the analysis:

**Observations:**
* **B-mode (top image):** A pleural line is visible at a depth of approximately 1.5 cm.
* **M-mode (bottom image):** 
    * Above the pleural line (depth < 1.5 cm), the image shows continuous, parallel horizontal lines, representing the static chest wall tissue.
    * Below the pleural line (depth > 1.5 cm), there is a distinctive granular, speckled, or "sandy" pattern. This pattern repeats periodically, corresponding to the respiratory cycles of the patient.
    * This combination of horizontal lines above and a granular pattern below is known as the **"seashore sign."**

**Conclusion:**
**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.** The presence of the "seashore sign" on M-mode is a definitive indicator that the visceral and parietal pleura are moving against each other during respiration.
