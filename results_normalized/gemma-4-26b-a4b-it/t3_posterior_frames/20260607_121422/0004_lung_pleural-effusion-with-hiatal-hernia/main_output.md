# 0004_lung_pleural-effusion-with-hiatal-hernia

In these sequential ultrasound frames of the posterior lung zone, several key signs are observed:

*   **Effusion signs:** There is a large, anechoic (black) area situated superior to the lung parenchyma, which is characteristic of a **pleural effusion**. The consolidated lung appears to be "floating" within this fluid collection, a finding referred to as the **jellyfish sign**.
*   **Consolidation signs:** The lung parenchyma itself is no longer aerated and instead appears echogenic and solid, with a texture similar to the liver, known as **tissue-like hepatization**. Within this consolidated lung, small, bright (hyperechoic) spots can be seen, which are characteristic of **air bronchograms**.

Because both a significant pleural effusion and alveolar consolidation are present, the classification is as follows:

**plaps = true**
**type = "both"**
