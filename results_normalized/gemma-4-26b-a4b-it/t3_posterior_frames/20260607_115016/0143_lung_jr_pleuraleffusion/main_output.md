# 0143_lung_jr_pleuraleffusion

Based on the provided video clip of a lung ultrasound (LUS) at the PLAPS point, here is the analysis:

**Observations:**
*   **Hepatization:** The lung tissue appearing in the posterior/lateral zone has a solid, granular, and echogenic texture, resembling the appearance of liver tissue (hepatization). This indicates that the lung is no longer aerated but has become solid.
*   **Shred Sign:** There is a clear, irregular, and "shredded" appearance at the border between the darker area (representing aerated lung) and the brighter, consolidated lung tissue. This is a characteristic sign of alveolar consolidation.
*   **Air Bronchograms:** Within the hepatized lung tissue, small, punctate, hyperechoic (bright) foci can be observed, which represent air trapped within the bronchi (air bronchograms).
*   **Effusion signs:** No definitive signs of a significant pleural effusion, such as the quad sign, sinusoid sign, or jellyfish sign, are clearly visualized in these frames. The primary finding is the solid-appearing lung tissue.

**Conclusion:**
*   **plaps = true**
*   **type = "consolidation"**
