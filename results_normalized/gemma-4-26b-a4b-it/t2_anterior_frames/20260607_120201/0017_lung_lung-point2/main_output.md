# 0017_lung_lung-point2

The provided images are sequential frames from a lung ultrasound (LUS) video clip focusing on an anterior lung zone.

**B-lines Assessment:**
In the B-mode image (top part of each frame), there are extensive hyperechoic vertical artifacts originating from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen. These artifacts are confluent, merging into a diffuse, bright white area that obscures the normal A-line pattern. This appearance is characteristic of **ground-glass** B-lines, which typically indicate alveolar edema or diffuse interstitial lung disease.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
There is no evidence of lung consolidation in these images. The M-mode image (bottom part of each frame) shows the characteristic **"seashore sign"**: the upper part is composed of horizontal lines (representing the static chest wall), while the part below the pleural line exhibits a granular, moving pattern (representing the "sand" of the moving lung). This "seashore sign" confirms the presence of normal **lung sliding**. There is no tissue-like hepatization, shred sign, or air bronchograms visible in the B-mode.

**Conclusions:**
- **B-lines:** present (**ground_glass**)
- **Consolidation:** absent
