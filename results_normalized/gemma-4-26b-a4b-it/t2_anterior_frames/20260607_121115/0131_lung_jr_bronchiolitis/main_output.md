# 0131_lung_jr_bronchiolitis

The sequential ultrasound frames of the left anterior lung zone show a progression in the appearance of lung artifacts.

Initially, some horizontal **A-lines** (reverberation artifacts) and potentially sparse, discrete B-lines can be observed. However, as the sequence progresses, there is a transition to a prominent pattern of **confluent B-lines**. These hyperechoic vertical artifacts arise from the pleural line, extend to the bottom of the screen, and merge together to form a diffuse, bright white "sheet" that completely obscures the underlying A-lines. This appearance is characteristic of the **ground-glass** subtype of B-lines, which often indicates diffuse interstitial or alveolar disease.

Regarding consolidation, there is no evidence of a discrete, tissue-like (hepatized) lung mass, a "shred sign" at the border of aerated and non-aerated lung, or internal air bronchograms. The brightening is due to confluent artifacts rather than a solid consolidation pattern.

**Conclusions:**
- **B-lines Assessment:** B-lines are present, exhibiting a **ground-glass** pattern (confluent, coalescing vertical artifacts creating a bright white sheet).
- **Consolidation Assessment:** Consolidation is **absent**.
