* **Observation**: In multiple frames (such as frames 1, 2, and 7), there are dense, confluent, hyperechoic vertical artifacts that emerge from the pleural line and merge into a diffuse bright area, obscuring any A-lines. This creates a "white-out" appearance of the lung parenchyma.
* **Conclusion**: B-lines are present and exhibit a **ground-glass** pattern.

### Consolidation Assessment
* **Observation**: Frames 3, 4, and 5 clearly demonstrate an area of lung consolidation. The consolidated lung appears hypoechoic (darker) compared to the aerated lung, exhibiting a tissue-like or **hepatized** appearance. The interface between this consolidated area and the aerated lung is irregular and jagged, which is characteristic of the **shred sign**. Additionally, small, bright, punctate hyperechoic foci are visible within the consolidated tissue, representing **air bronchograms**.
* **Conclusion**: **Consolidation is present**, showing features of hepatization, the shred sign, and air bronchograms.
