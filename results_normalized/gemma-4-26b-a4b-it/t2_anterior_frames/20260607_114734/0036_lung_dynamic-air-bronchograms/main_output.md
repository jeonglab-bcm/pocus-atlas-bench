# 0036_lung_dynamic-air-bronchograms

The lung ultrasound video clip shows the following findings:

**B-lines Assessment:**
There are numerous vertical, hyperechoic artifacts (B-lines) originating from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen. These B-lines are highly confluent, merging together to create a diffuse, bright white "sheet" that obscures the normal A-line pattern and dark lung parenchyma. This appearance is characteristic of a **ground-glass** pattern, which typically indicates diffuse interstitial or alveolar disease (e.g., pulmonary edema).

**Consolidation Assessment:**
In the lower portion of the ultrasound image, there is a large area of lung that has lost its normal aeration. This region displays **tissue-like hepatization**, where the lung parenchyma appears solid and echogenic, resembling the texture of the liver. The interface between this consolidated tissue and the overlying aerated (ground-glass) lung is irregular and jagged, demonstrating the **shred sign**.

**Conclusions:**
* **B-lines:** present (subtype: **ground-glass**)
* **Consolidation:** present (predominant type: **hepatization** with a **shred sign**)
