# 0045_lung_double-lung-point

The video displays a sequential set of ultrasound frames from a left anterior lung zone.

### B-lines Assessment
In the provided video, multiple hyperechoic vertical artifacts, known as **B-lines (lung rockets)**, are clearly visible. These artifacts originate from the pleural line and extend to the bottom of the ultrasound screen. The B-lines are quite numerous and appear to merge, creating a diffuse, bright appearance that obscures the normal horizontal A-line pattern. This confluent pattern is characteristic of a **ground-glass** subtype of B-lines, which is often associated with conditions like alveolar edema or diffuse interstitial lung disease.

### Consolidation Assessment
The ultrasound image does not show signs of alveolar consolidation. There is no evidence of tissue-like hepatization (where lung tissue appears solid like the liver), no irregular "shred sign" at the lung-pleura interface, and no visible air bronchograms within a consolidated area. The brightness seen below the pleural line is due to the presence of confluent B-line artifacts rather than solid lung tissue. Therefore, **consolidation is absent**.

### Conclusion
- **B-lines**: present, subtype ground-glass
- **Consolidation**: false
