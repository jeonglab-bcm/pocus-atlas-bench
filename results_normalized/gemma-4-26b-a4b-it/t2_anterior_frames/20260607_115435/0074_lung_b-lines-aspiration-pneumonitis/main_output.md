# 0074_lung_b-lines-aspiration-pneumonitis

The sequential ultrasound images show an anterior lung zone.

In the initial frames, there is a prominent, bright, hyperechoic vertical artifact extending from the pleural line to the bottom of the screen. These artifacts are so numerous and confluent that they merge into a diffuse, bright white sheet, which obscures the underlying A-lines. This pattern is characteristic of **ground-glass B-lines**.

In frames 3 and 4, a large, anechoic (black) area is visible adjacent to the bright lung tissue, which is consistent with a pleural effusion. The lung tissue itself remains highly echogenic due to the confluent B-lines.

There is no evidence of lung consolidation, as the echogenic area is due to B-line artifacts rather than tissue-like hepatization, a shredded sign, or air bronchograms.

**Conclusions:**
* **B-lines:** Present, ground-glass subtype.
* **Consolidation:** Absent.
