# 0033_lung_air-bronchograms-in-lll-pneumonia

The sequential frames from the lung ultrasound of the left lung (zone 2) show the following:

**B-lines Assessment:**
Multiple hyperechoic vertical artifacts (B-lines or lung rockets) are clearly visible, arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen. These B-lines are not discrete or well-spaced; instead, they are highly confluent and coalescing, creating a diffuse, bright, "white-out" appearance of the lung parenchyma. This pattern is characteristic of **ground-glass B-lines**, which typically indicate diffuse alveolar or interstitial processes such as pulmonary edema.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
There is no evidence of alveolar consolidation in these frames. The lung parenchyma does not exhibit a tissue-like (hepatized) appearance, there is no visible "shred sign" at the lung-pleura interface, and no air bronchograms are observed. The lung appears to be highly aerated, albeit with significant interstitial/alveolar fluid causing the ground-glass B-line pattern.

**Conclusions:**
* **lung_rockets:** true
* **b_line_subtype:** ground_glass
* **consolidation:** false
* **consolidation_type:** null
