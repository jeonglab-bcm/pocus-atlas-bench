# 0122_lung_jr_pna

Based on the sequential frames of the lung ultrasound:

**B-lines Assessment:**
The images show the presence of hyperechoic vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen, which are **B-lines (lung rockets = true)**. In the initial frames, these B-lines appear as discrete, well-spaced vertical artifacts (septal pattern). As the sequence progresses, these lines become increasingly confluent and coalescing, merging into a diffuse, bright white sheet (ground-glass pattern). Because both discrete (septal) and confluent (ground-glass) patterns are observed at different time points in the clip, the subtype is classified as **mixed**.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
There is no evidence of alveolar consolidation. The lung parenchyma does not exhibit tissue-like hepatization, a shred sign, or air bronchograms. The lung appears to be aerated, with the increased brightness being due to B-line artifacts rather than consolidated tissue (**consolidation = false**).

**Conclusions:**
- **B-lines:** Present, mixed pattern (transitioning from septal to ground-glass).
- **Consolidation:** Absent.
