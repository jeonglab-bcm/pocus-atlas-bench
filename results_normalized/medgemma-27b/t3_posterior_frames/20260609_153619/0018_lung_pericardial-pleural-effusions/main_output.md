# 0018_lung_pericardial-pleural-effusions

The image shows a lung ultrasound frame. The area of interest is the posterior lung zone.

**Observations:**
- There is a clear pleural line visible near the top of the image.
- Below the pleural line, there is a large, anechoic (black) area bounded by the pleural line, the lung line (which is somewhat indistinct), and the rib shadows on the sides. This morphology strongly suggests the **Quad sign**, indicative of pleural effusion.
- The lung parenchyma below the pleural line appears relatively homogeneous and lacks the typical bright, shimmering appearance of well-aerated lung (A-lines). There's no clear evidence of consolidation signs like tissue-like hepatization, shred sign, or air bronchograms.
- The lung line itself does not exhibit clear cyclical movement typical of the sinusoid sign (though this is a static frame).

**Conclusion:**
Based on the presence of the Quad sign, there is evidence of pleural effusion in the posterior lung zone. There are no clear signs of consolidation.
