# 0029_lung_parapneumonic-effusion

**Observation:**

The image shows a lung ultrasound frame from the posterior lung zone (PLAPS).

*   **Pleural Line:** A distinct, bright line is visible, representing the visceral pleura.
*   **Lung Line:** A slightly less bright line is seen below the pleural line, representing the parietal pleura.
*   **A-Lines:** Vertical, thin, bright lines extending from the pleural line towards the probe (bottom of the image). These are artifacts from the skin and ribs and indicate normal aerated lung.
*   **Sliding:** There is movement between the pleural and lung lines.

**Analysis:**

There are no clear signs of effusion (like a quad sign, sinusoid sign, or jellyfish sign) or consolidation (like tissue-like hepatization, shred sign, or air bronchograms). The image predominantly shows A-lines and lung sliding, which are characteristic of normal aerated lung tissue.

**Conclusion:**

Based on the absence of effusion or consolidation signs and the presence of A-lines and lung sliding, the posterior lung zone appears normal. Therefore, the PLAPS pattern is false.
