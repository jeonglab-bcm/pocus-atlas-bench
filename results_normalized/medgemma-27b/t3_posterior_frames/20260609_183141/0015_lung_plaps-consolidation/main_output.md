# 0015_lung_plaps-consolidation

**Analysis:**

The image shows a lung ultrasound scan. The region of interest is the posterior lung zone.

1.  **Effusion Signs:**
    *   **Quad Sign:** There is no clear quadrilateral space formed by pleural line, lung line, and rib shadows.
    *   **Sinusoid Sign:** This is a dynamic sign, not directly visible in a static image, but the lung line appears relatively static.
    *   **Jellyfish Sign:** No floating atelectatic lung within fluid is observed.

2.  **Consolidation Signs:**
    *   **Tissue-like hepatization:** The lung parenchyma in the lower part of the image appears solid and echogenic (brighter than normal lung), with loss of normal aeration artifacts (A-lines). This suggests hepatization.
    *   **Shred Sign:** The border between the echogenic area and the more echolucent area above appears somewhat irregular but not distinctly shredded.
    *   **Air Bronchograms:** There are multiple small, hyperechoic (bright) foci scattered within the echogenic lung parenchyma. These are consistent with air bronchograms within consolidated lung.

3.  **Overall Impression:** The lower portion of the lung field shows echogenic tissue-like lung parenchyma with air bronchograms, characteristic of alveolar consolidation. There is no clear evidence of free pleural effusion (no quad sign, jellyfish sign, or obvious sinusoid sign).

**Conclusion:**

Based on the presence of tissue-like hepatization and air bronchograms, the image shows signs of alveolar consolidation in the posterior lung zone. There are no clear signs of pleural effusion.

**Classification:**

*   **plaps = true**: Yes, there are signs of consolidation.
*   **type = "consolidation"**: Predominantly signs of consolidation (tissue-like appearance, air bronchograms) are present.
