# 0132_lung_jr_empyema

**Observation:**

The image is a B-mode ultrasound scan of the lung. The upper part shows the rib shadow (hyperechoic curved line) and the pleural line (hyperechoic line just beneath the rib). Below the pleural line, there is a dark (anechoic) area extending downwards. Within this dark area, there are some hyperechoic foci scattered throughout, and a distinct area of bright, granular echotexture (tissue-like hepatization) is visible in the lower part of the image, bordered by a shredded deep border. There is also evidence of air bronchograms (small hyperechoic dots) within the hepatized area. The lung markings (A-lines) are present above the pleural line.

**Analysis:**

1.  **Effusion signs:** The presence of the anechoic space below the pleural line, bounded by the rib shadow laterally, could suggest a possible quad sign, although the lower border isn't perfectly defined as a lung line. However, the main feature is not a clear fluid collection like a typical quad sign.
2.  **Consolidation signs:** There is clear evidence of tissue-like hepatization (bright granular texture) in the lower portion of the image, indicating consolidated lung. The shredded deep border is also visible between the consolidated and aerated lung. Air bronchograms are present within the consolidated area.

**Conclusion:**

Based on the presence of tissue-like hepatization, air bronchograms, and a shredded deep border, the image clearly shows signs of alveolar consolidation. While there might be a small amount of fluid present, the predominant finding is consolidation.

*   **plaps = true** (signs of effusion and/or consolidation are present)
*   **type = "consolidation"** (predominantly consolidation signs)
