# 0021_lung_large-pleural-effusion

The image shows a lung ultrasound frame from the posterior lung zone.

**Observations:**
- The image displays a predominantly black (anechoic) area below the pleural line, which is the interface between the lung and the chest wall.
- The lung line, which represents the interface between the aerated lung and the fluid or consolidated tissue, is visible within this dark area.
- The lung line appears somewhat irregular and undulating, suggesting movement within the fluid.
- The rib shadows are visible on the lateral aspects of the image.
- The area below the pleural line and above the lung line forms a roughly quadrilateral shape bounded by the rib shadows laterally. This morphology is consistent with the "quad sign".
- The absence of clear, bright, liver-like texture suggests the absence of significant consolidation. The area is primarily fluid.

**Analysis:**
- The presence of the "quad sign" is a strong indicator of pleural effusion.
- The undulating lung line within the anechoic space might suggest the "jellyfish sign" or the "sinusoid sign" (though dynamic assessment in M-mode would be needed to confirm the latter).
- There are no signs of consolidation, such as hepatization, shred sign, or air bronchograms.

**Conclusion:**
Based on the prominent "quad sign" and the anechoic nature of the space below the pleural line, the image shows signs consistent with pleural effusion. There are no clear signs of consolidation. Therefore, the classification is "plaps = true", "type = effusion".
