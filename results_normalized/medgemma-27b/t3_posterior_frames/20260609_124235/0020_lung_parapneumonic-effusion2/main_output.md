# 0020_lung_parapneumonic-effusion2

**Observations:**

The image shows a lung ultrasound scan of the posterior lung zone.
- The upper portion of the image displays the rib shadow and the pleural line.
- Below the pleural line, there is an area of lung tissue.
- There is a distinct, well-defined, hypoechoic (dark) area below the pleural line, bounded by the pleural line superiorly, the lung line inferiorly, and the rib shadow laterally. This morphology strongly suggests the presence of pleural fluid, forming a "quadrilateral" shape. This is characteristic of the "Quad sign".
- The lung tissue below the fluid collection appears somewhat heterogeneous but does not show classic signs of consolidation (like hepatization, shred sign, or air bronchograms).

**Conclusion:**

The presence of the Quad sign, indicating a collection of fluid in the pleural space bounded by the pleura, lung, and rib, confirms the presence of a pleural effusion. There are no clear signs of alveolar consolidation (hepatization, shred sign, air bronchograms). Therefore, the PLAPS pattern is present, and it is characterized by effusion.

**Classification:**
- plaps = true
- type = "effusion"
