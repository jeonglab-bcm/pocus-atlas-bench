# 0029_lung_parapneumonic-effusion

**Analysis:**

1.  **Location:** The image shows the posterior lung zone.
2.  **Pleural Line:** A distinct pleural line is visible, indicating the visceral pleura.
3.  **Lung Line:** A lung line is present below the pleural line.
4.  **A-lines:** Multiple vertical, hyperechoic lines (A-lines) are extending from the pleural line towards the far wall (depth).
5.  **Fluid:** There is a significant anechoic (black) space between the pleural line and the lung line, bounded laterally by the rib shadow. This space appears to be fluid, characteristic of a pleural effusion. The shape suggests a potential "quad sign".
6.  **Lung Tissue:** The lung tissue below the lung line appears relatively hypoechoic compared to the fluid but not distinctly hepatized or tissue-like. There is no clear evidence of consolidation signs like air bronchograms or shred sign.
7.  **Movement:** The image is static, so dynamic signs like the sinusoid sign cannot be assessed directly. However, the presence of a large anechoic space with a clear lung line suggests fluid.

**Conclusion:**

Based on the presence of a significant anechoic space bounded by a pleural line, lung line, and rib shadow, which is characteristic of pleural fluid, the image shows signs consistent with a pleural effusion. There are no clear signs of consolidation. Therefore, this image represents a PLAPS pattern due to effusion.

**Classification:**

*   **plaps = true** (Presence of effusion signs)
*   **type = "effusion"** (Predominantly fluid collection signs)
