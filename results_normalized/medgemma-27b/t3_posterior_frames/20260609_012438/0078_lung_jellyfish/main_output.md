# 0078_lung_jellyfish

Based on the provided ultrasound image of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point):

**Observations:**
1.  **Pleural Line:** A distinct, bright, horizontal line is visible, indicating the visceral pleura.
2.  **Lung Line:** Below the pleural line, there is a slightly less bright, irregular line representing the parietal pleura/lung interface.
3.  **Fluid Collection:** There appears to be an anechoic (dark) area between the pleural line and the lung line. The shape resembles a quadrilateral, bounded by the rib shadow laterally, the pleural line superiorly, the lung line inferiorly, and the lateral edge of the probe/rib shadow. This morphology is consistent with the **Quad sign**, which is indicative of pleural effusion.
4.  **Lung Sliding:** There is apparent movement of the lung line relative to the pleural line, suggesting lung sliding. This is better assessed in M-mode, but the presence of a dynamic interface is visible.
5.  **Consolidation Signs:** There are no clear signs of consolidation like tissue-like hepatization, shred sign, or air bronchograms. The lung parenchyma below the fluid collection appears relatively normal, although the resolution might be limited.

**Conclusion:**
The presence of the Quad sign (anechoic space bounded by pleural line, lung line, and rib) strongly suggests a pleural effusion. Lung sliding is also apparent. There are no definitive signs of alveolar consolidation. Therefore, the pattern is consistent with pleural effusion.

**Classification:**
- plaps = true (presence of effusion sign)
- type = "effusion" (predominantly effusion signs observed)
