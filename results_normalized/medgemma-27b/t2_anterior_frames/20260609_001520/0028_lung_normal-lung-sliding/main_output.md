# 0028_lung_normal-lung-sliding

**Frame Analysis:**

The image shows a B-mode ultrasound view of the anterior lung zone. The pleural line is visible at the top. Below the pleural line, there are numerous vertical, hyperechoic lines extending towards the bottom of the screen. These lines are relatively close together, and in some areas, they appear to merge, obscuring the A-lines (horizontal reverberations). The lung parenchyma between these lines appears darker, but the lines themselves are confluent in places.

**B-lines Assessment:**

*   **Presence:** Yes, numerous vertical hyperechoic lines extending from the pleural line are present.
*   **Subtype:** Given the confluence and merging of lines, obscuring A-lines in areas, the pattern is best described as **ground_glass**. There might be some septal lines present, but the overall appearance leans towards confluence.

**Consolidation Assessment:**

*   **Presence:** No clear signs of consolidation are evident. The lung parenchyma below the pleural line does not appear uniformly echogenic like the liver (hepatization). There are no visible air bronchograms or a distinct shredded sign. The texture appears more like fluid-filled septa or interstitial thickening rather than solid alveolar filling.

**Conclusion:**

*   **lung_rockets = true** (B-lines are present)
*   **B-lines subtype = ground_glass** (Confluent B-lines obscuring A-lines)
*   **consolidation = false** (No signs of consolidation)
*   **consolidation_type = null** (Since consolidation is false)
