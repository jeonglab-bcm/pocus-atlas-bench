*   **Presence:** There are multiple vertical hyperechoic lines originating from the pleural line and extending towards the bottom of the image. These lines appear to move with respiration (suggesting they are associated with lung sliding, although motion is not captured in a still image).
*   **Subtype:** The B-lines are numerous and appear to merge, creating a more confluent pattern, particularly towards the lower part of the image. There are areas where distinct lines can be seen, but overall, they are quite close together. This pattern resembles ground-glass appearance in some areas and septal pattern in others, suggesting a mixed pattern.

**Consolidation Assessment:**

*   **Presence:** The lung parenchyma appears relatively hypoechoic compared to the ribs, suggesting some degree of fluid or edema within the lung tissue. There is no clear evidence of tissue-like hepatization (solid, echogenic appearance like the liver), shred sign (irregular deep border), or air bronchograms (hyperechoic foci within consolidated tissue). The parenchyma seems more granular or reticular than consolidated.

*   **lung_rockets = true** (B-lines are present)
*   **subtype = mixed** (presence of both septal and ground-glass patterns)
