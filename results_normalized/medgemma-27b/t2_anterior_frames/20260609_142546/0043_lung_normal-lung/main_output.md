# 0043_lung_normal-lung

**Frame Analysis:**

The image shows a lung ultrasound frame from the anterior lung zone. The frame depicts the pleural line at the top and the lung parenchyma below.

**B-lines Assessment:**

*   **Presence:** There are vertical hyperechoic artifacts originating from the pleural line and extending towards the bottom of the image. These lines appear somewhat spaced but are not distinctly separate throughout the entire frame. Some areas show more confluence than others.
*   **Classification:** Given the presence of multiple vertical lines, some discrete and some merging, this pattern is best classified as **mixed**. There are areas that could be considered septal (spaced) and areas where the lines are becoming more confluent (ground glass).

**Consolidation Assessment:**

*   **Presence:** The lung parenchyma below the pleural line appears relatively homogeneous and uniformly bright (hyperechoic) in most areas. There is a loss of the normal A-lines, which are typically seen in well-aerated lung. The texture does not appear distinctly tissue-like (hepatization) nor does it show clear signs of shredding or distinct air bronchograms.
*   **Classification:** The uniform brightness and lack of A-lines, along with the B-line pattern, suggests interstitial or alveolar changes rather than clear consolidation signs like hepatization, shred sign, or air bronchograms. Therefore, **consolidation = false**.

**Conclusion:**

*   **lung_rockets = true** (B-lines are present)
*   **B-line subtype = mixed** (Combination of septal and ground-glass patterns)
*   **consolidation = false** (No definitive signs of consolidation)
*   **consolidation_type = null**
