**Assessment of Pleural Sliding:**

*   **Pleural Line Texture:** The pleural line in both frames shows some texture (shimmering/granular appearance), rather than being perfectly smooth and static. This suggests the *possibility* of sliding.
*   **A-lines:** The presence of A-lines is consistent with both sliding and non-sliding conditions, but their static nature *could* suggest absent sliding if they are perfectly unchanging. However, slight variations might be hard to discern in a still image.
*   **Sub-pleural Texture:** The granular texture below the pleural line in both frames is more suggestive of sliding (seashore sign) than the smooth, static appearance seen with absent sliding.
*   **Comparison between Frames:** The image quality isn't ideal for direct frame-to-frame comparison of speckle patterns, but the pleural line itself seems to maintain a similar level of texture in both frames, rather than changing dramatically (which might happen if the lung point is being scanned across).

**Conclusion:**

Based on the presence of a somewhat granular/shimmering pleural line and the granular texture in the sub-pleural area, it is more likely that **pleural sliding is PRESENT** in these frames. The pleural line is not perfectly smooth and static, nor does it exhibit the classic "barcode" or "stratosphere" appearance characteristic of absent sliding. The A-lines are present but their static nature is not definitively confirmed, and the granular subpleural texture points towards sliding.
