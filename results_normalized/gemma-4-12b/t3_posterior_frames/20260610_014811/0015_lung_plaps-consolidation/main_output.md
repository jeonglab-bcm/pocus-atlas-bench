# 0015_lung_plaps-consolidation

The ultrasound images of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point) show several key features indicative of a pulmonary pathology:

*   **Tissue-like hepatization**: The lung parenchyma in the dependent portion (center and left of the frame) appears solid and echogenic, resembling the texture of liver tissue. This is a hallmark of pulmonary consolidation where the air is replaced by fluid, cells, or debris.
*   **Air bronchograms**: Within this hepatized area, there are multiple punctate and linear hyperechoic (bright) foci. These represent air-filled bronchi surrounded by consolidated lung.
*   **Shred sign**: There is an irregular, "shredded" interface between the consolidated lung and the adjacent aerated lung, which is a classic sign of a consolidated area.
*   **Effusion**: While there is a large hypoechoic area on the right, it does not clearly display a "quad sign" or a "jellyfish sign" in these frames, suggesting it is primarily consolidated lung tissue rather than a large free pleural effusion.

Based on these observations:
**plaps = true**
**type = "consolidation"**
