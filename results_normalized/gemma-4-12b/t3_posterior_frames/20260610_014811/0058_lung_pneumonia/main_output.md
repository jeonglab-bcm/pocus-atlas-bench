# 0058_lung_pneumonia

The ultrasound sequence shows several key features of a **PLAPS (Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome)** pattern in the posterior lung zone:

*   **Effusion signs**: There is a prominent **quad sign** characterized by a large anechoic (black) space bounded by the pleural line superiorly and the consolidated lung tissue inferiorly, indicating a significant pleural effusion.
*   **Consolidation signs**: The lung parenchyma below the fluid appears solid and echogenic, exhibiting **tissue-like hepatization**. The border between the aerated/fluid area and the consolidated lung is irregular, representing a **shred sign**. Furthermore, multiple bright, punctate hyperechoic foci are clearly visible within the consolidated area, which are classic **air bronchograms**.

Given the clear presence of both significant pleural fluid and consolidated lung tissue with air bronchograms, the diagnosis is a mixed PLAPS pattern.

**Conclusion:**
plaps = true
type = "both"
