In the provided ultrasound images of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point), the following observations can be made:

*   **Tissue-like hepatization**: The posterior lung parenchyma exhibits a solid, echogenic appearance that mimics the texture of the liver, indicating significant alveolar consolidation.
*   **Shred sign**: There is a clearly visible, irregular, and "shredded" border between the consolidated (hepatized) lung tissue and the adjacent aerated lung (the darker area to the left).
*   **Jellyfish sign**: The consolidated lung segment appears to be "floating" or undulating within a surrounding hypoechoic space, which is characteristic of a pleural effusion.

Because both signs of consolidation (hepatization, shred sign) and signs of pleural effusion (jellyfish sign) are present, the image demonstrates a clear PLAPS pattern.
