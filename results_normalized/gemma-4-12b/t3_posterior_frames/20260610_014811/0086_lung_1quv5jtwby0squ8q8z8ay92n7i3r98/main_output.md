The ultrasound image of the posterior lung zone demonstrates a clear **PLAPS** (Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome) pattern.

Specifically, the following features are observed:
*   **Jellyfish sign:** There is a large hypoechoic (dark) area containing irregular, undulating hyperechoic structures that resemble a floating jellyfish, indicating atelectatic lung tissue within a pleural effusion.
*   **Tissue-like hepatization:** The surrounding lung parenchyma is highly echogenic and appears solid, mimicking the texture of liver tissue (hepatized), which is a hallmark of lung consolidation.
*   **Air bronchograms:** Small punctate hyperechoic foci can be seen within the hepatized area, representing air-filled bronchi.
*   **Shred sign:** The borders between the consolidated and aerated zones are irregular and "shredded."

Because both significant effusion and consolidation signs are present, the classification is as follows:

**plaps = true**
**type = "both"**
