# 0098_lung_blunt-trauma-effusion

The ultrasound images of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point) demonstrate several characteristic signs of both pleural effusion and alveolar consolidation. 

There is a prominent anechoic (black) area representing a pleural effusion. Within this fluid collection, a piece of lung tissue is visible that appears solid and echogenic, resembling liver texture, which is a classic example of **tissue-like hepatization**. This consolidated lung has an irregular, "ragged" border where it meets other areas, consistent with a **shred sign**. Furthermore, the appearance of this consolidated tissue "floating" within the surrounding fluid collection is highly characteristic of the **jellyfish sign**. Some internal hyperechoic spots within the hepatized area may also represent **air bronchograms**.

Conclusion:
**plaps = true**
**type = "both"**
