# 0007_lung_pleural-effusion-with-compressed-lung-and-spine-sign

The ultrasound images show a classic PLAPS (Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome) pattern in the posterior lung zone.

**Observations:**
- **Tissue-like hepatization**: The lung parenchyma appears solid and echogenic, closely resembling the texture of the liver. This is a hallmark sign of alveolar consolidation.
- **Air bronchograms**: Small, bright, punctate, and linear hyperechoic foci are visible within the consolidated (hepatized) lung tissue, representing air-filled bronchi.
- **Effusion**: There is a large, anechoic (black) area adjacent to the consolidated lung, indicating a significant pleural effusion. The consolidated lung appears to be "floating" within this fluid, which is characteristic of a jellyfish sign.
- **Shred sign**: An irregular, shredded border can be seen between the consolidated lung tissue and the surrounding areas.

Based on these findings, both consolidation and effusion are clearly present.

**Conclusion:**
plaps = true
type = "both"
