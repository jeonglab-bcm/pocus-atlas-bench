The ultrasound images of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point) show several characteristic findings:

1.  **Effusion signs**: There is a large anechoic (black) space posterior to the lung, which is indicative of a **pleural effusion**. The consolidated lung tissue appears to be floating within this fluid, which can be described as a **jellyfish sign**.
2.  **Consolidation signs**: The lung parenchyma in this region has lost its normal aeration and appears solid and echogenic, very similar to the texture of the liver. This is a classic example of **tissue-like hepatization**. 
3.  **Boundary signs**: There is an irregular, jagged border between the consolidated lung and the surrounding structures, consistent with a **shred sign**. 
4.  **Air bronchograms**: Small hyperechoic (bright) punctate foci are visible within the hepatized lung, likely representing **air bronchograms**.

Because both significant pleural effusion and lung consolidation are clearly visible in the posterior zone, the findings are classified as follows:

**plaps = true**
**type = both**
