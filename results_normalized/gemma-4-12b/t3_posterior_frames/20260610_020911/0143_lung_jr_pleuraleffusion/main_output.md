# 0143_lung_jr_pleuraleffusion

The sequence of lung ultrasound (LUS) frames shows a clear PLAPS (Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome) pattern in the posterior lung zone.

**Observations:**
- **Pleural Effusion:** There is a prominent anechoic (black) space situated immediately beneath the pleural line. This fluid collection occupies a significant portion of the ultrasound field, characteristic of a pleural effusion.
- **Alveolar Consolidation:** Inferior to the fluid collection, the lung parenchyma appears dense, solid, and echogenic, resembling liver tissue. This is the **tissue-like hepatization** sign.
- **Shredded Sign:** The interface between the fluid/consolidated area and the remaining lung tissue is irregular and jagged, which is consistent with a shredded sign.

Since both a clear fluid collection (effusion) and solid-appearing lung tissue (consolidation) are visible in the dependent zone, this is a classic example of a PLAPS pattern.

**Conclusion:**
- **plaps = true**
- **type = "both"**
