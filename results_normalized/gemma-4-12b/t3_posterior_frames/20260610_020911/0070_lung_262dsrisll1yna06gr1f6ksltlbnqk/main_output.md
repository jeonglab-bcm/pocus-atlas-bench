In these lung ultrasound (LUS) frames, there are clear signs of **Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome (PLAPS)** in the posterior/lateral lung zone.

Specifically, I observe:
- **Tissue-like hepatization**: The lung parenchyma appears solid and echogenic, resembling the texture of the liver, which is a hallmark of alveolar consolidation.
- **Shred sign**: There is an irregular, "shredded" appearance at the deep border between the consolidated lung and the adjacent aerated regions.
- **Air bronchograms**: Punctate hyperechoic (bright) foci are visible within the hepatized lung tissue, representing air-filled bronchi.
- **Pleural effusion signs**: A large hypoechoic/anechoic area is visible, suggesting a significant fluid collection (effusion). Within this fluid, some of the consolidated lung appears to be "floating," which is characteristic of the **jellyfish sign**.

Because both consolidated lung (hepatization, shred sign, air bronchograms) and pleural fluid (anechoic area, jellyfish sign) are clearly visible, the classification is:

**plaps = true**
