In the provided lung ultrasound (LUS) sequence, several characteristic signs of the **PLAPS (Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome)** pattern are visible in the posterior/lateral lung zone:

1.  **Tissue-like hepatization**: The lung parenchyma above the fluid collection appears solid and echogenic, resembling the texture of liver tissue. It lacks the normal aeration artifacts (such as A-lines or lung sliding).
2.  **Shred sign**: There is a distinct, irregular, and "shredded" border between the consolidated (hepatized) lung and the deeper, aerated/fluid-filled region.
3.  **Pleural Effusion**: A large, hypoechoic (dark) area is present at the bottom of the image, characteristic of a pleural effusion.
4.  **Jellyfish sign**: The consolidated lung tissue appears to be floating and undulating within the surrounding pleural fluid, creating a "jellyfish" appearance, which is a hallmark of PLAPS.

Because both hepatized lung (consolidation) and a significant fluid collection (effusion) are clearly demonstrated, the classification is as follows:

**plaps = true**
**type = "both"**
