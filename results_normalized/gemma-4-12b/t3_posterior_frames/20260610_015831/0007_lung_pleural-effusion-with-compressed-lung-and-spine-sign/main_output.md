# 0007_lung_pleural-effusion-with-compressed-lung-and-spine-sign

The ultrasound images of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point) show clear signs of both consolidation and pleural effusion:

1.  **Tissue-like hepatization**: There is a large, solid-appearing area of lung parenchyma that has lost its normal aeration and has a texture similar to liver tissue.
2.  **Air bronchograms**: Within the hepatized lung, multiple small, punctate, hyperechoic (bright) foci are visible, representing air within the bronchi.
3.  **Shred sign**: The border between the consolidated lung and the surrounding aerated or fluid-filled space is irregular and "shredded."
4.  **Pleural Effusion**: A large anechoic (black) space is visible, particularly prominent in the second frame, indicating a significant pleural effusion.
5.  **Jellyfish sign**: In some frames, the consolidated lung appears to be floating or undulating within the surrounding fluid collection.

Because both hepatized consolidation (with air bronchograms and a shred sign) and a large pleural effusion are clearly visible, the findings are classified as follows:

**plaps = true**
**type = "both"**
