# 0004_lung_pleural-effusion-with-hiatal-hernia

In these lung ultrasound (LUS) frames of the posterior lung zone, several key signs of a PLAPS (Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome) pattern are clearly visible:

*   **Effusion signs:** There is a large, anechoic (black) space between the pleural line and the lung tissue, representing a significant pleural effusion. This space is bounded by the pleural line superiorly and the lung line inferiorly, creating a clear **quad sign**. Additionally, the consolidated lung tissue appears to "float" within this fluid collection, demonstrating the **jellyfish sign**.
*   **Consolidation signs:** The lung parenchyma deep to the pleural line has lost its normal aerated appearance and instead shows **tissue-like hepatization**, resembling the texture of the liver. Within this consolidated area, multiple bright (hyperechoic) linear and punctate structures are visible, which are **air bronchograms**. The border between the aerated and consolidated regions is irregular, indicative of a **shred sign**.

Since both significant pleural effusion and alveolar consolidation are present, the pattern is classified as follows:

**plaps = true**
**type = "both"**
