# 0030_lung_parapneumonic-effusion-with-b-lines

The ultrasound frames of the posterior lung zone show several characteristic signs of alveolar consolidation:

*   **Tissue-like hepatization**: The lung parenchyma in the dependent (bottom right) portion of the image appears solid and echogenic, similar in texture to liver tissue, rather than the normal aerated lung.
*   **Shred sign**: There is a visible, irregular, and "shredded" border between the consolidated lung and the surrounding aerated lung.
*   **Air bronchograms**: Small, punctate hyperechoic (bright) foci are visible within the consolidated area, representing air-filled bronchi trapped in consolidated tissue.

No clear signs of a large pleural effusion, such as a distinct quad sign or a floating jellyfish sign, are predominantly visible; the main finding is consolidated lung tissue.

plaps = true
type = "consolidation"
