# 0126_lung_jr_loculated-pleural-effusion

In these sequential images from a lung ultrasound (LUS) of the posterior lung zone, there is clear evidence of a PLAPS pattern:

1.  **Pleural Effusion**: A large, anechoic (black) area is visible at the bottom of the frame, characteristic of a pleural effusion. Within this fluid collection, the lung tissue appears to be floating and undulating, which is the **jellyfish sign**.
2.  **Alveolar Consolidation**: The lung parenchyma above the fluid collection is not aerated; it appears solid and echogenic, resembling liver tissue (**tissue-like hepatization**). The border between this consolidated lung and the surrounding fluid/aerated areas is irregular and fragmented, consistent with a **shred sign**.

Because both significant pleural effusion and lung consolidation are clearly visible in the posterior zone, the finding is classified as both.

**Conclusion:**
plaps = true
type = "both"
