# 0073_lung_2017-10-9-dove-in-pleural-fluid

In this posterior lung zone ultrasound, a large anechoic (black) space is visible, indicating a significant pleural effusion. Within this fluid collection, there is a prominent, irregularly shaped, and echogenic (white) structure that appears to be "floating" or undulating. This is the classic **jellyfish sign**, which represents a piece of consolidated/atelectatic lung tissue within the effusion. Because there is a clear presence of both free fluid (effusion) and consolidated lung tissue, this represents a typical PLAPS (Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome) pattern.

**Conclusion:**
plaps = true
type = "both"
