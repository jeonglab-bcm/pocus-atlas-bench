# 0020_lung_parapneumonic-effusion2

In the provided ultrasound images of the posterior lung zone, several signs of PLAPS (Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome) are visible:

1.  **Tissue-like hepatization**: The lung parenchyma in the dependent (posterior/lateral) zone appears solid and echogenic, closely resembling the liver texture seen on the opposite side of the pleural line.
2.  **Shred sign**: There is a clearly visible, irregular, and jagged interface between the consolidated lung tissue and the adjacent aerated lung.
3.  **Effusion signs**: A large anechoic (black) space is present, indicating a significant pleural effusion. The presence of consolidated lung tissue within this fluid area also suggests a jellyfish-like appearance.

Because both prominent consolidation (hepatization and shred sign) and pleural effusion are clearly visible, the classification is as follows:

plaps = true
type = "both"
