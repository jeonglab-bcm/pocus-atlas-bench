# 0007_lung_pleural-effusion-with-compressed-lung-and-spine-sign

The ultrasound images of the posterior lung zone (PLAPS point) demonstrate several key features:

1.  **Tissue-like hepatization**: A large portion of the image (on the right and center) shows a solid, homogeneous, and echogenic organ that resembles liver tissue. In the context of a lung ultrasound, this indicates severe alveolar consolidation, where the lung parenchyma has become solid and lost its normal aeration.
2.  **Pleural effusion**: There is a large, well-defined anechoic (black) space on the left side of the image, which represents a significant pleural effusion.
3.  **Shred sign**: The border between the consolidated (hepatized) lung and the fluid-filled space is irregular and "shredded" in several frames, particularly visible in the first, third, and sixth images.

Because both signs of consolidation (hepatization, shred sign) and signs of effusion are clearly present, the findings meet the criteria for a PLAPS pattern.

**Conclusion:**
**plaps = true**
**type = "both"**
