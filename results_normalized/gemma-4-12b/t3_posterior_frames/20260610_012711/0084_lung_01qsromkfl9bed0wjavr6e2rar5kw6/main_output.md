The ultrasound images show features characteristic of a posterior lung zone (PLAPS) syndrome. 

1.  **Effusion signs:** There is a large collection of anechoic (black) fluid in the posterior/lateral zone. Within this fluid, there is a clearly visible **jellyfish sign**—a floating, undulating piece of consolidated lung tissue that moves with respiration. A **quad sign** can also be appreciated where the fluid is bounded by the pleural line, lung line, and rib shadows.
2.  **Consolidation signs:** The lung parenchyma in the posterior zone exhibits **tissue-like hepatization**, appearing solid and echogenic similar to liver tissue. There is a discernible **shred sign** at the irregular border between the consolidated/fluid-filled area and the adjacent aerated lung. Small punctate hyperechoic foci, likely **air bronchograms**, can be seen within the hepatized lung.

Because both pleural effusion (jellyfish sign) and alveolar consolidation (hepatization, shred sign) are clearly present, the finding is classified as follows:

**plaps = true**
**type = "both"**
