# 0015_lung_plaps-consolidation

In these lung ultrasound (LUS) images of the posterior lung zone, the following observations can be made:

*   **Tissue-like hepatization**: The lung parenchyma directly beneath the pleural line appears solid and echogenic, closely resembling the texture of liver tissue. This is a classic sign of alveolar consolidation where air is replaced by fluid, cells, or tissue.
*   **Air bronchograms**: There are several punctate and linear hyperechoic foci within the hepatized area, representing air-filled bronchi.
*   **Absence of Effusion Signs**: There is no clear anechoic (black) quadrilateral space (quad sign), no floating "jellyfish" appearance, and no large fluid collection is visible. The hepatized tissue appears to be in direct contact with the pleural line.

Based on these findings, the lung shows clear signs of consolidation without significant pleural effusion.

**Conclusion:**
plaps = true
type = "consolidation"
