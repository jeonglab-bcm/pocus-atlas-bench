**Signs of sliding:**

- **Comet-tail artifacts or B-lines:** Yes, multiple B-lines are visible originating from the pleural line.
- **Seashore sign (on M-mode - but this is B-mode):** The granular texture below the pleural line is consistent with the "seashore" appearance in B-mode, which is a sign of sliding.
- **Speckle pattern below the pleural line differs between consecutive frames:** Comparing frames, the speckle pattern in the sub-pleural region changes slightly, suggesting movement of the visceral pleura against the parietal pleura.

**Signs of absent sliding:**
- **Smooth, sharply defined, static pleural line:** It doesn't look perfectly static; there's a slight blurriness/shimmering.
- **Stratosphere / barcode sign:** This is an M-mode finding, and we only have B-mode.
- **Static A-lines:** There aren't prominent horizontal A-lines here; it's more of a B-line/shimmering field.
- **No variation in sub-pleural speckle:** There is some variation.

**Conclusion:**
Pleural sliding is **present**.

**Step-by-step plan:**
1.  Analyze each frame for signs of movement or texture at the pleural line.
2.  Identify B-lines and their origin.
3.  Evaluate the "seashore" appearance (granular texture below the pleural line).
4.  Compare consecutive frames for changes in the speckle pattern.
5.  Synthesize observations to conclude whether sliding is present, absent, or both (lung point).
