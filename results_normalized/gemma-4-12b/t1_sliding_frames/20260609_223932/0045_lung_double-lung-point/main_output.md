# 0045_lung_double-lung-point

Based on a frame-by-frame analysis of the lung ultrasound (LUS) video clip, here is the observation:

### **Frame-by-Frame Observations:**
*   **Frames 1-8:** Throughout the entire clip, the **pleural line** (the bright horizontal interface) displays a "shimmering" or granular appearance rather than being a sharp, static white line. 
*   **Speckle Pattern:** Most importantly, the **speckle pattern below the pleural line** (the area representing the lung parenchyma) shows continuous, granular variation between consecutive frames. This movement indicates that the visceral and parietal pleura are sliding against each other.
*   **Artifacts:** There are vertical hyperechoic artifacts (B-lines) originating from the pleural line, which are often associated with a normal sliding surface in a well-inflated lung.

### **Signs Summary:**
*   **Sliding PRESENT:** Shimmering pleural line, granular speckle pattern below the line (seashore sign), and B-lines.
*   **Sliding ABSENT:** None (the line is not static, and there is no "barcode" appearance).
*   **Both (Lung Point):** Not observed in this specific field of view; the entire visible segment appears to be sliding.

### **Conclusion:**
**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**
