# 0041_lung_2018-1-16-normal-lung-slide

Based on the analysis of the four sequential frames from the lung ultrasound (LUS) video clip, here is the step-by-step evaluation:

### **Observations Frame-by-Frame:**
*   **Pleural Line Texture:** Across all frames, the pleural line (the bright horizontal interface) appears textured and "shimmery" rather than a smooth, static line.
*   **B-lines/Comet-tails:** There are prominent, bright, vertical artifacts (B-lines) originating from the pleural line into the sub-pleural space.
*   **Speckle Variation:** By comparing the first frame (top-left) to the fourth frame (bottom-right), there is a noticeable change in the speckle pattern in the granular area just below the pleural line. This shifting "grain" indicates that the pleural surface is moving relative to the chest wall.
*   **Pattern Type:** The area below the pleural line has a "sandy," granular appearance (consistent with a seashore sign), rather than a uniform, horizontal "barcode" (stratosphere sign).

### **Conclusion:**
**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**

The combination of the shimmering pleural line, the presence of B-lines, and the distinct variation in the sub-pleural speckle pattern between consecutive frames confirms that the lung is sliding against the chest wall.
