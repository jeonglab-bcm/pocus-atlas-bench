# 0133_lung_jr_subpleuralconsolidation

Based on the analysis of the sequential lung ultrasound (LUS) frames:

**Frame-by-Frame Observation:**
*   **Frames 1, 3, 5, 6, and 10:** These frames clearly show a large area of lung consolidation. The lung parenchyma in this region has a solid, echogenic appearance similar to liver tissue, which is known as **hepatization**.
*   **Frames 2, 4, 7, and 8:** These frames highlight the interface between the consolidated and aerated lung. An irregular, "shredded" border is visible, which is a classic **shred sign**. Additionally, some punctate hyperechoic foci (small bright spots) can be seen within the consolidated area, representing **air bronchograms**.
*   **Frame 9:** This frame provides a particularly clear view of the **shred sign** at the superior margin of the consolidation.
*   Throughout the clip, the predominant finding is consolidation, which largely obscures any typical B-line artifacts.

**Assessment Conclusions:**

*   **B-lines Assessment:**
    *   **lung_rockets = false** (The primary finding is consolidation rather than discrete or confluent B-lines).

*   **Consolidation Assessment:**
    *   **consolidation = true** (Evidence of hepatization, shred sign, and air bronchograms is present).
    *   **consolidation_type = "shred_sign"** (While hepatization and air bronchograms are present, the irregular, shredded border is a very prominent and characteristic feature in these images).
