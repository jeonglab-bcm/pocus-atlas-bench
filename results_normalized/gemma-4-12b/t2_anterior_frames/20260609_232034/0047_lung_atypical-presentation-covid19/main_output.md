# 0047_lung_atypical-presentation-covid19

Based on the analysis of the provided lung ultrasound (LUS) video clip of the anterior lung zone:

### **B-lines Assessment**
The ultrasound shows multiple hyperechoic vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line. These artifacts are not discrete; rather, they are **coalescing** to form a diffuse, bright white sheet that completely obscures the normal A-lines (horizontal reverberation artifacts). This pattern is characteristic of a "white lung" appearance.
*   **lung_rockets**: true
*   **B-line_subtype**: ground_glass

### **Consolidation Assessment**
While the lung parenchyma appears very bright and lacks normal aeration artifacts, it does not exhibit the characteristic granular, solid texture of liver tissue (hepatization). There is no clear "shred sign" (an irregular, shredded border) or visible air bronchograms (punctate hyperechoic foci within a consolidated area). The appearance is consistent with severe interstitial/alveolar edema represented by dense ground-glass opacification rather than true alveolar consolidation.
*   **consolidation**: false
*   **consolidation_type**: null

**Summary:**
The clip demonstrates a **ground-glass** pattern characterized by coalescing B-lines that create a diffuse white sheet, indicating significant pulmonary edema or diffuse interstitial disease without evidence of consolidation.
