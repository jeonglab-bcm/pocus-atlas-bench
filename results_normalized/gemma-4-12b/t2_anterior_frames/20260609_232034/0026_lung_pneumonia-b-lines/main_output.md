# 0026_lung_pneumonia-b-lines

In these lung ultrasound (LUS) frames of an anterior zone, the following observations can be made:

### Frame-by-Frame Analysis
Across all frames in the sequence, there is a prominent anechoic (black) area at the top, which is highly suggestive of a **pleural effusion**. Directly below this fluid collection, a very thick, hyperechoic vertical artifact is visible. This artifact originates from the pleural surface and extends all the way to the bottom of the screen without fading. The B-line is notably wide and confluent, merging into a diffuse white sheet that obscures any underlying lung architecture or A-lines. There are no signs of solid tissue texture (hepatization), irregular shredded borders, or punctate air bronchograms.

### B-lines Assessment
*   **B-lines present (lung_rockets = true)**: Yes, there is a prominent vertical artifact.
*   **Subtype**: **ground_glass**. The B-lines are confluent and coalesced, creating a diffuse white sheet that obscures the underlying lung parenchyma. This pattern is typically associated with pulmonary edema or diffuse interstitial disease.

### Consolidation Assessment
*   **Consolidation present (consolidation = false)**: No clear signs of alveolar consolidation are observed. The lung does not exhibit a solid, tissue-like appearance (hepatization), nor are there shredded borders or air bronchograms. The white appearance is due to the B-line artifact rather than solid consolidation.
*   **Consolidation type**: **null**.

**Conclusions:**
