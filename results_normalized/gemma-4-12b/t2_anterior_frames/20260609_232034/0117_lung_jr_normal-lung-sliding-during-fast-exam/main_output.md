# 0117_lung_jr_normal-lung-sliding-during-fast-exam

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames from the lung ultrasound (LUS) video clip:

**Observations:**
The ultrasound images show a high density of vertical hyperechoic artifacts (B-lines) arising from the pleural line. These B-lines are not discrete or well-spaced; instead, they are confluent, merging together to create a diffuse "white lung" appearance that completely obscures the horizontal A-lines. 

Furthermore, there is a prominent area of pulmonary consolidation in the anterior zone. This region exhibits a solid, echogenic texture similar to liver tissue (hepatization). There is also an irregular, shredded border visible at the inferior margin of this consolidated area, characteristic of a "shred sign."

**Conclusions:**

**B-lines Assessment:**
*   **B-lines present (lung_rockets = true)**
*   **B-line subtype: ground_glass** (due to the confluent, coalescing nature of the B-lines creating a diffuse white sheet).

**Consolidation Assessment:**
*   **Consolidation present (consolidation = true)**
*   **Consolidation type: hepatization** (The primary observation is the solid, tissue-like appearance of the consolidated lung).
