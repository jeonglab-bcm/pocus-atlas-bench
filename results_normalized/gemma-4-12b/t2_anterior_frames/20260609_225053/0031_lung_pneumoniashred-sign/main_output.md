# 0031_lung_pneumoniashred-sign

The sequence of lung ultrasound (LUS) images shows an anterior lung zone with prominent pathological findings.

### Frame-by-Frame Analysis:
*   **Frames 1-3:** These images show a "white lung" appearance. Numerous hyperechoic vertical artifacts (B-lines) are present. They are confluent and merge together to form a diffuse, bright white sheet that obscures the horizontal A-lines. This is characteristic of a ground-glass pattern.
*   **Frames 4-8:** A distinct area of consolidation becomes clearly visible at the top of the ultrasound field (closest to the probe). This region has a solid, echogenic texture similar to liver tissue, known as **hepatization**. The border between this consolidated area and the aerated lung below is irregular and jagged, which is the **shred sign**.

### B-lines Assessment:
*   **B-lines present (lung_rockets = true)**: The images show multiple vertical hyperechoic artifacts.
*   **Subtype**: **ground_glass**. The B-lines are confluent and coalescing into a diffuse white sheet, obscuring A-lines.

### Consolidation Assessment:
*   **Consolidation present (consolidation = true)**: There is a clear area of alveolar consolidation.
*   **Consolidation type**: **hepatization** and **shred_sign**. The lung parenchyma in the superior portion appears solid and tissue-like (hepatized), with a clearly visible irregular (shredded) border at its deep margin.

**Conclusion:**
The ultrasound demonstrates **ground-glass B-lines** and a clear region of **hepatized consolidation** with a prominent **shred sign**.
