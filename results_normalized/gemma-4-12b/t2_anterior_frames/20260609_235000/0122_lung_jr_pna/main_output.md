# 0122_lung_jr_pna

In these lung ultrasound (LUS) images of the anterior lung zone, the following features are observed:

**B-lines Assessment:**
- **Presence:** B-lines (lung rockets) are clearly present. They appear as multiple, hyperechoic, vertical artifacts originating from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen without fading.
- **Subtype:** The B-lines are notably confluent in several areas, merging into a diffuse white appearance that obscures the normal A-lines. This is characteristic of a **ground-glass** pattern, indicating significant alveolar edema or diffuse interstitial disease. Because some discrete lines are also visible, a **mixed** pattern could also be argued, but the overall appearance is dominated by confluence.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
- **Presence:** Alveolar consolidation is clearly present in the anterior zone.
- **Signs:** 
    - The lung parenchyma has a solid, echogenic appearance that mimics the texture of liver tissue, known as **tissue-like hepatization**.
    - Within this hepatized area, there are numerous small, punctate hyperechoic foci, which represent **air bronchograms**.
- **Classification:** The lung is consolidated. The predominant type is **hepatization**, accompanied by prominent air bronchograms.

**Conclusion:**
The ultrasound images show a consolidated lung with a **ground-glass** B-line pattern (mixed) and **hepatization** with **air bronchograms**, which are classic signs of severe lung consolidation.
