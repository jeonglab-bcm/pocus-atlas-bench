# 0027_lung_subpleural-consolidation

Based on the sequential frames of the lung ultrasound (LUS) video, here is the analysis:

### Frame-by-Frame Analysis
The video clip shows an anterior lung zone with a prominent, hypoechoic (dark) area immediately beneath the pleural line. This area represents a region of consolidated lung parenchyma. The border between this consolidated area and the surrounding lung tissue is notably irregular and "shredded," which is a classic ultrasound finding in pulmonary consolidation. No clear hyperechoic vertical artifacts (B-lines) are observed in any of the frames.

### B-lines Assessment
- **B-lines absent (lung_rockets = false)**: The images do not show hyperechoic vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen. The presence of consolidation obscures the normal aeration patterns required to produce B-lines.

### Consolidation Assessment
- **Tissue-like hepatization**: While the area is consolidated, it is very hypoechoic, more resembling a fluid-filled or dense solid area than the typical echogenicity of a hepatized liver.
- **Shred sign**: A clear, irregular, "shredded" border is visible at the deep margin of the consolidated area, where it meets the aerated lung.
- **Air bronchograms**: There are no distinct punctate or linear hyperechoic foci visible within the consolidated region.
- **Conclusion**: **consolidation = true**
- **consolidation_type = "shred_sign"**: The irregular, shredded appearance of the border is the most prominent consolidation sign in these frames.
