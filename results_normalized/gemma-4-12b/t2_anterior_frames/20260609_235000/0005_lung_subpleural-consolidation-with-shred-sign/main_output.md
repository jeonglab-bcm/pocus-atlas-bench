# 0005_lung_subpleural-consolidation-with-shred-sign

The lung ultrasound (LUS) images of the anterior lung zone show a clear pleural line from which numerous vertical, hyperechoic artifacts originate. These artifacts are dense, confluent, and merge into a diffuse white sheet that obscures the horizontal A-lines, extending all the way to the bottom of the screen. This appearance is characteristic of "ground glass" B-lines, which typically indicate alveolar edema or diffuse interstitial disease. There is no evidence of tissue-like hepatization, a shredded border, or air bronchograms, indicating the absence of alveolar consolidation in this area.
