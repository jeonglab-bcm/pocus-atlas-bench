Based on the analysis of the sequential ultrasound frames, here is the assessment:

### Frame-by-Frame Analysis:
*   **Frame 1:** Shows dense, confluent vertical artifacts (B-lines) that merge to create a "white lung" appearance. A solid, hyperechoic area is visible at the top, indicating early consolidation.
*   **Frame 2:** Continues to show prominent ground-glass B-lines. The solid area at the top appears more textured, resembling hepatized lung.

### Final Assessment:

**B-lines Assessment:**
- **lung_rockets = true**: There are prominent vertical artifacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen.
- **B-line subtype = ground_glass**: The B-lines are highly confluent, merging into a diffuse white sheet that obscures normal A-lines, characteristic of alveolar edema or diffuse interstitial disease.

**Consolidation Assessment:**
- **consolidation = true**: A large portion of the lung parenchyma appears solid and lacks normal aeration.
- **consolidation_type = hepatization**: The consolidated area has a solid, echogenic texture that strongly resembles liver tissue (hepatization).

**Summary:** The ultrasound demonstrates a combination of **ground-glass B-lines** and **hepatized consolidation** in the anterior lung zone.
