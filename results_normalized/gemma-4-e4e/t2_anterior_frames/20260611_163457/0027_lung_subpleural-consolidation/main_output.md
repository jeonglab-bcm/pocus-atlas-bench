# 0027_lung_subpleural-consolidation

## B-lines Assessment

**Observation:** In all frames, there are vertical, hyperechoic artifacts originating from the pleural line. These artifacts are not discrete but instead merge together, forming a confluent, diffuse white sheet that extends downward, obscuring the normal lung architecture.

**Classification:**
*   **lung_rockets = true**
*   **Type:** ground_glass

## Consolidation Assessment

**Observation:** The lung parenchyma appears extremely dense, solid, and highly echogenic (bright white) throughout the visualized zone. This density resembles the echogenicity of liver tissue (hepatization), with a marked loss of normal lung aeration. There is no clearly discernible irregular border (shred sign) between consolidated and aerated tissue, and while the density is high, classic air bronchograms are not distinctly identifiable within the confluent opacity.

**Classification:**
*   **consolidation = true**
*   **consolidation_type = "hepatization"**

## Conclusion

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames, the lung ultrasound demonstrates a **ground-glass pattern** of B-lines (confluent vertical artifacts) and **alveolar consolidation**, characterized specifically by a **hepatization** pattern (solid, dense, liver-like echogenicity).
