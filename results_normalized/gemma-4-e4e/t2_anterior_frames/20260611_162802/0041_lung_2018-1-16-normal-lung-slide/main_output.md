## B-lines Assessment

**Assessment:**
B-lines are clearly present throughout the analyzed frames. They appear as numerous, highly hyperechoic (bright white) vertical artifacts originating from the pleural line and extending downwards. In some regions, the B-lines are distinct and well-spaced (septal pattern), while in other areas, they merge and become confluent, forming a dense white sheet (ground glass pattern).

**Conclusion:**
*   **lung_rockets = true**
*   **B-line subtype = "mixed"**

## Consolidation Assessment

**Assessment:**
There are no discernible features of alveolar consolidation. The overall appearance of the lung parenchyma is dominated by diffuse interstitial edema (indicated by the B-lines), not by a uniform, solid, tissue-like density (hepatization). No irregular, shredded boundaries (shred sign) or air-filled bronchi within a dense background (air bronchograms) are observed.

**Conclusion:**
*   **consolidation = false**

***

**Summary of Observations:**
The lung ultrasound demonstrates extensive interstitial disease. The pattern is characterized by a **mixed B-line pattern**, showing both discrete (septal) and coalescing (ground glass) vertical artifacts. There is no evidence of alveolar consolidation. This pattern is highly suggestive of diffuse interstitial pulmonary edema.
