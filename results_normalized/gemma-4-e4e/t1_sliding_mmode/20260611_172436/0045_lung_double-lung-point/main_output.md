# 0045_lung_double-lung-point

Based on the analysis of the sequential frames from the lung ultrasound, here is the detailed observation:

***

### Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frame 1:**
*   **Observation:** The pleural line (the bright horizontal interface at the top) is visible. Below this line, the sub-pleural area exhibits a granular, speckled pattern. The interface itself appears textured, not perfectly smooth or static.
*   **Sign:** Shimmer/Speckle pattern (suggestive of movement).

**Frame 2:**
*   **Observation:** The granular, speckled pattern continues below the pleural line. The interface remains dynamic and textured. The sub-pleural pattern does not appear static.
*   **Sign:** Shimmer/Speckle pattern (consistent with movement).

**Frame 3:**
*   **Observation:** The characteristic "shimmer" or highly textured, granular pattern is visible on the pleural line and extending into the sub-pleural space.
*   **Sign:** Shimmer/Speckle pattern.

**Frame 4:**
*   **Observation:** The pattern observed in the previous frames is maintained. The sub-pleural area is filled with a dynamic, granular texture, and there is no static, sharp interface.
*   **Sign:** Shimmer/Speckle pattern (consistent with movement).

***

### Conclusion

**Assessment:** Pleural sliding is **PRESENT**.

**Justification:**
Across all four frames, the ultrasound displays the characteristic features of lung sliding. Specifically, the sub-pleural region shows a dynamic, granular, and speckled pattern, which is indicative of the movement of the lung surface against the chest wall. This pattern is consistent with the **"Seashore Sign"** (a dynamic, sandy, granular appearance below the pleural line) rather than a static "Barcode Sign" (which would indicate collapsed or non-ventilated lung).
