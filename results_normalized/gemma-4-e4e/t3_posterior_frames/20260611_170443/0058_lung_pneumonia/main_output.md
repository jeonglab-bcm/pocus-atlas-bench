# 0058_lung_pneumonia

**Observation:**

The provided sequence of lung ultrasound frames demonstrates significant parenchymal pathology in the posterior/lateral zone. The lung parenchyma appears highly dense, echogenic, and heterogeneous, presenting a texture that is solid and bright rather than dark and aerated (loss of normal A-lines). This appearance is characteristic of **tissue-like hepatization** or consolidation. Within this dense, echogenic area, there are visible punctate and linear hypoechoic structures contrasting against the bright background. These structures represent **air bronchograms**, indicating air-filled bronchi within the consolidated lung tissue.

There are no clear signs of a significant pleural effusion, such as a definitive anechoic quad sign or dynamic fluid movement (sinusoid sign). The primary finding is the replacement of normal air by a solid or fluid-filled exudate within the alveolar spaces.

**Conclusion:**

*   **plaps = true**
*   **type = "consolidation"** (The primary and dominant pathological finding is the consolidated, hepatized lung with air bronchograms).
