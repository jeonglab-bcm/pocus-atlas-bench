# 0135_lung_jr_jellyfishsign

**Observation:**

The ultrasound sequence initially suggests a posterior zone with a clear pleural line and an anechoic (black) dependent space, which is characteristic of pleural effusion.

As the sequence progresses (particularly from frame 3 onwards), the appearance of the lung tissue in the dependent zone undergoes a dramatic change. The previously dark space becomes intensely echogenic (hyperechoic). This high-level, solid, and uniform echogenicity is characteristic of **tissue-like hepatization** (alveolar consolidation). The lung parenchyma no longer displays the normal fine, parallel A-lines (which represent air interfaces) and instead appears solid, dense, and opaque, strongly mimicking the texture of the liver.

**Conclusion:**

**plaps = true**
**type = "consolidation"**

*Rationale: While the initial frames may suggest a component of fluid, the dominant and unmistakable finding throughout the later frames is the severe alveolar consolidation, evidenced by the strong tissue-like/hepatized echogenicity of the posterior lung zone.*
