# 0004_lung_pleural-effusion-with-hiatal-hernia

**Description of Observations:**

The sequential lung ultrasound frames reveal significant abnormalities within the visualized posterior/lateral lung zone. The most prominent feature is the texture of the lung parenchyma. The lung does not demonstrate the characteristic fine, homogenous, and easily separable appearance of a normal, aerated lung.

The parenchyma instead appears dense, coarse, and heterogeneous, exhibiting a "tissue-like" or solid texture, strongly suggesting alveolar filling. This finding aligns with the concept of **tissue-like hepatization** (a key sign of consolidation). Furthermore, the boundaries between the consolidated tissue and any surrounding structures appear irregular, suggesting the presence of a **shred sign**. While classic free fluid collection signs (like the Quad sign) are not definitively identified, the dominant pathology is the widespread change in the internal lung structure.

**Conclusion:**

*   **plaps = true**: Consolidation signs are clearly present.
*   **type = "consolidation"**: The primary pattern observed is alveolar consolidation (hepatization) rather than a free fluid collection (effusion).
