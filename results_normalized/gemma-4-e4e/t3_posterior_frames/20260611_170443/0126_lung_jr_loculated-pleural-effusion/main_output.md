# 0126_lung_jr_loculated-pleural-effusion

**Observation:**

The sequential images of the lung show a clear pleural line. Deep to this line, the lung parenchyma appears markedly increased in echogenicity (brightness) and texture. Instead of exhibiting the characteristic "A-lines" (which signify normal aeration and air/fluid interfaces) or a clear, anechoic (black) pleural space, the deep lung zone presents as a solid, dense, and echogenic structure. This appearance is classic for **alveolar consolidation** (often described as "tissue-like hepatization"). There are no definitive classic signs of large free fluid collections (e.g., a clear Quad sign or Sinusoid sign) that dominate the field, but the extensive consolidation itself constitutes a form of PLAPS.

**Conclusion:**

*   **plaps = true**: One or more signs (consolidation/hepatization) are clearly present.
*   **type = "consolidation"**: The dominant finding is the solid, echogenic, consolidated lung parenchyma.

***

**(Note: While consolidation is the primary finding, PLAPS encompasses both consolidation and effusion. In this case, the ultrasound strongly demonstrates the consolidation component.)**
