# 0030_lung_parapneumonic-effusion-with-b-lines

**Image Description and Observation**

The provided ultrasound frames show the right lung (RT) in the mid-to-deep posterior zone (H/T Zone 2 Low). The lung parenchyma does not exhibit the typical clear, aerated appearance with visible A-lines (which indicate normal aeration). Instead, the entire visualized field of the lung parenchyma is diffusely echogenic, appearing dense and solid.

This appearance is consistent with **tissue-like hepatization**, where the lung tissue resembles the texture of the liver rather than the air-filled, black-appearing lung. The loss of normal aeration artifacts strongly suggests alveolar filling and consolidation. While there is no definitive large anechoic (black) space indicating a massive pleural effusion (which would show a clear quad sign), the primary pathology observed is the dense, solid, consolidated nature of the lung tissue.

**Conclusion**

**plaps = true**
**type = "consolidation"**

**Summary:** The image demonstrates classic signs of alveolar consolidation (tissue-like hepatization) in the posterior lung zone, characteristic of PLAPS.
