# 0058_lung_pneumonia

**Observation:**
The provided sequence of lung ultrasound (LUS) images of the posterior lung zone demonstrates features indicative of both pleural fluid accumulation and alveolar consolidation.

**Effusion Signs:**
1. **Pleural Fluid:** There is a clear, distinct **lung line** (the visceral pleura) visible separating the lung parenchyma from a large, **anechoic** (black) space in the posterior/posterior-lateral zone. This configuration is consistent with the presence of a pleural effusion.
2. **Quad Sign/Jellyfish Sign:** While a perfectly defined "Quad Sign" is not visible in static frames, the presence of a clear lung line above a significant anechoic zone confirms an effusion.

**Consolidation Signs:**
1. **Tissue-like Hepatization/Consolidation:** The lung parenchyma visible above the interface appears dense and echogenic, suggesting that the alveolar spaces have been filled with fluid (consolidation).
2. **Air Bronchograms:** Critically, within the consolidated/hepatized parenchyma, there are multiple **punctate to linear hyperechoic foci** that represent air-filled bronchi. These air bronchograms are a hallmark sign of alveolar filling and consolidation.
3. **Shred Sign:** The interface between the consolidated tissue and the surrounding aerated lung tissue (if present, or the fluid collection) is irregular, supporting a pathological process.

**Conclusion:**
*   **plaps = true**: Both effusion and consolidation signs are present.
*   **type = "both"**: There is evidence of pleural effusion (clear lung line over an anechoic space) and alveolar consolidation (parenchymal density and clear air bronchograms).
