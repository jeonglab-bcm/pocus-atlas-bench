3.  **Apply LUS Definitions (The Criteria):**

In these images, we see a distinct anechoic space (fluid) bounded by a pleural line and a lung line. This strongly suggests the formation of a fluid-filled space characteristic of an effusion.

        *   *Tissue-like hepatization:* Requires the parenchyma to look solid/echogenic like liver. Here, the area is *anechoic* (black), which is fluid, not solid tissue.
        *   *Shred sign/Air bronchograms:* These require involvement of the parenchymal tissue, which is not the primary finding here.

    *   This configuration perfectly matches the criteria for a **Quad Sign** (or clear evidence of a localized pleural effusion).
    *   There is no evidence of tissue-like echogenicity (hepatization) or other consolidation signs.

5.  **Formulate the Classification:**
    *   *plaps:* True (An effusion is present).
    *   *type:* "effusion" (The signs are purely related to fluid accumulation).
