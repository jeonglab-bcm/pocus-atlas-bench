# 0029_lung_parapneumonic-effusion

**Description of Observations:**

The initial frames (1-3) primarily show typical lung ultrasound characteristics for a normal posterior lung zone, with the presence of characteristic A-lines (suggestive of normal aeration and lung sliding).

Starting around frame 4 and continuing through frame 5, there is a significant change in the parenchymal appearance. The normal A-line pattern is lost or obscured. The lung parenchyma in the posterior zone takes on a markedly solid, homogeneous, and echogenic appearance. This texture is characteristic of tissue-like hepatization, indicating that the normally air-filled alveoli are now filled with fluid/consolidating tissue. No distinct, anechoic space is seen bounded by a clear pleural line and a lung line (i.e., the Quad sign is not present).

**Conclusion:**

*   **plaps = true**
*   **type = "consolidation"**

*(Reasoning: The dominant findings are signs of alveolar consolidation/hepatization (loss of A-lines, tissue-like appearance), rather than free fluid collection.)*
