**Observations:**

The sequential frames demonstrate a diffuse and pervasive abnormality within the posterior lung zone. The predominant pattern observed across all frames is a dense, highly echogenic, and disorganized parenchymal texture. This texture is characteristic of **tissue-like hepatization**, meaning the lung parenchyma appears solid and echogenic, closely mimicking the appearance of solid organ tissue such as the liver.

This pattern strongly suggests alveolar consolidation. While the images do not show a clear, large anechoic pocket consistent with a traditional quad sign for effusion, the dense internal echoes and structural disruption are consistent with significant underlying lung pathology. The irregular, dense nature of the consolidated tissue is further suggestive of extensive infiltrates and potential air-filled bronchi within the consolidated lung (air bronchograms), which contributes to the overall hepatized appearance.

**Conclusion:**

*   **plaps = true**
*   **type = "consolidation"** (The primary and dominant finding is the consolidation/hepatization of the lung parenchyma, rather than free fluid collection.)
