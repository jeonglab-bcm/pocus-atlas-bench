# 0004_lung_pleural-effusion-with-hiatal-hernia

**Observation:**

The provided lung ultrasound frames display significant pathology in the posterior/lateral dependent lung zone (PLAPS point).

**Assessment of Effusion Signs:**
*   No clear, anechoic (black) space forming a classic quad sign is visible.
*   No dynamic signs (Sinusoid or Jellyfish) can be confirmed from static images, though the overall view is dense.

**Assessment of Consolidation Signs:**
*   **Tissue-like hepatization:** The lung parenchyma is markedly hyperechoic (bright white) and appears dense and solid, losing the typical black, aerated appearance of normal lung tissue. This morphology strongly resembles the echogenicity of solid organs like the liver, characteristic of pulmonary consolidation/edema.
*   **Air bronchograms:** While the consolidation is dense, distinct, dark, branching air-filled bronchi within the solid tissue are not clearly identifiable in these specific frames.
*   **Shred sign:** The transition between the consolidated area and the surrounding tissue appears somewhat irregular, though the primary finding remains the solid appearance of the consolidated field.

**Conclusion:**

The most prominent pathological feature is the solid, echogenic appearance of the lung parenchyma, indicative of filling of the airspaces.

*   **plaps = true**
*   **type = "consolidation"** (The dominant pathology is the consolidation/hepatization of the lung tissue.)
