# 0100_lung_hemothorax

**Observation:**

The ultrasound image shows a lung field (likely the posterior/lateral zone). The most prominent feature is the parenchyma below the pleural line, which appears uniformly and densely echogenic (bright white). This appearance is consistent with **tissue-like hepatization**, where the normal air-filled (anechoic) lung is replaced by solid, consolidated tissue. The lung does not exhibit typical A-lines or the "pepper and salt" pattern of aeration.

No large, clearly defined anechoic (black) space characteristic of a massive pleural effusion (like a definitive Quad sign) is visible in this frame, but the consolidated tissue itself is the primary finding.

**Classification:**

*   **plaps = true**: Yes, signs of consolidation are present.
*   **type = "consolidation"**: The pattern is predominantly characterized by tissue-like hepatization (consolidated lung) rather than free fluid collection.

**Conclusion:**

PLAPS is present, classified as **consolidation** (tissue-like hepatization).
