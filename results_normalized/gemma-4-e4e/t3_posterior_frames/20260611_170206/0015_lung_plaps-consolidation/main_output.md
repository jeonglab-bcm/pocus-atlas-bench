# 0015_lung_plaps-consolidation

**Image Observation and Analysis:**

*   **Frames 1 & 2:** The lung field is relatively clear. No clear pleural effusion (no visible Quad sign or anechoic accumulation) or definitive signs of alveolar filling (no prominent B-lines or hepatization) are visible in the visualized field.
*   **Frames 3 & 4:** There is a significant change. The lung parenchyma immediately deep to the pleural line transitions from a relatively clear field to a highly **echogenic, speckled, and granular texture**. This appearance is highly suggestive of **tissue-like hepatization**, meaning the alveolar spaces are filled with fluid or inflammatory material, making the lung appear solid and echogenic rather than aerated.
*   **PLAPS Pattern Assessment:** The presence of this solid, textured lung pattern confirms a PLAPS pattern.
*   **Type Classification:** The dominant pathology is the change in lung parenchymal texture (hepatization/consolidation), not a significant free fluid collection (effusion).

**Conclusion:**

*   **plaps = true**
*   **type = "consolidation"** (The primary finding is tissue-like hepatization/consolidation.)
