# 0126_lung_jr_loculated-pleural-effusion

**Description of Observations:**

The provided lung ultrasound images from the posterior zone display significant changes in the lung parenchyma, consistent with alveolar filling rather than simple free fluid.

1.  **Pleural Line:** The visceral pleural line is clearly visible, indicating the lung is in contact with the pleural surface.
2.  **Parenchymal Echogenicity:** The area below the pleural line (the posterior dependent lung zone) exhibits a markedly increased, solid, and highly echogenic texture. This appearance is consistent with **tissue-like hepatization**. The normal, clear aeration and A-line pattern (if present higher up) are lost, replaced by a solid, "liver-like" texture due to filling of the alveoli with fluid or exudate.
3.  **Effusion Signs (Negative):** There is no evidence of a large, discrete anechoic (black) space bounded by the lung line and chest wall (ruling out a clear, obvious **Quad Sign**). The lung tissue itself appears to be dense, suggesting it is filled rather than pushed aside by fluid.
4.  **Consolidation Signs (Positive):** The hallmark of **Tissue-like hepatization** is prominent. While definitive **Air Bronchograms** and **Shred Sign** are difficult to confirm without dynamic M-mode or higher resolution, the overall appearance is classic for consolidated, exudative lung.

**Classification:**

*   **plaps = true**: Yes, consolidation signs are clearly present.
*   **type = "consolidation"**: The dominant finding is the solid, echogenic, tissue-like appearance of the lung parenchyma, which is characteristic of alveolar consolidation/hepatization.
