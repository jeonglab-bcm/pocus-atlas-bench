# 0132_lung_jr_empyema

**Observation:**

The provided sequential lung ultrasound frames demonstrate several findings consistent with Posterior Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome (PLAPS) in the dependent posterior/lateral zone.

1.  **Effusion Signs:** A large, distinct, anechoic (dark) area is visible at the base of the lung, which strongly suggests the presence of **pleural fluid (effusion)**. This fluid collection is bounded by a pleural line (superior boundary of the fluid) and the deeper lung tissue. The overall appearance is characteristic of a fluid accumulation consistent with a **quad sign** or significant pleural effusion.
2.  **Consolidation Signs:** The lung parenchyma adjacent to the fluid collection shows an irregular and heterogeneous texture. Furthermore, within this tissue, several punctate, hyperechoic foci are observable (particularly visible in the lower central portions of the image), which represent **air bronchograms**. This finding indicates that the lung tissue is filled with exudate/fluid, consistent with **alveolar consolidation** (hepatization).

**Conclusion:**

*   **plaps = true**: The presence of both pleural fluid and consolidated lung tissue confirms the PLAPS pattern.
*   **type = "both"**: Both significant pleural effusion and alveolar consolidation signs are clearly visible.
