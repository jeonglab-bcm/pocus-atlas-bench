# 0030_lung_parapneumonic-effusion-with-b-lines

**Description of Observations:**

The lung ultrasound images of the posterior/lateral chest zone reveal extensive pathology. The dominant features are multiple large, anechoic (black) areas scattered throughout the visualized lung fields. These areas are characteristic of an accumulation of fluid in the pleural space (pleural effusion).

While classic dynamic signs like the "Sinusoid sign" cannot be confirmed from static images, the presence of these large anechoic spaces bounded by chest wall shadows and lung tissue is consistent with the formation of **Quad signs** (a key sign of pleural effusion). The remaining lung tissue is either compressed by the fluid or obscured.

There is no clearly defined, solid, echogenic (hepatized) parenchymal texture or prominent air bronchograms visible that would classify the primary finding as alveolar consolidation.

**Conclusion:**

*   **plaps = true**
*   **type = "effusion"** (Predominantly fluid collection signs are observed.)
