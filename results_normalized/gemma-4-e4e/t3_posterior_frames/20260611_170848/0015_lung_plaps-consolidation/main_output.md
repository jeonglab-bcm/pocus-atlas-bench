# 0015_lung_plaps-consolidation

**Observation:**

The sequential frames consistently demonstrate a large, anechoic (black) area occupying the dependent, posterior/lateral aspect of the lung field. This anechoic space is bounded superiorly by a well-defined pleural line. The overall morphology strongly suggests a collection of free fluid in the pleural space.

Specific signs observed:
*   **Pleural Effusion:** The presence of a clear, anechoic space in the posterior zone is characteristic of fluid accumulation. The space forms a configuration consistent with the **Quad Sign** (an anechoic space bounded by the pleural line, the chest wall, and rib shadows).
*   **Consolidation:** While the lung tissue inferior to the fluid may appear moderately dense, there is no clear evidence of widespread tissue-like hepatization or prominent air bronchograms that would suggest consolidation is the dominant process.

**Conclusion:**

*   **plaps = true**
*   **type = "effusion"**
