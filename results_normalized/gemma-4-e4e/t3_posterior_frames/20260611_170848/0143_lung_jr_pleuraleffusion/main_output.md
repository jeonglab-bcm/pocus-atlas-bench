# 0143_lung_jr_pleuraleffusion

**Observation:**
The ultrasound images show the lung parenchyma immediately deep to the pleural line in the posterior zone. The most prominent feature is the appearance of the lung tissue. The lung parenchyma is markedly echogenic, appearing solid, granular, and uniformly gray/white, which strongly resembles the texture of the liver (hepatization). There is a significant loss of the normal aeration patterns (such as visible A-lines) characteristic of healthy lung tissue. No large, clearly anechoic pockets of free fluid (which would constitute a classic quad sign) are definitively visible; the dominant pattern is one of dense parenchymal consolidation.

**Conclusion:**
**plaps = true**
**type = "consolidation"**
