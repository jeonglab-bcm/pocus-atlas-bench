# 0126_lung_jr_loculated-pleural-effusion

**Image Analysis:**

The sequential frames consistently demonstrate a dense, solid, and highly echogenic appearance of the posterior lung parenchyma. This texture is not characteristic of normal aerated lung tissue (which should show clear A-lines) nor is it characteristic of a purely anechoic fluid collection.

**Effusion Signs Assessment:**
*   No distinct, large anechoic spaces (quad sign) are visible.
*   There is no evidence of dynamic movement or fluid-lung interface suggesting a sinusodial or jellyfish pattern.

**Consolidation Signs Assessment:**
*   The lung tissue appears dense and solid, exhibiting a highly echogenic, granular texture that strongly resembles liver tissue (**Tissue-like hepatization**).
*   The boundaries between the consolidated areas and any remaining aerated tissue appear irregular, consistent with the **Shred sign**.

**Conclusion:**

The findings are consistent with alveolar consolidation (e.g., pulmonary edema, pneumonia, ARDS) rather than a primary pleural effusion.
