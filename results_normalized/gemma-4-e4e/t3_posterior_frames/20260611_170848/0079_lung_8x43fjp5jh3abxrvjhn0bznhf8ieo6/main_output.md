**Observation:**

The lung ultrasound frames show significant pathology in the posterior lung zone, consistent with Posterior Lung Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome (PLAPS).

**Effusion Signs Observed:**
*   **Presence of Anechoic Zones:** Large, dark (anechoic/hypoechoic) areas are visible in the dependent portions of the lung zones, suggesting the presence of free fluid (pleural effusion).
*   **Quad Sign:** While the perfect, sharp quadrilateral cannot be fully measured from the static image, the configuration of fluid collection bounded by the ribs (lateral) and the lung/pleural line (superior/inferior) is evident, consistent with an effusion.

**Consolidation Signs Observed:**
*   **Tissue-like Hepatization:** Large sections of the lung parenchyma exhibit a solid, speckled, and highly echogenic texture, strongly suggesting the replacement of air-filled alveoli with fluid or cellular material (consolidation/atelectasis).
*   **Loss of Normal Aeration:** The classic appearance of multiple A-lines is absent, replaced by the dense, solid-looking texture characteristic of consolidated lung.
*   **Shred Sign:** The interface between the dense, echogenic consolidated tissue and the surrounding, less affected (or fluid-filled) zones is irregular and disorganized, consistent with the "shred sign."

**Conclusion:**

Based on the presence of both fluid collection (pleural effusion) and solid, dense lung changes (consolidation/hepatization), the following classification is made:

*   **plaps = true**
*   **type = "both"**
