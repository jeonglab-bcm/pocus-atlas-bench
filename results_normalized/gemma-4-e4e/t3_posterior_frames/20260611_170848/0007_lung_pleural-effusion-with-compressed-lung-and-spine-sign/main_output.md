# 0007_lung_pleural-effusion-with-compressed-lung-and-spine-sign

**Observation:**
The provided sequence of lung ultrasound images demonstrates a large, well-defined anechoic (black) space in the posterior/lateral aspect of the lung field across all frames. This space is bounded superiorly by a thin pleural line and laterally by rib shadows, meeting the lung parenchyma inferiorly. This is consistent with the presence of a **Quad sign**, a classic sign of pleural effusion. There is no evidence of widespread, solid, echogenic (hepatized) lung parenchyma filling the space; therefore, signs of alveolar consolidation (tissue-like hepatization or air bronchograms) are not visible. The primary and predominant finding is free fluid collection.

**Conclusion:**
- **plaps = true**
- **type = "effusion"**
