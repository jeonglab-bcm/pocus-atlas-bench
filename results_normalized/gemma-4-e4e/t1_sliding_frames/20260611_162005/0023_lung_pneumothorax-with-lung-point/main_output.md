# 0023_lung_pneumothorax-with-lung-point

This analysis is based on the visual evidence presented in the sequential frames of the lung ultrasound (LUS) clip.

### Frame-by-Frame Analysis

*   **Pleural Line:** In all frames, a distinct, hyperechoic (bright white) line, representing the pleural interface, is visible near the superior aspect of the image.
*   **Subpleural Pattern:** Below the pleural line, the subpleural parenchyma displays a granular, textured appearance. This pattern is consistent with the **Seashore sign**, where the complex, grainy pattern mimics sand moving on a beach.
*   **Stability/Movement:** While these are static frames, the consistent presence of the granular pattern (rather than a perfectly flat, horizontal line) suggests dynamic tissue interface activity. There are no signs of widespread consolidation that would obscure the pleural line completely.

### Assessment of Pleural Sliding

*   **Signs of Sliding Present:**
    *   The presence of the granular/textured pattern (Seashore sign) below the pleural line strongly suggests normal aeration and the presence of a mobile interface.
*   **Signs of Sliding Absent:**
    *   The pattern is not purely static and horizontal (which would represent the Barcode sign/Stratosphere), and the pleural line is clearly defined, indicating the lung tissue is not fully collapsed or consolidated.
*   **Signs of Both:**
    *   No alternating patterns or clear transition points are observed in these frames.

### Conclusion

The ultrasound images show a visible pleural line accompanied by a granular, textured pattern below it (the Seashore sign). This indicates that the pleura is likely mobile and moving relative to the chest wall during breathing.

**Conclusion: Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**
