**Frame-by-Frame Analysis:**

The provided images show a consistent pattern across all sequential frames. The pleural line (the bright interface) is clearly visible. Below this pleural line, the sub-pleural echoes exhibit a textured, granular, or speckled pattern. This texture is not static and uniform, indicating acoustic variation and movement rather than a fixed, smooth interface.

**Assessment of Signs:**

*   **Signs of sliding PRESENT:** The presence of a granular, textured sub-pleural pattern strongly suggests the **Seashore sign**. The non-smooth, active appearance of the interface indicates that the lung tissue is moving against the chest wall, which is the definition of pleural sliding. The pattern is inconsistent frame-to-frame, implying variation in speckle, which is a hallmark of sliding.
*   **Signs of sliding ABSENT:** There is no evidence of a smooth, sharply defined, static pleural line. The pattern does not resemble a barcode or "stratosphere" sign (which would consist only of static horizontal lines).

**Conclusion:**

Based on the dynamic, textured, and granular nature of the sub-pleural echoes observed across all frames, the sonographic findings are consistent with the **presence of pleural sliding**. The pattern shown is characteristic of a "seashore sign."
