# 0057_lung_blines-in-pulmonary-contusion

**Observation Analysis:**

*   **Frames 1 & 2:** In these initial frames, the pleural line appears bright and sharp. The artifacts immediately below the pleural line are predominantly horizontal and regular (consistent with A-lines/barcode pattern). The patterns appear highly uniform and static across these frames, suggesting a fixed interface.
*   **Frames 3, 4, 5, & 6:** As the sequence progresses, the appearance of the sub-pleural region changes dramatically. The artifacts are no longer strictly static horizontal lines. Instead, there is visible variation, granular texture, and speckle scattering immediately beneath the pleural line. In Frames 5 and 6, the pattern strongly suggests a "seashore sign"—a granular, textured pattern caused by the movement of the pleura as air moves across the interface. The pleural line itself appears to be shimmering or vibrating relative to the underlying tissue.

**Conclusion:**

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.**

The evidence for pleural sliding is established by the transition from potentially static artifacts (in Frames 1 & 2) to clear signs of movement and interface variation (shimmering, granular speckle/seashore sign) in the subsequent frames. This variation in sub-pleural speckle confirms that the pleural surface is sliding across the underlying lung tissue.
