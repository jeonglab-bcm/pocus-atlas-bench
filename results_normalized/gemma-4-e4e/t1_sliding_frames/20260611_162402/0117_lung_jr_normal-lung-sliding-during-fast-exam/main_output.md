### Frame-by-Frame Analysis

**Frames 1 & 2:**
*   **Observation:** The pattern below the hyperechoic pleural line is characterized by repetitive, horizontal, static lines (reverberations).
*   **Interpretation:** This pattern resembles the **Stratosphere/Barcode sign** (A-lines). In these frames, the interface appears static and fixed, suggesting that sliding is **absent** at these moments.

**Frame 3:**
*   **Observation:** The pattern below the pleural line shows a granular, sandy texture, appearing dynamic and moving.
*   **Interpretation:** This texture is characteristic of the **Seashore sign**. The dynamic speckle and texture indicate that the pleura is moving relative to the lung, suggesting sliding is **present** here.

**Frame 4:**
*   **Observation:** The pattern returns to the static, highly repetitive, horizontal reverberations seen in Frames 1 and 2.
*   **Interpretation:** The interface appears fixed, suggesting sliding is **absent**.

**Frame 5:**

*   **Interpretation:** This dynamic texture indicates that pleural movement is occurring, suggesting sliding is **present**.

---

### Conclusion

The analysis reveals that the ultrasound images do not present a uniform static interface, nor do they present a uniform dynamic interface. Instead, the image transitions between:
1.  A **static interface** (Barcode/Stratosphere pattern: Frames 1, 2, and 4)
2.  A **dynamic interface** (Seashore sign: Frames 3 and 5)

Because the sequence shows clear evidence of both fixed (non-moving) and moving (sliding) pleura within the same view, the conclusion is:

**Signs of BOTH (lung point)**
