# 0009_lung_lung-point-pneumothorax

This is an analysis of sequential lung ultrasound frames (LUS).

***

### **Frame-by-Frame Analysis**

All five frames exhibit a characteristic appearance for a normal lung field.
1.  **Pleural Line:** A visible, bright (hyperechoic) horizontal line represents the pleural interface in all frames.
2.  **Sub-pleural Field:** The area immediately below the pleural line is not dark and uniform (as in an A-line) nor is it a solid, static horizontal bar (as in a Barcode). Instead, it shows a complex, granular, "sandy" speckle pattern.
3.  **Consistency:** The texture and characteristics of the sub-pleural field appear consistent across the sequence.

### **Assessment of Pleural Sliding**

The analysis of the signs is as follows:

*   **Seashore Sign:** The pattern observed (granular, speckle-rich texture below the pleural line) is the classic **Seashore Sign**. The Seashore sign is the definitive ultrasonic representation of normal pleural sliding, where the lung parenchyma is moving against the pleural surface.
*   **A-Lines/Stratosphere:** The absence of perfectly static, uniformly dark A-lines (which would indicate lung collapse or fluid preventing movement) suggests the lung is moving.
*   **B-Lines:** While clear, discrete B-lines are not dominant, the granular nature indicates tissue movement and perfusion, consistent with sliding.

### **Conclusion**

**Pleural sliding is PRESENT.** The presence of the Seashore Sign—a granular, moving speckle pattern below the pleural line—confirms that there is normal movement and interface between the lung and the chest wall, indicating that pleural sliding is intact.
