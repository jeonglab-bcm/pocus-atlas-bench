# 0017_lung_lung-point2

**Frame-by-Frame Analysis**

**Frame 1:**
*   **Observations:** The pleural line is visible. Below the line, there are prominent vertical echoes (comet-tail artifacts or B-lines). The sub-pleural tissue shows a granular or "sandy" appearance.
*   **Sign Interpretation:** This pattern is consistent with the **seashore sign** (a granular/sandy pattern below the pleural line). This indicates that the pleural interface is moving and there is friction between the parietal and visceral pleura, suggesting **sliding is present**.

**Frame 2:**
*   **Observations:** The pleural line remains visible. The M-mode image still demonstrates vertical echoes (B-lines) and a dynamic, granular appearance in the sub-pleural region.
*   **Sign Interpretation:** The presence of B-lines and the dynamic nature of the sub-pleural tissue are consistent with **sliding being present**. The speckle pattern below the pleural line is textured and active.

**Frame 3:**
*   **Observations:** The pleural line is visible. Below the line, the pattern has changed significantly. It now consists primarily of short, horizontal, continuous echoes (A-lines), and the overall appearance is stable.
*   **Sign Interpretation:** This pattern is consistent with the **barcode sign** (a static, horizontal pattern). This indicates that the pleura is fixed and not moving relative to the chest wall, suggesting **sliding is absent**.

**Frame 4:**
*   **Observations:** The pleural line is visible. The M-mode image reverts to a pattern similar to Frames 1 and 2, showing distinct vertical echoes (B-lines) and a granular texture.
*   **Sign Interpretation:** The presence of B-lines and dynamic sub-pleural speckle indicates that **sliding is present** again.

**Conclusion**

Based on the sequential analysis:
*   **Sliding Present:** Observed in Frames 1, 2, and 4 (Seashore sign / B-lines).
*   **Sliding Absent:** Observed in Frame 3 (Barcode sign / static A-lines).

Since the sequence shows a visible transition from a dynamic (sliding) pattern to a static (non-sliding) pattern across the field of view, the finding is characteristic of the **lung point**.
