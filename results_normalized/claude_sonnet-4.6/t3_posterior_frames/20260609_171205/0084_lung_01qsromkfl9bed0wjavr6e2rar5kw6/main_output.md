# Lung Ultrasound — PLAPS Point Analysis

## Sequential Frame Observations

### Frames 2, 3, 4, 5, 9, 10 — Hepatization
A **rounded, dome-shaped structure** is visible above the diaphragm with a **homogeneous, echogenic (liver-like) parenchymal texture**, indicative of **tissue-like hepatization** — the hallmark of lung consolidation. The normal aeration artifacts (A-lines, comet tails) are entirely absent within this region.

### Frames 1, 7, 8 — Air Bronchograms
Within the consolidated tissue, **punctate and branching hyperechoic foci** are clearly visible — representing **air bronchograms** (air-filled bronchi trapped within consolidated parenchyma). This is pathognomonic of airspace consolidation.

### Frames 3, 4, 5, 9 — Anechoic Rim / Effusion Component
A **thin-to-moderate anechoic (dark) space** is identifiable adjacent to the consolidated lung and above/along the diaphragm, forming a partial **quad sign configuration**, suggesting an **associated pleural effusion**.

### Frame 6 — Shred Sign
The deep border of the consolidated lung appears **irregular and shredded**, consistent with the **shred sign** (interface between consolidated and partially-aerated lung).
