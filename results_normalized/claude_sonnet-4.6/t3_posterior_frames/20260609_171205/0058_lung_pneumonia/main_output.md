# 0058_lung_pneumonia

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point

---

### Frame-by-Frame Observations

#### **Early Frames (2/229 – 48/229)**
- Below the pleural line: an **anechoic (black) region** is visible, bounded superiorly by the pleural line and inferiorly by a deeper echogenic interface → consistent with a **Quad sign** (pleural effusion)
- Deep to this anechoic zone: emergence of a **heterogeneous, echogenic tissue-like region**

#### **Mid Frames (75/229 – 148/229)**
- The anechoic effusion layer persists superiorly
- The deeper parenchyma progressively takes on a **hepatized (liver-like) echotexture** — loss of normal aeration artifacts
- **Multiple punctate and linear hyperechoic foci** appear within the hepatized tissue → **air bronchograms**

#### **Late Frames (175/229 – 225/229)**
- Consolidation becomes dominant — **solid, liver-textured lung parenchyma** fills the field
- Deep border is **irregular and shredded** → **Shred sign**
- Air bronchograms remain prominent throughout
- A thin anechoic layer is still visible anterosuperiorly, consistent with residual effusion

### Conclusion

> **plaps = `true`**
> **type = `"both"`**

A **posterolateral pleural effusion** (anechoic quadrilateral space) overlies a **consolidated, hepatized lung** with **air bronchograms** and a **shred sign** — representing a classic PLAPS pattern combining both effusion and alveolar consolidation (e.g., parapneumonic effusion with pneumonia or aspiration).
