# 0051_lung_jellyfish-sign

# Lung Ultrasound PLAPS Analysis — Sequential Frame Review

---

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### Frames 1–2
- A **large anechoic (black) space** dominates the field, bounded superiorly by the pleural line and laterally by rib shadows → **Quad Sign present**
- A **bright, curvilinear echogenic structure** is visible floating centrally within the anechoic space
- In Frame 1, **punctate hyperechoic foci** are visible within that structure → suggestive of **air bronchograms**

### Frames 3–6
- The floating echogenic structure becomes increasingly prominent, taking on a **curved, folded, undulating morphology**
- It changes shape and position between frames — classic **Jellyfish Sign**: atelectatic lung floating freely within surrounding effusion
- The structure has **tissue-like (hepatized) echogenicity**, distinct from fluid

### Frames 7–9
- The anechoic effusion remains large
- The consolidated/atelectatic lung recedes partially out of view as it moves with respiration
- Residual bright echogenic foci remain visible (likely atelectatic lung edge or air bronchograms)

### Frame 10
- The floating consolidated lung re-enters the field within the effusion
- Confirms dynamic movement consistent with jellyfish sign

## Conclusion

> **plaps = `true`**
> **type = `"both"`**

A **large pleural effusion** is present (quad sign, jellyfish sign), with a **floating atelectatic/consolidated lung lobe** demonstrating tissue-like hepatization and likely air bronchograms. This is a classic **PLAPS-positive** posterior zone with **combined effusion and consolidation/atelectasis** — a pattern frequently seen in severe respiratory failure, pneumonia with parapneumonic effusion, or cardiac/hepatic causes of transudative effusion with compressive atelectasis.
