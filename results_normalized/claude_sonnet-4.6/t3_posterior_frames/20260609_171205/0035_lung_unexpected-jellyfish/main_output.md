# 0035_lung_unexpected-jellyfish

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point

---

### Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Frames 1–3 (Early frames):**
- A **large anechoic (black) space** is visible in the dependent region of the image, bounded superiorly by the pleural line and inferiorly — consistent with a **pleural effusion (Quad Sign positive)**
- Above/within the anechoic collection, **echogenic, solid-appearing tissue** is visible with a texture resembling liver parenchyma (**hepatization**)

**Frames 4–7 (Mid sequence):**
- The echogenic tissue becomes more clearly delineated and appears to **shift position slightly** between frames
- The tissue maintains a **liver-like echotexture** (hepatization), without normal aeration artifacts (no A-lines, no B-lines)
- The interface between the echogenic tissue and the anechoic fluid is visible — **irregular/shredded deep border** suggestive of a **Shred Sign**

**Frames 8–10 (Late frames):**
- The echogenic consolidated tissue appears to **undulate and float** within the surrounding anechoic effusion across sequential frames
- This **pendular motion** of the consolidated/atelectatic lung within fluid is the classic **Jellyfish Sign**

### Conclusion

> **plaps = true**
> **type = "both"**

A **large pleural effusion** is present (anechoic collection, quad sign, jellyfish sign), with **underlying lung consolidation/atelectasis** (hepatized echogenic tissue with a shredded deep border floating within the effusion). This is a classic PLAPS-positive pattern combining **effusion and consolidation**, most commonly seen in pneumonia with parapneumonic effusion, malignant effusion with compressive atelectasis, or heart failure with dependent consolidation.
