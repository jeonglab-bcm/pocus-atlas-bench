# 0025_lung_malignant-pleural-effusion

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point (Sequential Frames)

---

### Observations

#### Pleural Effusion Signs

| Sign | Observation |
|------|-------------|
| **Quad Sign** | Prominent **anechoic (black) space** is clearly visible, bounded superiorly by the pleural line and laterally by rib shadows — a well-defined quadrilateral fluid collection |
| **Sinusoid Sign** | Across frames, the deep lung interface demonstrates **respiratory variation in position**, moving cyclically toward the pleural line — consistent with dynamic fluid |
| **Jellyfish Sign** | In mid-to-late frames, an **echogenic mass of tissue undulates and floats** within the anechoic effusion, moving with respiration — classic jellyfish appearance of atelectatic lung suspended in fluid |

#### Consolidation Signs

| Sign | Observation |
|------|-------------|
| **Tissue-like Hepatization** | The floating/dependent lung tissue has a **liver-like echogenicity** — solid, homogeneous, loss of aeration artifacts |
| **Air Bronchograms** | Within the hepatized tissue, **punctate and linear hyperechoic foci** are visible, representing trapped air in bronchi |
| **Shred Sign** | The deep border of the consolidated tissue is **irregular and shredded**, abutting the fluid collection |

---

### Sequential Frame Dynamic Assessment

- **Early frames**: Large anechoic effusion dominates; consolidated lung partially visible
- **Mid frames**: Hepatized lung mass becomes more prominent, floating within effusion
- **Late frames**: Jellyfish movement clearly depicted — atelectatic lung swings with respiratory cycles

---

## Conclusion

$$\boxed{plaps = \textbf{true} \quad | \quad type = \textbf{"both"}}$$

**Large posterior pleural effusion** (quad sign + sinusoid sign + jellyfish sign) with underlying **compressive atelectasis/consolidation** (hepatization + air bronchograms + shred sign). This is a classic **PLAPS-positive** finding indicating posterolateral alveolar and pleural syndrome — most consistent with **parapneumonic effusion or heart failure with compressive atelectasis**.
