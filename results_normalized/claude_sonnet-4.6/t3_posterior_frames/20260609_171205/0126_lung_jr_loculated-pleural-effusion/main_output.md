# 0126_lung_jr_loculated-pleural-effusion

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point (Sequential Frames)

---

### Observations

#### Pleural Line & Near Field
- The pleural line appears **irregular and disrupted** across frames, rather than the smooth bright line seen in normal lung.
- In the **earliest frames (1–2)**, there is a relatively **anechoic (dark) region** immediately deep to the pleural line — this has the morphology consistent with a **small-to-moderate pleural effusion** (Quad sign morphology: bounded superiorly by pleural line, laterally by rib shadows, with a defined inferior lung line).

#### Deep/Mid Field — Dominant Finding
- Across **frames 2–10**, the lung parenchyma displays **heterogeneous, echogenic, tissue-like (hepatized) echotexture** — similar in appearance to liver or spleen.
- This represents **loss of normal aeration** and is consistent with **alveolar consolidation (hepatization)**.
- The **deep border is irregular and shredded** — consistent with the **Shred sign**, indicating the interface between consolidated and partially aerated lung.

#### Air Bronchograms
- Within the consolidated (hepatized) zone, multiple **punctate and linear hyperechoic foci** are visible — consistent with **air bronchograms** (air-filled bronchi within consolidated parenchyma).

#### Dynamic Assessment (Frame Sequence)
- Sequential frames show **respiratory variation** in the depth and extent of the consolidated/fluid zones — confirming real tissue movement and ruling out artifact.
- No classic jellyfish sign (floating atelectatic lung) is definitively identified, but the effusion appears relatively small adjacent to dominant consolidation.

### Conclusion

> **plaps = `true`**
> **type = `"both"`**

The posterior lung zone demonstrates **predominantly alveolar consolidation** (hepatization, shred sign, air bronchograms) with a **small associated pleural effusion** (anechoic quadrilateral collection in early frames). This pattern is consistent with **PLAPS** — most likely representing **pneumonia with parapneumonic effusion**, or atelectasis with reactive effusion.
