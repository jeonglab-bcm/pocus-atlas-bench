# 0135_lung_jr_jellyfishsign

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point (Sequential Frames)

---

### Frame-by-Frame Observations

| Frame | Finding |
|-------|---------|
| **1–2** | Predominantly **anechoic (black) space** below the pleural line — consistent with **free pleural fluid**. Minimal internal echogenicity. Rib shadows bounding laterally — early **Quad sign** configuration. |
| **3–4** | A large, **echogenic, tissue-like mass** begins to emerge from the inferior field — transitional zone between fluid and solid tissue. |
| **5–7** | Clear **hepatization**: the lung parenchyma displays **liver-like solid echogenicity**, loss of normal aeration artifacts, and a heterogeneous but tissue-dense texture. **Irregular deep border** visible — consistent with **shred sign**. |
| **8–10** | Fully developed **consolidated tissue** with **hyperechoic punctate/linear foci** embedded within — consistent with **air bronchograms**. The consolidated mass appears to be partially surrounded by anechoic fluid. |

---

### Key Signs Identified

| Sign | Present | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| **Quad sign** | ✅ | Anechoic fluid bounded by pleural/lung lines (early frames) |
| **Jellyfish sign** | ✅ | Echogenic consolidated lung floating within effusion |
| **Tissue-like hepatization** | ✅ | Liver-texture parenchyma (frames 4–10) |
| **Shred sign** | ✅ | Irregular deep border of consolidation |
| **Air bronchograms** | ✅ | Hyperechoic foci within consolidated tissue |
| **Sinusoid sign** | ⚠️ | Suggested by respiratory variation; M-mode not provided |

---

### Conclusion

```
plaps = true
type  = "both"
```

**Rationale:** The sequential frames demonstrate a **posterior pleural effusion** (anechoic quad sign in early frames) surrounding **compressive/consolidated atelectatic lung** (hepatization, shred sign, air bronchograms in later frames). The floating appearance of the consolidated segment within fluid suggests a **jellyfish sign**, confirming both pathologies coexist at this PLAPS point.

> ⚠️ *Clinical correlation recommended. This pattern (effusion + consolidation) is characteristic of PLAPS and may indicate pneumonia with parapneumonic effusion, heart failure with compressive atelectasis, or malignant pleural disease.*
