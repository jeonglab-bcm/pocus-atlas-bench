# 0007_lung_pleural-effusion-with-compressed-lung-and-spine-sign

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point (Sequential Frames)

---

### 🔍 Frame-by-Frame Observations

#### **Frames 1–3 (Early sequence)**
- A **large anechoic (black) space** is evident deep to the pleural line, bounded superiorly by the pleural line and inferiorly by the diaphragm/tissue interface
- In Frame 2, a **tubular/linear artifact** appears (possible probe angle or needle artifact), but the dominant finding is the anechoic fluid space
- Frame 3 shows the **largest extent of the anechoic space**, consistent with a **moderate-to-large pleural effusion** — **Quad Sign ✓**

---

#### **Frames 4–6 (Middle sequence)**
- Within the anechoic effusion, a **brighter, echogenic structure** begins to appear — this structure has:
  - **Rounded/lobulated contour**
  - **Hepatization-like (liver texture) echogenicity**
  - **Movement relative to surrounding fluid**
- This is consistent with **compressive atelectasis / consolidated lung floating within the effusion** → **Jellyfish Sign ✓**
- Small punctate anechoic foci within the echogenic structure may represent **air bronchograms ✓**

---

#### **Frames 7–10 (Late sequence)**
- The **floating consolidated lung tissue** becomes increasingly well-defined
- Two distinct anechoic structures are visible:
  - **Larger**: pleural fluid collection
  - **Smaller, rounded**: likely a vascular cross-section (e.g., pulmonary vein/artery) within the consolidated lung
- The **irregular deep border** of the consolidated tissue facing residual aerated lung suggests a **Shred Sign ✓**
- The consolidated parenchyma demonstrates **tissue-like hepatization** — solid, echogenic, loss of normal aeration artifacts ✓

**Interpretation:**
A **moderate-to-large pleural effusion** (Quad sign, Jellyfish sign) is present in the posterior dependent zone, with **compressive atelectasis/consolidation** of the adjacent lung parenchyma (hepatization, shred sign, air bronchograms). The consolidated lung is seen floating/undulating within the effusion (Jellyfish sign), confirming both pathologies coexist.

> **Clinical correlation:** This pattern is most commonly seen in **pneumonia with parapneumonic effusion**, **heart failure with compressive atelectasis**, or **malignant effusion with associated consolidation**. Clinical context and effusion characteristics (transudative vs. exudative) should guide management.
