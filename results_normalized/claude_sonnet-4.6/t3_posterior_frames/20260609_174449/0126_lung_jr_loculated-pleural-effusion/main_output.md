# Lung Ultrasound — PLAPS Point Analysis

## Sequential Frame Observations

### Frames 1–2 (Early Phase)
- A **relatively anechoic (dark) space** is visible in the upper field, bounded by the pleural line superiorly — consistent with a **small pleural effusion** and a partial **Quad sign**
- The lung line is displaced inferiorly

### Frames 3–10 (Progressive Respiratory Motion)
As the lung moves with respiration, increasingly visible echogenic tissue appears in the mid/upper field demonstrating:

| Finding | Observation |
|---|---|
| **Tissue-like hepatization** | Lung parenchyma has liver-like echogenicity — loss of normal aeration |
| **Shred sign** | Deep border of consolidated tissue is irregular and fragmented |
| **Air bronchograms** | Punctate and linear hyperechoic foci visible *within* the hepatized tissue |
| **Effusion remnant** | Residual anechoic halo partially surrounding the consolidated lobe (most visible frame 1) |

---

## Conclusion

> **plaps = `true`**
> **type = `"both"`**

The PLAPS point demonstrates **pulmonary consolidation** (hepatization + shred sign + air bronchograms) as the dominant finding, with a **concurrent small pleural effusion** partially surrounding the consolidated lung — visible as the anechoic space in early frames. This pattern is consistent with a **parapneumonic effusion with adjacent consolidation**, or atelectasis with reactive effusion.
