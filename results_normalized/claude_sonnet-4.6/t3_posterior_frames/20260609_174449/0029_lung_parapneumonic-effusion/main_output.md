# 0029_lung_parapneumonic-effusion

# PLAPS Point Lung Ultrasound Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### Frames 1–2 (Early systole/diastole equivalent — lung in deeper position)
- Large **anechoic (black) space** clearly visible in the dependent zone
- Superior boundary: **pleural line**
- Lateral boundaries: **rib acoustic shadows**
- Inferior boundary: **lung line** → classic **Quad Sign** ✅
- An echogenic structure is visible within the fluid, beginning to descend

### Frames 3–4 (Mid-cycle)
- The anechoic fluid collection persists and enlarges visually
- A **mobile, echogenic tissue mass** is seen floating freely within the effusion
- The echogenic structure undulates and changes shape → **Jellyfish Sign** ✅
- This represents atelectatic/collapsed lung suspended within the effusion

### Frames 5–6 (Mid-to-late cycle)
- The posterior lung parenchyma displays **liver-like echogenicity** (homogeneous, intermediate-gray echotexture)
- Loss of normal A-line/comet-tail artifacts → **Tissue sign / Hepatization** ✅
- Deep border of consolidated region appears **irregular and shredded** → **Shred Sign** ✅

### Frames 7–8 (Late cycle)
- Within the hepatized lung, **punctate and linear hyperechoic foci** are clearly identifiable
- These represent air-filled bronchi surrounded by consolidated parenchyma → **Air Bronchograms** ✅

### Frames 9–10 (Return phase)
- The consolidated lung mass is again seen **floating/rising** within the effusion
- Confirms dynamic movement of atelectatic lung → reinforces **Jellyfish Sign**
- Hepatization texture remains consistent throughout

## Conclusion

```
plaps = true
type  = "both"
```

**Interpretation:** There is a **significant posterolateral pleural effusion** (anechoic space, Quad sign, Jellyfish sign) with **underlying consolidated/atelectatic lung** (hepatization, shred sign, air bronchograms) in the dependent posterior zone. The air bronchograms suggest at least partial **pneumonic consolidation** rather than pure compressive atelectasis. This is a **classic PLAPS-positive pattern**, highly consistent with pneumonia complicated by parapneumonic effusion, or a large effusion with compressive consolidation.
