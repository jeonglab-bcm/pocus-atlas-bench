# Lung Ultrasound — PLAPS Point Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### Frames 1–2 (Early sequence)
- Heterogeneous, **echogenic tissue** in the posterior zone
- Texture closely resembles liver parenchyma → **hepatization pattern**
- **Punctate/linear hyperechoic foci** visible within the tissue → consistent with **air bronchograms**
- Upper portion shows a lobulated, non-aerated structure replacing normal lung architecture

### Frames 3–6 (Mid sequence)
- Consolidation persists with **tissue-like echogenicity**
- **Irregular, shredded deep border** between consolidated and potentially aerated lung → **Shred sign**

### Frames 7–10 (Late sequence)
- A **relatively hypoechoic (darker) region** becomes more visible in the near/mid field — bounded superiorly by the pleural line and flanked by rib shadows
- This is consistent with a **Quad sign** configuration, suggesting a **pleural effusion component**
- Echogenic consolidated tissue remains visible posterior/deep to this region
- The combination suggests atelectatic/consolidated lung floating within or adjacent to fluid → possible **Jellyfish sign** (incomplete due to image quality)
