# 0007_lung_pleural-effusion-with-compressed-lung-and-spine-sign

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point

---

### Systematic Frame-by-Frame Observations

**Frames 1–3 (Early sequence):**
- A **large anechoic (black) space** dominates the posterior zone, bordered superiorly by the pleural line and inferiorly by the diaphragm
- The **Quad sign** is clearly established — fluid bounded by pleural line, lung line, and rib/diaphragm margins
- In Frame 3, the fluid space is most expansive with minimal visible lung tissue, suggesting a **moderate-to-large effusion**

**Frames 4–6 (Mid sequence):**
- A **hyperechoic, liver-like (hepatized) tissue mass** becomes clearly visible within/above the anechoic fluid
- The parenchymal texture is **solid and echogenic**, consistent with **tissue-like hepatization**
- **Small rounded/punctate anechoic foci** are visible within the consolidated tissue — consistent with **fluid bronchograms** (air-filled bronchi replaced by fluid in the setting of consolidation)
- The consolidated lung appears to **partially float** within the surrounding effusion

**Frames 7–10 (Late sequence):**
- The consolidated lung segment **undulates and shifts position** across sequential frames — classic **Jellyfish sign** (atelectatic/consolidated lung floating freely within effusion)
- A **larger rounded anechoic structure** is visible at the inferior pole of the consolidation in frames 9–10, possibly representing a **fluid bronchogram** or localized fluid pocket
- The deep border of the consolidated tissue shows an **irregular, shredded margin** where it interfaces with residually aerated lung — **Shred sign**

**Interpretation:** There is a **moderate-to-large pleural effusion** in the posterior dependent zone with **compressive/obstructive atelectasis and underlying consolidation**. The consolidated/atelectatic lung floats within the effusion (Jellyfish sign), demonstrating hepatization with fluid bronchograms. This pattern is classic for **PLAPS** with combined effusion and consolidation, most commonly seen in pneumonia with parapneumonic effusion, or atelectasis secondary to large effusion.
