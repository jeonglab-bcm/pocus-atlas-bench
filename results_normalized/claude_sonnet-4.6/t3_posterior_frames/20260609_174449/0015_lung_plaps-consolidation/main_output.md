# 0015_lung_plaps-consolidation

# Lung Ultrasound — PLAPS Point Analysis

## Sequential Frame Observations

### Frame 1
- **Dominant finding**: Large **anechoic (black) space** occupying most of the field
- Pleural line visible superiorly; no aeration artifacts (no A-lines/B-lines)
- Classic **Quad sign** configuration: bounded by pleural line, rib shadows, and deep lung interface

### Frames 2–4
- The anechoic effusion persists
- A **hyperechoic, tissue-like structure** begins to emerge from the deeper field
- The structure has a **hepatized texture** — echogenicity resembling liver parenchyma
- **Hyperechoic punctate/linear foci** are visible within this tissue → consistent with **air bronchograms**

### Frames 5–7
- The hepatized lung becomes more prominent
- **Irregular deep border** between consolidated tissue and deeper aerated lung → **Shred sign**
- The consolidated segment appears to **undulate/shift position** across frames

### Frames 8–10
- The atelectatic/consolidated lung is seen **floating and moving** within the surrounding effusion
- This dynamic behavior represents the **Jellyfish sign**
- Air bronchograms remain visible within the hepatized parenchyma

## Conclusion

> **plaps = `true`**
> **type = `"both"`**

There is a **moderate-to-large pleural effusion** (anechoic quad sign) with **atelectatic/consolidated lung** floating within it (jellyfish sign). The consolidated lung shows **hepatization with air bronchograms** and an irregular deep border (shred sign), indicating associated **compressive or obstructive atelectasis with possible alveolar consolidation**.
