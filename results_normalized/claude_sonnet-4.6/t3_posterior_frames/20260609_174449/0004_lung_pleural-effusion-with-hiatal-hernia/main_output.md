# 0004_lung_pleural-effusion-with-hiatal-hernia

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point

### Frame-by-Frame Observations

#### Structural Landmarks
- **Diaphragm**: Highly echogenic triangular/wedge structure consistently visible on the right side of each frame
- **Pleural line**: Identifiable in the near field
- **Depth**: Structures visible to ~8 cm

---

### Detected Signs

#### 🔵 Effusion Component
| Sign | Observation |
|------|-------------|
| **Quad Sign** | Anechoic (dark) space visible in the dependent zone, bounded superiorly by pleural line and inferiorly by lung/diaphragm interface |
| **Sinusoid Sign** | The lung–fluid interface shows cyclical positional variation across sequential frames consistent with respiratory movement |

#### 🟠 Consolidation Component
| Sign | Observation |
|------|-------------|
| **Tissue-like Hepatization** | Central lung parenchyma demonstrates solid, liver-like echogenicity with loss of normal aeration artifacts |
| **Air Bronchograms** | Punctate and linear hyperechoic foci visible within the hepatized tissue across multiple frames |
| **Jellyfish Sign** | Consolidated atelectatic lung appears to undulate/float within surrounding effusion across sequential frames |
| **Shred Sign** | Irregular deep border between consolidated and more aerated regions |

---

### Sequential Dynamic Changes
- **Frames 1–3**: Predominantly fluid-dominant appearance; hepatized tissue begins emerging
- **Frames 4–7**: Consolidation becomes increasingly prominent; air bronchograms more defined
- **Frames 8–10**: Combined pattern clearly established; floating consolidated tissue visible

---

## Conclusion

```
plaps = true
type  = "both"
```

**Rationale**: There is clear evidence of a **pleural effusion** (anechoic quad sign with sinusoidal respiratory variation) combined with **lung consolidation** (hepatization, air bronchograms, shred sign, and jellyfish sign of atelectatic lung floating within fluid). This is a classic **PLAPS-positive** posterior zone with co-existing parapneumonic or reactive effusion alongside consolidation/atelectasis.
