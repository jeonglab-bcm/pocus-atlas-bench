# 0051_lung_jellyfish-sign

# Lung Ultrasound — PLAPS Point Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### Frames 1–3 (Early sequence)
- A **large anechoic (black) space** dominates the field, consistent with a **pleural effusion**
- A **hyperechoic, tissue-like structure** is visible within the effusion, showing liver-like (hepatized) echotexture → **consolidation/atelectasis**
- The structure appears to **float and undulate** within the surrounding fluid → early **Jellyfish sign**
- Punctate/linear hyperechoic foci are visible within the solid structure → **air bronchograms**

### Frames 4–6 (Mid sequence)
- The anechoic effusion remains large and clearly bounded
- The consolidated lung moves dynamically, changing position between frames — confirming the **Jellyfish sign**
- The **Quad sign** is appreciable: anechoic space bounded superiorly by pleural line, with rib shadows at margins
- The deep (visceral) border of the fluid is visible

### Frames 7–10 (Late sequence)
- The consolidated lung progressively occupies more of the frame as it swings inferiorly
- The effusion remains visible surrounding the atelectatic tissue
- Air bronchogram foci remain identifiable within the consolidated lung
- Final frames show the lung receding back, demonstrating respiratory cyclicity

## Conclusion

> **plaps = `true`**
> **type = `"both"`**

There is a **large pleural effusion** (Quad sign, Jellyfish sign) with a **floating atelectatic/consolidated lung segment** (hepatization, air bronchograms). This represents a classic PLAPS-positive pattern combining **free pleural fluid and compressive/obstructive lung consolidation/atelectasis** in the posterolateral dependent zone.
