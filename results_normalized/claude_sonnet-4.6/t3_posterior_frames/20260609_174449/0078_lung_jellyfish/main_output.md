# Lung Ultrasound Analysis — Right Posterior Zone (PLAPS Point)

## Sequential Frame-by-Frame Observations

### Frames 1–4 (Early Sweep)
- Relatively **homogeneous posterior lung field**
- No large anechoic quadrilateral space (no dominant quad sign)
- Mild diffuse echogenicity; no clear demarcation of fluid or solid consolidation yet
- Diaphragm visible inferiorly as a bright curvilinear reflector

### Frames 5–6 (Transition)
- **Progressive change** in parenchymal echogenicity
- Echogenic tissue begins to appear in the mid-to-upper field
- Loss of normal aeration artifact (no A-lines visible)

### Frames 7–10 (Late Sweep — Most Diagnostic)
| Finding | Observation |
|--------|-------------|
| **Hepatization** | Lung parenchyma echogenicity resembles liver texture — solid, homogeneous |
| **Shred sign** | Deep border is **irregular and shredded**, not smooth — interface between aerated and consolidated lung |
| **Air bronchograms** | Punctate/linear **hyperechoic foci** within consolidated tissue visible in frames 9–10 |
| **Possible small effusion** | Trace anechoic component adjacent to consolidated tissue; insufficient to classify as dominant |

---

## Conclusion

$$\boxed{PLAPS = \textbf{true}}$$
$$\boxed{type = \textbf{"consolidation"}}$$

**Dominant pattern:** Alveolar consolidation with **tissue-like hepatization**, **shred sign**, and **air bronchograms** — consistent with pneumonia or atelectasis at the right PLAPS point. No dominant effusion (no clear quad/sinusoid/jellyfish sign).
