### Sign-by-Sign Assessment

#### 🔵 Effusion Signs
- **Quad Sign ✅** — Anechoic (black) space clearly bounded superiorly by pleural line, with lateral rib shadows forming a quadrilateral geometry
- **Jellyfish Sign ✅** — The bright echogenic structure in the dependent portion visibly undulates and shifts position across frames, consistent with atelectatic lung **floating within** surrounding effusion
- **Sinusoid Sign** — Suggested by the respiratory-cycle-dependent movement of the deep lung line toward the pleural line (best confirmed in M-mode)

#### 🔴 Consolidation Signs
- **Tissue-like hepatization ✅** — Particularly in **frames 7–10**, the dependent lung parenchyma transitions to a **solid, liver-like echogenicity**, consistent with compressive atelectasis or frank consolidation
- **Air bronchograms ✅** — Punctate/linear hyperechoic foci are visible within the hepatized tissue in later frames

### Conclusion

> **plaps = `true`**
> **type = `"both"`**

**Both pleural effusion and pulmonary consolidation are present:**
- A **moderate-to-large pleural effusion** is identified by the quad sign and anechoic fluid collection
- **Jellyfish sign** confirms atelectatic lung floating within the effusion
- **Hepatization with air bronchograms** in the dependent zone indicates **compressive atelectasis/consolidation** beneath the effusion

This pattern is highly consistent with a **PLAPS-positive posterior zone**, commonly seen in pneumonia with parapneumonic effusion, or heart failure with compressive atelectasis.
