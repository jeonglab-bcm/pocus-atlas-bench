## Sign-by-Sign Assessment

### Effusion Signs
- ✅ **Quad Sign**: The anechoic space is clearly bounded superiorly by the pleural line, inferiorly by the lung/diaphragm interface, and laterally by rib shadows — forming a classic quadrilateral fluid pocket
- ✅ **Jellyfish Sign**: Across frames 5–8, a **rounded echogenic structure** is visible floating within the anechoic fluid, undulating with respiratory motion — consistent with atelectatic lung suspended in effusion

### Consolidation Signs
- ✅ **Tissue-like Hepatization**: The floating structure exhibits **homogeneous echogenicity resembling liver parenchyma**, indicating consolidated/atelectatic lung
- ⚠️ **Shred Sign**: Irregular deep border is suggested at the inferior margin of the floating tissue
- ❓ **Air Bronchograms**: Cannot be definitively confirmed in these frames

**Rationale**: This PLAPS-point clip demonstrates a **moderate-to-large pleural effusion** (quad sign) with a **floating consolidated/atelectatic lung lobe** (jellyfish sign + hepatization), representing the classic combined PLAPS pattern. This pattern is typically associated with:
- Heart failure (transudative effusion + compressive atelectasis), or
