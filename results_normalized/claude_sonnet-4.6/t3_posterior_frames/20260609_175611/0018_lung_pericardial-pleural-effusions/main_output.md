# 0018_lung_pericardial-pleural-effusions

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point (Sequential Frames)

---

### Systematic Observations

#### **Pleural/Superficial Layer**
- The pleural line is identifiable in the near field across all frames
- Rib shadows are visible bilaterally, forming the lateral boundaries of the field

#### **Anechoic Space (Effusion)**
- A **clear anechoic (black) quadrilateral space** is consistently visible in the dependent/posterior region
- Bounded superiorly by the pleural line and laterally by rib shadows → **Quad Sign ✓**
- The fluid collection appears substantial and persistent across all frames

#### **Echogenic Tissue Within/Adjacent to Fluid**
- In multiple frames (especially frames 3–10), a **tissue-like echogenic structure** is visible floating within or at the inferior margin of the anechoic space
- This structure demonstrates:
  - **Hepatized (liver-like) texture** — loss of aeration artifacts, solid echotexture → **Tissue Hepatization ✓**
  - An **irregular, shredded deep border** → **Shred Sign ✓**
  - The echogenic mass appears to **undulate/shift position** across sequential frames → **Jellyfish Sign ✓** (atelectatic lung floating within effusion)

### Conclusion

```
plaps = true
type  = "both"
```

**Reasoning:**
- **Effusion component**: Quad sign with large anechoic dependent collection
- **Consolidation component**: Hepatized lung parenchyma with shred sign and irregular borders
- **Jellyfish sign**: Atelectatic/consolidated lung floating within the surrounding effusion — the hallmark of compressive/relaxation atelectasis secondary to a significant pleural effusion

> This pattern is classic for a **moderate-to-large pleural effusion with compressive atelectasis** of the posterior basal lung segment, a common PLAPS-positive finding in critically ill patients (e.g., heart failure, pneumonia with parapneumonic effusion, or post-operative states).
