# Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point Assessment

## Sequential Frame Observations

### Frames 1–3 (Early sequence)
- A **large anechoic (black) space** dominates the superior/posterior field
- The dark space is clearly bounded by the **pleural line superiorly** and echogenic rib/diaphragm structures laterally → consistent with **Quad Sign**
- The effusion appears **moderate-to-large** in volume

### Frames 4–5
- Transition zone becomes visible: **tissue-like echogenicity** (liver-like texture) appears adjacent to the anechoic space
- This hepatized tissue represents **consolidated/atelectatic lung**
- The deep border of the solid tissue shows an **irregular, shredded interface** → consistent with **Shred Sign**

### Frames 6–7 (Mid-sequence)
- The **consolidated lung** is seen as a rounded, echogenic mass **floating within the effusion** → consistent with **Jellyfish Sign**
- The lung fragment undulates slightly between frames

### Frames 8–10 (Late sequence)
- Within the hepatized lung, **small punctate/linear hyperechoic foci** are visible → consistent with **Air Bronchograms**
- Two distinct anechoic regions are visible: the **pleural effusion** and a separate rounded structure (likely a vessel in cross-section)
- The effusion-consolidation interface remains clearly demarcated

## Conclusion

> **plaps = `true`**
> **type = `"both"`**

There is a **moderate-to-large pleural effusion** (quad sign + jellyfish sign) combined with **ipsilateral lung consolidation/atelectasis** (hepatization + shred sign + air bronchograms) in the posterior dependent zone. This is a **classic PLAPS-positive pattern** indicating both fluid and alveolar pathology — most consistent with **compressive atelectasis from effusion** (possibly with superimposed pneumonia given the air bronchograms).
