# 0029_lung_parapneumonic-effusion

# PLAPS Point Lung Ultrasound Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### Frames 1–3 (Early cycle)
- A **mixed echogenicity pattern** is emerging in the posterior/dependent zone
- An **anechoic (black) region** is visible adjacent to a brighter structure — consistent with early visualization of a **pleural effusion**
- The pleural line and a deeper lung line appear separated by this fluid space, forming a nascent **Quad sign**

### Frames 4–6 (Mid-cycle)
- The **anechoic effusion space** remains visible
- A **hyperechoic, solid-appearing mass** begins to dominate the field — the texture is coarse and homogeneous, **resembling liver parenchyma** → consistent with **hepatization (consolidation)**
- Small **hyperechoic punctate/linear foci** are visible within this solid region → **air bronchograms**

### Frames 7–10 (Late cycle)
- A **large, echogenic solid mass** occupies much of the posterior lung field — definitive **tissue-like hepatization**
- **Air bronchograms** are clearly visible as bright foci within the consolidated tissue
- The deep border of the consolidated region is **irregular and shredded** → **Shred sign**
- A **surrounding anechoic halo** persists adjacent to the consolidated tissue → **effusion component**

## Conclusion

> **plaps = true**
> **type = "both"**

There is **coexisting pleural effusion** (anechoic fluid collection, quad sign) **and alveolar consolidation** (hepatized lung, air bronchograms, shred sign) in the posterolateral dependent zone — a classic **PLAPS-positive** finding most consistent with **parapneumonic effusion with adjacent consolidation** (e.g., pneumonia with reactive effusion, or compressive/absorptive atelectasis).
