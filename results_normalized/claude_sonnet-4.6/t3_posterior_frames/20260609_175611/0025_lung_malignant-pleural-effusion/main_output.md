# 0025_lung_malignant-pleural-effusion

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point Assessment

### Technical Parameters
- **Probe**: S4-2 (phased array sector probe, cardiac preset)
- **Depth**: 20.0 cm | **Frame rate**: 27 Hz
- **Machine**: Philips | **View**: Posterolateral (PLAPS point)

---

### Frame-by-Frame Observations

| Finding | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Deep border** | Bright echogenic diaphragm visible inferiorly |
| **Pleural space** | Large **anechoic (black) region** occupying the mid-to-posterior field — consistent with **free fluid** |
| **Lung parenchyma** | Echogenic, **liver-like (hepatized)** tissue visible within/adjacent to the fluid collection |
| **Internal echotexture** | Multiple **punctate/irregular hyperechoic foci** within the hepatized lung — consistent with **air bronchograms** |
| **Deep lung border** | Irregular, **shredded margin** at the fluid-tissue interface — consistent with **shred sign** |
| **Dynamic changes** | Across sequential frames, the hepatized tissue shows undulating movement within the surrounding anechoic collection — consistent with **jellyfish sign** |

---

### Signs Identified

#### ✅ Effusion Signs
- **Quad sign**: Anechoic space bounded by pleural/rib shadows
- **Jellyfish sign**: Atelectatic hepatized lung floating/undulating within effusion (frames 5–8 most prominent)

#### ✅ Consolidation Signs
- **Hepatization**: Tissue-like echogenicity replacing aerated lung
- **Air bronchograms**: Punctate hyperechoic foci within consolidated parenchyma
- **Shred sign**: Irregular deep border at aerated/consolidated lung interface

---

### Conclusion

```
plaps = true
type  = "both"
```

**Interpretation**: This PLAPS-point clip demonstrates a **significant posterolateral pleural effusion** with underlying **compressive/obstructive lung consolidation**. The sequential frames confirm dynamic jellyfish movement of atelectatic hepatized lung within the surrounding effusion, alongside air bronchograms indicating partial aeration of the consolidated parenchyma. This pattern is characteristic of **large pleural effusion with compressive atelectasis**, potentially with co-existing pneumonia or significant pulmonary consolidation.

> ⚠️ *Clinical correlation with respiratory status, chest X-ray, and prior imaging is recommended to differentiate transudative from exudative effusion and assess for pneumonia vs. atelectasis.*
