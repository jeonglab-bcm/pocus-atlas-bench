# 0126_lung_jr_loculated-pleural-effusion

# Lung Ultrasound — PLAPS Point Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### Frame 1 (Early)
- A **prominent anechoic (black) space** is visible in the upper-middle field
- This dark space is bounded superiorly by the pleural line and inferiorly by a deeper reflective structure
- Consistent with **Quad Sign** → **pleural effusion**

### Frames 2–5 (Mid-sequence)
- The anechoic space **persists**, but an echogenic tissue-like mass begins to appear within/adjacent to it
- The echogenic zone shows **hepatized texture** (similar to liver parenchyma)
- An **irregular, shredded deep border** is visible → **Shred Sign**

### Frames 6–10 (Late)
- The **hepatized zone becomes more prominent** across frames with respiratory motion
- Hyperechoic linear/punctate foci are visible within the consolidated tissue → **Air Bronchograms**
- The consolidated tissue appears to **undulate within the fluid** in some frames → possible **Jellyfish Sign**
- The deep border remains irregular, confirming the shred sign

## Conclusion

> **plaps = `true`**
> **type = `"both"`**

Both a **pleural effusion** (anechoic quad sign space) and **alveolar consolidation** (hepatization, shred sign, air bronchograms) are present in the posterior dependent lung zone. This pattern is consistent with a **PLAPS-positive** scan — likely representing a **parapneumonic effusion with adjacent or dependent consolidation** (e.g., pneumonia with reactive effusion, or compressive/resorptive atelectasis within an effusion).
