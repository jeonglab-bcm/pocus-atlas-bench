# 0143_lung_jr_pleuraleffusion

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point

### Sequential Frame Observations

**Frames 1–4:**
- A hyperechoic pleural line is visible superficially
- Below the pleural line, there is **tissue-like (hepatized) echogenicity** — the lung parenchyma has lost normal aeration and resembles liver texture
- The deep border is **irregular and shredded**, consistent with the **shred sign** (interface between consolidated and residually aerated lung)
- Small hyperechoic punctate/linear foci are visible within the tissue — consistent with **static air bronchograms**

**Frames 5–6:**
- A relatively **anechoic (dark) space** becomes apparent adjacent to and surrounding the consolidated tissue
- This bounded hypoechoic region is consistent with a **small pleural effusion** (possible **quad sign** component)
- Dynamic change across frames hints at respiratory variation (**possible sinusoid sign**)

**Frames 7–10:**
- Return to dominant **hepatization pattern**
- Continued air bronchogram foci within consolidated parenchyma
- Irregular deep margin persists

---

### Conclusion

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| **PLAPS** | ✅ `true` |
| **Type** | `"both"` |
| **Primary** | Pulmonary consolidation (hepatization + shred sign + air bronchograms) |
| **Secondary** | Small adjacent pleural effusion (anechoic bounded space, frames 5–6) |

> **Summary:** This PLAPS-positive study demonstrates posterior lung consolidation with hepatization as the dominant finding, accompanied by a small pleural effusion. The combination is consistent with pneumonia-associated parapneumonic effusion or atelectasis with effusion.
