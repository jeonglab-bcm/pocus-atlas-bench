## Sign-by-Sign Assessment

### 🔵 Effusion Signs
- **Quad Sign ✅**: An anechoic quadrilateral space is visible between the pleural line (superiorly) and the lung line (inferiorly), bounded laterally by rib shadows — consistent with **pleural effusion**
- **Jellyfish Sign ✅** (frames 6–10): The echogenic consolidated tissue appears to float and undulate within the anechoic fluid across sequential frames — strongly suggestive of **atelectatic lung within effusion**
- **Sinusoid Sign**: Cannot fully assess without M-mode, but respiratory variation in lung line position is suggested

### 🟠 Consolidation Signs
- **Tissue-like hepatization ✅**: The deeper left-sided structure demonstrates **homogeneous, granular echogenicity resembling liver parenchyma** — indicating loss of aeration
- **Shred Sign ✅**: The deep border of the consolidated region is **irregular and shredded**, demarcating consolidated from partially aerated lung
- **Air bronchograms ✅**: Punctate/linear hyperechoic foci are visible **within** the hepatized tissue across multiple frames

## Conclusion

> **plaps = `true`**
> **type = `"both"`**

Both **pleural effusion** (quad sign, jellyfish sign) and **lung consolidation** (hepatization, shred sign, air bronchograms) are present in the posterior dependent zone. This is a **classic PLAPS pattern** indicating posterior alveolar and pleural pathology — most commonly seen in pneumonia with parapneumonic effusion, or in cardiac/non-cardiac pulmonary edema with dependent atelectasis.
