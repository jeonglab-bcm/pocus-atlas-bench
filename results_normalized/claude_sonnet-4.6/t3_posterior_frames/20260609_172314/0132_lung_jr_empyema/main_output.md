# 0132_lung_jr_empyema

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point (Sequential Frames)

---

### Systematic Observation

#### Superficial Structures
- Normal skin, subcutaneous tissue, and muscle layers are visible in the near field
- The **pleural line** is identifiable at the top of the deep field

#### Dominant Finding — Large Anechoic Space
Across **all 10 frames**, a **large, homogeneous anechoic (black) region** occupies the majority of the image depth — consistent with a **significant pleural effusion**

> ✅ **Quad Sign present**: The fluid is bounded superiorly by the pleural line and laterally by rib/soft tissue shadows, forming a classic quadrilateral fluid space

---

#### Dynamic Findings Across Frames

| Frames | Observation |
|--------|-------------|
| 1–2 | Large anechoic space dominates; faint deep echogenic structure emerging |
| 3–4 | Heterogeneous, **hepatized (tissue-like)** echogenic mass becomes visible within the fluid |
| 5–7 | **Floating, irregularly-bordered echogenic structure** clearly suspended within the effusion — undulating with respiration |
| 8–10 | Structure shifts position slightly, confirming **respiratory mobility** |

> ✅ **Jellyfish Sign present**: Atelectatic/consolidated lung tissue visibly **floating and undulating** within the surrounding effusion — characteristic "jellyfish in water" appearance

---

#### Consolidated Lung Tissue
The floating structure demonstrates:
- **Hepatization**: Solid, liver-like echotexture replacing normal aerated lung
- **Loss of A-lines** within the structure
- Irregular deep border suggesting a **shred sign** interface between consolidated and residually aerated lung
- Possible punctate hyperechoic foci consistent with **air bronchograms**

---

### Conclusion

```
plaps  = true
type   = "both"
```

**Rationale:**
- 🌊 **Large pleural effusion** — Quad sign confirmed (anechoic fluid with characteristic boundaries)
- 🪼 **Jellyfish sign** — compressive/relaxation atelectatic lung floating within effusion
- 🫁 **Consolidation** — hepatized, tissue-like echogenicity of the dependent atelectatic lung lobe (likely compressive atelectasis secondary to the effusion, though superimposed pneumonia cannot be excluded)

**Clinical correlation recommended** — consider thoracentesis evaluation; if consolidation features are disproportionate to effusion size, infectious consolidation (parapneumonic effusion) should be excluded.
