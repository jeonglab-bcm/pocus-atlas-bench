## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### Frames 1–3 (Early respiratory phase)
- **Large anechoic (black) space** occupies the central dependent zone between the pleural line superiorly and the spine posteriorly
- Bounded laterally by rib acoustic shadows → **Quad sign ✅**
- **Spine sign ✅**: The vertebral column is clearly visible *above* the diaphragm — this only occurs when fluid (not air) replaces aerated lung, confirming a **supradiaphragmatic effusion**

### Frames 4–7 (Mid-phase)
- Heterogeneous **tissue-like echogenic mass** begins entering the anechoic field from the left/anterior margin
- Echo texture is **liver-like (hepatized)** with loss of normal aeration artifacts → **Hepatization ✅**
- The consolidated tissue has an **irregular, shredded deep border** → **Shred sign ✅**

### Frames 8–10 (Late respiratory phase)
- The hepatized lung mass **floats and undulates** within the surrounding anechoic effusion
- Motion is buoyant and rhythmic with respiration → **Jellyfish sign ✅**
- Punctate and linear hyperechoic foci are visible *within* the consolidated parenchyma → **Air bronchograms ✅**

> **Interpretation**: This PLAPS-point clip demonstrates a **moderate-to-large pleural effusion** (anechoic quad, spine sign, jellyfish sign) with **underlying compressive/consolidative atelectasis** (hepatized lung with shred sign and air bronchograms) floating within the effusion.
