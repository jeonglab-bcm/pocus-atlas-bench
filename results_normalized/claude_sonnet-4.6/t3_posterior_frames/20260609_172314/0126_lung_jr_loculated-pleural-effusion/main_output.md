# 0126_lung_jr_loculated-pleural-effusion

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point

---

### Frame-by-Frame Observations

#### **Frames 1–2 (Early respiratory phase)**
- A **dark (anechoic) region** is visible in the near-to-mid field, bordered superiorly by the pleural line and laterally by rib shadows
- This quadrilateral anechoic space is consistent with a **Quad Sign** → **pleural effusion**

#### **Frames 3–10 (Progressive respiratory phases)**
- The deep lung parenchyma becomes increasingly visible, displaying:
  - **Tissue-like hepatization**: echogenicity resembling liver texture, loss of normal A-line/aeration pattern
  - **Shred sign**: irregular, jagged deep border between consolidated and partially aerated lung tissue
  - **Air bronchograms**: punctate and linear hyperechoic foci scattered within the hepatized tissue, most evident in frames 6–10
- The consolidated lung tissue undulates with respiration, potentially showing elements of a **Jellyfish sign** within the effusion

### Conclusion

> **plaps = `true`**
> **type = `"both"`**

There is evidence of **pleural effusion** (quad sign, early frames) **combined with pulmonary consolidation** (hepatization, shred sign, air bronchograms). This pattern is consistent with a **compressive/obstructive atelectasis with parapneumonic effusion**, or alternatively **pneumonia with reactive effusion** — clinical correlation is required.
