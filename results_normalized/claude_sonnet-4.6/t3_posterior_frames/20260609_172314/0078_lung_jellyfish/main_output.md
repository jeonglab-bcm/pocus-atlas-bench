# 0078_lung_jellyfish

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — Right Posterior Zone (PLAPS Point)

---

### Frame-by-Frame Observations

#### **Frames 1–5 (Early Phase)**
- The dependent (lower) portion of the field shows a **predominantly anechoic/hypoechoic space**
- The superior pleural line and inferior lung line are visible, with rib shadows laterally → consistent with the **Quad Sign**
- The echogenic content within this space is sparse, suggesting a **free fluid collection (pleural effusion)**
- The lung line is not sharply defined, showing subtle respiratory excursion → early **Sinusoid sign**

#### **Frames 6–10 (Later Phase)**
- A **large, solid, echogenic structure** emerges progressively and dominates the image
- Texture closely resembles liver parenchyma → **Tissue-like hepatization** (lung consolidation)
- The deep border of the solid tissue is **irregular and non-linear** → **Shred sign**
- Multiple **punctate and linear hyperechoic foci** are visible within the hepatized tissue → **Air bronchograms**
- Critically, the solid structure appears to **float and undulate** within the surrounding anechoic effusion across sequential frames → **Jellyfish sign**

**Interpretation:** This clip demonstrates a classic **PLAPS pattern combining pleural effusion and lung consolidation**. The early frames reveal a free fluid collection (quad sign), while the later frames show atelectatic/consolidated lung floating within the effusion (jellyfish sign), hepatized parenchyma with an irregular deep margin (shred sign), and internal air bronchograms. This pattern is typically seen in **pneumonia with parapneumonic effusion** or **compressive atelectasis** secondary to a moderate-to-large effusion.
