# 0143_lung_jr_pleuraleffusion

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point (Sequential Frames)

---

### Observations

#### Pleural Line
- A bright, hyperechoic pleural line is consistently visible in the near field across all frames.
- No normal sliding artifact or A-line pattern is present deep to it.

#### Deep Field — Anechoic Space
- A **large anechoic (black) region** dominates the deep portion of virtually all frames.
- It is bounded **superiorly** by the pleural line and laterally by rib/soft tissue interfaces — consistent with a **Quad sign** (pleural effusion).
- The effusion appears **substantial** in volume, occupying a large portion of the image depth.

#### Internal Structure Within the Dark Space
- In frames **5–10**, a **bright echogenic structure** is visible floating at the superior border of the anechoic space — it shifts slightly frame-to-frame.
- This echogenic mass has a **tissue-like (hepatized) texture**, irregular margins, and appears to undulate — consistent with:
  - **Jellyfish sign** (atelectatic/consolidated lung floating within effusion), and
  - **Tissue-like hepatization** of the consolidated lung

#### Shred Sign / Air Bronchograms
- The deep border of the echogenic tissue is **irregular and shredded**, suggesting the **shred sign**.
- Faint punctate hyperechoic foci within the echogenic tissue are suggestive of **air bronchograms**.

---

### Classification

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| **PLAPS** | ✅ **True** |
| **Type** | **Both** |
| **Effusion signs** | Quad sign ✅, Jellyfish sign ✅ |
| **Consolidation signs** | Hepatization ✅, Shred sign ✅, Air bronchograms ✅ |

---

### Conclusion

> **PLAPS = true | Type = "both"**
>
> This posterior zone demonstrates a **large pleural effusion** (Quad sign) with **atelectatic/consolidated lung** (jellyfish sign, hepatization, shred sign) floating within the fluid — a classic PLAPS pattern indicating posterior alveolar and pleural syndrome, commonly seen in pneumonia, cardiac failure, or malignant effusion with compressive atelectasis.
