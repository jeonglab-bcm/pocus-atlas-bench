# 0025_lung_malignant-pleural-effusion

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point

---

### Frame-by-Frame Observations

#### **Echogenicity & Texture**
Across all frames, the posterior lung zone demonstrates **solid, tissue-like (hepatized) echogenicity** replacing the normal aerated lung appearance. The echotexture closely resembles that of the liver or spleen — the hallmark of **pulmonary consolidation**.

#### **Air Bronchograms**
Within the hepatized tissue, multiple **punctate and branching hyperechoic foci** are visible (most prominent in frames 7–10). These represent **air-filled bronchi** (air bronchograms) surrounded by fluid-filled/collapsed alveoli — a classic sign of consolidation.

#### **Shred Sign**
The **deep border** of the consolidated tissue is **irregular and shredded**, consistent with the interface between consolidated and partially aerated lung parenchyma (**shred sign** ✓).

#### **Pleural Effusion Component**
In the **inferior/dependent portion** of multiple frames (particularly frames 2–4), there is a **crescentic anechoic space** between the visceral pleural line and the diaphragm, suggesting a **co-existing pleural effusion**. The **quad sign** geometry (bounded superiorly by pleural line, inferiorly by diaphragm/lung line) is partially visible.

### Conclusion

> **plaps = `true`**
> **type = `"both"`**

There is **dominant alveolar consolidation** with hepatization, air bronchograms, and a shredded deep border, **plus a co-existing pleural effusion** in the dependent zone — a classic **PLAPS pattern** consistent with pneumonia, aspiration, or other consolidative process with parapneumonic effusion.
