### Frame-by-Frame Observations

#### **Echogenicity & Texture**
Across all 10 frames, the posterior lung zone demonstrates a **solid, tissue-like (hepatized) echogenicity**, replacing normal aeration artifacts (no A-lines visible). The parenchymal texture closely mimics that of liver — a hallmark of **pulmonary consolidation**.

#### **Air Bronchograms**
Prominently visible in nearly every frame are **bright, hyperechoic branching/linear structures** ramifying through the hepatized lung tissue. These represent **air-filled bronchi within consolidated parenchyma** — classic dynamic or static air bronchograms. Their tree-like distribution is highly characteristic.

#### **Shred Sign**
The deep border of the consolidated zone appears **irregular and fragmented** (particularly frames 3, 4, 7, 8), forming the **shred sign** — the interface between consolidated and residually aerated lung.

#### **Anechoic Zones (Effusion Component)**
In frames **1, 6, 9, and 10**, a **rounded to ovoid anechoic (black) space** is visible adjacent to or partially surrounding the consolidation. This is **bounded superiorly by the pleural line** and laterally by rib shadows, consistent with a **quad sign** — indicating a **small pleural effusion**. The consolidation appears to be partially "floating" or surrounded by fluid in these frames, suggesting a possible early **jellyfish sign**.
