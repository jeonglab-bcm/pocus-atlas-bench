## Sign-Specific Analysis

### ✅ Tissue-Like Hepatization (Consolidation)
Present in **all frames** — the lung parenchyma has lost its normal aeration and appears **solid and liver-like** in echotexture, indistinguishable from hepatic tissue. This is the dominant finding.

### ✅ Air Bronchograms
Multiple **punctate and short linear hyperechoic foci** are visible within the consolidated parenchyma (most evident in frames 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10). These represent **static air bronchograms** — air trapped in bronchi surrounded by fluid-filled/consolidated alveoli.

### ✅ Anechoic Fluid Component (Effusion)
In **frames 3 and 7** (respiratory phases), a **curved anechoic (black) crescent** becomes visible at the superior margin of the consolidated lung — consistent with a **pleural effusion**. The variation in this fluid space across respiratory frames is compatible with a **sinusoid-type dynamic**.

### ✅ Jellyfish Sign (Probable)
The change in the shape and position of the consolidated lung across frames — particularly the rounded, undulating appearance in frames 3 and 7 versus the flatter profile in other frames — is consistent with **atelectatic/consolidated lung floating within surrounding fluid**, characteristic of the **jellyfish sign**.

### ✅ Shred Sign
The deep border of the consolidated tissue in some frames shows a slightly **irregular, shredded interface** between consolidated and partially aerated lung.
