# 0004_lung_pleural-effusion-with-hiatal-hernia

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point (Sequential Frames)

---

### Systematic Observations

#### 1. Pleural Line & Near Field
- The pleural line is visible in the near field across all frames
- No prominent A-lines are identified deep to the pleural line
- Normal sliding appears absent or attenuated over the central structure

#### 2. Central Dominant Finding — **Hepatization**
Across all 10 sequential frames, a **large, rounded, solid-appearing mass** occupies the central-to-left portion of the image. This structure demonstrates:
- **Tissue-like (hepatic) echotexture** — echogenicity and granularity closely resembling liver parenchyma
- Loss of normal aeration artifacts (no A-lines, no B-lines arising from this region)
- **Dynamic respiratory motion** — the consolidated structure visibly descends and shifts slightly across frames, confirming it is lung parenchyma moving with breathing

#### 3. Air Bronchograms
Within the hepatized tissue, **punctate and short linear hyperechoic foci** are present (best seen in frames 4–7), consistent with:
- **Static air bronchograms** — trapped air within bronchi surrounded by consolidated alveoli

#### 4. Deep Border
The deep margin of the consolidated tissue is **irregular and shredded** in several frames, consistent with a **shred sign** at the interface between consolidated and residually aerated lung

#### 5. Right-Sided Bright Structure
A **wedge-shaped hyperechoic structure** (right of each frame) represents the **diaphragm ± underlying liver/spleen** — a normal anatomical landmark at the PLAPS point

#### 6. Effusion Assessment
- **No clearly anechoic (black) quadrilateral space** is identified between the pleural line and a lung line (no Quad sign)
- **No floating atelectatic lung** within free fluid (no Jellyfish sign)
- A **very thin anechoic rim** may exist at the diaphragmatic interface in frames 8–10, but insufficient to qualify as a dominant effusion

### Conclusion

> **plaps = `true`**
> **type = `"consolidation"`**

The posterior lateral lung zone demonstrates a classic **PLAPS consolidation pattern**: hepatized lung with internal air bronchograms and an irregular shred sign at the deep margin. There is no dominant free pleural effusion. This pattern is consistent with **alveolar consolidation** (e.g., pneumonia, atelectasis, or contusion) in the dependent posterior zone.
