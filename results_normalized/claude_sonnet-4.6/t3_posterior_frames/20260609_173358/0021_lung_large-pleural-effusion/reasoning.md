# 0021_lung_large-pleural-effusion — Reasoning

Let me analyze these sequential frames from a lung ultrasound video clip of the PLAPS point (Posterolateral Alveolar and/or Pleural Syndrome).

Looking at the 10 sequential frames carefully:

1. The probe marker "P" is visible at the top of each frame, indicating the posterior/lateral lung zone assessment.

2. What I can observe across the frames:
- There appears to be a large anechoic (black) space in the lower portion of the images - this is consistent with a pleural effusion (Quad sign appearance)
- Above the anechoic space, there appears to be echogenic tissue with a texture similar to liver parenchyma (hepatization pattern)


- The consolidated lung tissue has an irregular, jagged border rather than a smooth edge
- I can see hyperechoic spots and lines within the consolidation that likely represent air bronchograms
- There's undulating material visible within the effusion across the frames, suggesting atelectatic lung floating in the fluid - the classic jellyfish sign
- The respiratory motion is evident in the later frames as the lung tissue shifts position with breathing

Looking at the frame progression, the early images show a stable effusion with consolidated tissue above it, but by frames 5-8 the consolidated lung starts to move and float within the effusion, becoming even more pronounced in frames 9-10. This pattern of floating consolidated/atelectatic lung within pleural effusion is characteristic of the jellyfish sign, and combined with the quad sign appearance of the effusion itself, this points to both effusion and consolidation with atelectasis present. I can also see air bronchograms within the consolidated tissue, which confirms the consolidation pattern. So my final assessment is PLAPS positive with both effusion and consolidation—the large anechoic space represents the pleural fluid, and the floating echogenic material within it shows the jellyfish sign from atelectatic lung.
