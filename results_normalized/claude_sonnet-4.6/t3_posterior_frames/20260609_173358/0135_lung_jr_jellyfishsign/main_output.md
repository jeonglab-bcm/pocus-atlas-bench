# Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point (Sequential Frames)

---

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### Frames 1–2 (Early):
- Predominantly **anechoic (black) space** deep to the pleural line
- Bounded superiorly by the pleural line and laterally by rib shadows
- Forms a **quadrilateral configuration → Quad Sign** suggesting free pleural fluid
- Lung line is pushed inferiorly by fluid

### Frames 3–5 (Transitional):
- Progressive appearance of **echogenic material** emerging from the dependent zone
- Irregular, poorly-defined deep border beginning to form
- Transition from anechoic fluid to tissue-like echogenicity

### Frames 6–10 (Late):
- **Tissue-like hepatization**: solid, homogeneously echogenic parenchyma resembling liver texture — loss of normal aeration artifacts
- **Shred sign**: irregular, ragged deep margin between consolidated and partially aerated lung
- **Air bronchograms**: punctate/linear hyperechoic foci visible within the consolidated tissue
- Consolidated lung mass occupies the majority of the field

## Conclusion

```
plaps = true
type = "both"
```

**Reasoning:** The early frames demonstrate a **pleural effusion** (quad sign, anechoic fluid), while the middle-to-late frames reveal **consolidated/hepatized lung** floating within or adjacent to the fluid — with shred sign and air bronchograms confirming alveolar consolidation. This pattern (effusion + consolidation together) is the hallmark PLAPS finding consistent with **pneumonia with parapneumonic effusion** or **atelectasis with effusion**.
