# 0025_lung_malignant-pleural-effusion

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point (Sequential Frames)

---

### Systematic Observation

#### Pleural Line / Superficial Zone
- The pleural line is identifiable across frames, though partially obscured by overlying tissue artifact in some views.
- No clear A-line reverberation pattern is present → normal aeration is absent.

---

#### Deep Lung Zone — Dominant Findings

**1. Hepatization (Tissue Sign)**
Across virtually all frames, the lung parenchyma has lost its normal echogenic appearance and instead demonstrates a **solid, homogeneous, liver-like echotexture** — the hallmark of alveolar consolidation.

**2. Air Bronchograms**
Multiple **punctate and linear hyperechoic foci** are distributed throughout the hepatized parenchyma. These bright structures within consolidated tissue represent **air-filled bronchi** (static air bronchograms). Their distribution and appearance are clearly visible in frames 2–10, giving a characteristic **"Swiss cheese"** or **"starry sky within consolidation"** pattern.

**3. Shred Sign**
The deep border of the consolidated zone is **irregular and fragmented** — not a smooth lung line — consistent with the **shred sign**, marking the interface between consolidated and residually aerated lung.

**4. Effusion Component**
In several frames (particularly frames 1, 5–6), an **anechoic (black) crescent/band** is visible in the inferior-dependent portion of the image, bounded superiorly by the pleural line and consistent with a **quad sign** — indicating a **small to moderate pleural effusion** layering posteriorly.

A potential **jellyfish sign** may be present in some frames, with the consolidated/atelectatic lung appearing to float within surrounding fluid.

### Conclusion

> **plaps = `true`**
> **type = `"both"`**

There is a **PLAPS-positive posterior zone** demonstrating:
- **Dominant alveolar consolidation** with hepatization, air bronchograms, and shred sign
- **Associated pleural effusion** (quad sign) in the dependent zone

This pattern is consistent with **posterior consolidation** (e.g., pneumonia, atelectasis, or contusion) **with parapneumonic or reactive effusion**, as commonly seen at the PLAPS point in the BLUE protocol framework.
