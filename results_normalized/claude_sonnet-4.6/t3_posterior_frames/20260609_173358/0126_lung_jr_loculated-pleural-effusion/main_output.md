# Lung Ultrasound — PLAPS Point Analysis

## Sequential Frame Observations

### Frames 1–2 (Early respiratory phase)
- A **predominantly anechoic (dark) region** is visible in the upper/mid field, bounded by echogenic borders — consistent with a **pleural effusion** (Quad sign morphology)
- Sparse echogenic material is faintly present within the fluid

### Frames 3–6 (Mid respiratory cycle)
- **Echogenic, heterogeneous tissue** progressively enters the field, appearing to **float and undulate** within the anechoic fluid space → consistent with a **Jellyfish sign** (atelectatic/consolidated lung moving within surrounding effusion)
- The tissue texture is coarse and liver-like (**hepatization**)

### Frames 7–10 (Late phase)
- **Tissue-like hepatization** is now prominent and fills a significant portion of the field
- The **deep margin is irregular and shredded** → **Shred sign**
- Scattered **hyperechoic foci** within the consolidated parenchyma → probable **air bronchograms**
- Anechoic fluid remains partially visible surrounding the consolidated tissue

## Conclusion

> **plaps = true**
> **type = "both"**

There is a **posterolateral pleural effusion** with a **floating, atelectatic/consolidated lung** (jellyfish sign) and underlying **pulmonary consolidation** with hepatization, shredded deep border, and probable air bronchograms. This is a classic combined PLAPS pattern.
