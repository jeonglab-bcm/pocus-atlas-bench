# 0018_lung_pericardial-pleural-effusions

## Lung Ultrasound Analysis — PLAPS Point (Sequential Frames)

---

### Systematic Observation

#### **Pleural Line & Superficial Zone**
- A hyperechoic pleural line is identifiable at the top of each frame.
- No normal A-line reverberation artifacts are present in the deep field — normal posterior aeration is absent.

#### **Anechoic (Black) Space — Effusion**
- Across **multiple frames**, a prominent **anechoic (black) quadrilateral space** is consistently visible in the dependent zone.
- This space is bounded:
  - **Superiorly** by the pleural line
  - **Inferiorly/deeply** by a lung/tissue interface
  - **Laterally** by rib/soft tissue margins
- This constitutes a clear **Quad Sign** → **pleural effusion confirmed**

#### **Floating Structure — Jellyfish Sign**
- Within the anechoic effusion (especially frames 3–6), an **echogenic tissue mass** is visible **floating and undulating** with respiratory motion.
- This freely mobile, irregular echogenic structure within the fluid is consistent with the **Jellyfish Sign** — representing **compressive/passive atelectasis** suspended in the effusion.

#### **Hepatized Lung — Consolidation**
- In frames 3–5 particularly, the lung tissue shows **liver-like echogenicity** (tissue sign / hepatization), losing the normal aerated appearance.
- The **deep border** of the consolidated lung is irregular and poorly defined — consistent with a **Shred Sign** (interface between consolidated and partially aerated lung).
- Small **punctate hyperechoic foci** are visible within the echogenic tissue in frames 4–5, suggesting **air bronchograms** within the consolidated parenchyma.

### Conclusion

> **plaps = `true`**
> **type = `"both"`**

This PLAPS point demonstrates a **combined pattern**:
- A **moderate-to-large pleural effusion** (quad sign + jellyfish sign)
- With underlying **consolidation** of the dependent lung (hepatization + shred sign + air bronchograms)

This pattern is classic for **compressive atelectasis within a parapneumonic or transudative/exudative effusion**, or alternatively **pneumonia with associated reactive effusion**.
