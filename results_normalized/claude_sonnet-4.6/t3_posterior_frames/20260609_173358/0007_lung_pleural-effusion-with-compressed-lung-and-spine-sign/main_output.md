# 0007_lung_pleural-effusion-with-compressed-lung-and-spine-sign

## PLAPS Point — Lung Ultrasound Analysis

---

### Frame-by-Frame Observations

#### **Frames 1–3 (Early sequence)**
- A **large anechoic (black) space** dominates the dependent portion of the field — well-defined, bounded superiorly by an echogenic line (pleural line) and laterally by rib shadows.
- This constitutes a clear **Quad Sign** → consistent with **pleural effusion**.
- In Frame 2, an echogenic structure appears to be **floating/undulating within the anechoic fluid** — consistent with a **Jellyfish Sign** (atelectatic lung suspended in effusion).

#### **Frames 4–5 (Transition)**
- The anechoic effusion persists inferiorly.
- Superiorly, the lung parenchyma becomes increasingly echogenic with a **solid, homogeneous, liver-like texture** — indicating **hepatization (consolidation)**.
- Multiple small anechoic foci begin to appear within the parenchyma.

#### **Frames 6–10 (Late sequence)**
- The **hepatized lung** is clearly established — echogenicity and texture closely resemble liver parenchyma.
- **Multiple punctate/round anechoic structures** within the consolidated tissue represent **fluid bronchograms** (larger, tubular) and **hyperechoic foci** consistent with **air bronchograms**.
- One frame shows a bright hyperechoic spot with posterior shadowing — likely a **static air bronchogram**.
- The deep border of the consolidation appears irregular in places — **Shred Sign** present at the interface between consolidated and partially aerated lung.
- A persistent anechoic layer below/adjacent to the consolidation confirms continued **effusion**.

### Conclusion

> **plaps = true**
> **type = "both"**

There is a **combined PLAPS pattern**: a **moderate-to-large pleural effusion** (quad sign, jellyfish sign) coexisting with **lobar/segmental consolidation** (hepatization, air and fluid bronchograms, shred sign) in the posterolateral dependent zone. This pattern is highly consistent with **pneumonia with parapneumonic effusion** or **atelectasis with effusion** in a critically ill patient.
