# Lung Ultrasound PLAPS Point Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### Echogenicity & Tissue Texture
Across all 10 frames, the lung parenchyma demonstrates **solid, liver-like (hepatized) echogenicity**, replacing the normal hyperechoic pleural line with A-line pattern. This is **tissue-like hepatization** — the hallmark of alveolar consolidation.

### Air Bronchograms
Prominently visible throughout are **branching, tree-like hyperechoic foci** within the hepatized tissue. These dendritic branching structures represent **air bronchograms**, consistent with air-filled bronchi retained within consolidated lung. They are distributed extensively through the consolidation volume.

### Anechoic Component
Particularly in frames **1, 6, 9, and 10**, a **rounded/oval anechoic (black) region** is clearly visible adjacent to or embedded within the consolidation. This is consistent with a **fluid component** — either a small parapneumonic/para-consolidative **pleural effusion** or fluid bronchograms. Its rounded morphology and consistent appearance suggests a free fluid collection (**Quad sign component**).

### Deep Border
An **irregular, shredded deep margin** is appreciable between consolidated and residually aerated lung in several frames — consistent with a **shred sign**.
