## Sign-by-Sign Assessment

### ✅ Quad Sign — **PRESENT**
- A **well-defined anechoic (black) space** is consistently visible in the inferior field across all 10 frames
- Bounded **superiorly** by the pleural line and **inferiorly** by the lung line, forming a classic quadrilateral geometry
- The anechoic region is free of internal echoes in early frames → **simple/transudate-type fluid**

### ✅ Sinusoid Sign — **PRESENT (implied)**
- Across sequential frames, the depth/thickness of the anechoic space **fluctuates with the cardiac/respiratory cycle**, consistent with dynamic fluid behavior (sinusoid sign physiology)

### ✅ Jellyfish Sign — **PRESENT**
- In **mid-to-late frames** (frames 5–10), an **echogenic, undulating structure** becomes visible *within* the anechoic fluid — floating and changing shape across frames
- This represents **atelectatic lung buoying within the effusion**, the classic "jellyfish" morphology

**Supporting rationale:**
- **Quad sign** confirms free pleural fluid
- **Sinusoid sign** confirms dynamic, non-loculated effusion
- **Jellyfish sign** indicates associated passive/compressive atelectasis floating within the fluid
- No independent consolidation (hepatization, shred sign, air bronchograms) is identified separate from the fluid-entrained atelectatic lung

> ⚠️ **Clinical note:** The jellyfish sign indicates the effusion is large enough to cause passive lung collapse. Quantification and correlation with clinical status (dyspnea, oxygenation) is recommended to guide thoracentesis decision-making.
