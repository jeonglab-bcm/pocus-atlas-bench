## Key Findings

### ✅ Signs Supporting Sliding PRESENT:
- The pleural line demonstrates a **granular/textured, shimmery appearance** in multiple frames (notably frames 3, 7, 8, 9) — consistent with visceral-parietal pleural interface movement
- **Sub-pleural speckle pattern varies** between consecutive frames, indicating lung tissue movement beneath the pleural interface
- The pleural line is **not** perfectly smooth or rigidly static across all frames
- Superficial soft tissue layers shift slightly between frames, confirming these are temporally distinct frames

### ⚠️ Neutral/Ambiguous Findings:
- **Horizontal A-lines** are visible below the pleural line — these are present in both normal lung (with sliding) and pneumothorax (without sliding), so they are non-discriminatory alone
- **No B-lines (comet-tail artifacts)** are clearly visible — absence of B-lines reduces likelihood of interstitial syndrome but does not confirm pneumothorax
- **No M-mode** provided for seashore vs. barcode sign confirmation

### ❌ Signs Against Absent Sliding:
- The pleural line is **not** uniformly smooth and static across all frames
- A-lines do **not** appear perfectly identical/frozen between all frames
- No transition point (lung point) is identified

---

## Conclusion

> ### **Pleural Sliding: PRESENT**

The sequential frames demonstrate a **shimmering, granular pleural interface** with **inter-frame variation in sub-pleural speckle**, consistent with normal visceral pleural gliding against the parietal pleura. This pattern does **not** support pneumothorax.

**Recommendation:** Confirmatory **M-mode** at this location should reveal the classic **"seashore sign"** (granular sandy pattern below the pleural line), which would definitively confirm sliding. The absence of B-lines in this region is a normal variant and does not independently indicate pathology.
