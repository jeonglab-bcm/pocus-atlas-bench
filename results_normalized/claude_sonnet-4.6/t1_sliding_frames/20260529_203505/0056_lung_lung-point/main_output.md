# 0056_lung_lung-point

# Lung Ultrasound Frame-by-Frame Analysis

## Structural Identification
All frames display:
- **Superficial layers**: Skin, subcutaneous tissue, and parallel echogenic muscle fascial bands (upper ~1 cm)
- **Pleural line**: Bright hyperechoic interface at ~1–1.5 cm depth
- **Sub-pleural zone**: Predominantly hypoechoic region below the pleural line

---

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

| Frames | Key Findings |
|--------|-------------|
| **1–2** | Pleural line is bright but relatively quiet; sub-pleural zone mostly dark; minimal echogenic foci below |
| **3–4** | Small bright foci begin appearing below the pleural line on the left side; slight textural change in pleural line |
| **5–6** | **Notable increase** in bright echogenic clusters below the pleural line — most prominent change across the series; pleural line appears more granular/shimmering |
| **7–8** | Echogenic foci redistribute; posterior acoustic shadowing artifacts shift position; sub-pleural speckle clearly differs from frames 5–6 |
| **9–10** | Bright foci again change in number and distribution; pleural line maintains granular texture |

---

## Sign Assessment

### ✅ Signs of Sliding PRESENT:
- **Shimmering/granular pleural line**: The pleural interface is not perfectly smooth or static — it shows a textured, bright quality
- **Sub-pleural speckle variation**: Clear frame-to-frame differences in the sub-pleural echogenic pattern (especially frames 1–3 vs. frames 5–7)
- **Shifting bright echogenic foci**: Consistent with B-lines/comet-tail artifacts originating from the pleural line and moving with respiration
- **No barcode/stratosphere sign**: Absent — there are no purely static horizontal parallel lines below the pleural interface

### ❌ Signs of Sliding ABSENT:
- None identified

### 🔄 Signs of Lung Point (BOTH):
- None identified — changes in sub-pleural pattern are relatively uniform across the field of view without a discrete transition point

---

## Conclusion

> **Pleural sliding is PRESENT**

The sequential frames demonstrate respiratory-phase–dependent variation in sub-pleural speckle pattern, a granular/shimmering pleural line, and dynamic bright echogenic foci (consistent with B-lines) that change position between frames. There is no barcode sign or static pleural line to suggest pneumothorax. These findings are consistent with **normal lung sliding**, indicating visceral-parietal pleural apposition is intact at this location.
