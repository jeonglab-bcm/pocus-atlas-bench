# 0057_lung_blines-in-pulmonary-contusion

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Frames 1–3
- Pleural line is visible at ~2–2.5 cm depth
- Moderate granularity at the pleural interface
- Sub-pleural speckle **changes subtly between frames**, indicating early sliding motion
- A-lines present but not perfectly static

### Frames 4–6
- Pleural line becomes **notably brighter and more textured** (shimmering quality)
- **Focal bright artifacts** (comet-tail/B-line type) arise from the pleural line bilaterally
- Sub-pleural speckle pattern is **clearly different** frame-to-frame
- Most active phase — consistent with lung apposition against chest wall during inspiration

### Frames 7–8
- Image becomes **predominantly dark** below a curved bright line
- The pleural line appears **smoother and more sharply defined**
- Very little sub-pleural texture variation
- Could represent lung moving away from chest wall (expiratory phase) or a transitional view

### Frames 9–10
- Return to **granular, shimmering** pleural line
- Sub-pleural speckle **resumes variation** between frames
- Horizontal A-lines present but not identical across frames

## Conclusion

> **Pleural sliding is PRESENT**

The dominant pattern across this clip is one of **active lung sliding**, evidenced by:
- A shimmering, textured pleural line in the majority of frames
- Frame-to-frame variation in sub-pleural speckle (the hallmark of sliding)
- Comet-tail artifacts originating at the pleural line

The transient smoother appearance in Frames 7–8 most likely reflects a **respiratory phase** during which the lung briefly retracts, rather than true absence of sliding (no barcode/stratosphere pattern is definitively established, and the bright line returns immediately). There is **no convincing lung point** identified, as the transition is respiratory-cyclic rather than positionally fixed.

**Clinical interpretation:** Normal lung sliding — **pneumothorax at this location is excluded.**
