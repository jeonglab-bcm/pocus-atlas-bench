## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Frames 1–3 (Early sequence)
- Pleural line is **bright and somewhat textured**, not sharply smooth/static
- A **large hypoechoic (dark) subpleural region** is visible, positioned centrally to slightly left
- Small **echogenic foci** (bright spots) appear along the left aspect of the pleural line
- Subtle variation in sub-pleural speckle beginning to emerge

### Frames 4–6 (Mid sequence)
- The **bright echogenic foci migrate centrally**, becoming more numerous and prominent
- The hypoechoic subpleural structure shifts position noticeably
- **Multiple comet-tail–like artifacts** arise from the pleural interface
- Sub-pleural texture is clearly changing between consecutive frames

### Frames 7–10 (Late sequence)
- The hypoechoic structure and echogenic foci have **shifted progressively rightward**
- The pleural line continues to show **textured/shimmering quality** rather than a rigid, static appearance
- Clear **inter-frame variation in sub-pleural speckle** persists throughout

## Conclusion

> **Pleural sliding is PRESENT**

The sequential migration of sub-pleural echogenic foci, the shifting hypoechoic subpleural consolidation (likely moving with tidal lung expansion), the textured/shimmering pleural line, and the clear inter-frame variation in sub-pleural speckle all confirm **active pleural sliding**. There is no evidence of a barcode/stratosphere sign or a static pleural line, and no lung point transition is identified.
