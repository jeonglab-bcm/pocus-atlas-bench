## Sliding Assessment

### ✅ Signs of Sliding PRESENT:
- **B-lines are present** — by definition, B-lines **arise from a moving pleural interface**; they cannot exist without visceral-parietal pleural contact and sliding
- **Pleural line is irregular/granular**, not smooth and static
- **Sub-pleural speckle varies** between sequential frames
- **No barcode/stratosphere sign**

### ❌ No Signs of Absent Sliding:
- No perfectly flat, static pleural line
- No horizontal-only M-mode pattern
- No fixed, immobile A-lines

### ⚠️ No Lung Point Detected:
- Sliding appears **uniform across the entire visible pleural segment**

---

## Conclusion

> **Pleural sliding is PRESENT bilaterally.**

The dominant pathological findings are:
1. **Multiple sub-pleural nodules** — representing miliary granulomas (Mycobacterium tuberculosis hematogenous seeding)
2. **B-line interstitial syndrome** — reflecting interstitial involvement from miliary infiltrates
3. **Intact pleural sliding** — confirming **no pneumothorax**

This pattern is consistent with the labeled diagnosis of **Miliary Tuberculosis**, where preserved pleural sliding with subpleural nodules and B-lines is the characteristic LUS appearance.
