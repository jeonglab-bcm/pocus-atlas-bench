# Right Base Lung Ultrasound — Frame-by-Frame Analysis

## Systematic Frame Review

### Frames 1 & 10 (Comparable end-expiratory phases)
- A **rounded, echogenic structure** (consolidated/atelectatic lung) is visible in the near-to-mid field
- Surrounded by an **anechoic crescent** — consistent with **pleural effusion**
- The lung tissue appears to "present" itself toward the transducer

### Frames 2–4 (Mid-respiratory transition)
- The consolidated lung recedes; a **relatively smooth, linear pleural line** becomes dominant
- Horizontal A-lines visible below
- Subpleural texture appears more static in these frames

### Frames 5–9 (Return/inspiratory phases)
- Progressive **re-emergence of the consolidated/atelectatic lung mass**
- Textural variation in the subpleural region increases
- The interface between effusion and consolidated lung shows **dynamic shimmer**

## Conclusion

### ✅ **Pleural Sliding: PRESENT**

**Supporting evidence:**
1. **Cyclical movement of consolidated/atelectatic lung** within the effusion space — the lung advances and retreats with each respiratory cycle (frames 1→2→3→4→5→...→10), which is the hallmark of preserved visceral pleural motion
2. **Subpleural speckle pattern changes** between consecutive frames — not the static, frozen appearance of absent sliding
3. **Absence of the barcode/stratosphere sign** — no frame shows purely horizontal static lines throughout
4. The **pleural effusion itself serves as an acoustic window**, making the respiratory excursion of the underlying lung clearly visible

> **Clinical Context:** The primary pathology here is a **right-sided pleural effusion with associated compressive atelectasis/consolidation**. Sliding is preserved, which argues against pneumothorax and is consistent with fluid-related lung collapse rather than a fixed pleural process.
