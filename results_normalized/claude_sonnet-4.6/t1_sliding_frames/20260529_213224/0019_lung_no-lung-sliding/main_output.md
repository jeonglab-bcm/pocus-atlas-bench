# 0019_lung_no-lung-sliding

## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Frames 1–3
- Pleural line is visible as a bright echogenic interface at ~1.0–1.5 cm depth
- Sub-pleural region is relatively **homogeneous and hypoechoic**
- A rounded hypoechoic structure is visible at the left edge (likely a vessel in cross-section)
- A-lines are faintly present below the pleural line

### Frames 4–5
- **Notable transition**: sub-pleural echogenicity begins to **increase and heterogenize**
- The pleural line texture appears to subtly brighten/shift
- This corresponds with the beginning of a respiratory phase change

### Frames 6–8
- Sub-pleural speckle pattern **demonstrably different** from frames 1–3
- The pleural line shows a slightly more granular/textured quality
- The overall acoustic shadow below the pleural line changes — consistent with lung movement into the field

### Frames 9–10
- Progressive increase in sub-pleural echogenicity
- Pleural interface is **brighter and less uniformly smooth** than in the first frames
- Comet-tail micro-artifacts appear to originate near the pleural line

## Key Observations

| Feature | Finding |
|---|---|
| Pleural line texture | Varies — shimmering noted in later frames |
| Sub-pleural speckle | **Changes between consecutive frames** |
| A-lines | Present but not perfectly static |
| Barcode/stratosphere sign | **Not observed** |
| Static, smooth pleural line throughout | **No** |

---

## Conclusion

> **✅ Pleural Sliding: PRESENT**

The sequential variation in sub-pleural speckle pattern, the progressive brightening and textural change of the pleural line across frames, and the absence of a persistent barcode sign all indicate **active visceral-parietal pleural sliding**, consistent with a **normal lung ultrasound** at this window.

**No evidence of pneumothorax at this location.**
