## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Early Frames (2/270 – 116/270)

| Feature | Observation |
|---|---|
| Pleural line texture | **Bright, granular, shimmering** interface |
| Sub-pleural speckle | **Clearly changes** between consecutive frames |
| Deep artifacts | Heterogeneous, non-static pattern below pleural line |
| Notable structure | Rounded hypoechoic area visible upper-right (likely consolidated/atelectatic lung or lesion) — **shifts position across frames** |

→ These features are **consistent with pleural sliding PRESENT**

---

### Transitional Frames (~116/270 – 147/270)

- The rounded sub-pleural structure **begins to disappear**
- The pleural line progressively **loses its granular texture**
- Sub-pleural speckle variation **diminishes noticeably**

→ **Transition zone**

---

### Late Frames (174/270 – 265/270)

| Feature | Observation |
|---|---|
| Pleural line texture | **Smooth, sharply defined, static** |
| Sub-pleural speckle | **No variation** between consecutive frames |
| Deep artifacts | Horizontal, repetitive **A-line** pattern — perfectly replicated frame to frame |
| Overall image | Progressively darker, uniform, and motionless below the pleural line |

→ These features are **consistent with pleural sliding ABSENT**

---

## Conclusion: **BOTH — Lung Point Present** ⚠️

The clip demonstrates a **clear spatial transition**:
- **Sliding present** in early frames (shimmering pleural line, dynamic sub-pleural speckle, moving structures)
- **Sliding absent** in late frames (smooth static pleural line, pure A-line reverberation, no sub-pleural variation)

This **alternating pattern across a single sweep** is **pathognomonic for a Lung Point**, which is the **only ultrasound sign with 100% specificity for pneumothorax**. The lung point represents the exact boundary where collapsed (non-sliding) lung meets the chest wall, adjacent to still-aerated (sliding) lung.

> **Clinical implication: Pneumothorax confirmed by lung point identification.**
