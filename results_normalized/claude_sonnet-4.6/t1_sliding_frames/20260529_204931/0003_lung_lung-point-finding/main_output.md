# Sequential Lung Ultrasound Frame Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame Observations

### Frames 1–3 (Early sequence)
- The pleural line presents as a **bright, slightly textured/shimmering interface**
- Sub-pleural speckle pattern shows **subtle variation** between consecutive frames
- Deeper regions show relatively uniform echogenicity
- No clearly identifiable B-lines but some comet-tail-like artifacts appear at the pleural line

### Frames 4–6 (Mid sequence)
- The pleural line maintains its **bright, granular character**
- **Sub-pleural speckle begins to vary more noticeably** between frames
- Deeper echogenicity starts to increase — consistent with lung tissue moving into the acoustic window during expiration

### Frames 7–10 (Late sequence)
- The pleural line continues to appear **textured/shimmering** rather than smooth and static
- **Increasing echogenicity in the deeper field** — likely representing consolidated/aerated lung or diaphragmatic structures moving with respiration
- Progressive changes in the sub-pleural zone between frames confirm **dynamic movement**

## Key Signs Summary

| Sign | Observation |

| Sub-pleural speckle | **Changes between frames** |

---

## Conclusion

> ✅ **Pleural sliding is PRESENT**

The pleural line demonstrates a shimmering, granular quality throughout all frames, and the sub-pleural speckle pattern varies between consecutive images — both hallmarks of normal lung sliding. The progressive change in deeper echogenicity further supports dynamic respiratory movement. There is **no evidence of absent sliding** (no stratosphere sign, no perfectly static and smooth pleural line).
