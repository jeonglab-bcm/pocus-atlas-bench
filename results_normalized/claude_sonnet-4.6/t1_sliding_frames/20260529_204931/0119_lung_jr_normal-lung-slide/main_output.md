## Key Findings

### Signs of ABSENT Sliding:
- ✅ **Pleural line**: Smooth, sharply demarcated, and **largely static** across frames
- ✅ **A-lines**: Prominent, regular, horizontally stacked reverberation artifacts — appearing **consistent and unchanged** across frames (consistent with sub-pleural air)
- ✅ **Sub-pleural speckle**: Negligible frame-to-frame variation in the deep field
- ✅ **No B-lines** (vertical comet-tail artifacts) originating from the pleural line
- ✅ No seashore sign equivalent; pattern is consistent with a **2D barcode/stratosphere analog**

### Signs Against Sliding Present:
- ❌ No granular or shimmering pleural line texture
- ❌ No comet-tail / B-line artifacts
- ❌ No significant inter-frame speckle variation below the pleural line

### Signs of Lung Point:
- ⚠️ Frames 8–10 show *minor* variation in pleural line position/brightness — this could represent a subtle **lung point** at the edge of the scanning window, but is inconclusive without M-mode

---

## Conclusion

> **Pleural sliding is ABSENT** across the majority of these frames.

The combination of a **smooth, static pleural line** + **prominent, unchanging A-lines** + **no sub-pleural speckle variation** + **absence of B-lines** constitutes the classic 2D ultrasound pattern of **absent lung sliding**.

### Clinical Interpretation:
In the appropriate clinical context, this pattern raises concern for:
1. **Pneumothorax** (highest priority)
2. Mainstem intubation (contralateral lung)
3. Severe bullous emphysema
4. Pleural adhesions / pleurodesis
5. Apnea/breath-holding

> ⚠️ **Recommendation**: M-mode examination to confirm **stratosphere/barcode sign**, and assessment for a **lung point** (pathognomonic for pneumothorax) would be strongly advised for definitive diagnosis.
