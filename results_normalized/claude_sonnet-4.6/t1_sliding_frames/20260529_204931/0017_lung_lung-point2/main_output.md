# Lung Ultrasound Sequential Frame Analysis

## Frame-by-Frame B-Mode Observations

### Frames 1–4 (Early temporal sweep, cursor ~−4 to −2.5)
- Pleural line appears **smooth, sharply defined, and static**
- Sub-pleural speckle pattern shows **minimal variation** between frames
- A-lines remain essentially unchanged
- No comet-tail artifacts or B-lines arising from pleural line
- M-mode pattern below pleural line: **predominantly horizontal parallel lines → Barcode/Stratosphere sign**

### Frames 5–7 (Mid sweep, cursor ~−2 to −1)
- **Transition zone visible**: Right side of pleural line begins to show a curved, brighter echogenic structure (visceral pleura intermittently entering the field)
- Pleural line texture begins to show subtle granularity on the right portion
- M-mode: **transition from barcode toward mixed pattern**

### Frames 8–10 (Late sweep, cursor ~−1 to 0)
- Pleural line on the right becomes **shimmering and textured**
- Sub-pleural speckle pattern shows **clear variation between frames**
- The rounded echogenic structure (lung edge) is more prominent, moving in and out
- M-mode: **granular sandy pattern below the pleural line → Seashore sign emerging**

## Conclusion

> ## ⚠️ LUNG POINT — Present
> 
> These frames demonstrate a **lung point**: a pathognomonic transition where **pleural sliding is absent on one portion** (barcode sign, static pleural line) and **present on another** (seashore sign, shimmering pleural line). The M-mode displays alternating barcode and seashore patterns as the lung cyclically slides in and out of frame at this specific location.
> 
> **The lung point sign is 100% specific for pneumothorax.** It represents the precise anatomical boundary between the pneumothorax (where visceral and parietal pleura are separated by air) and normal aerated lung.
