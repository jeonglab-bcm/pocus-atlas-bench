## Key Findings

### ✅ Signs of Sliding PRESENT (Left/Central Region)
- **Shimmering, granular pleural line** in the left-central portion across multiple frames
- **Frame-to-frame variation in sub-pleural speckle** — the pattern beneath the pleural line demonstrably changes between consecutive frames on the left side
- Slight comet-tail-type artifacts originating from the pleural line in frames 1–2 and 6–7

### 🚫 Signs of Sliding ABSENT (Right Region)
- A **smooth, sharply defined, static pleural line** segment on the right in frames 3–5 and 8–10
- **No sub-pleural speckle variation** on the right side
- **A-lines appear relatively undisturbed** in the right sub-pleural region
- The right-sided pleural line does not shimmer or change texture between frames

### ⚠️ Sign of BOTH — **Lung Point**
- There is a **visible transition zone** where:
  - The **left/central pleural line actively shimmers** (sliding present)
  - The **right-sided segment is static and sharp** (sliding absent)
- This transition **oscillates** with the respiratory cycle — the dark anechoic wedge on the right **appears and disappears** rhythmically across frames (frames 3–5 vs. 6–7, then 8–10)
- This is the **lung point sign** — the margin of a partially collapsed lung periodically swinging into contact with the chest wall

---

## 🔴 Conclusion

> **LUNG POINT IDENTIFIED**
> 
> The sequential frames demonstrate **pleural sliding present on the left/central region** and **absent on the right**, with a **dynamic transition point** that shifts with respiration. This pattern is **pathognomonic for pneumothorax** — the lung point marks the lateral boundary of the pneumothorax, where the partially collapsed lung intermittently re-contacts the parietal pleura during the respiratory cycle.

**Clinical implication:** The presence of a lung point confirms pneumothorax and also allows estimation of its size — the more lateral the lung point, the larger the pneumothorax.
