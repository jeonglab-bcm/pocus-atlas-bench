| Frames | Pleural Line Appearance | Sub-pleural Region | Notable Features |
|--------|------------------------|-------------------|-----------------|

---

## Key Findings

### ✅ Signs Supporting Sliding **PRESENT**
- **Inter-frame sub-pleural speckle variation**: The texture pattern below the pleural line changes between consecutive frames — characteristic of lung-chest wall motion
- **Progressive change in deep structure echogenicity** across the respiratory cycle
- **Pleural line shows subtle texture/granularity** (not completely smooth and static)
- **A-lines vary** in prominence across frames (appearing in later frames), consistent with normal lung cycling in and out of the focal zone

### ⚠️ Features to Note
- In later frames (9–10), the pleural line appears **slightly sharper and smoother** with prominent A-lines — this is normal for expiration phases
- No B-lines identified
- No discrete transition (lung point) is evident

---

## Conclusion

> **Pleural sliding: PRESENT**

The inter-frame variation in sub-pleural speckle pattern is the primary indicator of lung sliding. The evolving appearance from early to late frames reflects normal **respiratory cycling** — lung contact during inspiration producing speckle variation, with A-lines more prominent during expiration. This is **not consistent with pneumothorax** (which would show a completely static, unchanging pleural line and fixed A-lines across all frames).
