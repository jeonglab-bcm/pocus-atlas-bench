## Key Findings

### ✅ Signs of Sliding PRESENT

1. **Cyclic sector width expansion** — The ultrasound cone dramatically widens (frames 4–6) then narrows again (frames 8–10), representing the aerated lung sliding in and out of the probe's acoustic window with respiration

2. **Shimmering/granular pleural line** — Most prominent in frames 4–6, the pleural line becomes bright, textured, and irregular — the hallmark of a sliding pleural interface

3. **Sub-pleural speckle variation** — The tissue below the pleural line shows marked frame-to-frame changes in echogenicity and granularity (dense in frames 4–6, sparse in frames 1, 8–10), consistent with dynamic B-mode sliding

4. **Respiratory periodicity** — The cycle of narrow→wide→narrow maps to a complete respiratory excursion, confirmed against the ECG rhythm strip

### ❌ Signs of Absent Sliding — NOT Present
- No static, perfectly smooth, unchanging pleural line
- No barcode/stratosphere pattern
- A-lines are **not** fixed across all frames

### ⚪ Lung Point — NOT Present
- No transition zone within a frame where one region slides and another does not

---

## Conclusion

> **Pleural sliding is PRESENT**

The sequential frames demonstrate clear dynamic changes consistent with normal visceral-parietal pleural apposition and sliding. The periodic expansion of the acoustic window, shimmering pleural line, and varying sub-pleural speckle are all incompatible with pneumothorax (absent sliding). This study is **negative for pneumothorax** at this probe location.
