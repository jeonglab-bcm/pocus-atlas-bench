## Frame-by-Frame Analysis

### Pleural Line Characteristics
Across all 10 frames, the pleural line presents as a **bright, smooth, sharply defined hyperechoic line** running horizontally through the mid-field. Its position and brightness remain **remarkably consistent** from frame to frame.

### Sub-Pleural Region
- **A-lines** (horizontal reverberation artifacts) are visible below the pleural line in all frames
- These A-lines appear **static and unchanged** across consecutive frames
- The sub-pleural **speckle pattern shows minimal variation** between frames despite apparent chest wall movement above

### Chest Wall vs. Pleural Interface
- The superficial layers (muscles/soft tissue) **do show some inter-frame variation**, confirming these are temporally distinct frames
- Despite this chest wall motion, the **pleural line and sub-pleural pattern remain fixed** — the two layers do not appear to move relative to each other

### Absent Signs
- ❌ No shimmering or granularity at the pleural line
- ❌ No B-lines or comet-tail artifacts
- ❌ No dynamic speckle variation below the pleural line
- ❌ No visible lung point transition

---

## Conclusion: **Pleural Sliding ABSENT**

The combination of a **smooth, static pleural line** with **unchanging A-lines** and **no sub-pleural speckle variation** is consistent with the **stratosphere/barcode pattern equivalent** on B-mode, indicating **absent lung sliding**.

> ⚠️ **Clinical correlation required** — absent sliding raises concern for **pneumothorax**, but can also be seen with pleurodesis, pleural adhesions, main-stem intubation, or apnea.
